Future Sight is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2007 that ended the Time Spiral block and its long Dominaria storyline. Its gimmick was a preview of Magic's possible futures: 81 "futureshifted" cards in a special frame showed off mechanics that didn't exist yet, and several of those ideas, along with Tarmogoyf and a cycle of Pacts, ended up shaping Modern and Legacy for years.

## The story

The Time Spiral block is set on Dominaria about three centuries after the Phyrexian invasion and the Apocalypse that ended it. Those wars, and the time magic used during them, tore holes in reality. **Time rifts** opened across the plane, mana dried up, whole regions were trapped in time, and the rifts threatened to spread to the rest of the Multiverse. Through Time Spiral and Planar Chaos, a group of planeswalkers and mortal heroes worked to seal the rifts one by one, often at terrible cost. By the start of Future Sight, Teferi had given up his planeswalker spark to close one rift, Freyalise and Lord Windgrace had sacrificed themselves to close others, and Karn had gone missing while trying to seal the rift over Tolaria.

Future Sight, told in the novel of the same name by Scott McGough and John Delaney, covers the last rifts. The planeswalker **Jeska**, who had lived earlier lives as Phage and as the demigod Karona, grew impatient and acted alone. Pushed by the scheming planeswalker **Leshrac**, she captured the young Keldon warrior **Radha**, who had an unignited spark, and used her to seal a rift at an enormous cost in lives. Leshrac then tried to take power for himself but was outplayed by the elder dragon **Nicol Bolas**, who used Leshrac's own spark to seal another rift before leaving Dominaria.

The final rift was sealed by Jeska together with Radha and the artificer **Venser**, whose spark ignites during the story. Jeska gave up her spark, and her life, to repair the damage. That act is known as **the Mending**, and it changed planeswalkers across the Multiverse: they lost the near-godlike power of older walkers like Urza, which is why the planeswalkers on modern cards are powerful but mortal. Venser left as a new planeswalker, Teferi set out to make amends for his past, and Jhoira went looking for the archmage Jodah. The set's dark, apocalyptic look came from that last stand.

## Mechanics

### Futureshifted cards

Future Sight's 81 futureshifted cards used a sleek alternate frame and showed mechanics, card types and themes Wizards might print one day. Some were one-offs. Others became real: **Tarmogoyf** looked like an odd card at the time, **Thornweald Archer** printed deathtouch as a keyword, and **Horizon Canopy** previewed a land design that Modern Horizons later turned into a full cycle. The joke card **Steamflogger Boss** even referenced Contraptions, a card type that didn't exist until Unstable a decade later.

### Delve

Delve lets you exile cards from your graveyard to pay for generic mana in a spell's cost, one card per mana. **Logic Knot** is a counterspell whose X can be paid by exiling cards, and **Tombstalker** is a big flier made cheap by a full graveyard. Delve was such a strong design that it returned in Khans of Tarkir, where it produced some of Modern's most powerful spells.

### Grandeur

Grandeur appears on legendary creatures: you discard another copy of the same card for a bonus effect. **Korlash, Heir to Blackblade** grows with your Swamps and, with grandeur, ramps you by searching for Swamps. Grandeur gave a use to the extra copies of a legend you couldn't keep on the battlefield at the same time.

### The other keywords

The set's preview keywords included **absorb** (prevent some damage to a creature; Lymph Sliver gives it to all Slivers), **aura swap** (exchange an Aura on the battlefield with one in your hand; Arcanum Wings), **fortify** (Equipment for lands; Darksteel Garrison), **frenzy** (a bonus when the creature isn't blocked; Frenzy Sliver), **gravestorm** (copy a spell once for each permanent put into a graveyard this turn; Bitter Ordeal), **poisonous** (give poison counters on combat damage; Virulent Sliver) and **transfigure** (sacrifice a creature to search for another with the same mana value; Fleshwrither). Future Sight also kept the block's suspend, split second, flash and Sliver themes. For a guide to the keywords that survived, see the [MTG keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary).

### The Pacts

Five rares cost `{0}` now and make you pay later: at the start of your next upkeep you must pay their real cost or lose the game. **Pact of Negation** counters a spell, **Summoner's Pact** finds a green creature, **Slaughter Pact** destroys a nonblack creature, **Pact of the Titan** makes a 4/4 Giant, and **Intervention Pact** prevents damage. Combo decks love them because the payment never comes due if you win first.

## Limited

Time Spiral block was drafted oldest set first, as all block drafts were until Mirrodin Besieged: Time Spiral, then Planar Chaos, then Future Sight. The block was known for its complexity: suspend, morph, split second, Slivers, timeshifted reprints and Future Sight's preview mechanics all shared the same table, and drafters needed a strong grasp of the rules.

Future Sight's commons and uncommons included solid removal and value creatures. **Death Rattle** was delve removal for nongreen creatures, **Fatal Attraction** finished off most early creatures, **Riddle of Lightning** used scry to deal damage, and **Thornweald Archer** was a cheap blocker that could trade with anything. The block's Slivers spread across all colors, so Sliver decks could form when packs cooperated. Specific draft archetypes for each color pair are not well documented for this block, so think in terms of tempo, card advantage and the morph and suspend mind games rather than set signposts. For general drafting advice, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Standard (2007-2008).** Future Sight entered a Standard full of Time Spiral block cards, and its impact grew over the following year. Tarmogoyf quickly became a staple for green decks, and Magus of the Moon punished control decks built on nonbasic lands, especially in red-green aggro. Venser, Shaper Savant became a flexible blue answer, bouncing either a spell or a permanent. Future Sight rotated out of Standard with the rest of the block when Shards of Alara arrived in October 2008. No Future Sight cards were banned in Standard.

**Modern.** Future Sight is among the most influential pre-Modern-era sets in Modern. Tarmogoyf was a defining creature for most of the format's early history. Street Wraith is a key part of Living End and similar cascade decks because it can be cycled without mana. Summoner's Pact is part of Amulet Titan, Pact of Negation appears in combo decks, Horizon Canopy and Grove of the Burnwillows are useful lands, and Aven Mindcensor and Magus of the Moon have had their seasons. Bridge from Below is banned in Modern as of October 2026, and Grove of the Burnwillows's pairing with Zendikar's Punishing Fire is part of why that burn spell is also banned there.

**Legacy and Vintage.** Narcomoeba and Bridge from Below are core pieces of Dredge. Dryad Arbor is a creature that green tutors like Natural Order can find. Sword of the Meek combines with Thopter Foundry for an infinite combo. Tarmogoyf was a mainstay of Legacy fair decks for years.

**Commander.** Akroma's Memorial, Sliver Legion, Summoner's Pact, Rites of Flourishing and the futureshifted lands are the most common sights.

**Pauper.** Narcomoeba is banned in Pauper.

For the rest of the block, see our guides to [Time Spiral](/blog/time-spiral-mtg-set-guide) and [Planar Chaos](/blog/planar-chaos-mtg-set-guide).

## Cards from Future Sight worth owning

- **Tarmogoyf**: a historic staple, and Standard-legal again as of October 2026.
- **Street Wraith**: the free cycler that makes Living End work.
- **Summoner's Pact** and **Pact of Negation**: combo pieces in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Horizon Canopy**: a strong land in Modern and Legacy.
- **Dryad Arbor**: a Legacy and Commander utility land.
- **Akroma's Memorial** and **Sliver Legion**: Commander favorites.

## The takeaway

Future Sight was a gamble: a set full of cards that pointed at a future that hadn't been designed yet. It paid off. Delve, deathtouch, the Pacts and Tarmogoyf all outlived the block, and the Mending changed every planeswalker that came after. If you want to play its cards now, start with Modern, where Street Wraith and Tarmogoyf still have homes, and see our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) before you sketch a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
