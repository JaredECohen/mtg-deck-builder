In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), a Goblins deck is a red tribal deck that wins by flooding the battlefield with cheap Goblin creatures, then making them bigger or faster with lords and pump effects until the opponent can't block them all. Most of its cards cost one to three mana, many make extra bodies, and several reward you for each other Goblin you control. In Commander, the same idea scales up around Krenko, Mob Boss, which can turn a modest board into dozens of tokens in a few turns.

If you're new to creature-type decks in general, the [tribal decks guide](/blog/mtg-tribal-decks-guide) covers how they work across every type. This guide focuses on Goblins: what makes the tribe tick, the card roles to fill, how to play it, and how the deck changes in Commander.

## Why Goblins work

Goblins have been part of Magic since the very first sets, and they have one of the deepest card pools of any creature type. Three things define the tribe:

- **They're cheap.** A huge number of Goblins cost one or two mana. That lets you deploy several creatures in the first few turns.
- **They come in groups.** Many Goblins create Goblin tokens when they enter, when they die or when they attack. One card often means two or three bodies.
- **They reward each other.** Lords and other payoffs scale with the number of Goblins you control, so a wide board gets stronger with every creature you add.

The downside is that individual Goblins are small and fragile. Most are 1/1s or 2/2s. A Goblins deck depends on going wide fast enough that losing a few creatures doesn't matter, and on finishing the game before the opponent stabilizes.

## The roles in a Goblins deck

Good tribal decks fill specific roles, not just "play every Goblin." For Goblins, those roles look like this.

### Aggressive one-drops

Your best starts begin on turn one. Cards that matter here:

- **Goblin Guide:** a one-mana 2/2 with haste. When it attacks, the defending player reveals the top card of their library, and if it's a land, they put it into their hand. That drawback is real, but the early damage is usually worth it.
- **Goblin Lackey:** a one-mana 1/1. Whenever it deals damage to a player, you may put a Goblin permanent card from your hand onto the battlefield. If it connects on turn two, you can cheat a much bigger threat into play.
- **Goblin Bushwhacker:** a one-mana 1/1 with kicker. If you pay the extra red mana when you cast it, your creatures get +1/+0 and haste until end of turn. It works as a cheap creature early or a surprise finisher late.

### Lords

A lord is a creature that boosts the other members of its type. Goblins have several, and they're why a board of 1/1s becomes lethal:

- **Goblin King:** other Goblins get +1/+1 and mountainwalk, which makes them unblockable against an opponent who controls a Mountain.
- **Goblin Chieftain:** a 2/2 with haste that gives other Goblins +1/+1 and haste.
- **Goblin Warchief:** makes your Goblin spells cost one less and gives your Goblins haste. It's less a size boost and more an engine for dumping your hand quickly.

Haste matters more than usual in this deck. Goblins that can attack the turn they enter dodge sorcery-speed removal and punish opponents who tap out.

### Token makers

Token makers give you extra bodies, which turn every lord into a bigger threat:

- **Mogg War Marshal:** a two-mana 1/1 with echo that creates a 1/1 Goblin token when it enters or dies. Even if you don't pay the echo, you end up with two bodies.
- **Krenko's Command:** a two-mana sorcery that creates two 1/1 Goblin tokens.
- **Beetleback Chief:** a four-mana 2/2 that brings two 1/1 Goblin tokens with it.
- **Siege-Gang Commander:** a five-mana 2/2 that creates three 1/1 Goblin tokens when it enters, and can pay mana and sacrifice a Goblin to deal 2 damage to any target.

For more on building around tokens generally, see the [tokens deck guide](/blog/mtg-tokens-deck-guide).

### Card advantage and tutors

Aggressive decks run out of cards. Goblins solve this with creatures that find other Goblins:

- **Goblin Matron:** when it enters, you search your library for a Goblin card and put it into your hand.
- **Goblin Ringleader:** when it enters, you reveal the top four cards of your library and put all the Goblins among them into your hand.

These turn one card into two and let you assemble the right lord or finisher at the right time.

### Sacrifice outlets and reach

Small creatures that will die anyway are perfect fuel for sacrifice effects:

- **Skirk Prospector:** sacrifice a Goblin to add one red mana. It lets you cast big spells or a burst of creatures earlier than normal.
- **Goblin Bombardment:** an enchantment that lets you sacrifice a creature to deal 1 damage to any target. With a wide board, that's a lot of damage the opponent can't block.

This overlap with sacrifice strategies is real. The [aristocrats guide](/blog/aristocrats-sacrifice-deck-guide) explains how those engines work in more depth.

## How to play a Goblins deck

### The early turns

Your goal is to make the opponent react to you from turn one. A typical strong start is a one-drop on turn one, two cheap Goblins or a token maker on turn two, and a lord on turn three. By turn four you want enough power on the battlefield to threaten lethal within a turn or two.

Mulligan aggressively for speed. A seven-card hand with three lands and four Goblins that cost three or more is slower than it looks.

### Timing your lords

Lords are the deck's force multipliers, so their timing matters:

- **Play them when you have bodies to boost.** A lord on an empty board is just a medium creature.
- **Use haste lords for surprise damage.** Casting Goblin Chieftain before combat can add a lot of unexpected damage.
- **Play around board wipes.** Against decks with sweepers, hold back a lord or a token maker so you can rebuild after a wipe.

### Know when to stop attacking

Goblins wants to attack, but not into a bad board. If the opponent has a big blocker, throwing 1/1s into it loses creatures for nothing. Build up for a turn, add a lord, then swing with everything when the math works.

### The weaknesses

- **Sweepers.** A card like Pyroclasm, which deals 2 damage to each creature, can wipe out most of a Goblin board at once.
- **Lifegain.** Every point of life the opponent gains costs you another attack.
- **Large blockers.** One big creature can wall several small ones.

The [how to play against aggro guide](/blog/how-to-play-against-aggro) is written from the other side of the table, and it's a useful look at what opponents will try to do to you.

## Goblins in Commander

Commander is a 100-card singleton format, so you can't run four copies of your best Goblins. You get one of each, plus a commander that's always available. That changes the deck in two ways: your commander becomes your most important engine, and you need far more total Goblins and token makers to make the tribe work.

### Krenko, Mob Boss

Krenko, Mob Boss is the classic Goblins commander. It costs four mana and has an ability that taps to create a number of 1/1 red Goblin tokens equal to the number of Goblins you control. Each activation counts Krenko itself, so the token count roughly doubles every turn you activate it.

What that means for building around it:

- **Haste is critical.** Krenko doesn't have haste, so it normally can't use its tap ability the turn it enters. Cards that give haste, like Lightning Greaves, let you activate it immediately and also protect it from targeted removal.
- **Untap effects multiply it.** Anything that untaps Krenko lets you activate it again in the same turn.
- **Protect it.** Krenko is a fragile 3/3. If it dies early, you lose your engine, so include some ways to protect it.
- **Have a way to cash in the tokens.** A board of fifty 1/1s doesn't always win by itself. Mass pump effects, haste-granting effects and damage engines like Impact Tremors, which deals 1 damage to each opponent whenever a creature enters under your control, turn the tokens into a win.

### Other Goblin commanders

Krenko isn't the only option. Krenko, Tin Street Kingpin grows as it attacks and makes tokens based on its power. Muxus, Goblin Grandee reveals cards from the top of your library and puts Goblins from among them onto the battlefield, which suits a deck full of high-impact Goblins. Each pushes the deck toward a slightly different plan, but all of them want lots of Goblins and something to do with them.

If you've never built a Commander deck before, the general process starts with picking a commander and a plan, then filling ramp, card draw, removal and synergy slots around it. You can also start a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/) and refine it from there.

### Threat assessment

Krenko decks draw attention. Once other players at a multiplayer table see an unanswered Krenko, it often becomes the first target for removal. Plan for that by keeping a backup engine and enough card draw to rebuild.

## The takeaway

Goblins is a go-wide aggro deck built on cheap creatures, lords and token makers. Start fast, play your lords when there are bodies to boost, and finish the game before sweepers and big blockers catch up. In Commander, build around Krenko, Mob Boss with haste, protection and a way to turn a mountain of tokens into damage.
