Eventide is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in July 2008, the second and final set of the Shadowmoor block and the end of the four-set Lorwyn-Shadowmoor story. It finishes the tale of the faerie queen Oona on the darkened plane of Shadowmoor, and mechanically it swaps Shadowmoor's allied-color hybrid cards for enemy-color hybrid, giving us Figure of Destiny, Glen Elendra Archmage, Flickerwisp and five filter lands.

## The story

Lorwyn was a sunny, idyllic plane of elves, kithkin, giants and faeries. Every few centuries an event called the Great Aurora flips it into its dark mirror, Shadowmoor, and most of its inhabitants forget the world was ever any different. At the end of Morningtide, the aurora came, and Shadowmoor and Eventide take place in the long night that followed. In Eventide the change is complete: hags, selkies, duergar and hobgoblins thrive in the gloom, while the elves and kithkin retreat behind their walls.

The villain behind it all is **Oona**, the queen and mother of the faeries. She had secretly been steering the auroras for millennia so that she alone would keep her memories and her power across both halves of the plane. To survive this aurora she had grown a faerie duplicate of herself, **Maralen**, but Maralen developed a will of her own and turned against her creator.

The flamekin **Ashling**, whose ritual at the moment of the aurora went wrong when she seized the power of a great fire elemental for herself, became **Ashling, the Extinguisher**, a destroyer who burned her way across Shadowmoor. In Eventide, Maralen, the kithkin **Brigid Baeli**, the merrow captain **Sygg** and a water spirit called the Source of the Wanderbrine finally subdue her. Oona tries to steal Ashling's elemental power; Maralen takes it instead, and Ashling, a flamekin again, joins the fight against the queen.

The final campaign takes them to Oona's stronghold. The elf **Rhys**, Ashling and the **Sapling of Colfenor** slip into Glen Elendra while Maralen battles Oona in the sky. With the Sapling's sacrifice and the combined power of Maralen, the elemental and the Source, Oona's body is destroyed. Maralen becomes the new faerie queen and day returns to the plane. Oona's consciousness survives in the land itself, a thread picked up decades later in Lorwyn Eclipsed. The source is the novel *Eventide* by Cory J. Herndon and Scott McGough, published in June 2008.

## Mechanics

### Enemy-color hybrid mana

Hybrid symbols like `{R/W}` can be paid with either color. Shadowmoor used the five allied pairs; Eventide uses the enemy pairs: white-black, blue-red, black-green, red-white and green-blue. A card like **Figure of Destiny** fits a mono-red deck, a mono-white deck or anything in between. The set's cycles of Lieges, Hedge-Mages, Hatchlings and Mimics are all built on these pairs.

### Retrace

You can cast a card with retrace from your graveyard by discarding a land card as an extra cost. Late-game lands become extra spells. **Raven's Crime** is a one-mana discard spell you can keep recasting; **Flame Jab** does the same with 1 damage; **Monstrify** gives +4/+4.

### Chroma

Chroma is an ability word for effects that count mana symbols of one color. **Light from Within** gives each of your creatures +1/+1 for each white symbol in its cost, and **Primalcrux** is a `{G}{G}{G}{G}{G}{G}` trampler whose size equals the green symbols among your permanents.

### Persist, wither, the untap symbol and -1/-1 counters

These return from Shadowmoor. Persist brings a creature back once with a -1/-1 counter if it had none, as **Glen Elendra Archmage** does after it sacrifices itself to counter a noncreature spell. Wither deals damage to creatures as -1/-1 counters, as on **Puncture Blast**. The untap symbol (shown on cards as a white Q) is a cost that untaps a permanent instead of tapping it. A common cycle of "untappers" rewards casting spells of their color; **Nettle Sentinel** is a 2/2 for one that only untaps when you cast a green spell. For more keywords, see our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Eventide was drafted together with Shadowmoor in the block format. Because the two sets covered all ten color pairs between them, Shadowmoor gave the allied pairs and Eventide the enemy pairs, and decks often centered on a pair with heavy hybrid support. Monocolored decks were unusually viable too, since hybrid cards could go in either of their colors, and the chroma cards rewarded staying deep in one.

Each enemy pair had a recognizable theme: white-black, like every pair, had a Mimic and a Hatchling that rewarded casting spells of its colors; blue-red Noggles were evasive and tricky; black-green leaned on -1/-1 counters and persist; red-white went wide with tokens and double strike; green-blue wanted big creatures and card advantage. Strong commons included **Unmake**, **Puncture Blast**, **Noggle Bandit** and the retrace spells, which turned flooding into card advantage. Hybrid removal was precious because almost every deck could cast it. Our [draft vs sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide) covers how block formats like this one were played.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

At release, Eventide got a lukewarm review for Constructed; one columnist called it among the weakest Constructed sets in years. Figure of Destiny was the exception. It slotted straight into Kithkin and red aggro decks, and coverage of the time credits it with reviving white-based beatdown. Standard in late 2008 and 2009 revolved around Faeries, Five-Color Control, Kithkin, Reveillark, Doran and Elves. Glen Elendra Archmage and Stillmoon Cavalier earned sideboard and control slots, and the filter lands smoothed mana for multicolor decks until the October 2009 rotation.

Its biggest immediate splash came in Extended. At Pro Tour Berlin in November 2008, Luis Scott-Vargas won with an Elves combo deck; six of the eight quarterfinalists played some version of it. Nettle Sentinel, untapping with each green spell alongside Heritage Druid and Glimpse of Nature, was the engine that made the deck work.

As of October 2026, here is where Eventide still matters:

- **Modern**: Slippery Bogle is a core creature in Bogles (also called Hexproof). Flickerwisp shows up in blink and creature-value decks.
- **Pauper**: Nettle Sentinel is a staple of Elves, and Slippery Bogle and Unmake are Pauper-legal commons.
- **Commander**: the filter lands are common in two-color decks, and Glen Elendra Archmage, Flickerwisp, Murkfiend Liege, Creakwood Liege and Regal Force are well-loved.
- **Legacy and Vintage**: Eventide cards appear here mainly through Elves (Nettle Sentinel) and occasional Flickerwisp builds.

No Eventide card is banned or restricted in Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper or Commander. For format basics, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained), and for more on the story's return, the [Lorwyn Eclipsed set guide](/blog/lorwyn-eclipsed-mtg-set-guide).

## Cards from Eventide worth owning

- **The five filter lands**: they fix mana in any multicolor Commander deck.
- **Figure of Destiny**: a flexible one-drop that never stops being useful in red or white aggro.
- **Glen Elendra Archmage**: a sticky counterspell on a creature, great in Commander.
- **Flickerwisp**: a staple blink creature with lots of tricks.
- **Slippery Bogle**: the cheapest hexproof creature for Bogles in Modern and Pauper.
- **Nettle Sentinel**: a key Elves piece in Pauper.
- **Murkfiend Liege**: a green-blue lord that untaps your team every turn.
