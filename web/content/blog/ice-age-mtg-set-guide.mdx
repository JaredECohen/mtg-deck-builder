Ice Age is a Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1995, and it was the first standalone expansion: a set you could open and play with by itself, basic lands and all. It took the game to a frozen Dominaria in the centuries after the Brothers' War, introduced snow-covered lands, cumulative upkeep and the cantrip, and printed some of the most influential cards ever made, including Brainstorm, Necropotence, Demonic Consultation and the original painlands.

## The setting and flavor

Ice Age doesn't have a plot in the modern sense. There were no main characters on the cards driving a story arc, and the tie-in fiction of the time (short stories in The Duelist magazine and a comic series) has since been treated as loose, older canon. What it does have is a strong, consistent world.

The Brothers' War between Urza and Mishra ended with the Sylex Blast, an explosion so powerful it changed Dominaria's climate. Centuries later, the continent of Terisiare is buried under glaciers and blizzards. The great civilizations of the war era are gone, and new cultures are struggling to survive on the ice. The set's look borrows heavily from Norse myth and culture, with Scandinavian-style names, runes and armor.

Each color has a home:

- **White** is Kjeldor, a kingdom of knights and soldiers, seen on cards like Kjeldoran Royal Guard, Kjeldoran Skycaptain and the legendary General Jarkeld.
- **Red** is Balduvia, a land of hardy barbarians and shamans, like Balduvian Barbarians and Balduvian Hydra.
- **Green** is the Fyndhorn forest and its elves, who worship the planeswalker Freyalise; Fyndhorn Elves and Freyalise's Winds come from here.
- **Black** belongs to Lim-Dûl, a necromancer who raises armies of the dead, shown in cards like Legions of Lim-Dûl and Lim-Dûl's Hex. Lim-Dûl's undead war is the setting's main threat, and it forces old enemies like Kjeldor and Balduvia to cooperate, which is the flavor behind the set's allied-color cards.
- **Blue** has the scholars and illusionists of the north, with cards like Krovikan Sorcerer and Illusions of Grandeur.
- **Artifacts** come from the Soldevi, who dig up and rebuild old war machines, seen in Soldevi Golem.

The story continued in Alliances, released in 1996, and much later in Coldsnap (2006), which was designed as a "lost" third set of the block.

## Mechanics

### Snow-covered lands

Ice Age printed a full set of snow-covered basic lands. On their own they're just basics, but some cards care about them. Snow Devil gives a creature flying and, if you control a snow land, first strike when it blocks. Whiteout returns to your hand if you sacrifice a snow land. Arcum's Weathervane can turn lands snow or not. The theme was added late in development and was thin, so it barely mattered at the time. Its descendants did: snow was revived in Coldsnap and again in Kaldheim (2021), and the snow supertype is still in the rules.

### Cumulative upkeep

A permanent with cumulative upkeep gets an age counter at the start of each of your upkeeps, and you have to pay its upkeep cost once per counter or sacrifice it. It's a cost that grows every turn, which made it a natural drawback for powerful effects. Mystic Remora, a one-mana enchantment that draws you cards when opponents cast noncreature spells, has cumulative upkeep `{1}`; Illusions of Grandeur gains you 20 life up front and costs `{2}` per counter to keep. Polar Kraken makes you sacrifice lands instead of paying mana.

### Cantrips

Ice Age introduced cheap spells that replace themselves with a card. Most of them, like Ray of Erasure, draw the card at the beginning of the next turn's upkeep rather than immediately; designers weren't yet sure an immediate draw wouldn't be too strong. Brainstorm, which draws three and puts two back, is the famous exception: it works immediately and became one of the best cards ever printed.

### Firsts

Ice Age was the first expansion with true reprints (Swords to Plowshares, Counterspell, Dark Ritual, Disenchant, Giant Growth and others, so the set worked on its own), the first with legendary creatures of a single color, the first with a cycle of color hosers (including Hydroblast and Pyroblast) and the first to print the five allied painlands: Adarkar Wastes, Underground River, Sulfurous Springs, Karplusan Forest and Brushland.

## Limited

Ice Age wasn't designed with booster draft in mind; in 1995, sets weren't built to support Limited the way they are today. It was sold in 60-card starter decks and 15-card boosters, and Sealed Deck and casual drafts were played, but there were no designed archetypes for color pairs.

The design goals actually pushed against fast games. The team wanted flying to feel special and games to last longer, so the set has very few evasive creatures. As a result, Ice Age Limited is remembered for long games and ground stalls, decided by the few fliers, big creatures like Balduvian Hydra, and removal such as Incinerate, Swords to Plowshares and Lim-Dûl's Hex. Cumulative upkeep cards forced constant decisions about when to stop paying. If you play it today in a cube or a nostalgia draft, expect slow, grindy games where card advantage matters more than tempo.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Ice Age arrived in a young Type II, the format now called Standard. The Standard timeline on the MTG Wiki records the key moments.

- **Mid-1995.** Ice Age joined Fourth Edition, Fallen Empires and later Chronicles and Homelands in Type II. Zuran Orb proved so good at gaining life in land-destruction and prison decks that it was restricted in November 1995 and eventually banned in 1997.
- **"Necro Summer" (1996).** Once Alliances arrived, the Necro deck dominated Type II. Necropotence let black decks trade life for an enormous amount of card advantage, and the deck paired it with fast mana like Dark Ritual and disruption like Hymn to Tourach. Its reign is still called Necro Summer or Black Summer. Ironically, a magazine at the time had called Necropotence one of the worst rares in the set.
- **Other decks.** Red-green Stormbind decks, prison decks using Zuran Orb and control decks with Jester's Cap were all part of that era.

Ice Age left Type II when new deck construction rules took effect on January 1, 1997, returned for part of 1997 under the revised rules, and then left for good.

Its biggest legacy is in eternal formats and Commander. As of October 2026:

- **Legacy.** Brainstorm is one of the most-played cards in the format; it combines with fetchlands to shuffle away the two cards you put back, which is why blue decks in Legacy play it so often. Swords to Plowshares and Counterspell (this set reprinted both) are staples, Hydroblast and Pyroblast are common sideboard cards, and Fyndhorn Elves supports Elves. Necropotence and Demonic Consultation are banned. Our [Legacy guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) covers how these fit together.
- **Vintage.** Brainstorm, Necropotence and Demonic Consultation are restricted. See our [Vintage guide](/blog/mtg-vintage-format-guide) for how restriction works.
- **Commander.** Demonic Consultation is a key part of a famous competitive combo with Thassa's Oracle, Mystic Remora is a staple in competitive blue decks, Necropotence is legal and powerful, and Brainstorm shows up everywhere. Amulet of Quoz, the set's ante card, is banned in every format that allows Ice Age cards. Our [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format.
- **Modern and Pauper.** Ice Age predates Modern, so only cards reprinted in Modern-legal sets (like the painlands, snow-covered basics, Pyroclasm and Incinerate) are legal there. Commons like Brainstorm and Counterspell are legal in Pauper.

The Reserved List covers 50 Ice Age cards, but they're mostly niche rares. The set's most important cards, Brainstorm, Necropotence and Demonic Consultation, aren't on it and have been reprinted.

## Cards from Ice Age worth owning

- **Brainstorm**: one mana of card selection that defines Legacy blue decks.
- **Necropotence**: a legendary card advantage engine for black Commander decks.
- **Demonic Consultation**: a cheap tutor at the heart of competitive Commander combo.
- **Mystic Remora**: early card draw for blue Commander decks.
- **Swords to Plowshares**: the best one-mana removal in white.
- **The painlands**: dependable two-color mana in any format.

## The takeaway

Ice Age proved that Magic could reinvent itself with each new set, and it gave the game ideas it still uses: cantrips, cumulative upkeep, snow and painlands. Its cards are the backbone of many Legacy and Commander decks. If you want to put it to work, a Legacy blue deck that leans on Brainstorm and Counterspell is the classic home for its best cards.
