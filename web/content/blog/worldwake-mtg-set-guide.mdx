Worldwake is the second set of Magic: The Gathering's original Zendikar block, released in February 2010. It continued the story of an adventure-world plane full of buried ruins and angry land, and it set up the Eldrazi's return in Rise of the Eldrazi. It is best remembered for a stack of cards that are still format-defining: Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Stoneforge Mystic, the five creature lands, Death's Shadow and Amulet of Vigor.

## The story

Zendikar is a wild, dangerous plane where the land itself moves. A phenomenon called the Roil reshapes the terrain without warning, floating rocks called hedrons hang in the sky, and explorers from the Kor, merfolk, vampire and human peoples dig through ruins for treasure. That "adventure world" feel is what the block's cards are built around.

Underneath the adventure is a much older secret. Thousands of years before the set, three planeswalkers, the vampire Sorin Markov, the kor lithomancer Nahiri and the dragon Ugin, trapped the Eldrazi, colossal and alien beings that devour whole worlds, inside Zendikar. The hedrons are pieces of that prison. The Roil is, in part, the plane reacting to the monsters inside it.

Worldwake tells the middle of the story. Several planeswalkers are converging on the Eye of Ugin, a hidden chamber at the heart of the old seal. Chandra Nalaar has come chasing the secret of an ancient scroll, Jace Beleren has come after the same secret, and Sarkhan Vol, acting for Nicol Bolas, is waiting for them. The Eye was built so that it could only be opened by several planeswalkers together, and by the end of this chapter that is exactly what happens: the seal starts to fail. The Eye of Ugin card itself hints at it with its flavor text about an eye closing and a race awakening, and its ability reduces the cost of Eldrazi spells, a card type that didn't exist yet when Worldwake came out. Rise of the Eldrazi picks up from there, with the three Eldrazi titans loose on the plane.

On the local level, Worldwake spends time with Zendikar's inhabitants: Anowon, the Ruin Sage, a vampire scholar obsessed with the ruins, the merfolk Thada Adel, and the ogre warlord Kazuul. The land "waking up" to fight back, which gives the set its name, is shown through the Zendikons and the creature lands.

## Mechanics

### Landfall

Landfall abilities trigger whenever a land enters the battlefield under your control. Zendikar block made playing a land each turn feel like casting a spell. In Worldwake, Searing Blaze deals 1 damage to a player and 1 to a creature, or 3 and 3 if you played a land that turn. Admonition Angel exiles a nonland permanent every time you make a land drop. Avenger of Zendikar makes a 0/1 Plant for each land you control, then grows all of them with each land after.

### Multikicker

Multikicker is kicker you can pay any number of times. Each payment adds to the effect. Everflowing Chalice is the best-known example: a mana rock that costs 0, and gets one charge counter (and one more mana) for each time you pay the extra two generic mana. Joraga Warcaller grows with each kick and pumps your other Elves. Wolfbriar Elemental makes a 2/2 Wolf per kick. Comet Storm splits X damage among more targets each time you kick it.

### Creature lands

Worldwake's rare cycle of five dual lands, often called "manlands," enter tapped and tap for one of two colors, but you can pay mana to turn them into creatures until end of turn. Celestial Colonnade becomes a 4/4 flier with vigilance, Raging Ravine a 3/3 that grows when it attacks, Creeping Tar Pit an unblockable 3/2, Stirring Wildwood a 3/4 with reach, and Lavaclaw Reaches a 2/2 that pumps its power. A land that attacks dodges sorcery-speed removal and board wipes, which is why these became control-deck finishers.

### Zendikons

Zendikons are Auras that enchant a land and turn it into a creature permanently. Guardian Zendikon makes a land a 2/6 Wall with defender, for example. When the enchanted land dies, it returns to your hand, so you don't lose the land.

### Allies, quests and traps

Allies, quests and traps returned from Zendikar. Allies trigger whenever another Ally enters under your control: Hada Freeblade gets a counter and Talus Paladin can give your Allies lifelink. Quests are enchantments that collect counters until they pay off, like Quest for the Goblin Lord. Traps are spells with a cheaper alternative cost if your opponent did something specific that turn.

## Limited

Worldwake was drafted alongside Zendikar, with two packs of Zendikar followed by one pack of Worldwake, since drafts of that era opened the oldest set first. Zendikar's draft format is remembered as one of the fastest in Magic's history: landfall rewards aggressive starts, there were lots of cheap evasive creatures, and many games ended before turn seven. Worldwake slowed things slightly with a few more defensive cards, such as Guardian Zendikon, but the format stayed aggressive.

What mattered at lower rarities:

- **Removal**: Smother at uncommon, Searing Blaze and Mire's Toll at common, and Vapor Snare to steal a creature in blue.
- **Allies**: Ally decks in white and green could snowball if they collected enough of them, with cards like Hada Freeblade and Join the Ranks.
- **Multikicker creatures** like Bloodhusk Ritualist scale well into the late game, so they were rarely dead draws.
- **Aggressive one- and two-drops**, such as Pulse Tracker, Kor Firewalker and Loam Lion.

The best plan was usually a two-color aggressive deck with a low curve and enough removal to clear blockers. If you want to brush up on the theory, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) covers why tempo and cheap removal matter so much in a fast format.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2010-2011

Worldwake arrived in a Standard dominated by Jund (Bloodbraid Elf, Blightning and friends from Shards of Alara block). Raging Ravine slotted straight into Jund mana bases as a land that became a threat. Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Celestial Colonnade became the core of blue-white control decks, and Abyssal Persecutor gave black decks a cheap, huge flier.

The real story came in early 2011, after Scars of Mirrodin had joined Standard. "Caw-Blade" paired Stoneforge Mystic with Squadron Hawk, Jace, the Mind Sculptor and powerful Equipment like Sword of Feast and Famine (and, from New Phyrexia, Batterskull), and it won so much that Wizards stepped in. On June 20, 2011, Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic were banned in Standard, the format's first ban in about six years. Worldwake then left Standard with the rest of Zendikar block when Innistrad released in September 2011.

### Modern

Both Jace and Stoneforge were on Modern's first banned list when the format launched in 2011. Jace came off the list in February 2018 and Stoneforge in August 2019, and both have been legal since.

Other Worldwake cards became Modern pillars:

- **Celestial Colonnade** was the finisher of white-blue control decks for years.
- **Death's Shadow** became the namesake of an aggressive Modern deck that pays life on purpose with fetchlands and shocklands to make the one-drop huge.
- **Amulet of Vigor** powers Amulet Titan, which untaps bounce lands to make huge amounts of mana on turn two or three.
- **Eye of Ugin** helped Eldrazi decks dominate early 2016 and was banned in Modern in April 2016. It remains banned as of October 2026.
- **Searing Blaze** has been a sideboard and main-deck option for Modern burn.

### Legacy, Vintage and Commander

Jace, the Mind Sculptor is a long-time Legacy and Vintage control card, and Stoneforge Mystic with Batterskull or Kaldra Compleat is a recurring Legacy package. Lodestone Golem was restricted in Vintage in 2016 after it starred in artifact prison decks. In Commander, Avenger of Zendikar, Everflowing Chalice, Bojuka Bog and Omnath, Locus of Mana all have homes. As of October 2026, nothing from Worldwake is banned in Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Worldwake worth owning

- **Jace, the Mind Sculptor**: one of the most famous planeswalkers ever, still played in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Stoneforge Mystic**: the best Equipment tutor in the game.
- **Celestial Colonnade**: a creature land for any white-blue deck.
- **Creeping Tar Pit** and **Raging Ravine**: creature lands for blue-black and red-green decks.
- **Amulet of Vigor**: the engine of Modern's Amulet Titan.
- **Death's Shadow**: a build-around one-drop with a lasting Modern following.
- **Bojuka Bog**: graveyard hate on a land, a Commander staple.

## The takeaway

Worldwake is small, but it punched far above its size. If you play Modern, you have probably faced at least one of its cards this year, whether Colonnade, a Death's Shadow or an Amulet. A good way to feel the set is to build around its creature lands and Jace in a control shell.
