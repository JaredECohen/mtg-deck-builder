Throne of Eldraine is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2019 that introduced the plane of Eldraine, a world built from Arthurian legend and European fairy tales. Its story follows the royal twins Will and Rowan Kenrith as they search for their missing father, and its Adventure cards, Food tokens and five Castles changed how players thought about card value. It's also one of the most heavily banned sets in Magic's history, starting with Oko, Thief of Crowns.

## The story

Eldraine is split in two. The **Realm** is made of five human courts, each tied to a virtue and a color: Ardenvale (white, loyalty), Vantress (blue, knowledge), Locthwain (black, persistence), Embereth (red, courage) and Garenbrig (green, strength). Beyond their borders lie the **Wilds**, the enchanted forests and swamps ruled by fae, witches, giants and talking beasts. The High King, Algenus Kenrith, holds the courts together, and the set opens as he's about to be crowned.

During his Grand Procession, the king disappears. His children Will and Rowan, teenagers with a talent for magic, set out to find him, and the trail leads into the Wilds. Behind the kidnapping is **Oko**, a fae trickster planeswalker who loves chaos for its own sake. He uses his shapeshifting magic to turn the High King into a stag and puts him in a magical slumber, and he has also taken control of **Garruk Wildspeaker**. Garruk is still carrying the curse from the Chain Veil that drives him to hunt planeswalkers, and Oko bends him into a servant he calls "Dog" to lead the Wild Hunt after the king.

The twins chase the story through fairy-tale set pieces: witches' cottages, giants' castles, enchanted mirrors and the courts' own rivalries. Oko's plan is to have the stag killed in a way that sets the humans of the Realm against the fae of the Wilds and starts a war. When the stag finally falls, its blood breaks the hold of the Hunt. Garruk is freed from his curse, and together with Will and Rowan he corners Oko, who slips away by planeswalking before he can face judgment.

The High King is restored with the help of his wife, Queen Linden, and the courts are reunited. But the twins learn painful truths about their own origins along the way, including that their birth mother was a witch, and the shock ignites their planeswalker sparks. Will and Rowan leave Eldraine, a thread that brings them to Strixhaven and later back home in Wilds of Eldraine.

## Mechanics

### Adventure

An Adventure card is a creature or other permanent with a smaller instant or sorcery attached in a frame inside the text box. You can cast either half. If you cast the Adventure, the card goes to exile instead of the graveyard, and later you can cast the creature from exile, so one card gives you two spells. **Bonecrusher Giant** (rare) is the classic example: its Adventure, Stomp, is a two-mana instant that deals 2 damage to any target, and then you get a 4/3 for three that punishes opponents for targeting it.

### Food

Food tokens are artifacts with "`{2}`, `{T}`, sacrifice this: you gain 3 life." On their own they're a small life buffer, but many cards sacrifice Food for bigger effects. **Gilded Goose** makes Food and turns it into mana, and **Savvy Hunter** draws cards off pairs of Food.

### Adamant

Adamant is an ability word that checks whether at least three mana of one color was spent to cast the spell. **Searing Barrage** (common) deals 5 damage to a creature, plus 3 to its controller if you spent three red. Adamant pulled players toward mono-colored decks.

### Mono-color and multicolor themes, and the Castles

The Realm's five courts reward heavy commitment to one color, with cards like **Fireborn Knight** using hybrid mana, while the Wilds pull toward multicolor decks. The rare cycle of **Castles**, like **Castle Locthwain** and **Castle Ardenvale**, enter untapped if you control a basic of that type and have an activated ability for the late game. For the basics of card frames like Adventure, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Each two-color pair has a pair of signpost uncommons, one gold and one hybrid:

- **White-blue (Shinechaser, Arcanist's Owl): artifacts and enchantments.** Fliers that care about noncreature permanents.
- **Blue-black (Drown in the Loch, Covetous Urge): mill.** Fill graveyards and turn them into removal and counters.
- **Black-red (Steelclaw Lance, Elite Headhunter): Knights and sacrifice.** Aggressive Knights that trade up.
- **Red-green (Grumgully, the Generous, Rampart Smasher): non-Humans.** Beasts and Giants that grow with counters.
- **Green-white (Wandermare, Oakhame Ranger): Adventures.** Two-for-one creatures and a wide board.
- **White-black (Wintermoor Commander, Resolute Rider): Knights.** Sticky Knights that win long races.
- **Blue-red (Improbable Alliance, Loch Dragon): draw two.** Reward drawing your second card each turn.
- **Black-green (Savvy Hunter, Deathless Knight): Food.** Make Food, then cash it in for value.
- **Red-white (Inspiring Veteran, Fireborn Knight): Knights aggro.** A fast deck that pumps its team.
- **Green-blue (Maraleaf Pixie, Thunderous Snapper): big spells.** Ramp into mana value 5 or more and draw.

Adventure creatures at common, like **Ardenvale Tactician** and **Faerie Guidemother**, gave almost every deck a two-for-one, and cheap removal like Scorching Dragonfire and **Bake into a Pie** was premium. The format was moderately fast, with Knights decks punishing slow starts. Our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains how to value two-for-ones like these.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Throne of Eldraine defined two years of Standard, and much of that story is told by bans.

- **November 18, 2019:** Oko, Thief of Crowns and Once Upon a Time were banned in Standard, along with Field of the Dead and Veil of Summer. Simic Food, built around Oko and Gilded Goose, had taken over the format within weeks of release.
- **December 16, 2019:** Oko was banned in Pioneer, alongside Nexus of Fate.
- **January 13, 2020:** Oko was banned in Modern, along with Mox Opal and Mycosynth Lattice.
- **March 9, 2020:** Once Upon a Time was banned in Modern.
- **June 1, 2020:** Fires of Invention was banned in Standard, along with Agent of Treachery. Jeskai and four-color Fires decks used it to cast big spells for free.
- **August 3, 2020:** Cauldron Familiar was banned in Standard, with Growth Spiral, Teferi, Time Raveler and Wilderness Reclamation, ending the Witch's Oven sacrifice deck.
- **October 12, 2020:** Lucky Clover was banned in Standard along with Omnath, Locus of Creation and Escape to the Wilds, after Temur Adventure decks with Edgewall Innkeeper and Bonecrusher Giant were expected to dominate.
- **February 15, 2021:** Mystic Sanctuary was banned in Modern, and Oko was banned in Legacy that same month.

Between the bans, the set fed nearly every deck. Embercleave powered mono-red and Gruul aggro, Murderous Rider was the default black removal, Lovestruck Beast anchored green aggro, and the Castles quietly improved mono-color mana bases. The set rotated out of Standard in September 2021, when Innistrad: Midnight Hunt arrived. Our [ban list guide](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) covers how Wizards decides on bans like these.

Its afterlife is just as big. In Pioneer, Bonecrusher Giant, Murderous Rider and Lovestruck Beast became long-time staples, and Witch's Oven and Cauldron Familiar formed the core of Rakdos sacrifice decks. In Modern and Legacy, Brazen Borrower is a fixture in blue tempo decks, and Bonecrusher Giant saw years of Modern play. In Commander, The Great Henge, Korvold, Chulane, Gilded Goose and the Castles are popular picks. Powerful engines like Korvold are worth matching to your table's [Commander bracket](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). As of October 2026, Oko remains banned in Pioneer, Modern and Legacy.

## Cards from Throne of Eldraine worth owning

- **Bonecrusher Giant**: removal and a body in one card for any red deck.
- **Brazen Borrower**: a flexible tempo card in Modern and Legacy.
- **Murderous Rider**: instant-speed removal that becomes a creature, for Pioneer black decks.
- **The Great Henge**: a card-draw engine for creature-heavy Commander decks.
- **Embercleave**: a game-ending Equipment in aggro and Commander.
- **Witch's Oven**: a sacrifice outlet for Pioneer and Commander combo decks.
- **The Castle cycle**: lands that add late-game value at almost no cost.

Throne of Eldraine taught a generation of players that one card can be two spells, and its best Adventure cards are still played everywhere they're legal. If you're building with it today, Pioneer is the best home for its staples, and you can test a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
