Double Masters is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in August 2020, built around one gimmick: everything is doubled. Every pack held two rares and two foils, every box came with two box toppers, and drafters took two cards with the first pick of each pack. It's a curated collection of Modern, Legacy and Commander staples, with a heavy lean toward artifacts.

| Fact | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | 2XM |
| Release date | August 7, 2020 |
| Universe | No single plane; a greatest-hits collection drawn from across Magic's history |
| Set size | 332 cards (91 commons, 80 uncommons, 121 rares, 40 mythic rares) |
| Product | 15-card Draft Boosters with two rares or mythics and two foils, two borderless box toppers per box, and a premium VIP Edition pack |
| Headline themes | Artifacts and Equipment, metalcraft, ferocious, morbid and revolt, sacrifice, plus the "double" draft rule |
| Formats | No new cards, so every card keeps its existing legality. Nothing in the set is Standard-legal today; most cards are legal in Modern, Legacy and Commander, and Force of Will, Sneak Attack and Land Tax are Legacy, Vintage and Commander cards |

## What the set was for

Masters sets exist to put older, hard-to-find cards back into circulation, and they're also designed to be drafted. Double Masters arrived at an unusual moment: in-person play had largely stopped in 2020, and Wizards pushed the set's value up with a pack structure that guaranteed two rares and two foils. That made it as much a collector's product as a draft set.

The card selection reflects that. The set leans on artifacts, with Mox Opal, Chrome Mox, the Swords of X and Y, Wurmcoil Engine, Batterskull and Blightsteel Colossus all in the rare and mythic slots. Around them are Commander favorites like Doubling Season, Cyclonic Rift, Avenger of Zendikar and Atraxa, Praetors' Voice, and eternal-format cards such as Force of Will and Jace, the Mind Sculptor. The two-of-everything theme also shows up in pairs of cards that work together, like Dark Depths and Thespian's Stage, which combine to make a 20/20 flier.

Commander players got a lot of attention too. The mythic slot includes a run of popular multicolor legends, many of them first printed in Commander products: Atraxa, Praetors' Voice, Breya, Etherium Shaper, Kaalia of the Vast, Riku of Two Reflections, Sen Triplets and Karrthus, Tyrant of Jund. Each one is a ready-made commander, and in draft they act as gold bombs that reward you for staying in their colors. Alongside them sit utility pieces that show up in almost every Commander collection, such as Lightning Greaves, Blasphemous Act and Toxic Deluge.

The draft rule was the other headline. At the start of each pack, you take two cards instead of one. That means you can take both rares, or a rare plus the removal spell that goes with it, and it pushes drafters toward building around a synergy from the very first pick.

## Key cards for Limited

Because packs are so rare-heavy, the commons and uncommons do most of the shaping in draft. The best ones were removal and artifact glue:

- **Removal:** Path to Exile, Pongify, Fatal Push, Galvanic Blast, Executioner's Capsule, Lightning Axe, Abrade and Unlicensed Disintegration.
- **Artifact engines:** card flow from Ichor Wellspring and Thirst for Knowledge, plus Myr Retriever, Chief of the Foundry and Cranial Plating.
- **Fixing:** Everflowing Chalice, Chromatic Star, Springleaf Drum and Ash Barrens helped you splash the second rare you took on turn one.

## Limited

Double Masters is a high-power draft format. With two rares and two foils in every pack, plus the two-pick rule, you see far more bombs than in a normal set, and removal is the most valuable thing you can pick at common. The gold uncommons point to each archetype:

- **White-blue:** artifacts, with Glassdust Hulk getting bigger and unblockable as you play them.
- **Blue-black:** artifact creatures and value, with Sphinx Summoner fetching more of them.
- **Black-red:** artifacts and removal, with Unlicensed Disintegration as the payoff.
- **Red-green:** ferocious and big creatures, with Ghor-Clan Rampager.
- **White-black:** revolt and sacrifice, with Hidden Stockpile making Servos.
- **Blue-red:** spells and artifacts, with Izzet Charm.
- **Black-green:** morbid, with Deathreap Ritual drawing a card each turn something dies.
- **Red-white:** Equipment, with Weapons Trainer and Puresteel Paladin.
- **Green-white:** going wide with tokens and pump.
- **Green-blue:** ramping into the set's biggest threats.

Artifacts tie a lot of these together, so blue and black artifact decks tended to be deep, and colorless cards like Chief of the Foundry slot into almost any deck. Tempo matters less than raw card quality here: removal-heavy decks that answer the opposing rares usually come out ahead. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Double Masters added no new cards, so it didn't change what was legal. What it changed was supply. Reprinting Modern and Legacy staples like Karn Liberated, the Swords, Stoneforge Mystic, Blood Moon and the Tron lands made those decks easier to assemble, and Commander players got fresh copies of Doubling Season, Cyclonic Rift and Toxic Deluge.

Several of its cards have changed status since. Mox Opal was banned in Modern when Double Masters came out (it had been banned in January 2020) and was unbanned on December 16, 2024, putting it back into Modern artifact decks. Chrome Mox and Dark Depths remain banned in Modern as of October 2026.

In Commander, Mana Crypt was banned in September 2024, along with Dockside Extortionist and Jeweled Lotus, and Sundering Titan is also on the Commander banned list. As of October 2026, Scryfall lists Cyclonic Rift, Chrome Mox, Force of Will, Crop Rotation and Ad Nauseam from this set as Game Changers, which matters for [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). For the formats these cards play in, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained), the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) and the [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Double Masters worth owning

- **Mox Opal:** the engine of Modern artifact aggro again since its 2024 unban.
- **The Swords (Fire and Ice, Feast and Famine, Light and Shadow, War and Peace, Body and Mind):** playable Equipment in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Wurmcoil Engine:** a top colorless threat for Commander and ramp decks.
- **Doubling Season:** one of the strongest token and counters enchantments in Commander.
- **Cyclonic Rift:** a Commander staple and Game Changer.
- **Force of Will:** the defining free counterspell of Legacy.
- **Stoneforge Mystic:** a Modern and Legacy Equipment engine.
- **The Tron lands:** cheap commons that build a whole Modern archetype.
