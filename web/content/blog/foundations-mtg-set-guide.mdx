Magic: The Gathering Foundations is a core set, released in November 2024, built to be the long-term base of the Standard format. Instead of telling one story on one plane, it collects new cards and well-known reprints from across the Multiverse, and Wizards has committed to keeping it legal in Standard until at least 2029. The headline is accessibility: simple evergreen mechanics, a family of beginner products, and a card pool full of staples that will stay useful for years.

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | FDN |
| Release date | November 15, 2024 |
| Setting | No single plane; cards drawn from across the Multiverse |
| Main set size | 271 cards plus basic lands, with extra Standard-legal cards from the Beginner Box and Starter Collection |
| Headline mechanics | Evergreen keywords plus flash, flashback, kicker, landfall, morbid, prowess, raid and threshold |

Unlike the other sets in Standard, Foundations isn't part of the normal rotation. It isn't leaving with the large rotation Wizards has scheduled for early 2027.

## What Foundations is for

Foundations exists to solve a problem with rotating formats: every few years, the staples you bought for Standard rotate out, and new players have no stable place to start. Wizards' answer was a base set that sits in Standard far longer than a normal expansion. Its official preview coverage says the cards will be Standard-legal until at least 2029, and the designers have described that date as a minimum rather than a deadline. Wizards also said it intends to keep printing Foundations products over that period, so the cards stay available.

That changes how you should think about the set. A strong Foundations card keeps earning its slot: a deck built around Llanowar Elves or Burst Lightning in 2025 can still use those cards years later. It also means Foundations is designed with restraint. Most of its new cards are clean, readable designs, and its biggest effects are mostly familiar reprints rather than brand-new mechanics that might warp the format.

There's no central story, but the set does nod to Magic's history. Its cards revisit familiar faces, with planeswalker cards for Ajani, Kaito, Chandra, Vivien and Liliana, and legends like Kiora, the Rising Tide and Loot, Exuberant Explorer. Think of it as a sampler of the Multiverse rather than a chapter in the ongoing saga.

### The beginner products: Beginner Box, Starter Collection and Jumpstart

Foundations launched alongside several products aimed at new and returning players, and it's worth knowing which cards count for Standard:

- **Beginner Box.** A learn-to-play box built from 20-card themed decks that you shuffle together in pairs, with playmats and guides. Its cards are part of the Foundations set (cards like Elspeth's Smite and Ancestor Dragon carry Beginner Box printings), so they're Standard-legal.
- **Starter Collection.** A box of playable cards meant to seed a first collection. Its exclusive printings, including Duress, Negate, Maelstrom Pulse and Wilt-Leaf Liege, are also numbered in the Foundations set and are Standard-legal.
- **Foundations Jumpstart.** Jumpstart boosters are 20-card themed half-decks: open two, shuffle them together, and play. Foundations Jumpstart is a separate product with its own set code (J25). Its reprints of Foundations cards work like any other printing, but the cards that debuted in Foundations Jumpstart are not legal in Standard; they're for formats like Pioneer, Modern and Commander.

If you're brand new, the Beginner Box or a pair of Jumpstart packs is a gentle start, and our guide to [how to play Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers the rules you'll need.

## Mechanics

Foundations has no new keyword of its own. Instead, it showcases evergreen keywords (flying, trample, lifelink, deathtouch and the rest) and a handful of returning mechanics, each tied to a color pair in Limited:

- **Flashback** lets you cast an instant or sorcery a second time from your graveyard for its flashback cost. Think Twice draws a card, then can be flashed back for `{2}{U}` to draw another.
- **Kicker** is an optional extra cost for a bigger effect. Burst Lightning deals 2 damage for `{R}`, or 4 if you pay `{4}` more.
- **Landfall** triggers when a land enters under your control. Tatyova, Benthic Druid gains you 1 life and draws a card on every land drop.
- **Morbid** checks whether a creature died this turn. Wardens of the Cycle gives you a choice of life or a card at your end step if one did.
- **Prowess** gives a creature +1/+1 whenever you cast a noncreature spell, as on Lightshell Duo.
- **Raid** rewards you for attacking. Searslicer Goblin makes a 1/1 Goblin at your end step if you attacked that turn.
- **Threshold** turns on once seven or more cards are in your graveyard. Kiora, the Rising Tide can create an 8/8 Octopus when she attacks with threshold.
- **Flash** appears on a lot of creatures, like Spectral Sailor and Brineborn Cutthroat, so blue decks can hold up mana.

These are the mechanics a new player will meet again and again, which is the point of a base set.

## Limited

Foundations Limited is built to teach. Each color pair has a gold uncommon signpost and a straightforward theme:

| Pair | Theme | Signpost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Fliers | Empyrean Eagle |
| Blue-Black | Looting and threshold | Dreadwing Scavenger |
| Black-Red | Raid aggro | Perforating Artist |
| Red-Green | Big creatures (power 4 or more) | Ruby, Daring Tracker |
| Green-White | +1/+1 counters | Good-Fortune Unicorn |
| White-Black | Lifegain | Fiendish Panda |
| Blue-Red | Instants and sorceries | Balmor, Battlemage Captain |
| Black-Green | Morbid and sacrifice | Wardens of the Cycle |
| Red-White | Go-wide tokens | Heroic Reinforcements |
| Green-Blue | Landfall ramp | Tatyova, Benthic Druid |

The format plays like a classic core set. Removal is plentiful and simple (Bake into a Pie, Burst Lightning, Banishing Light), bombs like Sire of Seven Deaths and the mythic planeswalkers decide games when they land, and evasive creatures matter more than tricky synergies. Because it's a deliberately fundamentals-first format, it's a good place to practice the basics in our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Foundations' role in Standard has been supporting, not dominating. In its first months, Llanowar Elves found a home in Golgari midrange and green aggro decks, Burst Lightning replaced Shock in red aggro, Maelstrom Pulse gave black-green decks flexible removal, and Soulstone Sanctuary became one of the most played lands in the format. Day of Judgment, Elspeth's Smite, Authority of the Consuls, Duress and Negate filled sideboards. Matt Nass's Domain Overlords list that won Pro Tour Aetherdrift in February 2025 included Foundations cards Day of Judgment, Negate and Elspeth's Smite.

No Foundations card has been banned in Standard or any other major format as of October 2026. That fits the design goal: a set meant to sit in Standard for five years needs to be reliable, not oppressive.

Beyond Standard, the reprints matter most. Foundations made older cards such as Maelstrom Pulse and Doubling Season legal in Pioneer, and its Commander staples (Doubling Season, Phyrexian Arena, Rune-Scarred Demon, Solemn Simulacrum and Muldrotha, the Gravetide) made those cards easier to find for casual players.

## Cards from Foundations worth owning

- **Llanowar Elves**: a staple mana creature for green decks in Standard for years to come.
- **Burst Lightning**: efficient red removal and reach that fits nearly every red deck.
- **Soulstone Sanctuary**: a utility land that turns flood into a threat.
- **Day of Judgment**: the reliable four-mana board wipe every white control deck wants.
- **Doubling Season**: one of Commander's most iconic token and counter cards.
- **Vampire Nighthawk**: a long-standing midrange creature that's strong in any black deck.

## The takeaway

Foundations is the set to start with if you're new to Standard, because its cards won't rotate out from under you before 2029 at the earliest. Pick up its staples first, then add newer sets around them.
