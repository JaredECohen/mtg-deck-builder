Phyrexia: All Will Be One is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2023, set on New Phyrexia, the metal plane once called Mirrodin. A team of planeswalkers invades the Phyrexian homeworld to stop Elesh Norn's plan to conquer the Multiverse, and the cards bring back poison in a new form called toxic, alongside oil counters and compleated planeswalkers. It's the third chapter of the Phyrexian saga that ends in March of the Machine.

## The story

Phyrexia has been preparing an invasion for years, and by this set the threat is out in the open. Elesh Norn, the Grand Cenobite of the Machine Orthodoxy, now rules New Phyrexia, and her servants have grown a giant false World Tree called **Realmbreaker** that can push through the Blind Eternities and link the planes together. Once it's finished, Phyrexian armies will be able to walk from world to world.

A strike team of planeswalkers goes in to stop it. The group includes Kaya, Kaito Shizuki, Tyvar Kell, Elspeth Tirel, Nahiri, Jace Beleren, Vraska and the Wanderer. Their weapon is a copy of the Golgothian Sylex, the artifact that once ended the Brothers' War. The plan is to carry it down through New Phyrexia's layered spheres to the base of Realmbreaker and set it off.

The mission goes wrong from the moment they arrive. Phyrexian defenses scatter the team, the Wanderer is torn off the plane, and the survivors meet the last of the Mirran resistance, led by Koth and the healer Melira, who protect them with hexgold, a metal that slows the Phyrexian oil. They push down through the spheres one by one, and the toll mounts. Nahiri is infected. Jace breaks off to find Vraska, who has been compleated in Sheoldred's domain, and the compleated Vraska turns her sting on him. Tibalt, also compleated, is killed in the fighting. The team even finds Karn, broken and drained, at the roots of the tree.

By the time the remaining heroes reach Realmbreaker's core, they're too late: the tree has already connected to other planes. The Sylex plan collapses in a final struggle and Elspeth disappears with the bomb, so the Multiverse is spared its blast but not the Phyrexians. Elesh Norn appears with compleated versions of Nahiri, Nissa and Tamiyo, Kaya, Kaito and Tyvar are taken prisoner, and Norn gives the order that launches the invasion of every plane. The war itself plays out in March of the Machine.

## Mechanics

### Toxic

Toxic is a keyword with a number. When a creature with toxic deals combat damage to a player, that player also gets that many poison counters, and ten poison counters loses the game. Unlike infect, toxic creatures still deal normal damage, so they pressure life totals and poison at the same time. **Venerated Rotpriest** (rare, `{G}`) is a 1/2 with toxic 1 that also gives a poison counter whenever one of your creatures becomes the target of a spell, which made it the engine of green toxic decks.

### Corrupted

Corrupted is an ability word that turns on once an opponent has three or more poison counters. **Vivisection Evangelist** destroys a creature or planeswalker when it enters if an opponent is corrupted. It rewards you for landing a few poison counters early, even in a deck that isn't trying to win by poison.

### Oil counters

Oil counters are a set-wide resource that cards add and remove for their own effects. **Mindsplice Apparatus** collects one each upkeep and makes your instants and sorceries cheaper for each counter, while **Archfiend of the Dross** is a four-mana 6/6 flier that loses a counter each upkeep and costs you the game once it runs out.

### For Mirrodin!

Equipment with For Mirrodin! makes a 2/2 red Rebel token when it enters and attaches to it, so the card is never a dead draw. **Hexgold Halberd** is a two-mana example, and **Bladehold War-Whip** hands the token double strike.

### Compleated and Phyrexian mana

Phyrexian mana symbols (shown as `{W/P}` and friends) can be paid with mana of that color or 2 life. The compleated planeswalkers carry one such symbol in their cost, and if you pay life for it, they enter with fewer loyalty counters. **Jace, the Perfected Mind** costs `{2}{U}{U/P}` and is the clearest example. Proliferate also returns as a core theme, adding one more of each counter to any permanents and players you choose, which grows poison, oil, +1/+1 and loyalty counters alike.

## Limited

Each two-color pair has a gold uncommon that points at its plan:

- **White-blue (Cephalopod Sentry): artifacts.** Fliers that grow with the number of artifacts you control.
- **Blue-black (Voidwing Hybrid): proliferate.** Toxic fliers and proliferate spells that keep rebuying the Hybrid.
- **Black-red (Charforger): sacrifice and oil.** Turn dying creatures and artifacts into oil and value.
- **Red-green (Cinderslash Ravager): oil counters.** Cheapen big creatures with oil permanents and ping the opposing board.
- **Green-white (Slaughter Singer): toxic aggro.** Attack with toxic creatures and pump them on the way in.
- **White-black (Vivisection Evangelist): corrupted.** Land a few poison counters, then cash in corrupted bonuses.
- **Blue-red (Serum-Core Chimera): noncreature spells.** Build oil on spell-triggered creatures and draw cards.
- **Black-green (Necrogen Rotpriest): toxic.** Stack extra poison on every hit and race to ten.
- **Red-white (Bladehold War-Whip): For Mirrodin! equipment.** Rebel tokens and Equipment that keep attacking.
- **Green-blue (Tainted Observer): proliferate.** Grow counters of every kind across the board.

The big draft decision is how hard to commit to poison. A deck with five or six toxic creatures that never reaches ten poison is often weaker than a normal creature deck, so the toxic archetypes wanted to be all in. Removal like Vraska's Fall, Drown in Ichor and the Aura **Planar Disruption** was valued, and the format rewarded curving out with cheap creatures. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Phyrexia: All Will Be One entered a Standard that had been dominated by midrange decks, and toxic decks were the first attempt to speed things up. Green-white and Bant toxic lists built around Venerated Rotpriest, Skrelv, Bloated Contaminator, Slaughter Singer and protection spells like **Tyvar's Stand** found success in the set's first weeks, though they never became the format's top deck.

The set's longer-lasting Standard cards were less flashy. Sheoldred's Edict became a staple removal spell for black midrange, and Atraxa, Grand Unifier became the payoff for Domain Ramp, a five-color deck that played big spells off Leyline Binding. Magic.gg's review of 2023 Standard ranked Domain Ramp second among the year's winning decks, behind Esper Midrange, and The Eternal Wanderer was a common top end for control. No cards from the set were banned in Standard during its run, and it rotated out in the summer of 2025 alongside Dominaria United, The Brothers' War and March of the Machine. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains how that cycle works.

In Pioneer, the reprinted fast lands made aggressive two-color mana bases better, and Sheoldred's Edict joined the removal suites of black midrange decks. Atraxa has been a reanimation and cheat-into-play target in both Pioneer and Modern, where it shows up in Goryo's Vengeance decks because one trigger refills your hand.

Commander is where the set has had its widest reach. Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines doubles enter-the-battlefield value and stops it for opponents, Mondrak doubles tokens, Atraxa is a popular commander and card-advantage engine, and Phyrexian Arena was reprinted as a long-time staple. Effects as strong as Elesh Norn's are worth weighing against your table's [Commander bracket](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). As of October 2026, these remain some of the set's most-played cards.

## Cards from Phyrexia: All Will Be One worth owning

- **Atraxa, Grand Unifier**: a top reanimation target and a strong Commander card.
- **Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines**: a Commander powerhouse for any deck with enter-the-battlefield triggers.
- **Sheoldred's Edict**: flexible instant-speed removal for black decks in every format.
- **The fast land reprints**: Blackcleave Cliffs, Copperline Gorge, Darkslick Shores, Razorverge Thicket and Seachrome Coast are useful in Pioneer and Modern mana bases.
- **Mondrak, Glory Dominus**: a token doubler for white Commander decks.
- **Phyrexian Arena**: classic card advantage for black Commander decks.
- **The Eternal Wanderer**: a strong finisher for white control decks.

Phyrexia: All Will Be One rewards picking a lane. Toxic decks want to commit, while the set's strongest Constructed cards are removal, mana and big payoffs that fit any midrange plan.
