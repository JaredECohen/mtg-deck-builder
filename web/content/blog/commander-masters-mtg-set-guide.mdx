Commander Masters is a Magic: The Gathering supplemental set released in August 2023, and the first Masters set built entirely around the Commander format. It packed draft boosters with Commander staples and legendary creatures, let you draft a four-player Commander deck on the spot, and shipped alongside four preconstructed decks with 40 brand-new cards between them. The headline reprints were Jeweled Lotus, The Ur-Dragon and a flood of everyday staples like Sol Ring.

## What the set was for

Masters sets have always been reprint vehicles for cards that are hard to find, but earlier ones such as Modern Masters and Double Masters were aimed at tournament formats first. Commander Masters flipped that. Every slot was chosen for the most popular format in the game, and the draft itself was designed to be played as a four-player game of Commander rather than as one-on-one Limited.

That gave the set two jobs. For collectors and deckbuilders, it put cards like Smothering Tithe, Demonic Tutor, Cyclonic Rift, Fierce Guardianship, Doubling Season and The Great Henge back into circulation. For players, it offered a fun weekend event in the style of Commander Legends: open three packs, take two cards at a time, and end the night with a working Commander deck. If you're new to the format itself, start with our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

## The decks

Each preconstructed deck is a 100-card Commander deck with two foil legends (the face commander and an alternate), ten new cards and a pile of reprints that fit the theme.

### Eldrazi Unbound (colorless)

Led by **Zhulodok, Void Gorger**, a six-mana Eldrazi that gives your colorless spells with mana value 7 or more two separate cascade triggers when you cast them from your hand. It was the first fully colorless Commander precon. The deck ramps with mana rocks into big colorless threats, with each one cascading into two more spells, while new cards like Ugin's Mastery and Skittering Cicada reward every colorless spell you cast. Booster pulls such as Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger and Kozilek, the Great Distortion are natural upgrades. **Omarthis, Ghostfire Initiate** is the alternate commander, a colorless X-cost Spirit Snake that grows when you put counters on your other colorless creatures.

### Enduring Enchantments (white, black, green)

Led by **Anikthea, Hand of Erebos**, a menace Demigod that exiles a non-Aura enchantment from your graveyard when it enters or attacks and makes a token copy that's also a 3/3 Zombie. The deck plays Sagas, enchantment creatures and cards that sacrifice enchantments for value. **Narci, Fable Singer** is the alternate, drawing cards when you sacrifice enchantments and draining opponents when a Saga's final chapter resolves.

### Planeswalker Party (white, blue, red)

Led by **Commodore Guff**, a three-color planeswalker that can be your commander. Each end step it adds loyalty to another planeswalker you control, its +1 makes Wizard tokens that produce mana for planeswalker spells, and its -3 draws cards and deals damage based on the planeswalkers you control. The deck runs a large number of walkers with support like Gatewatch Beacon and Onakke Oathkeeper. **Leori, Sparktouched Hunter** is the alternate, copying planeswalker abilities after it connects.

### Sliver Swarm (all five colors)

Led by **Sliver Gravemother**, which turns off the legend rule for your Slivers and gives every Sliver card in your graveyard encore, so a board wipe becomes an attack with token copies. New Slivers like Hatchery Sliver, Taunting Sliver and Lazotep Sliver feed the swarm, and **Rukarumel, Biologist** is the alternate commander, making your creatures share a chosen type so they count as Slivers too.

## Limited

Commander Masters was a draft set, but not a normal one. Each Commander Draft Booster had 20 game cards, including several legendary creatures and at least two cards of rare or higher, and players took two cards per pick from three packs. You then built a 60-card deck that included your commander and played four-player free-for-all Commander.

Two special rules shaped the format. First, you could partner any two mono-colored or colorless legendary creatures from your pool, which made almost every legend a potential commander and gave you a way to reach two colors. Second, color identity still applied, so your commander choice decided which cards were playable. The Prismatic Piper existed so that nobody was left without a commander.

Designers described the color pairs loosely, since your real archetype was whatever commander you ended up with. The rough themes were:

- **White-blue:** go-wide artifacts
- **Blue-black:** reanimation
- **Black-red:** sacrifice
- **Red-green:** big power
- **Green-white:** tokens and +1/+1 counters
- **White-black:** tokens with sacrifice payoffs
- **Black-green:** slower tokens and death triggers
- **Blue-green:** ramp
- **Blue-red:** spells
- **Red-white:** Equipment

Because games had four players, cards that hit "each opponent," goad effects and myriad were much better than they'd be in two-player Limited, and the format played slowly, with plenty of time to deploy big threats. Interaction was best saved for whoever was winning. For more on drafting in a group setting, see our [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Commander Masters had little impact on tournament formats, by design. It added no new cards to Modern or Pioneer, and the precons' new cards are only legal in eternal formats where they're too slow to matter. Its Modern relevance came from reprinting cards already played there, such as Urza, Lord High Artificer, Path to Exile and the Urza lands.

Commander is a different story. As of October 2026:

- **Jeweled Lotus** was banned in Commander on September 23, 2024, along with Mana Crypt, Dockside Extortionist and Nadu, Winged Wisdom. The Rules Committee said fast mana was making early turns too explosive. It's still legal in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Several of its reprints are on the official Game Changers list** used by the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained): Smothering Tithe, Demonic Tutor, Cyclonic Rift, Fierce Guardianship and Narset, Parter of Veils. Each one counts toward a deck's bracket.
- **Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Command Tower and Exotic Orchard** are among the most-played cards in the format, and this set made them easy to find again.
- **Zhulodok, Void Gorger** became a popular colorless commander, and Sliver Gravemother gave Sliver players a five-color option that rebuilds after wipes.

## Cards from Commander Masters worth owning

- **Sol Ring**: the most-played card in Commander.
- **Smothering Tithe**: a mana engine for any white deck (it's a Game Changer).
- **Demonic Tutor**: the cleanest tutor ever printed.
- **Cyclonic Rift**: a game-ending bounce spell in blue decks.
- **Fierce Guardianship and Deflecting Swat**: free interaction when your commander is out.
- **Doubling Season**: a staple of token and planeswalker decks.
- **The Ur-Dragon**: the definitive Dragon commander.
- **Zhulodok, Void Gorger**: the best reason to build colorless Eldrazi.

Commander Masters is the best one-stop shop for Commander staples that Wizards has printed. Its precons are good bases to upgrade (our [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) covers the basics), and its reprints fill holes in almost any deck.
