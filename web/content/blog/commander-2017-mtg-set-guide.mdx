Commander 2017 is a set of four preconstructed Magic: The Gathering Commander decks released in August 2017, each built around a creature type: Dragons, Cats, Vampires and Wizards. Its new mechanic, eminence, gave its four face commanders abilities that work even from the command zone, and two of them, **The Ur-Dragon** and **Edgar Markov**, became some of the most popular commanders ever printed. It also gave the format staples like **Teferi's Protection** and **Path of Ancestry**.

## What the set was for

Commander 2017 changed how Wizards made Commander products. Earlier annual sets had five decks built around color combinations. Starting here, the yearly product had four decks, matching the four-player table Wizards saw as the best Commander experience, and each deck was built around a theme instead of a color wedge or shard. Fans had asked for tribal decks for years, so the first theme-based product picked four of the most requested creature types.

The new mechanic, **eminence**, is an ability word on the four face commanders. An eminence ability works while the commander is on the battlefield or in the command zone, so it affects the game from turn one even before you cast your commander. Because each eminence ability rewards one creature type, it pushes you to build a true tribal deck.

The decks also lean on a set of new tribal support cards that work with any creature type, such as **Path of Ancestry**, **Herald's Horn** and the **Kindred** cycle. Those cards are why Commander 2017 still matters for players who never owned the decks.

## The decks

### Draconic Domination (five-color Dragons)

**The Ur-Dragon** (mythic, 10/10 flier for `{4}{W}{U}{B}{R}{G}`) makes your other Dragon spells cost `{1}` less through eminence. When your Dragons attack, you draw a card for each one and may put a permanent from your hand onto the battlefield. The deck is a ramp deck that casts Dragons early and snowballs. Its secondary commanders are **O-Kagachi, Vengeful Kami**, which exiles a permanent from a player who attacked you, **Wasitora, Nekoru Queen**, a Jund Cat Dragon that forces sacrifices, and **Ramos, Dragon Engine**, a colorless Dragon that grows and makes mana as you cast spells.

### Feline Ferocity (green-white Cats)

**Arahbo, Roar of the World** (mythic) gives another Cat +3/+3 at the start of each of your combats through eminence, and can pay `{1}{G}{W}` to double an attacking Cat's power and give it trample. The deck pairs Cats with Equipment. **Nazahn, Revered Bladesmith** searches for Equipment and makes it attack-ready, and **Mirri, Weatherlight Duelist** limits how many creatures can block or attack.

### Vampiric Bloodlust (red-white-black Vampires)

**Edgar Markov** (mythic) creates a 1/1 Vampire token every time you cast another Vampire spell through eminence, even if Edgar is still in the command zone. It also has first strike and haste and grows your Vampires when it attacks. The deck is an aggressive lifegain and tokens deck. **Licia, Sanguine Tribune** gets cheaper as you gain life, and **Mathas, Fiend Seeker** places bounties on opposing creatures.

### Arcane Wizardry (blue-black-red Wizards)

**Inalla, Archmage Ritualist** (mythic) copies each nontoken Wizard that enters for `{1}` through eminence, and can tap five Wizards to make a player lose 7 life. The deck wants Wizards with strong enter-the-battlefield effects and graveyard recursion. **Kess, Dissident Mage** lets you recast an instant or sorcery from your graveyard each turn, and **Mairsil, the Pretender** borrows activated abilities from exiled cards.

### Curses

A cycle of five uncommon curses rewards both you and any opponent who attacks the cursed player. **Curse of Opulence** gives everyone attacking that player a Gold token, **Curse of Verbosity** draws them a card and **Curse of Disturbance** makes them a Zombie. They push combat toward one opponent while you share in the profit, which makes them good tools for political multiplayer play.

### Notable reprints

The decks also include **Sol Ring**, **Swords to Plowshares**, **Skullclamp**, **Chaos Warp**, **Blood Artist**, **Sanguine Bond**, **Black Market**, **Sword of the Animist**, **Crux of Fate** and **Utvara Hellkite**, along with a large set of tri-lands and other multicolor lands that made the five-color Dragon deck playable.

## How it plays

Commander 2017 decks are focused and fun out of the box, because each one has a clear creature type to build around. They're still slow by modern standards, with some clunky reprints and a lot of taplands, so in today's [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) the unmodified decks fit the casual end. **Teferi's Protection** is a Game Changer as of October 2026, so Vampiric Bloodlust technically includes one out of the box.

Upgrade paths are straightforward because tribal decks have obvious targets:

- **Draconic Domination** wants more ramp and cost reducers. The Ur-Dragon decks usually run lots of mana rocks and dorks so they can cast Dragons on turn three or four.
- **Feline Ferocity** needs more Cats and cheaper Equipment, and it gets much better with Arahbo leading a few strong Cats.
- **Vampiric Bloodlust** gets the biggest boost from cheap Vampires, since Edgar's eminence makes a token for each one. Many players swap in one- and two-mana Vampires from newer sets.
- **Arcane Wizardry** improves with more Wizards that have strong enter-the-battlefield effects, which Inalla copies.

The curses deserve a mention too. They look like small, cheap enchantments, but in a four-player game they quietly decide who gets attacked. Put **Curse of Opulence** on the player with the strongest board, and the rest of the table now has a mana reason to hit that player, while you collect Gold tokens no matter who attacks. Swapping a few of the precon's weaker cards for more curses is a cheap way to make any of the four decks more political.

Our [tribal decks guide](/blog/mtg-tribal-decks-guide) covers how to balance creature-type payoffs against general good cards, and [how to upgrade a Commander precon](/blog/how-to-upgrade-a-commander-precon) walks through a full upgrade.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Commander 2017 cards aren't legal in Standard, Pioneer or Modern, and the set had little effect on Legacy or Vintage.

Its effect on Commander was large. **The Ur-Dragon** has long been the most popular five-color commander, and **Edgar Markov** became one of the most popular commanders of any kind. **Arahbo, Roar of the World** and **Kess, Dissident Mage** also rank near the top of their color identities. The new tribal support cards, especially **Path of Ancestry**, **Herald's Horn** and **Kindred Discovery**, became default picks for creature-type decks.

**Teferi's Protection** became the white answer to a board wipe or a lethal attack, and it's one of the most played white cards in the format. As of October 2026 it's on the Game Changers list, so it counts toward a deck's bracket. No Commander 2017 card is banned in Commander.

## Cards from Commander 2017 worth owning

- **Teferi's Protection**: the best defensive spell white has in Commander.
- **Path of Ancestry**: an easy include in nearly any multicolor deck.
- **Herald's Horn**: cost reduction and card advantage for every tribal deck.
- **Kindred Discovery**: steady card draw for creature-type decks.
- **Curse of Opulence**: cheap ramp that also points the table at someone else.
- **The Ur-Dragon** and **Edgar Markov**: two of the most popular commanders ever printed.

## Takeaway

Commander 2017 is the product that made tribal Commander mainstream. If you want to play one of its decks, start with the commander you like most, add cheap creatures of its type and better mana, and keep the tribal support cards. If you're new to the format, start with [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) and let the [deck builder](/) suggest an upgraded list.
