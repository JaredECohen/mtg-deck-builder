Mercadian Masques is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 1999, the first set of the Masques block. It strands the crew of the skyship Weatherlight on the strange plane of Mercadia, a world of corrupt merchants and oppressed rebels. Designed as a deliberate step down in power after the broken Urza's block, it's often called a weak set, but it still printed Rishadan Port, Gush, Food Chain, Misdirection and a famous reprint of Brainstorm.

## The story

Mercadian Masques is part of the Weatherlight Saga, the long storyline that runs from Weatherlight through Apocalypse. After escaping Rath at the end of the Tempest block, the Weatherlight crash-lands on Mercadia, a plane none of the crew recognizes. The main sources are Francis Lebaron's novel *Mercadian Masques* and a comic and player's guide from the same year.

The crew's troubles start immediately. The Cho-Arrim, a spiritual people who live in the jungles, steal the ship, and soldiers from Mercadia City arrest the crew. The Cho-Arrim's leader, Cho-Manno, believes the Weatherlight's arrival is a sign that their sky god Ramos is returning to end Mercadian rule. Orim, the crew's healer, is captured with the ship and comes to sympathize with the Cho-Arrim and their cause.

Mercadia City is a place where nothing is what it seems. Its nobles are idle and greedy, its commoners are paranoid, and its unusually large and clever goblins quietly run much of the city while acting servile. A guild of assassins and thieves called the Caterans will do anything for pay. The city's magistrate offers Gerrard Capashen a deal: lead a force of soldiers and Cateran mercenaries to recover the Weatherlight, and he may earn back his ship. That's the flavor behind the set's two big creature groups: the Rebels who fight Mercadia, and the Mercenaries it hires.

Takara, a member of the party whose loyalties are not what they seem, brokers another bargain with the magistrate: the crew will repair the Weatherlight and restore its flight systems for him in exchange for their freedom. Some of the crew travel to Saprazzo, a city of merfolk who can switch between tails and legs, to find an Eye of Ramos needed to repair the ship, while others stay behind as captives. Eventually Takara engineers an escape. Along the way, Gerrard's group finds ancient Thran ruins and fights a mechanical war engine, the Henge Guardian. The block continues in Nemesis and Prophecy before the crew returns to Dominaria for the Invasion.

## Mechanics

Masques introduced no new keywords, which was one reason players called it mechanically bland. It did have several distinctive themes, and designers later pointed to them as an argument for ability words.

### Rebels and Mercenaries (recruiting)

Rebels and Mercenaries have activated abilities that search your library for another creature of the same type and put it onto the battlefield. Rebels search for something bigger, so Ramosian Sergeant (`{3}`, tap) can find a Rebel with mana value 2 or less, which can find a 3, and so on up the chain to Ramosian Sky Marshal. Mercenaries search downward, from big creatures like Cateran Overlord to smaller ones. A Rebel chain turns one cheap creature into a stream of creatures from your deck, which made Rebels the set's main Constructed archetype.

### Spellshapers

Spellshapers are creatures that tap, discard a card and pay mana to produce a classic spell effect. Kris Mage pings for 1, Waterfront Bouncer bounces a creature, Seismic Mage destroys a land, and Undertaker returns a creature from your graveyard. They turn excess lands and dead cards into repeatable effects, at the cost of card disadvantage.

### Free spells

Masques brought back alternate costs for instants and sorceries, usually tied to controlling a basic land type. Gush draws two cards if you return two Islands instead of paying mana. Snuff Out destroys a nonblack creature for 4 life if you control a Swamp. Invigorate gives +4/+4 for free if you let an opponent gain 3 life. A rare cycle of pitch spells, like Misdirection, can be cast by exiling a card of the same color from your hand. Our [guide to reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) explains how alternative costs work.

### Depletion and storage lands

Depletion lands like Saprazzan Skerry enter tapped with two counters and tap for two mana until the counters run out. Storage lands like Saprazzan Cove can bank mana over several turns and release it all at once.

## Limited

Masques was drafted as its own block format, but it predates the detailed archetype analysis that later sets got, and most players remember it for its play patterns rather than its strategy guides. What's safe to say from the card file is that it was a slow, grindy environment. Creatures were modest, and many cards rewarded patience: storage lands, spellshapers that cashed in extra cards, and Rishadan Pirates like Rishadan Footpad that force opponents to pay mana or sacrifice a permanent.

Removal at common and uncommon included Vendetta in black, Arrest in white and Kris Mage as a repeatable pinger in red. Rebel recruiters at common could take over a long game if left alone, since each activation pulled another creature out of your deck. Snuff Out and other free spells let you develop and interact in the same turn. For modern advice on judging cards like these, see our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Mercadian Masques joined Type II in November 1999, just as Tempest block rotated out, alongside Urza's block and Sixth Edition. When Urza's block rotated in November 2000, Masques block plus Invasion and Sixth (then Seventh) Edition made up the format until Odyssey arrived in October 2001.

- **Rebels** was the set's signature deck. Ramosian Sergeant and Ramosian Lieutenant started the recruiting chain, and Nemesis added Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero, which made the deck much stronger. White Weenie Rebels and blue-white "Counter Rebels" were both fixtures of Standard in 2000 and 2001.
- **Rishadan Port** showed up in a wide range of decks as a way to keep opponents off their key colors.

In Masques Block Constructed, Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero and Rishadan Port were both banned in June 2000. No Mercadian Masques card was banned in Type II.

The set's long-term impact is in eternal formats. Its Brainstorm reprint is an early printing of a card that has been a Legacy staple for the format's whole history; Brainstorm has been restricted in Vintage since June 2008. Gush has had the most turbulent ban history of any Masques card: it was banned in Legacy in June 2003 and remains banned, it has moved on and off Vintage's restricted list several times and has been restricted since April 2017, and it has been banned in Pauper since May 2019. Invigorate was banned in Pauper in January 2013. Rishadan Port is a long-time Legacy card in prison and Death and Taxes-style decks, and Snuff Out and Misdirection have both seen Legacy play. None of these cards are legal in Modern, since Masques predates Eighth Edition.

In Commander, Food Chain is a well-known combo engine, and Bribery, Conspiracy, Black Market and Dust Bowl are popular. Because Masques has no Reserved List cards, many of these have been reprinted. Our [Vintage format guide](/blog/mtg-vintage-format-guide) explains how the restricted list works.

## Cards from Mercadian Masques worth owning

- **Brainstorm**: Legacy's signature cantrip.
- **Rishadan Port**: mana denial for Legacy and Commander.
- **Food Chain**: a combo centerpiece for Commander.
- **Misdirection**: a free interaction spell for blue eternal decks.
- **Bribery**: steals your opponent's best creature from their deck.
- **Snuff Out**: free removal that's always a sideboard option in Legacy.

## The takeaway

Mercadian Masques was intentionally toned down, and its Limited and Standard reputations reflect that. Its best cards, though, were good enough to outlast the whole block: Brainstorm, Rishadan Port, Gush and Food Chain are still part of the eternal-format conversation. If you want to play with them, Legacy is the place: a blue tempo deck with Brainstorm and free interaction, or a white deck that uses Rishadan Port to deny mana.
