Alara Reborn is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2009 that finished the Shards of Alara block, and it is still the only set in which every card is multicolored. It introduced cascade, printed Bloodbraid Elf and Maelstrom Pulse, and concluded the story of Ajani Goldmane's hunt for his brother's killer as the five shards of Alara crashed back together.

## The story

Alara was once a single plane. Long ago an event called the Sundering split it into five "shards", each of which kept only three of Magic's five colors of mana. Bant became a sunlit kingdom of knights and angels, Esper a world of artifice where people grafted metal into their bodies, Grixis a dying land of the undead, Jund a volcanic hunting ground ruled by dragons, and Naya a jungle of giant beasts and the leonin and elves who worship them. The block's storyline is told mainly in Doug Beyer's novel *Alara Unbroken*.

The hero is Ajani Goldmane, a white-furred leonin from Naya who was barely tolerated by his own pride. His brother Jazal, the pride's leader, is murdered in a magical attack, and Ajani's grief ignites his planeswalker spark. He first lands on Jund, where another planeswalker, the dragon-obsessed Sarkhan Vol, encourages him to use his anger. Ajani's search for the killer takes him across the shards, and along the way he is nursed back to health on Bant by Elspeth Tirel.

Behind everything is Nicol Bolas, the ancient dragon planeswalker. The shards were drifting back together on their own, and Bolas used agents across Alara to speed up the process, destroying the obelisks that held the worlds apart. When the shards fully merge in the event called the Conflux, a storm of raw mana called the Maelstrom forms where they meet. Bolas plans to devour that power to regain the godlike strength he lost long ago.

Alara Reborn covers the climax. Ajani learns that Jazal's death was arranged as part of Bolas's schemes, and he leads the armies of Naya and Jund against Bolas and the dragon horde under Sarkhan. As Bolas feeds on the Maelstrom, Ajani severs everyone's mana bonds, takes in the last of the storm's power, and uses it to drive Bolas off the plane. Alara survives as one patchwork world, and Bolas never forgets the leonin who beat him.

## Mechanics

### All multicolored

Every card in Alara Reborn has at least two colors, and many have three. That was the set's big surprise, and it shaped everything from removal to fixing. There are no lands or colorless artifacts in the set, so mana fixing came from the earlier Alara sets, plus landcycling creatures like Valley Rannet, which can fetch a Mountain or Forest for `{2}`.

### Cascade

Cascade was the set's new keyword. When you cast a spell with cascade, you exile cards from the top of your library until you hit a nonland card with a lower mana value, and you may cast that card for free. The rest go to the bottom in a random order. Bloodbraid Elf is the classic example: a four-mana 3/2 with haste that brings a free spell costing three or less along with it. Cascade gives you two spells for the price of one, and because you choose the rest of your deck, you control what it can hit.

### Hybrid mana in gold cards

Some cards mixed regular mana symbols with hybrid symbols. Thopter Foundry costs `{W/B}{U}`, so it is a white-blue-black card that needs only two colors to cast. This was the first time hybrid symbols appeared alongside regular colored symbols in the same cost.

### Returning mechanics

Each shard's keyword returned. Exalted (Bant) gives a creature +1/+1 when it attacks alone, as on Ethercaste Knight. Unearth (Grixis) lets you return a creature from your graveyard for one turn with haste, as with Anathemancer. Devour (Jund) lets a creature sacrifice others as it enters to get +1/+1 counters, as on the tokens made by Dragon Broodmother. Cycling returned on the Sojourner cycle, such as Jund Sojourners, which deals 1 damage when you cycle it or when it dies.

## Limited

Alara Reborn was drafted as part of the full block, with one pack each from Shards of Alara, Conflux and Alara Reborn, and it was also drafted on its own as triple Reborn during its release. Because every card was gold, drafting it felt different from any other set. You had to commit to colors earlier, and three-color decks built around a shard were normal, with the earlier sets supplying tri-lands, panoramas and other fixing.

The main archetypes followed the shards:

- **Jund (black-red-green)** was the aggressive cascade deck. Putrid Leech, Jund Hackblade, Bloodbraid Elf and Bituminous Blast gave it early pressure and card advantage, and strategy writers at the time called cascade the strongest thing you could do in the format.
- **Bant (green-white-blue)** played exalted creatures like Ethercaste Knight and Qasali Pridemage and attacked with one big creature at a time.
- **Esper (white-blue-black)** used artifact creatures like Arsenal Thresher and Glassdust Hulk along with fliers.
- **Grixis (blue-black-red)** leaned on removal such as Terminate and Slave of Bolas, plus unearth creatures.
- **Naya (red-green-white)** played big creatures and rewarded you for having one with power 5 or greater.

Cascade commons were a key reason the format was considered strong for experienced players, since a deck full of good cheap cards made every cascade better. For general drafting tips, see our guide to [reading signals in a draft](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Alara Reborn gave Standard its most famous midrange deck of the era: Jund. Built around Bloodbraid Elf, Maelstrom Pulse, Putrid Leech, Sprouting Thrinax, Blightning and Broodmate Dragon, plus Lightning Bolt from Magic 2010, it was a pile of efficient cards that each generated value. At the 2009 World Championship, three Jund players reached the Top 8, and at Pro Tour San Diego in February 2010, Jund took both finals slots, with Simon Görtzen winning the title. Jund stayed near the top of Standard until the Shards of Alara block rotated out in October 2010. No Alara Reborn card was banned in Standard.

### Modern and Legacy

Alara Reborn's cards became even more important in Modern.

- **Bloodbraid Elf** was a pillar of Modern Jund. It was banned in Modern in early 2013 and unbanned in February 2018.
- **Violent Outburst** and **Ardent Plea** became the engines of cascade combo decks like Living End. Both cost three, so in a deck with no other spells under three mana, cascade reliably finds a zero-cost card like Living End, which can only be cast by suspend or by effects like cascade. Violent Outburst was banned in Modern in March 2024, then unbanned in the May 18, 2026 announcement, so as of October 2026 it is legal again.
- **Maelstrom Pulse** has been a staple answer in green-black midrange decks.
- **Thopter Foundry** combined with Sword of the Meek in Modern, Legacy and Extended combo decks.
- **Qasali Pridemage** and **Meddling Mage** have shown up in Legacy, and Terminate is a long-time removal spell.

In Commander, Uril, the Miststalker and Sen Triplets lead decks, and Maelstrom Pulse and Time Sieve are common inclusions. For more on how bans like Bloodbraid Elf's work, see our [ban list guide](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) and the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

## Cards from Alara Reborn worth owning

- **Bloodbraid Elf**: a fan-favorite value creature for Jund in Modern and Commander.
- **Maelstrom Pulse**: flexible removal that hits any nonland permanent.
- **Violent Outburst**: a cascade enabler for Living End and similar decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Ardent Plea**: the white-blue cascade enabler for the same decks.
- **Qasali Pridemage**: a cheap creature that answers artifacts and enchantments.
- **Thopter Foundry**: a combo piece and artifact-sacrifice engine.
- **Terminate**: simple, reliable black-red removal.

Alara Reborn's all-gold experiment worked, and cascade turned out to be one of the most powerful mechanics of its era.
