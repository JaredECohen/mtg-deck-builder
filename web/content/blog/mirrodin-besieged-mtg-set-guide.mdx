Mirrodin Besieged is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2011, the second set of the Scars of Mirrodin block. It shows the metal plane of Mirrodin in open war, with every card sorted onto either the Mirran side or the Phyrexian side, and it gave the game Sword of Feast and Famine, Inkmoth Nexus, Blightsteel Colossus and Hero of Bladehold.

## The story

Scars of Mirrodin brought the Phyrexians back, and Mirrodin Besieged is the moment the infection turns into a war. The oil the Phyrexians spread, which converts flesh and metal into Phyrexian machinery, is now everywhere. Whole regions of the plane have changed, and that is why this small set came with its own ten basic lands: the landscape art extends the Scars panoramas to show the corruption creeping across them.

Three planeswalkers stand with the defenders. **Koth of the Hammer**, a Vulshok fire-mage, leads what becomes the Mirran Resistance. **Venser**, the Dominarian artificer, and **Elspeth Tirel**, who first tells Koth she won't join another doomed fight, are drawn in anyway. The Resistance finds unexpected hope in **Melira**, a Sylvok girl born without metal in her body who turns out to be immune to the oil and able to protect others from it.

Meanwhile the silver golem **Karn**, who created Mirrodin long ago, has retreated into the plane's core. The Phyrexians worship him as their new Father of Machines, and the oil has worked into his mind, leaving him struggling against the role they want to force on him. On the other side, the Phyrexian leaders, including the elf **Glissa**, now openly a traitor to the plane she once defended, and the artifact mage **Tezzeret**, push the invasion forward. Thrun, the Last Troll, appears in the Wizards story "WAR!" as one of the Mirran fighters.

The set's own art and flavor text frame the war as still undecided, which is why the two factions were split evenly. The novel *Scars of Mirrodin: The Quest for Karn* by Robert B. Wintermute follows Venser and Koth across the whole block. The answer to who wins came with the next set, New Phyrexia, where the Mirrans lose and the plane is renamed.

## Mechanics

Nearly every card carries a Mirran or Phyrexian watermark, and the two new keywords belong one to each side.

### Battle cry (Mirran)

Whenever a creature with battle cry attacks, every other attacking creature gets +1/+0 until end of turn. It rewards going wide. **Signal Pest** is a 0/1 for one that can only be blocked by fliers or reach creatures and pumps the rest of your team, and **Hero of Bladehold** combines battle cry with two tapped-and-attacking Soldier tokens every time it attacks.

### Living weapon (Phyrexian)

Living weapon Equipment creates a 0/0 black Phyrexian Germ token when it enters and attaches to it, so the Equipment is a creature right away and stays behind as Equipment once the Germ dies. **Flayer Husk** is a one-mana 1/1 that later gives +1/+1, and **Mortarpod** makes a Germ that can be sacrificed to deal 1 damage to any target.

### Infect, metalcraft, proliferate and imprint

All four return from Scars of Mirrodin. Infect creatures deal damage to creatures as -1/-1 counters and to players as poison counters; ten poison counters wins. **Phyrexian Crusader** is a 2/2 infect knight with first strike and protection from red and from white. Metalcraft rewards having three or more artifacts, proliferate adds one more of each counter already on a permanent or player, and imprint exiles a card to power an artifact, as **Knowledge Pool** does with the top three cards of every library.

### The Zeniths and the mirrored pairs

Five rare Zeniths, one per color, are X spells that shuffle themselves back into the library after resolving. **Green Sun's Zenith** puts a green creature with mana value X or less onto the battlefield; **Black Sun's Zenith** puts X -1/-1 counters on every creature. The set also pairs cards across factions: **Mirran Crusader** and Phyrexian Crusader, **Peace Strider** and **Pierce Strider**, and Go for the Throat with **Crush**.

## Limited

Mirrodin Besieged changed how Magic was drafted. Before it, block drafts opened the oldest set first; from this set on, the newest set came first. The format was one pack of Mirrodin Besieged followed by two packs of Scars of Mirrodin. The new set also slowed Limited down compared to the fast, artifact-heavy Scars format.

Drafters mostly followed the two factions. Mirran decks wanted metalcraft artifacts, battle cry attackers like **Accorder Paladin** and cheap equipment, while Phyrexian decks leaned into infect with **Septic Rats**, **Plague Myr** and **Viridian Corrupter** and into living weapon Equipment. Infect was a real all-in plan: a poison deck could rarely switch to dealing regular damage, so mixing infect and normal creatures was a classic trap. Removal such as **Burn the Impure** (which punishes infect creatures), **Banishment Decree** and the colorless **Mortarpod** kept board stalls from settling in. For reading those trade-offs in a draft, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Mirrodin Besieged entered a Standard already dominated by blue-white Stoneforge Mystic decks, and it handed them their best weapon. **Caw-Blade** paired Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Stoneforge Mystic and Squadron Hawk with Swords; Stoneforge could fetch Sword of Feast and Famine, and a creature carrying it untapped all your lands after hitting, letting the deck attack and still hold up counterspells. Coverage from the time lists Caw-Blade, Caw-Go, Grixis Tezzeret and Valakut decks as the main archetypes after the set's release.

The deck was so dominant that on June 20, 2011 Wizards banned Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic in Standard, effective July 1, 2011. Sword of Feast and Famine itself was never banned, and post-ban Caw-Blade lists kept playing it. Hero of Bladehold and Thrun, the Last Troll were both important threats through the rest of the set's Standard life, which ended at the October 2012 rotation.

The set's lasting impact, as of October 2026:

- **Modern**: Inkmoth Nexus is a core piece of Infect, and Go for the Throat is a common removal choice. Green Sun's Zenith was banned in Modern for over a decade and was unbanned in December 2024.
- **Legacy**: Green Sun's Zenith appears in green creature decks, Inkmoth Nexus in Infect, and Sword of Feast and Famine in Stoneforge decks.
- **Vintage**: Blightsteel Colossus is the classic target for Tinker.
- **Commander**: Consecrated Sphinx is on the official Commander Game Changers list. Massacre Wurm, Hero of Bladehold, Black Sun's Zenith, Myr Turbine and Thopter Assembly are all popular.

None of the set's cards are banned or restricted in Modern, Legacy, Vintage or Commander. For how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Mirrodin Besieged worth owning

- **Sword of Feast and Famine**: still one of the best Swords in Legacy and Commander.
- **Inkmoth Nexus**: the land every Infect deck wants.
- **Green Sun's Zenith**: a repeatable creature tutor in Legacy, Modern and Commander.
- **Consecrated Sphinx**: a card-draw engine in multiplayer games.
- **Go for the Throat**: efficient instant-speed removal in every format that allows it.
- **Blightsteel Colossus**: a Vintage and Commander finisher.
- **Hero of Bladehold**: a strong four-drop in white token decks.

Mirrodin Besieged is a small set with an unusually long reach. If you are building in Modern, it is a reliable source of removal and Infect pieces; in Commander, it offers some of the strongest card draw and artifact payoffs ever printed.
