Rivals of Ixalan is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2018, the small second set of the Ixalan block. Four factions of pirates, vampires, merfolk and dinosaur riders finally reach the golden city of Orazca and fight over the Immortal Sun, while Jace and Vraska uncover why it was hidden there. The set added ascend, a cycle of Elder Dinosaurs and some of the most-played midrange creatures of its era.

## The story

Ixalan's four factions spent the first set searching for Orazca, a city of gold hidden in the jungle. In Rivals they arrive. The **Legion of Dusk** are conquering vampires from across the sea, led here by Vona of Iedo and Mavren Fein. The **Sun Empire** is a human empire whose warriors ride dinosaurs, and its poet-warrior Huatli is the story's other lead. The **River Heralds** are merfolk who have guarded the city's secret for generations, and the **Brazen Coalition** are pirates. Hidden at the top of Orazca's tallest temple is the **Immortal Sun**, an artifact everyone wants.

The merfolk shaman Kumena reaches the city first and touches the Sun, but the vampires throw him from the tower. Huatli travels with the minotaur planeswalker Angrath, who is desperate to get home to his daughters and can't, because the Immortal Sun stops planeswalkers from leaving Ixalan. The two argue all the way to Orazca, and in the final struggle for the Sun, Huatli's magic wakes the Elder Dinosaurs, the colossal beasts that the cycle of legendary rares depicts.

Meanwhile, Jace and Vraska, who began as enemies, wash ashore together after falling down a waterfall. Jace's lost memories come back, including his childhood on the plane of Vryn, and the two become friends. At Orazca they find **Azor**, the sphinx who founded Ravnica's guild system. Azor reveals that he built the Immortal Sun as a trap: he and Ugin meant to lure Nicol Bolas to Ixalan and pin him there, but the plan never sprang. Jace, acting as the Living Guildpact, judges Azor and sentences him to guard a lonely island forever.

The ending sets up the next chapter. Vraska had come to Ixalan as Bolas's agent, sent to retrieve the Sun. Learning what Bolas plans for Ravnica, she and Jace agree to work against him from the inside, and she has Jace hide her memories of their friendship so Bolas can't read them. She hands the Sun over to Tezzeret, who carries it to Bolas, and leaves. Angrath goes home to his family, Huatli sets out to find stories beyond the Sun Empire, and Elenda, the long-lost vampire saint, rises to call her Legion back to its old ways. The thread pays off in War of the Spark.

## Mechanics

### Ascend

Ascend checks whether you control ten or more permanents. Once you do, you get the **city's blessing** for the rest of the game, even if you lose permanents later. Many cards get stronger with it. **Wayward Swordtooth** (rare) lets you play an extra land each turn from the start, but its 5/5 body can only attack or block once you have the blessing. Lands, tokens and Treasure all count, so wide decks get there quickly.

### Enrage

Enrage triggers whenever the creature is dealt damage, so it rewards Dinosaurs that are big enough to survive a fight or a burn spell. **Siegehorn Ceratops** gets two +1/+1 counters each time it's damaged, and **Polyraptor** copies itself, which makes it an infinite-combo piece with the right damage effect.

### Explore

When a creature explores, you reveal the top card of your library. A land goes to your hand; anything else gives the creature a +1/+1 counter, and you choose whether to keep the card on top or mill it. **Jadelight Ranger** explores twice, giving card selection and a bigger body for three mana.

### Raid, Treasure and the flip enchantments

Raid, the Pirates' mechanic, rewards you for attacking before casting a spell, as with **Swaggering Corsair**. Treasure tokens, artifacts you sacrifice for one mana of any color, return from Ixalan. Legendary enchantments like **Journey to Eternity** and **Hadana's Climb** transform into powerful legendary lands once their condition is met. ## Limited

Rivals of Ixalan was drafted as two packs of Rivals followed by one pack of the original Ixalan, and both sets were built around four tribes spread across the ten color pairs:

- **White-black (Legion Lieutenant): Vampires.** Lifelink and go-wide aggression.
- **Green-blue (Merfolk Mistbinder): Merfolk.** Explore, counters and hexproof tricks.
- **Blue-red (Storm Fleet Sprinter): Pirates tempo.** Evasive attackers and raid.
- **Black-red (Dire Fleet Neckbreaker): Pirates aggro.** Pump attacking Pirates and race.
- **Blue-black (Deadeye Brawler): Pirates value.** Deathtouch and ascend card draw.
- **Red-green (Raging Regisaur): Dinosaurs.** Big enrage creatures and burn.
- **Green-white (Atzocan Seer): Dinosaurs.** Fixing, ramp and rebuying your best Dinosaur.
- **Red-white (Relentless Raptor): Dinosaurs aggro.** Must-attack creatures and combat tricks.
- **White-blue (Resplendent Griffin): ascend fliers.** Fliers that grow once you have the city's blessing.
- **Black-green (Jungle Creeper): grinding.** Recursive creatures and removal for long games.

Removal was the priority at common, with cards like **Impale**, **Bombard** and **Moment of Craving**, and Ravenous Chupacabra was one of the best uncommons in the set. Ascend asked you to keep a board full of permanents, which favored token makers and cheap creatures. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Rivals of Ixalan entered Standard in January 2018, just after Wizards banned four cards to weaken Energy decks. No Rivals of Ixalan cards were banned in Standard, but several became fixtures.

Rekindling Phoenix was the set's breakout threat. Because it returns on its own, it was very hard for midrange and control decks to remove for good. It powered red-green Monsters decks alongside Glorybringer, including a Grand Prix Memphis win in 2018, and it was a common four-drop in red decks for the rest of its time in Standard. Jadelight Ranger had an even longer run. It showed up in green midrange through 2018 and became a core card in Golgari Midrange after Guilds of Ravnica arrived that fall, alongside Ravenous Chupacabra. Merfolk and Vampire decks also used the set's tribal cards. The set rotated out in October 2019, when Throne of Eldraine arrived. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains the yearly cycle.

In Pioneer, the set's cards are role-players rather than format-defining staples. Midrange decks have used Jadelight Ranger and Ravenous Chupacabra, and Merfolk decks pick up Silvergill Adept and Kumena. In Modern, Blood Sun is an occasional sideboard card against decks that rely on land abilities.

Commander is where the set gets its most play. Ghalta, Zacama and Etali lead Dinosaur and big-creature decks, Elenda is a popular Vampire commander, Kumena leads Merfolk, and Ravenous Chupacabra is a common removal creature in black decks. As of October 2026, Ghalta remains one of the most recognizable green commanders, and our guide to [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format if you're starting there.

## Cards from Rivals of Ixalan worth owning

- **Ravenous Chupacabra**: removal on a body for any black deck.
- **Rekindling Phoenix**: a resilient threat for red decks and Commander.
- **Jadelight Ranger**: card selection and a growing body for green midrange.
- **Ghalta, Primal Hunger**: a cheap-to-cast finisher for creature-heavy green decks.
- **Zacama, Primal Calamity**: a Naya Commander centerpiece with built-in utility.
- **Elenda, the Dusk Rose**: a Vampire payoff and commander.
- **Silvergill Adept and Kumena**: the backbone of Merfolk decks.
