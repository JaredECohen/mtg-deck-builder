## Short answer

You play control by deciding, every turn, which threat you are allowed to answer and which one you will take on the life total. In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), the deck works when your answers line up with the opponent's threats one for one and your card draw replaces the answer you just spent. It falls apart when you answer a creature that was going to die anyway, or when you tap out for a finisher and the next threat resolves uncontested. The official lists make the split concrete: one is a 60-card Pioneer permission deck, the other is a Commander spell-discount deck that was printed under a control name.

Start with the mana. Dimir Control plays 26 lands. Its nonland curve is not a pile of one-mana counters; it has a real six- and seven-mana top end. You cannot play that curve on 22 lands and then blame the archetype when the finisher stays stuck. Seize Control, Mizzix of the Izmagnus, plays 39 lands in the 99 and a curve that peaks at four and five mana, because the commander is a cost reducer and the spells are the deck. Playing Mizzix like Dimir, holding up one blue mana and never casting the five-drop, wastes the card the deck is built around.

The goldfish is the practice reminder. Neither list won a solitaire game. Practice for control is not how fast can I kill a goldfish. It is which of these two hands I keep when I am on the draw against a two-drop, and which answer I spend on the first creature that is actually going to end the game. Sideboarding, for the Pioneer list, is where the deck changes shape between games. The [sideboarding guide](/blog/sideboarding-101) is the companion. The lists below are the measured shape.

### Dimir Control

Dimir Control is an official Pioneer Challenger Deck released 2022-11-04. The decklist and the Wizards source link come from MTGJSON file DimirControl_Q08. Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/news/pioneer-challenger-decks-2022-decklists-2022-09-29.

Read this as a deck that is trying not to race. A goldfish win rate near zero is the expected shape, not a bug in the list.

The nonland mana curve is 8 at mana value 1, 10 at mana value 2, 8 at mana value 3, 2 at mana value 4, 3 at mana value 6, 3 at mana value 7 or more. The list runs 26 lands in the library.

Colored sources, using Scryfall produced_mana and counting basic lands as the color on their type line, restricted to this deck's colors: 14 blue, 15 black.

MTGJSON AllPricesToday (2026-11-01) prices the exact printing in the product at $43.07 for main deck, sideboard, and commander, using TCGplayer nonfoil retail when it exists. The three most expensive nonbasic names are 1 Watery Grave at $9.99 total, 2 Drowned Catacomb at $4.56 total, 4 Fatal Push at $3.28 total, which is 41.4% of the priced total. Basic lands in the product add $2.57 on their own, because the price is the printing Wizards put in the deck, not the cheapest Plains on the market.

The Deck Lab goldfish played 120 solitaire games (seed 1729, control policy, opponent at 20 life, 12 turns) and never won one. The simulator casts spells and turns creatures sideways. It does not assemble a named combo, storm off, or mill an opponent out. A zero win rate means this list does not win a goldfish race. It does not mean the deck cannot win a real match.

Budget benchmarks, not upgrade advice: the cheapest same-type card within one mana value in the other official lists we priced, still legal in the format. Shark Typhoon at $2.22 versus Swarm Intelligence at $0.38 (a $1.84 gap on one copy). Those stand-ins do not do the same job. The gap is how much you are paying for the specific card, not a sleeve-in recommendation.

## Common mistakes

- Playing the Pioneer land count in the Commander deck, or the reverse. 26 lands is a 60-card number. 39 is a 99-card number.
- Tapping out for card draw into an empty board and an opponent who still has a threat. The life total only buys time if an answer is available.
- Cutting the top end because the goldfish never cast it. The seven-mana cards are why the early turns exist.
- Assuming Mizzix's counters are the same job as Dimir's counters. One deck wants the spell to resolve for less mana. The other wants the opponent's spell not to resolve.
- Sleeveing the 2022 Challenger list into an event without reading the ban list that day. The bulk file said the cards were legal on 2026-11-01. That is a dated fact, not a promise.

## FAQ

### How many counterspells does a control deck need?

Enough that you can hold one up on the turns that matter, and not so many that your hand is answers to problems you do not have. The Dimir curve has 8 one-drops and 10 two-drops in the nonlands. That is the product's mix of early interaction and setup, not a requirement to play 20 counters.

### Why does control feel like it never does anything?

Because the early plan is not a creature. The goldfish agrees: zero wins. The something happens after the opponent's hand is empty.

### Can I goldfish a control deck to practice?

You can, and you should expect it not to win. Use the games to see whether you cast your lands and your draw spells. Use a friend, or the builder, when you want to practice the answers.

### Is a shock land required?

Watery Grave is the most expensive line on the Dimir product. The deck wants blue and black sources on time. A slower land that enters tapped changes the turn you can hold up a two-mana answer. That is a real cost, separate from the dollar cost.
