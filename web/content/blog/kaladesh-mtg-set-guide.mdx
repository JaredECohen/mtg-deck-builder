Kaladesh is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2016, set on Chandra Nalaar's home plane, a bright, inventive world powered by a magical energy called aether. It introduced energy counters, Vehicles and fabricate, and its story follows the Gatewatch to a grand Inventors' Fair that hides a government crackdown. It also produced some of the most powerful and most-banned Standard cards of its decade, plus a run of staples still played in Pioneer, Modern and Commander.

## The story

Kaladesh is a plane where natural spellcasters are rare and inventors do the work that wizards do elsewhere. Thopters fill the sky, automatons walk the streets, and everything runs on aether, a shimmering energy that the ruling Consulate controls and rations. Artifacts are built to be beautiful as well as useful. One thing is strictly forbidden: fire magic, which is punishable by death.

That rule shaped Chandra Nalaar's childhood. Her parents, the inventors Pia and Kiran Nalaar, quietly moved aether to people who needed it, and when they discovered their daughter was a pyromancer they fled the capital, Ghirapur. The Consulate's Captain Baral found them. He burned their village, killed Kiran and took Chandra to be executed. Her spark ignited on the pyre and she planeswalked away, believing her mother was dead too. Pia survived, spent years in prison and became a revolutionary.

When the set opens, the Consulate is hosting the Inventors' Fair, a festival of invention in Ghirapur. Chandra learns her mother is alive and goes home, and the rest of the Gatewatch (Jace, Gideon, Nissa and Liliana) follows. They aren't the only off-world visitors. The planeswalker Dovin Baan, an expert at finding the flaws in any system, has been brought in to run inspections, and the Fair's new Head Judge is Tezzeret, an artifice-obsessed planeswalker working for Nicol Bolas.

The Fair turns out to be a trap. The Consulate uses it to seize the most promising inventions and detain their creators, and Tezzeret stages a quicksmithing duel with Pia as bait for Chandra and the renegades. Kaladesh ends with the Consulate's crackdown tightening and the renegade movement, led in part by Pia, preparing to fight back. That uprising, and Tezzeret's real goal, a device called the Planar Bridge, are the subject of the next set, Aether Revolt.

## Mechanics

### Energy

Energy counters (written `{E}`) are a resource that you, the player, collect. Cards give you energy when they enter, attack or resolve, and other cards let you spend it. Energy doesn't empty between turns, so you can save it up and spend it on any card that uses it. **Harnessed Lightning** (uncommon, `{1}{R}`) gives you three energy, then lets you pay any amount to deal that much damage to a creature, and leftover energy carries over to your other cards.

### Vehicles and crew

Vehicles are artifacts that turn into artifact creatures when you crew them. To crew, tap any number of your untapped creatures with total power equal to or greater than the crew number. The Vehicle then attacks or blocks until end of turn. Because crewing isn't a tap-symbol ability, a creature that just arrived can crew. **Smuggler's Copter** (rare) is a two-mana 3/3 flier with crew 1 that loots whenever it attacks or blocks. Vehicles dodge sorcery-speed removal and creature sweepers, which is a big part of why they were so good.

### Fabricate

Fabricate N gives you a choice when the creature enters: put N +1/+1 counters on it, or create N 1/1 colorless Servo artifact tokens. You go tall or wide as the situation demands, and the Servos feed artifact synergies. **Angel of Invention** (mythic) has fabricate 2 and makes your other creatures bigger, so either mode is strong.

### Artifacts everywhere

Kaladesh is an artifact set at every rarity. Cards care about controlling artifacts, like **Toolcraft Exemplar**, and the land **Inventors' Fair** tutors one up. Kaladesh also introduced the word "create" for making tokens, which every set since has used. Artifact-heavy cards are easier to read once you know the basics in our guide to [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Kaladesh's ten two-color archetypes split between its three big themes:

- **White-blue: enter-the-battlefield triggers.** Value creatures and flickers.
- **Blue-black: artifact control.** Durable blockers, removal and card draw.
- **Black-red: artifact aggro.** Cheap artifact-matters attackers.
- **Red-green: energy beatdown.** Creatures that cash in energy to grow or trample.
- **Green-white: fabricate.** Go tall or go wide with Servos and counters.
- **White-black: recursion.** Bring artifacts and creatures back for value.
- **Blue-red: energy and artifacts.** Thopters, energy and tricks.
- **Black-green: fabricate midrange.** Counters, Servos and solid bodies.
- **Red-white: Vehicles.** Crew big Vehicles with small creatures and race.
- **Green-blue: energy tempo.** Evasive creatures that feed and spend energy.

Vehicles at common shaped the format. **Renegade Freighter**, a crew 2 Vehicle that hits for 5 trample, was one of the strongest commons in the set, and **Sky Skiff** gave decks an extra flier. Removal included **Tidy Conclusion** and **Die Young** in black, Harnessed Lightning in red and **Revoke Privileges** in white, which also stops a creature from crewing. The common "Thriving" creature cycle and puzzleknots gave every color a little energy to work with. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2016 to 2018

Kaladesh's Standard years were dominated by its own cards, and Wizards had to step in several times:

- **Smuggler's Copter** went into nearly every aggressive deck. It was banned in Standard on January 9, 2017, along with Emrakul, the Promised End and Reflector Mage.
- **Aetherworks Marvel** powered Temur and four-color "Marvel" decks that used energy to cast huge spells like Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger for free as early as turn four. It was banned on June 14, 2017.
- **Saheeli Rai** became half of the Copycat combo once Aether Revolt added Felidar Guardian. The two together made infinite cats, and Felidar Guardian was banned in April 2017.
- **Attune with Aether** fueled Temur Energy, the best deck of late 2017. It was banned on January 15, 2018, alongside Aether Revolt's Rogue Refiner.

Between the bans, Mardu Vehicles (Toolcraft Exemplar, Veteran Motorist, Cultivator's Caravan and, later, Aether Revolt's Heart of Kiran), Temur and Four-Color Energy, and control decks with Torrential Gearhulk and Fumigate defined the format. Chandra, Torch of Defiance was one of the most powerful planeswalkers in Standard throughout. Kaladesh rotated out in October 2018, when Guilds of Ravnica arrived.

### Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander

Kaladesh is one of Pioneer's most important sets, as of October 2026. The fastlands (Spirebluff Canal, Botanical Sanctum, Blooming Marsh, Concealed Courtyard and Inspiring Vantage) appear in two-color decks across the format. Smuggler's Copter was banned in Pioneer in December 2019 and unbanned in December 2023, when it quickly returned to aggressive decks. Skysovereign, Consul Flagship is a key part of Greasefang combo, Torrential Gearhulk has been a control finisher, and energy aggro decks have used Harnessed Lightning and Bristling Hydra. Aetherflux Reservoir found a Pioneer combo home alongside Amalia Benavides Aguirre and Wildgrowth Walker, until Amalia was banned.

In Modern, Cathartic Reunion helps Dredge fill its graveyard, the fastlands show up in many decks, and Inventors' Fair is part of artifact strategies. Chandra, Torch of Defiance has been played in Modern and even Legacy. Paradoxical Outcome powers storm decks in Legacy and Vintage, and Fleetwheel Cruiser has turned up in Vintage artifact decks.

In Commander, Panharmonicon and Aetherflux Reservoir are classics, and Paradoxical Outcome and the fastlands are common choices. Our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains how those decks are put together.

## Cards from Kaladesh worth owning

- **The fastlands**: untapped dual lands that matter in Pioneer, Modern and Commander.
- **Chandra, Torch of Defiance**: a planeswalker that's been played in nearly every format.
- **Smuggler's Copter**: a Pioneer aggro staple again since its unban.
- **Torrential Gearhulk**: a flash finisher for control decks.
- **Skysovereign, Consul Flagship**: a Pioneer Vehicle with a combo role.
- **Panharmonicon**: a Commander favorite for enter-the-battlefield decks.
- **Aetherflux Reservoir**: a life-gain win condition in Commander.
- **Paradoxical Outcome**: a key storm piece in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Inventors' Fair**: a utility land for artifact decks.

Kaladesh is a set about resources: energy you bank, Vehicles you crew, Servos you spend. When you build with it, count how much energy you produce, and make sure your creatures can crew your Vehicles. For Constructed today, the fastlands, Chandra and the Pioneer Vehicles are the cards to know.
