Urza's Legacy is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 1999, the second set of the Urza block. It continued the story of the planeswalker Urza's war against Phyrexia, kept the block's echo and cycling mechanics, and printed some of the most powerful cards in Magic's history, including Tinker, Grim Monolith, Mother of Runes and Memory Jar, the first card to receive an emergency ban. It was also the first Magic set to include premium foil cards in its boosters.

## The story

The Urza block tells the story that leads up to the Weatherlight Saga. Long after the Brothers' War, Urza has become a planeswalker obsessed with one goal: defeating Phyrexia and its dark god Yawgmoth, the power that corrupted his brother Mishra during the Brothers' War. Urza's Saga, the first set, showed him rediscovering Phyrexia's threat. Urza's Legacy, told in the novel *Time Streams* by J. Robert King, follows his next plan.

Urza sets up an academy on the island of **Tolaria**, where he and his friend, the mage **Barrin**, teach a generation of gifted students. Two of them become major characters for years to come: **Teferi**, a young prankster from Zhalfir with a talent for time magic, and **Jhoira** of the Ghitu, a brilliant artificer. Urza's real project is secret. He wants to travel back in time and stop the ancient Thran from becoming the Phyrexians in the first place, and he builds a silver golem, **Karn**, to make the journey.

Phyrexia gets there first. A Phyrexian sleeper agent, a castaway named **Kerrick** whom Jhoira protects and grows close to, sabotages the time experiments. The result is a catastrophe that rips Tolaria's time apart, leaving pockets of the island where time runs much faster or much slower than the rest of the world. Kerrick survives inside a fast-time rift, renames himself **K'rrik**, and tries to build an army of Phyrexian creatures there over what is, for him, a very long time. Urza and Barrin eventually return and destroy what he built, but Urza has to give up on time travel.

The ending sets up the rest of the block. Unable to change the past, Urza decides to build the weapons and the people who will fight Phyrexia in the future, which leads to his breeding program and the flying ship *Weatherlight* in [Urza's Destiny](/blog/urzas-destiny-mtg-set-guide). The cards show it all: Tolarian students, Phyrexian sleeper agents, Karn's experiments and Urza's war machines.

## Mechanics

### Echo

Echo returned from Urza's Saga. A creature with echo costs its mana cost again at the start of your next upkeep, or you sacrifice it. **Avalanche Riders** is a 2/2 haste creature that destroys a land when it enters, so even if you skip the echo payment, you got a land destruction spell. **Karmic Guide** returns a creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield when it enters, which made it a combo and reanimation piece whether or not you paid echo.

### Cycling

Cycling, also from Urza's Saga, lets you pay a cost and discard a card to draw a new one. In Urza's Legacy it appears on cards like **Miscalculation**, a counterspell that taxes `{2}`, and **Radiant's Judgment**, which destroys a creature with power 4 or greater. **Cloud of Faeries** combines cycling with the set's untap theme.

### Untap effects

Urza's block was famous for spells and creatures that untapped your lands, effectively making them free. In Urza's Legacy, **Frantic Search** draws two, discards two and untaps up to three lands, **Snap** bounces a creature and untaps two lands, **Cloud of Faeries** untaps two lands when it enters, and **Palinchron** untaps up to seven lands and can return to your hand. These cards powered the combo decks of the era and are a big reason the block is remembered as one of Magic's most broken.

### Creature lands

Urza's Legacy printed a cycle of five lands that can turn into creatures: **Faerie Conclave**, **Forbidding Watchtower**, **Ghitu Encampment**, **Spawning Pool** and **Treetop Village**. Each enters tapped, taps for one color, and can become a creature until end of turn for two mana. Treetop Village, a 3/3 trampling Ape, was the most played.

## Limited

Urza's Legacy was drafted alongside Urza's Saga, usually with Urza's Saga packs first and the newer set last. Booster draft was well established by 1999, but detailed records of how the format played are thin, so it's best not to treat specific archetypes as settled history.

What the cards themselves show is a format where cheap removal and evasive threats mattered. **Bone Shredder**, a flier that destroys a nonartifact, nonblack creature when it enters, and **Radiant's Judgment**, which can be cycled when there's nothing big to kill, were valuable. **Thornwind Faeries** pinged creatures and players, and echo creatures traded tempo for raw power. Cycling made the format smoother than many older sets, since dead cards could be traded for new ones.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Standard (1999-2001).** Urza's Legacy landed during what players call "Combo Winter," a stretch when Urza's Saga's combo decks dominated tournaments. Memory Jar made it worse almost immediately: a fast combo deck built around it, known as Broken Jar, showed up as soon as the set was tournament legal, and Wizards issued the first emergency ban in Magic's history. The ban was announced in March 1999 and took effect on April 1, 1999, covering the Constructed formats of the time. Once the dust settled, Urza's Legacy's fair cards proved their worth: Rancor became a staple of aggressive green decks, Mother of Runes protected white creatures, Avalanche Riders appeared in land destruction, and the creature lands, especially Treetop Village, showed up in many decks. Urza's Legacy rotated out with the rest of the Urza block when Odyssey arrived in October 2001.

**Legacy.** Mother of Runes is a core piece of Death and Taxes. Crop Rotation appears in Lands and Dark Depths combo decks. Grim Monolith has been used in artifact combo decks, and Defense Grid in combo sideboards against countermagic. Tinker, Memory Jar and Frantic Search are banned in Legacy as of October 2026. See our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) for more.

**Vintage.** Tinker and Memory Jar are restricted, and Tinker remains one of the format's signature cards, since it can put a giant artifact creature onto the battlefield as early as turn one or two. Goblin Welder has a long history in artifact-heavy Vintage decks. See the [Vintage format guide](/blog/mtg-vintage-format-guide).

**Commander.** Grim Monolith, Mother of Runes, Deranged Hermit, Goblin Welder, Phyrexian Reclamation, Karmic Guide and Palinchron are common, and the set's enchantments like No Mercy and Crawlspace protect players in multiplayer. Tinker is banned in Commander. For how bans and power levels work at the casual table, see [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

**Pauper.** Rancor, Snap and Crop Rotation have all been played in Pauper. Frantic Search and Cloud of Faeries are banned there.

## Cards from Urza's Legacy worth owning

- **Mother of Runes**: still a Legacy and Commander staple.
- **Rancor**: a long-lived Aura for green aggro in Pauper, Modern and Commander.
- **Grim Monolith**: a powerful Commander mana rock (on the Reserved List).
- **Crop Rotation**: a land tutor for Legacy and Commander lands decks.
- **Goblin Welder**: a Commander and Vintage artifact engine.
- **Treetop Village**: the best of the creature land cycle.
- **Tinker**: restricted in Vintage, and the set's most famous card.

## The takeaway

Urza's Legacy is a set from Magic's most powerful era, and it shows: free spells, artifact mana and the first emergency ban all came from it. Its fair cards, like Mother of Runes and Rancor, have aged just as well as its broken ones.
