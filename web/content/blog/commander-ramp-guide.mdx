Most Magic: The Gathering Commander decks want somewhere around 10 ramp cards, with the exact number depending on how expensive your commander and your spells are. The best ramp is cheap, usually two mana or less, and the right kind depends on your colors: green has the deepest land ramp, while every deck can use mana rocks. Ramp is how a Commander deck gets ahead of three opponents, but too much of it leaves you with mana and nothing to spend it on.

The [ramp deck guide](/blog/mtg-ramp-deck-guide) covers ramp as a strategy in 60-card formats, and the [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) puts ramp into a full deck template. This guide zooms in on the Commander-specific questions: how much, which kind and when.

## Why ramp matters more in Commander

Commander games are long. Each player starts at 40 life, there are usually three opponents to get through, and decks are built around powerful cards that cost five, six or more mana. A player who reaches six mana on turn four instead of turn six gets two extra turns of doing big things.

Your commander adds another reason. It starts in the command zone, and each time you cast it from there, it costs `{2}` more for each previous time you cast it from the command zone that game. If your commander gets removed a couple of times, ramp is what lets you keep casting it.

## How many ramp cards to run

As rough guidance:

| Deck type | Ramp cards | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low-curve deck, commander costs 3 or less | 6-8 | Your spells are cheap, so extra mana has less to do |
| Typical deck, commander costs 4-5 | 9-11 | The standard starting point |
| High-curve deck, commander costs 6 or more | 12-15 | Ramp is part of the game plan |
| Dedicated ramp or big mana deck | 15 or more | Often green, with land ramp payoffs |

These numbers sit alongside your land count, they don't replace it. A typical deck runs around 36 to 38 lands plus its ramp. Cutting lands to make room for rocks is risky, because rocks don't count toward your land drops and are easier to remove.

Count cards that do double duty. A creature that ramps when it enters is ramp and a body. A rock that can be sacrificed to draw a card is ramp early and card draw late.

## The three kinds of ramp

### Mana rocks

**Mana rocks** are artifacts that tap for mana. They're the most universal form of ramp because any deck can play them.

- **Sol Ring** costs `{1}` and taps for `{C}{C}`. It's in a huge share of Commander decks for a reason.
- **Arcane Signet** and **Commander's Sphere** tap for one mana of any color in your commander's color identity. Commander's Sphere can also be sacrificed to draw a card.
- **Mind Stone** taps for `{C}`, and can be sacrificed later to draw a card.
- **Signets**, such as Dimir Signet, cost one mana to activate and give you two colored mana, which fixes two colors at once. **Talismans**, such as Talisman of Dominance, tap for colorless for free or for one of two colors at the cost of 1 damage.

Strengths: fast, colorless, castable in any deck, and they put out mana the turn after you cast them. Weaknesses: they die to artifact removal and artifact board wipes, and many of them only produce colorless mana.

### Land ramp

**Land ramp** puts extra lands onto the battlefield, usually from your library. It's mostly green.

- **Rampant Growth** puts a basic land onto the battlefield tapped.
- **Cultivate** and **Kodama's Reach** find two basic lands: one onto the battlefield tapped and one into your hand.
- **Nature's Lore** and **Three Visits** find a Forest card and put it onto the battlefield untapped. Because they search for the Forest type rather than a basic, they can find nonbasic lands with that type too.
- **Farseek** finds a Plains, Island, Swamp or Mountain card and puts it onto the battlefield tapped.

Strengths: lands are very hard to remove, they survive almost every board wipe, they fix colors, and they trigger landfall abilities. Weaknesses: land ramp usually costs more mana for the same acceleration, and most of it is green. Landfall decks get double value from it, as the [landfall guide](/blog/landfall-deck-guide) explains.

### Mana creatures

**Mana creatures** (often called dorks) are creatures that tap for mana, like Llanowar Elves and Birds of Paradise. They're the fastest ramp available, often just one mana. But they're creatures, so they can't tap for mana the turn you cast them unless they have haste, and they die to creature removal and board wipes, which are common in Commander.

### Which kind to use

Most decks want a mix, weighted by colors and plan:

- **Green decks** can lean on land ramp and dorks, with a few rocks.
- **Non-green decks** rely mostly on rocks.
- **Decks that run their own board wipes** should favor rocks and land ramp, since their dorks would die to their own sweepers.
- **Artifact decks** get extra value from rocks, which count as artifacts for their synergies.

## Ramp by color

Each color has a different relationship with ramp.

- **Green** has the most and the best: land ramp of every size, mana creatures, and cards that let you play extra lands each turn.
- **White** tends toward catch-up ramp: cards that give you lands only if an opponent controls more lands than you, like Knight of the White Orchid. Smothering Tithe makes Treasure tokens when opponents draw cards unless they pay.
- **Red** makes Treasure tokens (artifacts you can sacrifice for one mana of any color) and has rituals, spells that give you a burst of mana for one turn. Unexpected Windfall, for example, has you discard a card as an additional cost, then draws two cards and makes two Treasures.
- **Black** also has rituals, like Dark Ritual, and some Treasure makers, but leans heavily on rocks for lasting ramp.
- **Blue** has very little dedicated ramp and relies almost entirely on rocks.
- **Colorless** options fill gaps in any deck. Besides rocks, cards like Wayfarer's Bauble, Burnished Hart and Solemn Simulacrum fetch basic lands and work in every color identity.

One-shot mana, like rituals and Treasure, is a burst rather than permanent acceleration. It's good for casting a big spell early, but it doesn't keep you ahead the way a rock or extra land does. Count it separately from your main ramp.

## Ramp and the mana curve

Ramp only helps if it comes down early. The ideal ramp card costs two mana or less, so that it turns your turn-three play into a turn-four play.

- **One- and two-mana ramp** is the core. Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Rampant Growth and two-mana rocks all fit here.
- **Three-mana ramp** like Cultivate is still good, and it gives you two lands' worth of value.
- **Four-mana ramp and up** is risky. By the time you cast it, you could have cast a real threat instead. Run it only if it does something else too.

Also look at what you're ramping into. If your deck has nothing to cast with eight mana, the ninth ramp card does nothing. Make sure the top of your curve has enough strong spells to use the extra mana. The [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) covers checking your curve as a whole.

### Late-game ramp is a dead draw

Topdecking a mana rock on turn ten rarely helps. That's why rocks and ramp cards with a second use are valuable: a Mind Stone or Commander's Sphere can turn into a card, and a Solemn Simulacrum can block and draw when it dies.

### Ramp can draw attention

In multiplayer games, a player who's far ahead on mana early is often seen as the biggest threat. That isn't a reason to skip ramp, but it's worth knowing that a fast start can make you the target. The [Commander brackets guide](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) is a good place to read about how fast mana fits into a table's expected power level.

## The takeaway

Start with about 10 ramp cards, adding more for an expensive commander and fewer for a cheap deck, and keep your land count around 36 to 38 on top of that. Favor ramp that costs two mana or less. Use rocks in any deck, land ramp and dorks in green, and count one-shot mana like rituals and Treasure separately. Make sure your deck has something worth ramping into, and prefer ramp that does something useful late in the game.
