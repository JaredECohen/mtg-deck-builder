Jumpstart is a Magic: The Gathering product line built around a simple idea: open two 20-card packs, shuffle them together and play, with no deckbuilding at all. The original Jumpstart released on July 17, 2020 (delayed from June by the COVID-19 pandemic), and Jumpstart 2022 followed on December 2, 2022. Each pack is a themed half-deck, from Dogs and Unicorns to Goblins and Reanimation, and both sets hid strong reprints like Rhystic Study, Reanimate, Craterhoof Behemoth and Goldspan Dragon among their themes.

## What the set was for

Jumpstart was designed for players who want to skip deckbuilding. Each 20-card pack contains seven or eight lands and twelve or thirteen spells with a shared theme, and comes with a summary card naming that theme. Two packs make a ready-to-play 40-card deck, the same size as a draft deck, so you get a coherent, randomly paired deck in seconds. The MTG Wiki compares the idea to board games like Smash Up, which combine two factions to make a new one.

Every pack holds at least one rare, and one in three has a second. Themes come in common, rare and mythic "rarities," but that marks how many variations exist, not power level: common themes have four different lists, rare themes two, and mythic themes one. Across the 46 themes, that adds up to 121 possible pack contents in each set.

The line became a big part of how Wizards brings new players in. After the 2020 set, Jumpstart Boosters replaced Theme Boosters for several premier sets, the format moved to MTG Arena as Jump In!, and later physical sets followed, including Foundations Jumpstart in 2024. If you're teaching someone the game, a pair of Jumpstart packs is one of the easiest starting points; see [how to play Magic: The Gathering](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering).

## Jumpstart (2020)

The first Jumpstart was tied to Core Set 2021: 169 of its reprints carry the M21 symbol, and the five M21 planeswalkers each got a mythic theme (Basri, Teferi, Liliana, Chandra and Garruk). Every color has two mythic, three rare and four common themes, plus the five-color Rainbow pack. A few examples:

- **White:** Basri (+1/+1 counters), Unicorns, Angels, Dogs, Doctor (lifegain), Legion (go-wide tokens)
- **Blue:** Teferi (card draw), Milling, Pirates, Spirits, Under the Sea, Wizards
- **Black:** Liliana (Zombies), Phyrexian, Discarding, Rogues, Reanimated, Vampires
- **Red:** Chandra (direct damage), Seismic, Dragons, Lightning, Minotaurs, Goblins
- **Green:** Garruk, Walls, Cats, Elves, Lands, Dinosaurs, Tree-Hugging (Treefolk)

The new cards filled out themes and added commanders. **Tinybones, Trinket Thief** draws you a card each end step if an opponent discarded, and can make empty-handed opponents lose 10 life. **Muxus, Goblin Grandee** dumps the Goblins from your top six cards onto the battlefield. **Bruvac the Grandiloquent** doubles mill, and **Emiel the Blessed** is a Unicorn that blinks your creatures. The set also debuted the five common **Thriving** lands, such as **Thriving Grove**, which taps for its own color or one other color you choose.

## Jumpstart 2022

Jumpstart 2022 kept the format and themes structure but swapped in new themes and a new-to-Magic card in every pack, plus an anime-style Booster Fun card. Its packs could also be mixed with the set-specific Jumpstart Boosters released for Dominaria United, The Brothers' War and later sets. Themes ranged from the familiar (Knights, Merfolk, Demons, Faeries, Boneyard reanimation) to the odd, like **Unlucky Thirteen**, built around **Triskaidekaphobia**, which makes any player at exactly 13 life lose the game at the beginning of your upkeep.

Its 51 new cards include a run of Commander-friendly legends: **Kibo, Uktabi Prince** (a Monkey that hands out Bananas), **Ashcoat of the Shadow Swarm** for Rats, **Alandra, Sky Dreamer** for card-draw Drakes, **Lita, Mechanical Engineer** for artifact creatures, and **Preston, the Vanisher**, which copies creatures you put onto the battlefield without casting them as 0/1 Illusions.

## How it plays

There's no draft. The intended way to play is to open packs, pair two of them, and play two-player games; the 2020 set's official method was to open four packs and choose how to combine them into two decks. Multiplayer works too. Because each half-deck is built to a similar curve and plan, pairings like Goblins plus Dragons or Reanimated plus Milling usually make functional decks, and the fun comes from discovering combinations.

Games feel like a mid-powered draft. The rare themes are not reliably stronger than common ones, and some pairings are much better than others, so it's normal to swap one pack after a few games. Jumpstart also works as a casual draft alternative for groups: everyone opens two packs, and the decks are ready at once. Players who want more customization often treat a stack of Jumpstart packs as a pile of building blocks for a cheap casual deck; the [budget decks guide](/blog/budget-mtg-decks-under-50) covers that mindset.

On MTG Arena, the 2020 set's packs were adapted: some cards were swapped for others that work better in a digital game or in the Historic format, and players choose from offered themes rather than opening random packs.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Jumpstart wasn't built for competitive play, and its new cards never entered Standard, Pioneer or Modern. Its impact was on supply and on Commander. Reprints of Rhystic Study, Reanimate and Craterhoof Behemoth in a product aimed at beginners put long-sought cards into many new collections, and the new legends found a home as commanders and in casual Commander decks.

As of October 2026, **Rhystic Study** is on Commander's Game Changers list, which limits how freely it can be played in the lower Commander brackets. The rest of the set's notable cards are ordinary Commander staples rather than format-warping threats.

## Cards from Jumpstart worth owning

- **Rhystic Study:** one of the most famous card-advantage enchantments in Commander.
- **Reanimate:** the cheapest reanimation spell, a staple in Legacy and Commander.
- **Craterhoof Behemoth:** the classic finisher for green creature decks.
- **Goldspan Dragon:** a strong Dragon or Treasure-deck threat.
- **Branching Evolution:** a counter doubler at three mana for any +1/+1 counters deck.
- **The Thriving lands:** cheap, easy mana fixing for budget Commander decks.
- **Tinybones, Trinket Thief and Kibo, Uktabi Prince:** fun, low-cost commanders to build around.

Jumpstart is the fastest way to get two people playing Magic, and its packs leave you with a pile of themed cards to keep building with. If one of its new legends caught your eye, try turning it into a full Commander deck; [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format, and the [MTG Deck Lab builder](/) can put together the 99.
