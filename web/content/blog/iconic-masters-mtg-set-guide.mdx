Iconic Masters is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in November 2017, built around the game's most famous big creatures: dragons, angels, demons, sphinxes and the Phyrexian Praetors. It was the second Masters set of 2017, after Modern Masters 2017, and alongside the iconic monsters it carried Modern staples and a few old Vintage cards like Mana Drain and Necropotence.

## What the set was for

Masters sets do two jobs: they reprint cards in demand for non-rotating formats, and they give experienced players a premium draft. Iconic Masters added a theme on top. Its mythic and rare slots were filled with creatures that define what Magic looks like on a box cover, such as Avacyn, Angel of Hope, Kokusho, the Evening Star, Consecrated Sphinx and the Praetors from New Phyrexia (Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite, Jin-Gitaxias, Core Augur, Sheoldred, Whispering One, Urabrask the Hidden and Vorinclex, Voice of Hunger).

The set has no story of its own. Its cards come from many planes, so a single draft might put a Kamigawa dragon spirit next to an Innistrad angel and a Tarkir monk. If you want a single plane's story, the original sets are the place to look; Iconic Masters is about the creatures themselves. It was previewed at the HASCON convention in September 2017, where the set had its first public prerelease.

### The creature cycles

Two full cycles anchor the rare and mythic slots. The five Praetors from New Phyrexia are all mythic in Iconic Masters, and each is an expensive legend that bends the game around its color: Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite pumps your creatures and shrinks your opponent's, Sheoldred, Whispering One forces sacrifices and reanimates every upkeep, and Jin-Gitaxias, Core Augur draws you seven cards each turn while shrinking opponents' maximum hand size. The five legendary dragon spirits from Champions of Kamigawa (Kokusho, the Evening Star, Keiga, the Tide Star, Yosei, the Morning Star, Jugan, the Rising Star and Ryusei, the Falling Star) are all rares here, and each has a big effect when it dies.

Around them, the set filled each color with its signature big creatures: angels like Avacyn, Angel of Hope, Archangel of Thune, Restoration Angel and Serra Angel; demons like Rune-Scarred Demon, Lord of the Pit and Abyssal Persecutor; sphinxes like Consecrated Sphinx and Sphinx of Uthuun; and dragons like Thundermaw Hellkite, Bogardan Hellkite and Hoarding Dragon. That's why the draft format had so many bombs: almost every rare slot held a creature built to end the game.

## The reprints that mattered

The set's value for Constructed came from a mix of Modern staples and old cards that rarely get reprinted:

- **Mana Drain** returned for the first time in a paper booster since Legends. It's long been banned in Legacy, but it's legal in Vintage and a favorite in Commander and cube.
- **Necropotence** and **Channel** are classic Vintage-era cards. As of October 2026, Necropotence is banned in Legacy and restricted in Vintage, and it's on the Commander Game Changers list; Channel is banned in Legacy and Commander and restricted in Vintage.
- **Modern staples** included Thoughtseize, Cryptic Command, Aether Vial, Horizon Canopy, Ancestral Vision, Serum Powder, Mishra's Bauble, Monastery Swiftspear and Primeval Titan.
- **Commander favorites** included Consecrated Sphinx (also a Game Changer as of October 2026), Avacyn, Angel of Hope, Archangel of Thune, Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker, Ob Nixilis, the Fallen and the full cycle of Kamigawa dragon spirits. Primeval Titan is banned in Commander, so its reprint here served Modern players.

## Limited

Iconic Masters drafted as a powerful but muddy format. Its ten color pairs had signpost uncommons, such as Azorius Charm, Electrolyze, Lightning Helix, Vizkopa Guildmage, Corpsejack Menace, Rosheen Meanderer, Chronicler of Heroes and Jungle Barrier, but many pairs didn't have a single clear "X matters" theme. Instead, drafters tended to land in a few broad plans:

- **Skies and tempo.** Blue and white flyers backed by cheap interaction, using cards like Azorius Charm and prowess creatures like Seeker of the Way.
- **Midrange and control.** Slower blue-black or blue-based decks with removal and card advantage, sometimes milling the opponent.
- **Dragons.** Black-red and other red pairs built around Bladewing the Risen, Dragon Tempest, Dragon Egg and the set's many Dragon payoffs.
- **Ramp and counters.** Green decks that ramped into big creatures or built up +1/+1 counters with cards like Corpsejack Menace.

Many reviewers rated blue the deepest color, and the format leaned midrange because only a couple of archetypes were truly aggressive. The karoo bounce lands at uncommon (Azorius Chancery, Dimir Aqueduct and friends) helped decks splash a third color for a bomb. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

A reprint set doesn't create decks, so Iconic Masters' impact was about supply. Its Modern staples, especially Thoughtseize, Cryptic Command, Horizon Canopy and Aether Vial, became easier to find in late 2017 and early 2018. Primeval Titan's reprint fed Modern's Amulet Titan deck, which has stayed a recurring archetype ever since.

None of the set's reprints were banned because of this printing. The bans that apply to its cards are long-standing: Mana Drain, Necropotence, Channel and Windfall are banned in Legacy; Necropotence, Channel and Windfall are restricted in Vintage; and Primeval Titan and Channel are banned in Commander, all as of October 2026. For how the official ban lists work, see our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

## Cards from Iconic Masters worth owning

- **Mana Drain**: a classic counterspell for Commander and Vintage.
- **Consecrated Sphinx**: a Game Changer and one of Commander's best card advantage creatures.
- **Necropotence**: a Game Changer card-draw engine for black Commander decks.
- **Thoughtseize**: a discard spell played in Modern, Pioneer and Legacy.
- **Horizon Canopy**: a land that turns into a card late in the game.
- **Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite**: a game-ending threat in Commander and reanimator decks.
- **Kokusho, the Evening Star** and the other Kamigawa dragons: popular in Commander Dragons decks.
