Weatherlight is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1997, the third and final set of the Mirage block. It was the first set to tell an ordered story with recurring characters, launching the Weatherlight Saga that ran until 2001, and it was Magic's first set with a real graveyard theme. Null Rod, Doomsday, Buried Alive, Gemstone Mine and Mind Stone all debuted here.

## The story

Weatherlight opens the saga of the skyship *Weatherlight* and its crew. Four thousand years after the brothers Urza and Mishra opened a path to Phyrexia, its dark lord Yawgmoth is preparing to invade Dominaria. The plane's best hope is the **Legacy**, a set of artifacts that together form a weapon against Phyrexia. The skyship *Weatherlight* is the centerpiece of that weapon, and its crew searches for the missing pieces.

The crew is the heart of the story. **Gerrard Capashen** is the reluctant heir to the Legacy. **Sisay** is the ship's captain, **Tahngarth** a proud minotaur warrior, **Squee** a hapless goblin cabin boy, **Karn** a silver golem, **Mirri** a cat warrior, **Hanna** the ship's navigator, **Orim** its healer and **Ertai** a gifted, arrogant young wizard. Their nemesis is **Volrath**, the Evincar of the plane of Rath and Gerrard's former foster brother Vuel, who has sided with Phyrexia.

Two events drive the set. First, Volrath's horrors **Morinfen** and **Gallowbraid** attack the Urborg estate of the nobleman **Crovax**, who is bound to the angel Selenia. Gerrard's friend Rofellos is killed, Selenia is pulled away to Rath, and Gerrard, angry that the attack was really aimed at him, leaves the crew. Later, a Rathi fugitive named **Starke**, whose daughter is Volrath's hostage, betrays Sisay and delivers her through a portal to Volrath's Stronghold. The mercenary **Maraxus of Keld** works for Volrath in the background.

Sisay's kidnapping reunites the crew. Gerrard returns, Ertai joins as the ship's mage, and the *Weatherlight* sets course for Rath, which is where the Tempest block picks up. The set itself only hints at this through a handful of cards with story art, but the story was told in The Duelist magazine, comics and the anthology *Rath and Storm*, and it set the template for every Magic story that followed.

## Mechanics

Weatherlight had no new keywords. Its signature was a theme rather than a mechanic: the MTG Wiki calls it the first set with a major graveyard theme.

### Graveyard matters

Many cards care about what's in your graveyard or put things there. **Buried Alive** puts up to three creature cards from your library into your graveyard, ready to be reanimated. **Gaea's Blessing** shuffles cards from a graveyard back into a library, and if it's milled, it shuffles your whole graveyard back. **Bösium Strip** lets you cast instants and sorceries from the top of your graveyard. **Doomsday** builds a five-card library out of your library and graveyard and exiles everything else, a card that combo players have been solving for decades.

### Cumulative upkeep

At the start of your upkeep, you add an age counter to a permanent with cumulative upkeep, then pay the cost once per counter or sacrifice it. The cost grows every turn. **Gallowbraid** and **Morinfen** are 5-power legends that cost 1 life per age counter. **Mwonvuli Ooze** grows stronger the longer you keep paying.

### Flanking and phasing

Both return from Mirage. Flanking gives -1/-1 to any creature without flanking that blocks, as on **Shadow Rider**. Phasing makes a permanent blink out of existence every other turn, as on **Tolarian Drake**.

### Lands and artifacts

Weatherlight added unusual lands like **Gemstone Mine**, which taps for any color three times and then is sacrificed, and **Lotus Vale** and **Scorched Ruins**, which make three or four mana but cost you two lands to play. **Mind Stone** is a simple mana rock that cashes in for a card later.

## Limited

Weatherlight was a small set, and in 1997 Limited was mostly played as Sealed Deck and early booster drafts without the color-pair archetypes modern sets are designed around. It was drafted alongside Mirage and Visions in Mirage block formats, but it wasn't built as a deep draft environment, and reliable records of specific Weatherlight archetypes from that time are scarce.

In practice, playables came down to efficient creatures, removal and card advantage. Commons like **Spinning Darkness**, which you can cast for free by exiling the top three black cards of your graveyard, **Heavy Ballista**, **Fire Whip**, **Coils of the Medusa** and **Ophidian** were solid. The graveyard theme gave a few strong synergies, but much of the set was built for Constructed or flavor. For how drafting has changed since, see our [draft vs sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Weatherlight was a small, mostly supporting set for Type II (Standard). The MTG Wiki's Standard timeline places it in the format from late 1997 until the November 1998 rotation, when Urza's Saga arrived and the Mirage block left. That period's notable decks, according to the same timeline, included Deadguy Red, Draw-Go, ProsBloom, RecSur and Dream Halls combo; Weatherlight contributed role-players rather than the core of those decks.

Its real legacy is in the eternal formats. As of October 2026:

- **Legacy**: Buried Alive is a long-time Reanimator enabler, and Doomsday is the namesake of an entire combo archetype that stacks a tiny library and wins with a few cards. Null Rod is a sideboard answer to artifact decks.
- **Vintage**: Null Rod is a classic hoser against artifact-heavy decks, and Doomsday and Buried Alive appear in combo and Reanimator lists. None of the set's cards are restricted.
- **Commander**: Mind Stone and Gemstone Mine are common in mana bases and ramp suites; Peacekeeper, Firestorm and Gaea's Blessing show up too.
- **Pauper**: Mind Stone and Ophidian are Pauper-legal commons.
- **Modern**: Weatherlight predates Eighth Edition; only reprinted cards like Mind Stone are Modern-legal.

The Reserved List is Wizards' promise not to reprint certain older cards in traditional tournament-legal form; Null Rod, Abeyance, Peacekeeper, Firestorm, Lotus Vale and 34 other Weatherlight cards are on it, so their only tournament-legal printings are the originals. For format overviews, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) and the [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Weatherlight worth owning

- **Buried Alive**: the cleanest way to stock a graveyard for Reanimator.
- **Doomsday**: a unique combo card with a dedicated Legacy following.
- **Null Rod**: a powerful sideboard card against artifacts.
- **Mind Stone**: a reliable mana rock for Commander and Pauper.
- **Gemstone Mine**: color fixing for multicolor Commander decks.
- **Gaea's Blessing**: cheap insurance against mill and graveyard hate.
