Magic: The Gathering has a long history of "game in a box" products: ready-to-play decks sold together so a group could sit down and play without opening a single booster. This guide covers ten of them, from 1998's Anthologies to 2022's Game Night: Free-for-All, including the Battle Royale and Beatdown box sets, Deckmasters, Explorers of Ixalan and the Ravnica Guild Kits. None are Standard sets, but between them they reprinted cards like Swords to Plowshares, Lightning Bolt, Strip Mine, Necropotence and Path to Exile.

Formats: none of these products were sold as Standard sets, and their reprints follow each card's normal legality. The handful of new cards (in the three Game Night boxes) are legal only in eternal formats such as Commander, Legacy and Vintage.

## The early box sets (1998-2001)

Before Wizards made preconstructed Commander decks, it experimented with boxed sets aimed at new and casual players. They used white borders, like the core sets of the era, and mixed cards from many sets.

### Anthologies (1998)

Anthologies celebrated Magic's fifth anniversary with two 60-card decks built from cards spanning Alpha to Urza's Saga. **Dark Alliance** was an aggressive black-red Goblins deck with Goblin King, Goblin Matron and Goblin Recruiter, and **Defenders of the Cause** was a green-white deck with Swords to Plowshares, Armageddon, Serra Angel and Erhnam Djinn. The box also reprinted Strip Mine, Nevinyrral's Disk, Brushland, Lightning Bolt and Llanowar Elves.

### Battle Royale (1999)

Battle Royale was built to teach multiplayer Magic. It held four 40-card decks, Chargoyf, Cinder Heart, Spirit Gale and The Deluge, plus a rules booklet for group play. Card highlights included Reanimate, Living Death, Exhume, Land Tax, Windfall, Wrath of God, Counterspell and Dark Ritual, a strong list for a casual product.

### Beatdown (2000)

Beatdown contained two decks, the blue-black fliers deck **Aerodoom** and the red-green **Ground Pounder**, plus two Spindown life counters and a poster. Four cards got new art for the box: Erhnam Djinn, Ball Lightning, Clockwork Beast and Sengir Vampire. Brainstorm, Lightning Bolt, Fireball, Impulse and Rampant Growth were also in the mix.

### Deckmasters: Garfield vs. Finkel (2001)

Deckmasters packaged two decks made only from Ice Age and Alliances cards, built by Magic's creator Richard Garfield and the pro player Jon Finkel, in a metal tin. The box included four black-bordered premium cards, two per deck: Icy Manipulator, Incinerate, Lhurgoyf and Necropotence. The rest were white-bordered reprints, including Balduvian Horde, Pyroclasm, Hurricane, Dark Ritual and the painlands Underground River, Sulfurous Springs and Karplusan Forest. Garfield and Finkel then played a promotional match with the decks, which Finkel won two games to none.

## Explorers of Ixalan (2017)

Explorers of Ixalan was half Magic and half board game. It came with four 60-card decks, one for each Ixalan faction (the Legion of Dusk's Vampires, the Brazen Coalition's Pirates, the River Heralds' Merfolk and the Sun Empire's Dinosaurs), and 50 map tiles. Players explored an island by revealing tiles that triggered events, quests and sites while also playing a normal game of Magic. It supported two to four players, and every card was a reprint, including **Time Warp**, **Path to Exile**, **Shared Animosity**, **Aggravated Assault**, **Vampire Nighthawk**, **Mortify** and the tri-lands Jungle Shrine and Crumbling Necropolis.

## Guild Kits (2018-2019)

The Guild Kits accompanied the Guilds of Ravnica block. Each kit was a single 60-card deck for one guild built from cards across every Ravnica set, plus a premium foil, alternate-art legendary leader, guild-watermarked basic lands and accessories. The Guilds of Ravnica kits (November 2018) covered Boros, Dimir, Golgari, Izzet and Selesnya, and the Ravnica Allegiance kits (February 2019) covered Azorius, Gruul, Orzhov, Rakdos and Simic.

Because they drew on Ravnica's whole history, the kits were full of useful reprints. Highlights include **Deathrite Shaman**, **Abrupt Decay**, **Putrefy**, **Boros Charm**, **Guttersnipe**, **Aurelia, the Warleader** and **Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind** in the first wave, and **Birds of Paradise**, **Rakdos Charm**, **Utvara Hellkite**, **Ghost Council of Orzhova** and **Momir Vig, Simic Visionary** in the second. Every guild's Signet and bounce land showed up, which made the kits a good source of Commander mana.

## Game Night (2018-2022)

Game Night was Wizards' modern answer to the old box sets: one box, five decks, everything a group needs. Each version could be played by two to five players and came with life counters, tokens and +1/+1 counters. Our guide on [how to play Magic: The Gathering](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) is a good companion if you're teaching new players with one.

### Game Night (2018)

The first Game Night had five 60-card mono-color decks, each with a well-known theme and a new mythic rare built for group games: **Militant Angel** (white), **Inspired Sphinx** (blue), **Rot Hulk** (black), **Goblin Goliath** (red) and **Avatar of Growth** (green). Each of those cards scales with the number of opponents. Reprints included **Ghalta, Primal Hunger**, **Siege-Gang Commander**, **Zulaport Cutthroat** and **Liliana's Mastery**.

### Game Night 2019

Game Night 2019 built half of each deck from Core Set 2020 and half from older reprints, and it again added five new mythics: **Highcliff Felidar**, **Sphinx of Enlightenment**, **Calculating Lich**, **Fiendish Duo** and **Earthshaker Giant**. The decks were designed so you could mix the reprints of one color with the Core Set 2020 cards of another.

### Game Night: Free-for-All (2022)

The third box returned in October 2022 with five decks (Glorious Combat, Political Trickery, Dark Sacrifice, Draconic Fury and Boundless Elves) and five new legendary creatures in traditional foil, aimed squarely at Commander: **Zamriel, Seraph of Steel**, **Maeve, Insidious Singer**, **Vogar, Necropolis Tyrant**, **Nogi, Draco-Zealot** and **Imaryll, Elfhame Elite**. The reprint list was strong too: Swords to Plowshares, Path to Exile, Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, Beast Whisperer and Fact or Fiction.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

These boxes were made for kitchen tables, not tournaments, and none of them created a new deck. Their value was in reprints, especially in an era when Wizards reprinted far less.

- **Legacy and Vintage:** Swords to Plowshares, Brainstorm, Reanimate and Strip Mine are classic eternal cards. As of October 2026, Strip Mine, Windfall and Necropotence are banned in Legacy, and Strip Mine, Necropotence and Windfall are restricted in Vintage. Deathrite Shaman is banned in both Legacy and Modern. Our [Legacy guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) covers how that format treats these older cards.
- **Modern:** Abrupt Decay, Path to Exile and Boros Charm from the newer boxes are all Modern-legal staples of their colors.
- **Commander:** this is where the boxes still matter. The Game Night legends, especially Maeve, Insidious Singer and Imaryll, Elfhame Elite, are built for multiplayer, and the Guild Kits' Signets and bounce lands are format staples. Necropotence is on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026. See the [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) for how the format works.

## Cards from these boxes worth owning

- **Swords to Plowshares**: one of the best removal spells ever printed.
- **Brainstorm**: Legacy's defining cantrip.
- **Reanimate**: a cheap Legacy and Commander reanimation spell.
- **Abrupt Decay**: removal for Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Birds of Paradise**: the classic mana creature.
- **Time Warp**: an extra turn for blue Commander decks.
- **Beast Whisperer**: a card engine for creature-heavy Commander decks.
- **The Signets and bounce lands** from the Guild Kits: easy mana upgrades for any Commander deck.
