Kaldheim is the Norse-inspired Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2021, set on a plane of ten realms connected by a great World Tree. Vikings, elves, giants, trolls, demons and Valkyries all fight for their gods while the trickster Tibalt pushes the realms toward war. The set is remembered for foretell, snow, its double-faced Gods and a pair of Standard bans that came almost a year after release.

## The story

Kaldheim is built around the World Tree, whose branches can be seen in the sky of all ten realms. The realms drift around the tree and sometimes collide. A collision is called a Doomskar, and it's the plane's version of the end of the world: earthquakes, rifts and armies pouring from one realm into another.

The planeswalker Kaya, a ghost-assassin last seen leading the Orzhov on Ravnica, arrives on a well-paid job from an anonymous client. Her target is a monster that has supposedly already destroyed another world. Traveling with Inga Rune-Eyes and her Omenseekers, Kaya finds it in the forests of Aldergard: the Phyrexian praetor Vorinclex. It nearly kills the party, until a companion named Asi reveals himself as Alrund, God of the Cosmos, and drives it off.

Meanwhile the god of lies, Valki, isn't who he seems. Tibalt, the sadistic devil-blooded planeswalker from Innistrad, has imprisoned the real Valki and taken his place. In disguise, he warns the frost giants about waking trolls, promises the Skelle clan the return of their demon master Varragoth, and uses the stolen Sword of the Realms to open passages for monsters like the Cosmos Serpent Koma. When Kaya unmasks him, the elf planeswalker Tyvar Kell saves her from a troll ambush, and the two chase Tibalt to the demon realm of Immersturm. They take the Sword back, but they're too late: Tibalt has started a Doomskar, and Varragoth's demons flood into the human realm of Bretagard.

The final battle pulls every faction together. Alrund tells the clans to bring the Sword to Halvar, the god of battle, so he can end the Doomskar. Kaya, Tyvar and their allies fight their way through demons and the draugr armies of Karfell, with help from the elf king Harald and the Valkyries. A newcomer planeswalker from Theros, Niko Aris, arrives with them. Kaya finally ends Varragoth herself with an axe Tyvar made for her, and the Doomskar is stopped. Tibalt slips away, and Vorinclex is still loose somewhere on the plane. Kaya's mysterious employer later turns out to be Tezzeret, and the Phyrexian presence on Kaldheim becomes an early sign of the invasion that follows in Dominaria United.

## Mechanics

### Foretell

During your turn, you can pay `{2}` and exile a card with foretell face down. On a later turn, you cast it for its foretell cost, which is usually cheaper. It lets you spend spare mana early and cast two spells in one turn later, and it hides information from your opponent. **Doomskar** (rare) is a five-mana wrath that can be foretold and later cast for `{1}{W}{W}`. **Demon Bolt** (common) deals 4 damage to a creature or planeswalker and can be foretold for a later cast at `{R}`.

### Boast

Boast is an activated ability you can only use if the creature attacked this turn, and only once each turn. **Usher of the Fallen** (uncommon) is a one-mana 2/1 that makes a 1/1 Human Warrior token each turn it attacks and you pay `{1}{W}`. Boast rewards attacking and is strongest in aggressive decks.

### Snow

Snow returns with snow basic lands and snow duals. Many cards care about snow mana (the `{S}` symbol), which must come from a snow source, or about how many snow permanents you control. **Frost Bite** (common) deals 2 damage, or 3 if you have three or more snow permanents. In draft, the land slot of each pack could hold a snow dual or a snow-covered basic, so snow lands were a resource you had to draft.

### Changeling and typal themes

Changeling creatures are every creature type, which helps Kaldheim's typal theme: each realm has a creature type and a two-color pair. **Masked Vandal** (common) is a changeling two-drop that exiles a creature card from your graveyard to exile an opponent's artifact or enchantment.

### Double-faced Gods and Sagas

Kaldheim continued the modal double-faced cards that started in Zendikar Rising. Each God has a creature front and an artifact or other permanent on the back, and you choose which side to play. **Valki, God of Lies** (mythic) is a two-mana creature on the front and a seven-mana planeswalker, Tibalt, Cosmic Impostor, on the back. Sagas return too, mostly at uncommon in the two-color pairs. If you want a primer on modal faces, see our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained).

## Limited

Each realm pairs two colors with a creature type and a strategy, which gives the ten draft archetypes:

- **White-blue: Spirits and foretell.** Flash and flying Spirits that reward casting your second spell each turn.
- **Blue-black: snow Zombies.** Midrange snow decks; Narfi, Betrayer King keeps coming back.
- **Black-red: Berserkers and boast.** Aggressive decks that attack every turn; Kardur, Doomscourge forces bad attacks.
- **Red-green: Trolls and ramp.** Big snow creatures; Svella, Ice Shaper makes mana and pays off with a free spell.
- **Green-white: Humans and go-wide.** Token armies; Maja, Bretagard Protector pumps the team.
- **White-black: Angels.** Life gain and fliers, with Firja, Judge of Valor rewarding two spells a turn.
- **Blue-red: Giants and spells.** Instants and sorceries; Aegar, the Freezing Flame draws on excess damage.
- **Black-green: Elves and self-mill.** Harald, King of Skemfar digs for Elves and other key cards.
- **Red-white: Dwarves and equipment.** Vehicles, equipment and Auras; Koll, the Forgemaster returns your enchanted or equipped creatures.
- **Green-blue: changelings.** Shapeshifters and "choose a creature type" cards; Moritte of the Frost copies your best permanent.

Kaldheim was a slower, removal-rich format. Demon Bolt, Feed the Serpent, Frost Bite and Squash were premium removal at common, and foretell made it easy to plan two-spell turns. Snow added a drafting puzzle: how many snow lands you took determined how good your Frost Bites and Icehide Trolls were. If you're new to evaluating commons like these, our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains how each archetype wants to win.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Kaldheim joined a Standard that already had Zendikar Rising and the original Throne of Eldraine. **Goldspan Dragon** made an impact immediately. It was the marquee addition to Gruul Adventures, one of the top decks of that season, and later anchored Izzet Dragons builds. At the Kaldheim Championship in March 2021, **Alrund's Epiphany** was the most-played nonland Kaldheim card in Standard.

Epiphany truly took over after the September 2021 rotation, when Innistrad: Midnight Hunt added Galvanic Iteration to copy it. Izzet decks built to survive long enough to take two extra turns in a row dominated the format and made up most of the field at the 2021 World Championship. Monocolored snow aggro decks with **Faceless Haven** rose to fight them. On January 25, 2022, Wizards banned both Alrund's Epiphany and Faceless Haven in Standard. Through its time in Standard, cards like Esika's Chariot, Binding the Old Gods and Doomskar showed up in green midrange and white control.

The set's biggest problem card outside Standard was **Tibalt's Trickery**. In Modern, players cascaded into it and used it to counter their own cascade spell, which flipped them into a free, expensive card from the top of their library. Wizards banned it in Modern on February 15, 2021, less than two weeks after release. It remains legal in Pioneer.

As of October 2026, Kaldheim's clearest lasting contribution to Pioneer is its Pathway lands, and Goldspan Dragon and Showdown of the Skalds have also found homes in red decks there. In Commander the set is a deep well: Toski, Bearer of Secrets, Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider, Esika, God of the Tree, Koma, Cosmos Serpent and Tergrid, God of Fright are all popular choices, and typal tools like Maskwood Nexus and Reflections of Littjara help any creature-type deck. If you're weighing powerful cards like Tergrid for a casual table, check them against the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) your group uses.

## Cards from Kaldheim worth owning

- **Goldspan Dragon**: a fast, sticky threat for red decks and a Treasure engine in Commander.
- **The Pathway lands**: flexible dual lands for Pioneer and Commander mana bases.
- **Esika's Chariot**: a resilient token engine that's strong in any green Commander deck.
- **Vorinclex, Monstrous Raider**: one of the best counter doublers for +1/+1 counter and superfriends decks.
- **Toski, Bearer of Secrets**: a card-draw engine for go-wide Commander decks.
- **Maskwood Nexus**: a colorless way to turn any creature into every creature type.
- **Doomskar**: a wrath that's easy to set up a turn early.

Kaldheim rewards planning. Foretell turns spare mana into future tempo, and snow rewards counting your lands before you build. For a Pioneer deck today, start with its red cards and Pathways, and use the [Deck Lab builder](/) to test a list.
