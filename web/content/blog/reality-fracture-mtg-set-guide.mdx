Reality Fracture is the fall 2026 premier set for Magic: The Gathering, released in October 2026. It closes a three-year story arc: Jace Beleren, now calling himself the Theorist, has built a "perfect" copy of the Multiverse called the Echoverse and plans to lay it over the real one. Nearly every card has a twin from the other reality, and a new mechanic puts a Jace planeswalker token into almost every game.

## The story

The groundwork goes back several sets. After Jace and Vraska were compleated and then healed in the Phyrexian storyline, Jace took the portal-sniffing creature Loot and eventually reached Nicol Bolas's old prison, the Meditation Realm, during Tarkir: Dragonstorm. He seized Bolas's Spirit Gem to rewrite reality so the Phyrexians never hurt Vraska, the power broke him, and he was presumed dead. Secrets of Strixhaven revealed the truth: he survived and had been building a new Multiverse of his own.

That new reality is the Echoverse. On the surface it looks like a better world. There was no Phyrexian invasion, no Eldrazi corruption and no Bolas. But the Theorist got there by cutting away every part of himself that objected to the plan. Those pieces, the "aberrations," are earlier versions of Jace, with names like the Detective, the Guildpact and the Clean Slate. The Echoverse is also full of altered doubles of familiar faces, from a lich version of Strixhaven's Professor Opal to a version of Liliana. Merging the two realities would erase the people of the original Multiverse.

The point-of-view character is Tam, a Quandrix student from Secrets of Strixhaven. She turns out to be someone the Theorist created to kidnap Arcavios's oracle, Jadzi. Guilt-ridden and haunted by memories of her friends, she drifts toward the other side. Meanwhile Ajani and Chandra, who were on Arcavios when Jadzi vanished, put together a resistance with Vraska and Garruk. Garruk is recruited partly because Jace's illusions don't work on him. The Wildspeaker from the marketing turns out to be one of Jace's own aberrations, a gentle part of him that once stopped the young Jace from killing his abusive mentor, Alhammarret.

The finale plays out in the Theorist's tower, which he turns into a Fulcrum to fuse the realities. The heroes fight their Echoverse doubles, and Vraska gets close enough, disguised as her own echo, to stab the Theorist. As the aberrations rejoin him, the real Jace comes back for a moment, tells his friends that Tam is the key, and dies in Vraska's arms. A light leaves his body and passes to Tam, giving her a planeswalker spark. She reaches out to Emrakul, who devours the Echoverse before the overlay can finish. The story ends with Tam reconciled with her friends and mourning Jace on Vryn with Vraska, Ranna and Loot.

## Mechanics

### Empower Jace

Empower Jace is the headline mechanic and appears on dozens of cards. "Empower Jace N" adds N loyalty counters to a Jace planeswalker *token* you control. If you don't have one, you first create a blue Jace token with two abilities: −1 to surveil 1 and −3 to draw a card. It works a lot like amass: the first trigger makes the token, and every later one grows it.

A few rules matter. Empower never touches a nontoken Jace card, so it won't add loyalty to The Theorist, Jace Beleren. If you somehow have more than one Jace token, you pick which one gets the counters. And if a spell's targets are all illegal when it resolves, you don't get the empower either.

Example: **No Admittance** (common, `{1}{R}`) deals 3 damage to any target and then empowers Jace 1. In practice the Jace token is a steady source of card flow that your opponent has to attack, so it pulls combat away from your life total.

### Prepare

Prepare returns from Secrets of Strixhaven. These creatures carry a second spell in their text box. While the creature is *prepared*, you may cast a copy of that spell, and doing so makes the creature unprepared. Some enter prepared; others need a condition to become prepared again.

The twist this time is that most common and uncommon prepare creatures share one of five spells, one per Hexhaven school, instead of each having its own. **Konstrari Improviser** (common) and **Woodwork Prodigy** (uncommon) both carry Soul Tether, which makes a Heartwood token. That makes the spell easier to plan around in Limited, at the cost of some variety. The rares still get their own spells, like **Variable Chaser**, whose Arc of Fortune lets each player swap their hand for seven new cards.

### Heartwood tokens

Heartwood is a new predefined token: a red-green artifact that taps for `{R}` or `{G}`. It powers the Gruul ramp deck and anything that counts artifacts, such as **Aerid Konstrari**, which makes one when it enters or dies.

### Echoed pairs and supporting mechanics

Every booster contains an "echoed pair," two cards of the same rarity showing one character or idea in both realities. **Chandra, Torch of Defiance** (the red original, back as a reprint) is paired with **Chandra, Chill of Compliance**, her blue Echoverse self. Way of the Pyromancer and Way of the Cryomancer are another pair.

Surveil is the set's most common keyword and drives white-blue. Flashback, threshold and landfall fill support roles, and **Theorist's Sanctum** introduces "behold a Jace," which means showing a Jace from your hand or pointing to one you control. ## Limited

Reality Fracture has ten two-color archetypes. The five allied pairs are Hexhaven's schools (Fatehold, Theorix, Stingerquill, Konstrari and Vigorbloom), and the five enemy pairs follow the Echoverse versions of returning planeswalkers. Each pair has a gold signpost, usually a charm, so the uncommons you see early are a good signal.

- **White-blue (Fatehold): surveil.** Cheap creatures plus surveil payoffs. **Proctor of Potential** and **Surveillance Phantasm** reward filtering every turn.
- **White-black: attrition.** Creatures that leave value behind, plus removal like **Extended Absence**. **Graft Surgeon** and **Rank Rat** are good role-players.
- **Red-white: go wide.** Lots of bodies and team pumps. **Shatterwing Pegasus** and **Skilled Battlecarver** fit well.
- **Green-white (Vigorbloom): life gain.** Stacked lifegain triggers with **Unflinching Hortimancer** and **Greenhouse Propagator**.
- **Blue-black (Theorix): graveyard and threshold.** Slower, but **Theoretical Necromancer** and **Recursive Recruitment** grind well once the graveyard fills.
- **Green-blue: empower Jace.** Build the biggest Jace token at the table with **Mindseeker Oculus**, **Tam's Resistance** and **Protege's Awakening**.
- **Blue-red: prowess.** Cheap spells like **Twinned Vision** with prowess bodies like **Cryotheory Adept**.
- **Black-red (Stingerquill): noncombat damage.** Ping your opponent out with burn and drain effects, with **Master of Barbs** as a strong rare payoff.
- **Black-green: value creatures.** Bodies that matter when they enter, die and sit in the graveyard, plus **Something Worth Saving** to dig.
- **Red-green (Konstrari): Heartwood ramp.** Make Heartwood tokens and jump to big threats, using **Awaken the Inferno** as removal that can also fetch a land.

Early drafting data had blue's commons on top, though those were day-one numbers. Because the Jace token turns up so often, removal that hits planeswalkers (Extended Absence, **Compel Brutality**) is worth more than usual. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Reality Fracture is only a few weeks old, so treat everything here as early. There was no fall rotation in 2026, so the set joined a large, already-established Standard pool instead of resetting it.

In Frank Karsten's first look at tournament results after the set arrived, as reported in early October, the most-played new cards included Pompous Battlemage, Ajani Resolute, Master of Barbs, Chandra, Torch of Defiance, Liliana the Faultless, Stingerquill Charm and The Theorist, Jace Beleren. The archetypes picking up the new cards were Orzhov Life Gain (Liliana the Faultless, Ajani Resolute, Enlightened Confidant), Izzet Prowess (Pompous Battlemage) and Rakdos Lizards (Master of Barbs, Stingerquill Charm). Established decks such as Izzet Spellementals, Dimir Midrange and a breakout Jund Sacrifice list stayed near the top of that snapshot, with Boros Dragons rising against them.

None of the set's cards had been banned as of October 2026. Expect the picture to keep changing as the first big events with the set play out.

Beyond Standard, the reprints get the most attention. Tarmogoyf and Chandra, Torch of Defiance have long histories in Modern and Pioneer, and the new printings make them easier to find. For Commander, the set comes with a four-color preconstructed deck built around empower Jace, and cards like **Omnipresence**, **Return to the Light Realms** and **Tam, the Possibility** are the kind of big, swingy effects casual tables enjoy.

## Cards from Reality Fracture worth owning

- **Tarmogoyf**: a historic two-drop that's now legal in Standard and Pioneer as well as Modern.
- **Chandra, Torch of Defiance**: proven across several formats, and a staple wherever red midrange shows up.
- **The Theorist, Jace Beleren**: card advantage every turn cycle, a natural fit for blue Commander decks.
- **Ajani Resolute**: a cheap planeswalker that turns any life-gain deck into a threat.
- **The allied dual lands**: every Constructed deck in those colors wants them.
- **Emrakul, the Exigent Doom**: a ramp payoff that's also a land when you need one, with Commander appeal.
- **Stingerquill Charm**: a flexible instant that should outlast this season in Rakdos decks.

Reality Fracture is a planeswalker set at heart. In Limited, plan around the Jace token: protect yours and pressure your opponent's. In Constructed, start with the cheap, efficient pieces that slot into existing decks, like Pompous Battlemage and Liliana the Faultless. The splashy mythics are worth more once the format settles.
