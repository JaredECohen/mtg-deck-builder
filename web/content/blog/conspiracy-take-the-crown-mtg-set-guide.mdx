Conspiracy: Take the Crown is the Magic: The Gathering multiplayer draft set released in August 2016, the sequel to 2014's Conspiracy. It returns to the backstabbing city of Paliano on the plane of Fiora, where a ghost king has just been assassinated, and it introduced the monarch, goad and melee, three mechanics that went on to shape Commander. It also printed Leovold, Emissary of Trest, Sanctum Prelate and Recruiter of the Guard for Legacy.

## The story

Fiora is a plane of rival city-states, and the High City of Paliano is the one everyone wants to rule. It's a place where assassination is a normal part of politics. In the first Conspiracy, King Brago had been made immortal by his advisors, the Custodi priesthood, and then killed by the elf Selvala. Instead of freeing the city, his death left him ruling as a ghost: Brago, King Eternal.

Take the Crown opens with that ghost being removed for good. The noblewoman Marchesa d'Amati, known as the Black Rose, hires the planeswalker Kaya, a ghost assassin, to finish the job. Kaya slips through the palace walls and kills Brago's spirit in his own dining hall. The Custodi then crown Marchesa queen. Wizards played the twist into its marketing: the set was first announced under the name "The Reign of Brago," then renamed after a teaser showing Brago's assassination.

Marchesa's rule is shaky from day one. Adriana Vallore, Brago's captain of the guard, takes the death personally and leads a rebellion against the new queen. Leovold, the elven ambassador of the rival port city of Trest, works every side of the conflict through spies and leverage. And the goblin planeswalker Daretti stirs up riots in the streets. The set leaves Marchesa on the throne, with enemies everywhere, which is exactly the mood of the monarch mechanic. Kaya would later become a major character on Ravnica, starting with Ravnica Allegiance.

## Mechanics

### The monarch

The set's big idea. When a card tells you to "become the monarch," you take a game-wide designation. At the beginning of the monarch's end step, they draw an extra card. If a creature deals combat damage to the monarch, its controller takes the crown. In a multiplayer game this is a lightning rod: everyone wants to attack the monarch.

Cards like Palace Sentinels (a four-mana 2/4 that makes you the monarch) give the crown cheaply, while others reward keeping it, like Throne Warden and Garrulous Sycophant, which pay off at your end step only if you're the monarch. Queen Marchesa does the reverse: if someone else is the monarch, she makes a 1/1 deathtouch, haste Assassin every upkeep to go take it back.

### Goad

Goading a creature forces it to attack each combat until your next turn, and to attack someone other than you if it can. Grenzo, Havoc Raiser can goad a creature whenever one of yours hits a player, and Besmirch steals a creature for a turn, attacks with it, then goads it so it has to keep attacking your opponents.

### Melee

Whenever a creature with melee attacks, it gets +1/+1 for each opponent you attacked that combat. It rewards spreading your attacks across the table. Adriana, Captain of the Guard has melee and gives it to your other creatures too.

### Council's dilemma

A voting ability word. Every player votes for one of two options, and each vote adds to the result instead of the majority winning. Lieutenants of the Guard, for example, gets a +1/+1 counter for each "strength" vote and makes a Soldier token for each "numbers" vote. Expropriate is the mythic version: each "time" vote gives you an extra turn, and each "money" vote lets you take a permanent from the voter.

### Conspiracies and draft-matters cards

Conspiracies are a card type that starts the game in your command zone, never in your deck. They only work in Conspiracy Draft, which is why they're banned in every sanctioned format. Hidden agenda Conspiracies make you secretly name a card, then reveal it when the bonus matters. Take the Crown added double agenda, which names two cards, like Summoner's Bond. It also had colored common Conspiracies such as Adriana's Valor and Hired Heist.

Draft-matters cards act during the draft itself. Leovold's Operative lets you take two cards from a pack and skip the next one, Garbage Fire deals damage based on how late in a pack you took it, and Archdemon of Paliano is a strong four-mana 5/4 that forces you to draft your next three picks at random.

## Limited

Conspiracy: Take the Crown is built for an eight-player draft followed by free-for-all games of three to five players, not the usual one-on-one Limited match. Our [beginner's guide to playing](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers the basics; the multiplayer layer is what makes this set different.

Each two-color pair had a signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue**: Ascended Lawmage, a flier with hexproof for a defensive deck
- **Blue-black**: Shipwreck Singer, a flier that forces opposing creatures to attack and shrinks attackers
- **Black-red**: Carnage Gladiator, punishing anyone who blocks
- **Red-green**: Gruul War Chant, which gives your attackers +1/+0 and menace
- **Green-white**: Juniper Order Ranger, +1/+1 counters
- **White-black**: Knights of the Black Rose, the monarch deck
- **Blue-red**: Stormchaser Chimera, a flier that scries and pumps itself
- **Black-green**: Pharika's Mender, recursion
- **Red-white**: Akroan Hoplite, attacking in numbers
- **Green-blue**: Coiling Oracle, value creatures

The monarch shaped every game. Grabbing the crown early with Thorn of the Black Rose or Palace Sentinels and then defending it with deathtouch blockers and walls was a reliable plan, and Palace Jailer was among the best uncommons because it removes a creature and makes you the monarch at the same time. Goad and melee pushed games toward combat instead of stalemates, and the draft-matters cards added a mini-game to the draft itself. The pace was slower than a normal set, since most damage is split among several opponents.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

The set's competitive footprint is mostly in Legacy, Pauper and Commander, since its new cards never entered Standard or, at release, Modern.

- **Legacy.** Leovold was a centerpiece of Sultai (black-green-blue) control decks in its first year, locking out the extra draws from Brainstorm and other cantrips. Sanctum Prelate and Recruiter of the Guard became core cards in white Death and Taxes decks, where Recruiter finds Stoneforge Mystic or Mother of Runes and Prelate shuts off a whole mana value of spells.
- **Pauper.** The common monarch creatures gave Pauper a strong card advantage engine. In May 2019 Wizards said monarch decks didn't have problem win rates at that point, while acknowledging some players found the mechanic too strong, and Boros Monarch was one of the format's top decks around the January 2021 update. That update banned Fall from Favor, a later monarch card from Commander Legends, not a Take the Crown card. See our [Pauper guide](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg) for more.
- **Commander.** The monarch and goad became Commander staples, reprinted in Commander Legends and many precons since. As of October 2026, Leovold, Emissary of Trest is banned in Commander, as are all Conspiracy cards. No Take the Crown card is on the Game Changers list.

## Cards from Conspiracy: Take the Crown worth owning

- **Selvala, Heart of the Wilds**: a popular green mana engine and commander.
- **Recruiter of the Guard**: a tutor on a body for Legacy and Commander toolbox decks.
- **Sanctum Prelate**: a Legacy sideboard and main-deck hoser.
- **Palace Jailer**: removal plus the crown, great in any white Commander deck.
- **Queen Marchesa**: a Mardu commander built around taking and retaking the crown.
- **Grenzo, Havoc Raiser**: one of the best goad cards for multiplayer.
- **Show and Tell** (reprint): still a Legacy combo staple.

## The takeaway

Take the Crown is the set that gave multiplayer Magic its favorite politics tools.
