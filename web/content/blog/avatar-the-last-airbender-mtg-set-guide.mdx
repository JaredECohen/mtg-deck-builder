Avatar: The Last Airbender is the Universes Beyond expansion released in November 2025 that brings the Nickelodeon animated series to Magic: The Gathering as a full, Standard-legal set. It covers the whole story of the show, from Aang waking up in the iceberg to Sozin's Comet, and turns the four kinds of bending into four mechanics: airbend, earthbend, waterbend and firebending. It also produced some of the most dominant Standard cards of the past year, two of which are now banned.

## The story

The show is set in a world split into four nations, each tied to an element: the Water Tribes, the Earth Kingdom, the Fire Nation and the Air Nomads. In every generation one person, the Avatar, can bend all four elements and keep the peace between them. A century before the story starts, the Fire Nation launched a war of conquest, wiped out the Air Nomads, and the Avatar vanished.

The series is told in three seasons, called Books, and the set draws on all of them. In Book One: Water, the Water Tribe siblings Katara and Sokka find Aang, a twelve-year-old airbender, frozen in an iceberg along with his flying bison Appa. He's the missing Avatar, and he has to master all four elements before the comet that gives firebenders overwhelming power returns. They travel north to find a waterbending master, chased by Zuko, the Fire Nation's banished prince, who believes capturing the Avatar will restore his honor. The Book ends with the siege of the Northern Water Tribe, where the Moon Spirit is killed and Yue gives up her life to restore it.

In Book Two: Earth, Aang finds his earthbending teacher, Toph, a blind earthbender who senses the world through vibrations. The group reaches the great city of Ba Sing Se, which turns out to be controlled by the secret police of the Dai Li. Zuko's sister Azula infiltrates the city, and in its fall, she strikes Aang down with lightning. Zuko, torn between his uncle Iroh's guidance and his father's approval, sides with her.

Book Three: Fire follows the group into the Fire Nation. Their invasion on the Day of Black Sun, an eclipse that leaves firebenders powerless, fails, but Zuko finally breaks with his father and joins Aang as his firebending teacher. Fire Lord Ozai plans to use Sozin's Comet to burn the Earth Kingdom and crown himself Phoenix King. On the day of the comet, Zuko and Katara defeat Azula, Sokka, Toph and Suki stop the airship fleet, and Aang, who refuses to kill, takes away Ozai's bending instead. Zuko becomes Fire Lord and the war ends. Cards like Day of Black Sun, Sozin's Comet, Ozai, the Phoenix King and Fire Lord Zuko map directly onto those moments.

## Mechanics

### Airbend

To airbend a permanent, you exile it, and its owner may later cast it from exile for `{2}` instead of its normal mana cost. On an opponent's creature it's tempo removal: they lose the board position and have to spend mana to recast it. On your own permanents it's a way to reuse enter-the-battlefield abilities cheaply. Appa, Steadfast Guardian airbends any number of your other nonland permanents when it enters, and also makes an Ally token whenever you cast a spell from exile.

### Earthbend

Earthbend N turns a land you control into a 0/0 creature with haste and N +1/+1 counters, and it's still a land. When that creature dies or is exiled, the land returns to the battlefield tapped, so you don't lose the mana. Earthbender Ascension earthbends 2 and fetches a basic land when it enters, then rewards each land drop afterward. Earthbend was the engine of the ramp decks that defined the set's first Standard season.

### Waterbend

Waterbend is a cost: while paying a waterbend cost, you can tap your artifacts and creatures to help, with each one paying for one generic mana. It works like convoke for activated abilities. Katara, Water Tribe's Hope uses it to set your creatures' base power and toughness, and Giant Koi waterbends to become unblockable.

### Firebending

Firebending N is a triggered ability: whenever the creature attacks, you add N red mana that lasts until end of combat. That lets you attack and then cast instants, use abilities, or pump creatures during combat with the extra mana. Sozin's Comet gives all your creatures firebending 5 for a turn.

### Lessons and Allies

Lessons return from Strixhaven as an instant or sorcery subtype, and many cards reward having three or more Lessons in your graveyard, such as Accumulate Wisdom. Firebending Lesson is a cheap removal spell with a kicker, and Boomerang Basics is a one-mana bounce spell that draws a card if you target your own permanent. Allies is a creature type shared by the heroes and many of their friends, and several cards care about Allies or create Ally tokens. The set also uses Clue tokens, Sagas, and exhaust abilities, which can only be activated once.

## Limited

Avatar is a ten-archetype draft format. Wizards' own descriptions lay them out:

- **White-blue:** fliers.
- **Blue-black:** card draw and Clues, rewarding your second draw each turn.
- **Black-red:** Fire Nation aggro with firebending.
- **Red-green:** Earth Rumble ramp into creatures with 4 or more power, using earthbending.
- **Green-white:** Allies going wide.
- **White-black:** sacrifice, using small creatures and Clues.
- **Blue-red:** noncreature spells and Lessons in combat.
- **Black-green:** +1/+1 counters.
- **Red-white:** go-wide aggro with Allies and tokens.
- **Green-blue:** ramp and Lessons, filling the graveyard to power up Lesson payoffs.

Reviewers described the set as greedy: lots of rare bombs, mana fixing and synergy engines, but with aggressive and tempo decks fast enough to punish slow starts. Cheap removal like Firebending Lesson and Deadly Precision kept the format honest, and airbend gave every color a way to swing tempo. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Avatar's Standard story is mostly about one card. Badgermole Cub fueled ramp decks (Simic, Bant and Selesnya builds) that dominated the format through the winter and spring. At Pro Tour Lorwyn Eclipsed in early 2026, Wizards' coverage reported that Cub decks made up about 45% of the field, though they underperformed against the rest of the room there.

On August 10, 2026, Wizards banned Badgermole Cub and Gran-Gran in Standard, along with Stormchaser's Talent from Bloomburrow. The announcement said the Cub's speed and mana advantage compressed the format, and that its strongest deck had a win rate above 57% while holding the second-highest play rate. Gran-Gran was banned as a cheap, hard-to-answer mana engine for Jeskai Lessons. Stormchaser's Talent was banned as the keystone of Izzet Prowess, a deck that also leaned on Avatar's Boomerang Basics.

After the bans, Avatar is still everywhere. As of October 2026, MTGGoldfish's Standard data shows:

- **Mono-Green and Selesnya Landfall** built on four Earthbender Ascension and Ba Sing Se, among the most played decks in the format.
- **Azorius and Jeskai Momo** with four Momo, Friendly Flier at the bottom of a curve of fliers; Boros Dragons plays Momo too.
- **Bant Airbending Combo** with Appa, Steadfast Guardian, Aang, Swift Savior, Airbender's Reversal and Accumulate Wisdom.
- **Wan Shi Tong, Librarian** in Dimir Midrange and Azorius Control, and **Day of Black Sun** as a sweeper in Dimir lists.
- **Jund Sacrifice** with Obsessive Pursuit, Callous Inspector and Deadly Precision.

Outside Standard, the set's biggest footprint is in Commander, where its legendary heroes and villains became commanders and cards like Redirect Lightning, Bender's Waterskin, Abandoned Air Temple and Avatar's Wrath rank among its most-played on EDHREC. ## Cards from Avatar: The Last Airbender worth owning

- **Earthbender Ascension**: the backbone of Standard's landfall decks after the bans.
- **Momo, Friendly Flier**: a one-drop that makes every flier deck faster.
- **Wan Shi Tong, Librarian**: a flexible flash threat for blue control, and a punisher of search-heavy Commander tables.
- **Day of Black Sun**: a scalable sweeper that hits cheap creatures without wiping your own big threats.
- **Redirect Lightning**: a one-mana trick that steals a removal spell or a combo piece in Commander.
- **Avatar's Wrath**: a sweeper that keeps one creature and stops opponents from recasting from exile.
- **Avatar Aang**: the four-color showpiece for anyone building a bending Commander deck.

Avatar: The Last Airbender is one of the most influential Standard sets of the past year, even with its strongest card banned. If you play Standard, the landfall package (Earthbender Ascension and Ba Sing Se) and Momo are the pieces most likely to stay relevant.
