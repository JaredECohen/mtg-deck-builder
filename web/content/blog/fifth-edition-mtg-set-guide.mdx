Fifth Edition is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in March 1997, the fifth version of the game's basic set. It was a big shake-up: it cut long-time staples like Lightning Bolt and Swords to Plowshares, pulled in a large batch of Ice Age cards (including Necropotence and Brainstorm), and gave more than two hundred cards new art. It was the core set of Type II, today's Standard, for about two years.

## What the set was for

Before 2009, Magic's core sets were mostly reprints, a pool of simple, evergreen cards that defined the baseline for each Standard season. Fifth Edition's job was to refresh that baseline. Fourth Edition (1995) had mostly reprinted cards from the original sets, and Fifth was the first core set to draw heavily from the first wave of expansions, especially Ice Age, plus Fallen Empires, Homelands, The Dark, Legends, Arabian Nights and Antiquities. More than a hundred cards from Fourth Edition were dropped.

Because it's a core set, Fifth Edition has no story of its own. Its flavor text and art come from the reprinted cards' original sets, from the snowy Ice Age world of Terisiare to the ruins of the Fallen Empires.

### What was cut

The removals are the headline. Wizards cut cards that seemed a little too strong for the baseline, even if they weren't breaking tournaments: **Lightning Bolt**, **Swords to Plowshares**, **Serra Angel**, **Hypnotic Specter**, **Sengir Vampire** and **Savannah Lions** all left. Lightning Bolt and Swords to Plowshares had been fixtures of nearly every red and white deck, and losing them reshaped Type II. Cards that were already banned in the format at the time, like **Strip Mine**, **Channel** and **Mind Twist**, also went, as did weak and narrow cards like the "Laces" and the "Wards."

Not every powerful card left. **Dark Ritual**, **Mana Vault**, **Armageddon** and **Wrath of God** survived, and some players were surprised to see the white mass-destruction spells kept.

### What came in

The biggest addition was **Necropotence**, the Ice Age enchantment that had dominated the 1996 "Black Summer" of tournaments, which now stayed in Type II for years longer. Ice Age also contributed **Brainstorm**, the "pain lands" like **Adarkar Wastes** and **Underground River**, **Pyroblast** and **Hydroblast**, and the green ramp spell **Nature's Lore**. Fallen Empires added the sacrifice lands like **Ruins of Trokair** and the storage lands like **Bottomless Vault**. **Sylvan Library** came over from Legends and **City of Brass** from Arabian Nights. The Urza lands, **Urza's Tower**, **Urza's Mine** and **Urza's Power Plant**, returned from Antiquities.

### New art and new templating

Fifth Edition was the first core set to give a large share of its cards entirely new artwork, more than two hundred of them. Many cards also got cleaned-up rules text and new flavor text, and the set arrived with an updated rulebook. Interrupts, mana sources and the old timing rules were still part of the game at this point; the big rules overhaul with the stack came with Sixth Edition in 1999. If you want to compare old wording with today's, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Core sets of the 1990s weren't drafted the way sets are now. Booster drafts happened, but the eight-player, three-pack draft format and its archetype thinking weren't the norm for core sets, and Fifth Edition was mostly opened for Constructed play and casual Sealed. There's no reliable record of a "best archetype" in Fifth Edition Limited, so we won't invent one.

Played as Limited today, it's slow and creature-light by modern standards: removal like **Terror**, **Fireball** and **Pestilence** dominates, and the strongest creatures are big rares like **Shivan Dragon** and **Lord of the Pit**. It's a fun cube or nostalgia format, but expect lower creature quality than any modern set. If you want a modern take on Limited fundamentals, see [draft vs sealed](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Fifth Edition was the base set of Type II from 1997 into 1999, a stretch that covered Mirage, Visions, Weatherlight, the Tempest block and the start of the Urza block. Its biggest effects:

- **Necropotence stayed legal.** Ice Age rotated out of Type II, but because Necropotence was reprinted in Fifth, black "Necro" decks remained part of the format.
- **Burn changed.** Without Lightning Bolt, red decks relied on cards like Incinerate, and aggressive red "Sligh" strategies had to find new one-mana options.
- **White removal changed.** Without Swords to Plowshares, white decks leaned on Wrath of God, Disenchant and the Armageddon plan.
- **Prison and lock decks** had Stasis and Winter Orb available.

Fifth Edition left Type II when Classic Sixth Edition took over as the core set in 1999, and its cards that weren't reprinted rotated with it.

Its lasting impact comes from the cards it carried forward:

- **Legacy**: Brainstorm is one of the most important cards in the format, Sylvan Library is a green staple, and Counterspell, Dark Ritual and Animate Dead still see play. Necropotence and Mana Vault are banned in Legacy. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) covers the details.
- **Vintage**: Brainstorm, Necropotence and Mana Vault are restricted.
- **Commander**: Mana Vault, Sylvan Library, Necropotence, City of Brass, Nevinyrral's Disk, Ashnod's Altar and the pain lands are all well-loved. See [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).
- **Pauper**: Fifth Edition commons like Brainstorm, Counterspell, Dark Ritual and Pestilence are Pauper-legal, and Pestilence has been a staple there. Atog is banned in Pauper.
- **Every format**: Crusade and Pradesh Gypsies, both in Fifth Edition, are banned everywhere as part of Wizards' 2020 removal of cards with racist or culturally offensive depictions.
- **Modern**: Fifth Edition isn't legal in Modern, but many of its cards (Counterspell, Birds of Paradise, the Urza lands, Wrath of God) were reprinted in Modern-legal sets.

Fifth Edition has no Reserved List cards, so everything in it has been or can be reprinted.

## Cards from Fifth Edition worth owning

- **Brainstorm**: the defining blue cantrip of Legacy.
- **Sylvan Library**: card selection for green in Legacy and Commander.
- **Mana Vault**: fast mana for Commander.
- **Necropotence**: a Commander and Vintage powerhouse.
- **City of Brass**: five-color fixing for Commander.
- **The pain lands**: reliable two-color lands that are still reprinted often.
- **Counterspell** and **Dark Ritual**: classic commons for Pauper and Legacy.

## The takeaway

Fifth Edition is the core set that taught players nothing in the base set is permanent: Bolt and Swords left, Necropotence and Brainstorm came in. If you want to play its best cards today, Legacy is the place, with Brainstorm and Sylvan Library at the center of many blue and green decks.
