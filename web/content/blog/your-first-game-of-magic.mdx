The easiest way to learn your first game of Magic: The Gathering is to watch one. Below, two players sit down with simple decks, a mono-green creature deck and a mono-red deck, and play through the first five turns. Every decision is explained in plain words, so by the end you'll know what to do on your own turn and why.

For the full rules, read [how to play Magic: The Gathering](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering). For the numbers and symbols on a card, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## The two decks

Our players are Gina and Ravi. Each has a 60-card deck and starts at 20 life. The first player to reduce the other to 0 life wins.

**Gina's green deck** is built around creatures that get bigger as the game goes on:

| Card | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forest | none | A basic land. Tap it for one green mana. |
| Llanowar Elves | one green | A 1/1 creature that can tap to add one green mana |
| Grizzly Bears | one generic, one green | A plain 2/2 creature |
| Centaur Courser | two generic, one green | A plain 3/3 creature |
| Craw Wurm | four generic, two green | A big 6/4 creature |
| Giant Growth | one green | Instant: target creature gets +3/+3 until end of turn |

**Ravi's red deck** is built to deal damage quickly and burn away anything that gets in its way:

| Card | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mountain | none | A basic land. Tap it for one red mana. |
| Raging Goblin | one red | A 1/1 creature with haste |
| Gray Ogre | two generic, one red | A plain 2/2 creature |
| Hill Giant | three generic, one red | A plain 3/3 creature |
| Shock | one red | Instant: 2 damage to any target |
| Lightning Bolt | one red | Instant: 3 damage to any target |

A creature's two numbers are its power and toughness. A 2/2 deals 2 damage in combat and dies when it has taken 2 or more damage in one turn. "Generic" mana can be paid with any color. An **instant** is a spell you can cast at almost any time, even during your opponent's turn.

## Setting up the game

**Deciding who goes first.** Gina and Ravi flip a coin. Ravi wins and chooses to go first, which is called being "on the play." The player who goes first skips the draw on their very first turn.

**Shuffling.** Each player shuffles their deck thoroughly, then offers it to the opponent to cut or shuffle. Your deck is now called your **library**.

**Drawing seven.** Each player draws seven cards. This is the opening hand.

**The mulligan decision.** Before the game begins, each player decides whether to keep their hand or **mulligan**: shuffle it back and draw a fresh seven. Each time you mulligan, you put one card from your new hand on the bottom of your library once you decide to keep, so you end up with one fewer card per mulligan.

Ravi looks at three Mountains, Raging Goblin, Shock, Lightning Bolt and Gray Ogre. Three lands, cheap spells, a plan for the first three turns. He keeps.

Gina looks at one Forest, Grizzly Bears, Centaur Courser, Craw Wurm, Giant Growth and two more creatures. One land is not enough. She would need to draw a land on almost every turn just to cast anything. She mulligans, draws a new seven with four Forests, Llanowar Elves, Grizzly Bears and Giant Growth, and puts one Forest on the bottom of her library. She keeps six cards with three lands and a smooth start. For more on this decision, see the [mulligan guide](/blog/mtg-mulligan-guide).

## The shape of a turn

Every turn follows the same order:

1. **Untap** all your tapped cards.
2. **Upkeep**, a moment where some cards trigger. Our decks have none.
3. **Draw** a card (except the starting player on turn one).
4. **First main phase**: play a land, cast creatures and other spells.
5. **Combat**: attack with creatures if you want.
6. **Second main phase**: play a land or cast spells if you didn't before combat.
7. **End**: damage on creatures wears off and "until end of turn" effects end.

You can play one land per turn, during either main phase. Lands are your mana supply, so missing a land drop is almost always a mistake.

## Turn 1

**Ravi (on the play).** He skips his draw. He plays a Mountain, taps it for red mana and casts Raging Goblin.

Normally a creature can't attack on the turn it arrives. This is called **summoning sickness**: a creature has to be under your control since the start of your turn before it can attack or use an ability with the tap symbol. Raging Goblin has **haste**, which ignores that rule, so Ravi attacks right away. Gina has no creatures to block with, so she takes 1 damage. **Gina: 19.**

**Gina.** She draws a card, plays a Forest and casts Llanowar Elves. The Elves can tap for mana, but tapping is blocked by summoning sickness too, so they can't help her this turn. Next turn they'll give her an extra mana, which makes them a strong first play.

## Turn 2

**Ravi.** He untaps and draws. He plays a second Mountain, giving him two red mana.

He sees the Elves as a real threat. If they survive, Gina gets an extra mana every turn and can cast big creatures early. So he casts Shock targeting Llanowar Elves. Shock deals 2 damage, the Elves have 1 toughness, and they go to the **graveyard** (the discard pile for used and destroyed cards).

Could Gina have saved them? No. She tapped her only Forest on her own turn, and lands don't untap until your own untap step. The Elves couldn't tap for mana either, because Gina hasn't started a new turn since they arrived. With no mana available, she can't respond.

Ravi attacks with Raging Goblin again. **Gina: 18.**

**Gina.** She untaps, draws, plays a Forest and casts Grizzly Bears with her two lands. The Bears can't attack this turn, but they can block, and that already changes Ravi's plans.

## Turn 3

**Ravi.** He draws and plays a third Mountain. Should Raging Goblin attack? If it does, Gina blocks with Grizzly Bears. Blocking means putting your creature in front of an attacker. The two creatures deal damage to each other: the Goblin deals 1 to the Bears, the Bears deal 2 to the Goblin, and only the Goblin dies. Attacking would throw away a card for nothing, so Ravi doesn't attack.

Instead he spends all three mana on Gray Ogre.

**Gina.** She draws, plays her third Forest and casts Centaur Courser, a 3/3.

Should Grizzly Bears attack? Ravi has two untapped creatures: Raging Goblin and the Gray Ogre he just cast. Here's a key point for new players: **summoning sickness doesn't stop blocking.** The Ogre arrived this turn, but it can still block. If the Bears attack, the Ogre blocks and the two 2/2s trade, or Ravi blocks with both creatures and kills the Bears while losing only the Goblin. Neither outcome helps Gina, so she keeps her creatures home.

Life totals: **Ravi 20, Gina 18.**

## Turn 4

**Ravi.** He draws and plays a fourth Mountain. He casts Hill Giant, a 3/3, using all four lands. He doesn't attack, because every attack he could make runs into a bigger or equal blocker.

Notice that Ravi is now **tapped out**: all his lands are tapped, so he can't cast any instants until his next turn. Gina should remember that.

**Gina.** She draws and plays a fourth Forest. Her board is Grizzly Bears and Centaur Courser. Ravi's is Raging Goblin, Gray Ogre and Hill Giant. She has Giant Growth in hand and another Grizzly Bears she drew this turn.

She makes a smart choice: attack with Centaur Courser and **leave mana open** for Giant Growth. Grizzly Bears stays home to block.

Ravi blocks with his 3/3 Hill Giant, expecting a trade. Once blockers are declared, Gina casts Giant Growth on Courser. It becomes a 6/6 until end of turn. Combat damage happens: Courser deals 6 to the Giant, which dies. The Giant deals 3 to Courser, which survives because it now has 6 toughness. At the end of the turn Courser goes back to 3/3 and the damage on it wears off.

Ravi had no mana to answer, and an even trade became a free kill.

After combat, in her second main phase, Gina casts her second Grizzly Bears with two of her remaining three Forests.

The lesson for Ravi: an opponent who attacks into an even trade with mana open often has a trick.

## Turn 5

**Ravi.** He draws and plays a fifth Mountain. He's behind on board: Raging Goblin and Gray Ogre against a 3/3 and two 2/2s. He has Lightning Bolt in hand.

Instead of using Lightning Bolt now, he casts nothing and passes with all five Mountains untapped. Holding mana up lets you react to whatever your opponent does.

**Gina.** She draws a second Giant Growth and plays her fifth Forest. Both Grizzly Bears can now attack. The second one has been under her control since the start of this turn, so summoning sickness is over.

She attacks with Centaur Courser and one Grizzly Bears, keeping the other Bears home as a blocker against Ravi's small creatures.

Ravi blocks the Grizzly Bears with Gray Ogre and lets Courser through. Gina casts Giant Growth on her blocked Bears, planning to win the fight.

Now Ravi **responds**. With Giant Growth waiting to resolve, he casts Lightning Bolt targeting the same Grizzly Bears.

Spells cast in response to each other go on **the stack**, and the last spell cast resolves first. So Lightning Bolt resolves before Giant Growth. The Bears, still a 2/2, take 3 damage and die. Then Giant Growth tries to resolve, but its only target is gone, so it does nothing. Ravi traded one card for two, and his Ogre survives because nothing is left to deal damage to it.

Courser was unblocked, so it deals 3 damage to Ravi. **Ravi: 17.**

After combat, Gina has four untapped Forests. She keeps them open, since nothing in her hand costs four, and passes.

Life totals: **Ravi 17, Gina 18.**

## How the game ends

Over the next few turns, the decks play out their plans. Gina hits her sixth land and casts Craw Wurm, a 6/4 that Ravi's small creatures can't block profitably. Ravi keeps drawing burn spells, but each one forces a choice: kill a creature or hit Gina. Every spell he points at a creature is damage that never reaches her life total.

Green's creatures are simply bigger. Gina attacks every turn, and Ravi loses a creature each turn blocking just to survive. Once his board is gone, the next attack takes his life total to 0 and Ravi loses.

That's the most common way a game ends: one player's life total hits 0. A few other ways exist that you'll run into eventually:

- **Drawing from an empty library.** If you have to draw a card and your library is empty, you lose.
- **Poison.** A player with 10 or more poison counters loses. Some creatures give poison counters instead of normal damage.
- **Conceding.** You can concede at any time, and it's polite to do so when the game is clearly over.
- **Card effects.** A small number of cards say a player wins or loses the game.

## What this game teaches

Each turn above contains a habit worth keeping:

- **Mulligan unplayable hands.** One land is a mulligan, even with good spells.
- **Play a land every turn.**
- **Mana creatures are prime targets.** Ravi was right to Shock the Elves early.
- **Summoning sickness stops attacking and tapping, not blocking.** Gina didn't attack into a fresh Gray Ogre for this reason.
- **Don't attack into bad trades.** Ravi held back his Goblin on turn 3.
- **Open mana means a possible trick.** Ravi blocked into Giant Growth on turn 4. On turn 5, he was the one with mana open.
- **Instants can answer instants.** Lightning Bolt in response to Giant Growth swung a fight completely.

For the errors new players make most, see [MTG beginner mistakes](/blog/mtg-beginner-mistakes).
