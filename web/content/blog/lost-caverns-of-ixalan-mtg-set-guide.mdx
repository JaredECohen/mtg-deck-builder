The Lost Caverns of Ixalan is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in November 2023 that sends explorers below the surface of Ixalan, the plane of dinosaurs, pirates, vampires and merfolk, and reveals that the world is hollow. Inside is a second world with its own sun, lost civilizations and buried gods. The set introduced discover, craft, descend and Map tokens, reprinted Cavern of Souls, and printed removal like Get Lost that's still a Standard staple three years later.

Its cards stay legal in Pioneer, Modern and Commander. Our guide to [Standard rotation](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) covers what that means for your collection.

## The story

The story picks up about a year after the Phyrexian invasion. Ixalan's factions discover that their world is hollow, and that its core holds a lost civilization and a valuable new resource called cosmium. Each faction sends an expedition down, and each wants something different.

The Sun Empire's expedition is led by Huatli, the warrior-poet, and joined by the planeswalker Quintorius Kand, a Lorehold archaeologist searching for the legendary "Coin Empire," along with the artificer Saheeli. Below Orazca they find ruins, call up the ghost Abuelo, and eventually meet the Oltec, a people who have guarded the core for generations and turn out to be the Sun Empire's long-separated cousins. The River Heralds, the merfolk, are waiting for the gates to the core to open, and help both the Sun Empire and the stranded travelers.

The Legion of Dusk's expedition is darker. Vito, a fanatical splinter priest of the Church of Dusk, leads a group, including the cartographer Amalia, to find Aclazotz, a bat god imprisoned beneath the surface. He clears the way with blood sacrifice. The half-fae traveler Kellan, searching for his father, joins Amalia's group to Vito's displeasure. Elsewhere, the siren pirate Malcolm and the goblin Breeches investigate Downtown, a mining town whose people have vanished. They discover the Mycotyrant, a fungal mind woken by the invasion, which wants to spread across the surface.

Everything collides in the core. The gate opens onto the inner sky and its sun, Chimil. Vito frees Aclazotz with blood, the god turns its followers into bat-monsters, and it cages Chimil in cosmium, plunging the core into darkness. In the battle that follows, Huatli kills Vito with his own spear. The ending is unresolved by design: Aclazotz escapes toward the vampire homeland of Torrezon, the Mycotyrant is still spreading, Chimil is dimmed, and Quintorius accidentally wakes one more ancient giant. Amalia and Kellan leave the plane, while the Sun Empire and the Oltec reunite.

## Mechanics

### Discover

Discover N is the set's signature new mechanic: you exile cards from the top of your library until you hit a nonland card with mana value N or less, then either cast it for free or put it into your hand. The rest go to the bottom. It's a cascade-style effect, with the option to keep the card. Trumpeting Carnosaur, a six-mana 7/6, discovers 5 when it enters, so it brings a second spell with it.

### Explore and Map tokens

Explore returns from the original Ixalan block: reveal the top card of your library; if it's a land, put it into your hand; otherwise put a +1/+1 counter on the creature and choose whether to leave the card on top or put it into your graveyard. The new Map token is an artifact you can pay `{1}`, tap and sacrifice to make a creature you control explore, at sorcery speed. Spyglass Siren, a one-mana 1/1 flier, makes a Map when it enters.

### Craft

Craft is an activated ability on artifacts that transform. You pay the craft cost, exile the artifact along with the materials it asks for (often another artifact or a card from your graveyard), and it returns transformed into something more powerful. Many craft cards start as modest artifacts and become creatures or lands.

### Descend and fathomless descent

Descend cares about permanent cards in your graveyard. Descend 4 and descend 8 switch on an ability once you have at least that many, and "if you descended this turn" checks whether a permanent card went to your graveyard this turn. Fathomless descent counts the permanent cards in your graveyard directly. The Ancient One, a two-mana 8/8, can't attack or block until you reach descend 8.

### Caves and finality counters

Caves are a land subtype with their own payoffs, such as Glimpse the Core returning a Cave from your graveyard. A common cycle of Cave lands (Hidden Courtyard, Hidden Cataract and friends) can be sacrificed late in the game to discover 4. Finality counters also return: a creature with one is exiled instead of going to the graveyard if it would die.

## Limited

The Lost Caverns of Ixalan supports all ten color pairs, each anchored by a two-color uncommon:

- **White-blue artifacts and craft** (Master's Guide-Mural).
- **Blue-black descend** (Uchbenbak, the Great Mistake).
- **Black-red descend aggression** (Zoyowa Lava-Tongue).
- **Red-green Dinosaurs** (Itzquinth, Firstborn of Gishath).
- **Green-white counters and pumped creatures** (Kutzil, Malamet Exemplar).
- **White-black sacrifice** (Bartolomé del Presidio).
- **Blue-red Pirates and artifacts** (Captain Storm, Cosmium Raider).
- **Black-green graveyard** (Akawalli, the Seething Tower).
- **Red-white discover and go-wide** (Caparocti Sunborn).
- **Green-blue explore** (Nicanzil, Current Conductor).

Mana was plentiful in this format thanks to Treasure, Maps and landcycling creatures, so splashing was easy. The format rewarded tempo: cheap evasive commons like Miner's Guidewing, Oltec Cloud Guard and Waterwind Scout were hard to answer efficiently, and white and blue were widely regarded as the strongest colors. Discover Caves gave nearly every deck a late-game mana sink. For fundamentals that carry from set to set, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Lost Caverns arrived in late 2023 and became one of the most durable sets in Standard's three-year window. Much of its impact came from uncommons and cheap removal rather than chase mythics. Get Lost gave white a clean two-mana instant answer to nearly any threat, and Deep-Cavern Bat and Bitter Triumph became black midrange regulars.

The set also produced Standard's biggest ban in decades. On June 30, 2025, Wizards banned seven cards in Standard, including Abuelo's Awakening, which had become the reanimation engine for an Omniscience combo deck.

As of October 2026, nearly three years after release, MTGGoldfish's Standard data still shows the set's fingerprints across the format:

- **Get Lost** in Azorius Control, Boros Dragons, Bant Airbending, Jeskai Momo and Selesnya decks.
- **Cavern of Souls** in creature-type decks like Boros Dwarves, Boros Dragons and 5-color Elementals.
- **Tishana's Tidebinder, Restless Reef, Spyglass Siren and Bitter Triumph** in Dimir Midrange and Dimir reanimator lists.
- **Amalia Benavides Aguirre and Deep-Cavern Bat** in the format's Lifegain deck.
- **Greedy Freebooter and Fanatical Offering** in Jund Sacrifice.

In Pioneer, Lost Caverns made enough noise to earn two bans. Geological Appraiser was banned shortly after release because it enabled a discover combo that could win around turn three. Amalia Benavides Aguirre was later banned too, after a combo with Wildgrowth Walker let the deck loop explore and lifegain until Amalia reached 20 power and wiped the board. That combo then moved to Modern. Cavern of Souls was also a big addition to Pioneer, since this was its first printing in a Pioneer-legal set.

In Commander, the set's most-played cards on EDHREC include Cavern of Souls, Roaming Throne, Kutzil, Malamet Exemplar, Chimil, the Inner Sun and Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant. Dinosaur and Vampire decks got a big lift. For the differences between these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from The Lost Caverns of Ixalan worth owning

- **Cavern of Souls**: a premium land for creature-type decks in Pioneer, Modern and Commander.
- **Get Lost**: flexible two-mana removal that stays good in Pioneer after rotation.
- **Bitter Triumph and Deep-Cavern Bat**: efficient black cards for Pioneer midrange.
- **Roaming Throne**: doubles triggers in any tribal Commander deck.
- **Chimil, the Inner Sun**: uncounterable spells and a free discover every turn in casual games.
- **Ghalta, Stampede Tyrant**: the top end of Dinosaur Commander decks.
- **Trumpeting Carnosaur**: a two-for-one creature that doubles as removal.

The Lost Caverns of Ixalan has been one of Standard's backbone sets, and its best cards will keep working in Pioneer and Commander after it rotates out in early 2027. If you play Standard now, enjoy decks built around Get Lost and Cavern of Souls while you can, but think about what replaces them before rotation.
