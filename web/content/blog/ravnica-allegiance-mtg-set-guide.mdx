Ravnica Allegiance is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2019, the second half of the return to the city-plane Ravnica that began with Guilds of Ravnica. It covers the Azorius, Orzhov, Rakdos, Gruul and Simic guilds, and its story is the political lead-up to War of the Spark. It also printed Hydroid Krasis, Smothering Tithe, five shock lands and a pair of Standard-banned Simic cards.

## The story

Nicol Bolas, the elder dragon, is coming for Ravnica, and the dragon guildmaster Niv-Mizzet knows it. He has known about planeswalkers all along, and he hands Ral Zarek, the Izzet planeswalker, a nearly impossible job: get all ten guilds to agree to a change in the Guildpact, the ancient magic that binds Ravnica together, so Niv-Mizzet can take its power and stand against Bolas. Ral is also building the Interplanar Beacon, a tower meant to call other planeswalkers to Ravnica for help.

Getting ten rival guilds to agree on anything is the hard part. To win over the Orzhov, Ral helps stage a crisis that lets the ghost-assassin planeswalker Kaya destroy the Obzedat, the undead council that ran the guild. The plan was for Teysa Karlov to take over, but under Orzhov law the job falls to Kaya instead, along with every debt the guild is owed. In the Gruul Clans, Domri Rade topples Borborygmos with Bolas's help and pushes the guild toward chaos. And some guild leaders are already working for the dragon: Vraska of the Golgari and Dovin Baan, a planeswalker inside the Azorius.

The guild summit finally meets at New Prahv. Niv-Mizzet reveals the threat and asks to become the Living Guildpact. The Azorius guildmaster Isperia adjourns the vote until morning, and that night Vraska assassinates her, which wrecks the summit. Ral falls back on a riskier plan: a machine across the city that would let Niv-Mizzet ascend without every guild's consent. The guilds seize the Gruul and Golgari nodes by force, at a heavy cost. Then Dovin Baan sabotages the plan just before Bolas arrives.

Ral, Kaya and the Rakdos acrobat Hekara fight their way into the Beacon tower, where Vraska and a mind-controlled Lavinia wait. Hekara dies, Ral wins, and he switches on the Beacon. Planeswalkers start arriving from across the Multiverse, which is exactly what Bolas wanted. The story leads straight into War of the Spark.

## Mechanics

Each guild gets one mechanic, and the guild split cards from Guilds of Ravnica continue, with a hybrid-cost half and a gold half.

### Addendum (Azorius, white-blue)

Addendum gives an instant a bonus if you cast it during your main phase. **Sphinx's Insight** draws two cards, and gains you 2 life too in your main phase. It's a choice between flexibility and value.

### Afterlife (Orzhov, white-black)

Afterlife N makes N 1/1 white and black flying Spirit tokens when the creature dies. **Imperious Oligarch** is a two-mana 2/1 with vigilance that leaves a flier behind, so trading with it is always a little worse for your opponent.

### Spectacle (Rakdos, black-red)

Spectacle is a cheaper alternative cost you can use if an opponent lost life this turn. **Skewer the Critics** deals 3 damage for `{2}{R}`, or for just `{R}` after you've hit your opponent. Spectacle rewards attacking first and casting spells after combat.

### Riot (Gruul, red-green)

A creature with riot enters with your choice of a +1/+1 counter or haste. **Zhur-Taa Goblin** is a two-mana 2/2 that's either a 3/3 or a hasty attacker. **Rhythm of the Wild** gives riot to all your nontoken creatures.

### Adapt (Simic, green-blue)

Adapt N puts N +1/+1 counters on the creature, but only if it has none. **Pteramander** is a one-mana flier that can adapt 4, and the ability gets cheaper for each instant and sorcery in your graveyard. Many Simic cards also trigger whenever counters are put on a creature, so adapt feeds a counters engine. For how these keywords read on the card, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Ravnica Allegiance is a guild set, so the main archetypes are its five two-color guilds:

- **Azorius (white-blue): fliers and addendum.** Evasive creatures and instants you cast in your main phase.
- **Orzhov (white-black): afterlife and drain.** Sticky creatures and life drain that grinds the opponent out.
- **Rakdos (black-red): spectacle aggro.** Hit early, then cast cheap spectacle spells.
- **Gruul (red-green): riot beatdown.** Big, hasty creatures and combat tricks.
- **Simic (green-blue): adapt and counters.** Creatures that grow and reward you for adding counters.

Gates add a sixth, three-or-more-color option. Each guild's Guildgate appears twice at common, and payoffs like **Gate Colossus** reward a deck built around them. Removal was the key at common: **Final Payment**, **Get the Point**, **Grotesque Demise** and **Bring to Trial** each have their place, and **Skewer the Critics** pushes Rakdos ahead. Most decks stick to one guild, and the hybrid-cost split cards help you shift late in the draft. Our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) covers how to spot an open lane.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Ravnica Allegiance landed in a Standard already shaped by Guilds of Ravnica, and it changed the format right away. At Mythic Championship I in Cleveland in February 2019, Sultai Midrange was the most-played deck. **Hydroid Krasis** was the card that pushed Golgari players to splash blue. Mono-Blue Tempo, newly powered by Pteramander and **Essence Capture**, won the event in Autumn Burchett's hands, and Esper Control reached the final. Mono-red decks picked up Light Up the Stage and Skewer the Critics, and Gruul aggro used the set's riot creatures.

The set's longest-lived Standard engine was **Wilderness Reclamation**. Early on it paired with Nexus of Fate. After Throne of Eldraine arrived, it fueled Temur Reclamation, which untapped its lands to cast huge Expansion // Explosion spells at instant speed, with **Growth Spiral** smoothing out its land drops. On August 3, 2020, Wizards banned Wilderness Reclamation and Growth Spiral in Standard, along with Teferi, Time Raveler and Cauldron Familiar, to break up the ramp and Reclamation decks. The set rotated out a few weeks later, when Zendikar Rising arrived in September 2020. Wilderness Reclamation was also banned in Pioneer on February 15, 2021.

Light Up the Stage has been a long-running staple of red aggro in Pioneer and Modern. Hydroid Krasis, Absorb and Bedevil have all seen Pioneer play in midrange and control decks. The shock lands, reprinted here from their original Ravnica printings, are part of Pioneer and Modern mana bases.

[Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) is where Ravnica Allegiance has its widest reach. Smothering Tithe is one of the most-played white cards in the format, and Teysa Karlov, Judith, the Scourge Diva, Prime Speaker Vannifar and Hydroid Krasis are popular in their colors.

## Cards from Ravnica Allegiance worth owning

- **Smothering Tithe**: one of white's best ramp cards in Commander.
- **The shock lands** (Blood Crypt, Breeding Pool, Godless Shrine, Hallowed Fountain, Stomping Ground): untapped duals that fit every format they're legal in.
- **Light Up the Stage**: card advantage for red aggro in Pioneer and Modern.
- **Hydroid Krasis**: a flexible finisher for green-blue ramp in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Teysa Karlov**: doubles death triggers for aristocrats decks.
- **Kaya's Wrath**: a wrath that suits Orzhov control.
- **Wilderness Reclamation**: banned in Standard and Pioneer, but still a strong Commander engine.

Ravnica Allegiance is a set of guild identities: pick a guild, learn its keyword, and build to it. Its best cards work far outside their guilds, though.
