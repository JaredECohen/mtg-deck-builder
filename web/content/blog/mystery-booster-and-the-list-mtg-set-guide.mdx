Mystery Booster is a Magic: The Gathering product line built for chaos drafting: every pack mixes cards from across the game's history, so no two drafts look alike. The original Mystery Booster arrived in November 2019, Mystery Booster 2 followed in August 2024, and Mystery Booster Commander Edition is set to launch at MagicCon: Atlanta in November 2026. Alongside them sits The List, a rotating pool of reprints that Wizards slipped into regular booster packs from 2020 to early 2025. All of them share the same idea: old cards, random packs and a taste of the unexpected.

## What the set was for

Mystery Booster started as a convention product. Wizards wanted something fun to draft at Grand Prix and MagicFest events, where players had already opened plenty of the current set. The answer was a pack that pulled from a huge list of older cards and arranged them so a draft still worked: each pack holds commons and uncommons of each color, a multicolor or artifact card, a rare or mythic, and a special slot. There's no story. The appeal is the surprise of seeing cards from 1994 and 2019 in the same pack, and the old-school "chaos draft" feel.

The List grew out of the same thinking. Instead of a separate product, it was a pool of a few hundred reprints that could show up in the last slot of a Set Booster. Opening a random old card in your new-set pack was a small Mystery Booster moment inside every product.

## The original Mystery Booster (2019)

The first Mystery Booster had 1,694 cards in its main sheet, from basic-level commons to Commander staples. It came in two editions with different 15th cards:

- **Convention Edition** (November 2019): sold at Grand Prix and other conventions, with a **playtest card** in each pack. Playtest cards look like the rough drafts R&D uses when testing a set: a regular card frame with black-and-white text and art that looks pasted on. There are 121 of them, collected by Scryfall as CMB1.
- **Retail Edition** (March 2020): sold through Wizards Play Network stores, with a foil card in the special slot instead of a playtest card.

The playtest cards became the most talked-about part of the product. They poke fun at game design itself. Bear with Set's Mechanic is a 2/2 for `{1}{G}` that comes with a made-up keyword, aggressive, which gives you an extra combat that only aggressive creatures can attack in. Five Kids in a Trenchcoat counts as five creatures. Mirrored Lotus offers each opponent a free copy of itself, and Sliv-Mizzet, Hivemind gives every Sliver Niv-Mizzet's abilities. Form of the Mulldrifter lets you cast your creatures as if they were Mulldrifter. None of them are legal in sanctioned formats, but they're great in casual and cube play.

In 2021, Wizards reissued the Convention Edition for stores, with an adjusted card list and new art on two playtest cards. Scryfall tracks that reissue's playtest sheet as CMB2. Scryfall now files the original Mystery Booster's reprints under The List's code, PLST, rather than as a separate set.

## The List (2020 to 2025)

The List debuted with Zendikar Rising Set Boosters in September 2020. Each set had a pool of about 300 reprints picked from across Magic's history, and roughly a quarter of Set Boosters had one in their final slot. List cards carry a small planeswalker symbol in the bottom-left corner so you can tell them apart from the original printings.

When Play Boosters replaced Set and Draft Boosters with Murders at Karlov Manor in early 2024, The List shrank to 40 reprints per set (30 commons and uncommons and 10 rares and mythics), and it shared its slot with Special Guests. Starting with Bloomburrow, the classic List reprints stopped appearing in Play Boosters, leaving only Special Guests in that slot, and The List was retired by early 2025. Cards printed on The List keep their normal legality, so a List copy of a Commander staple works exactly like any other copy.

## Mystery Booster 2 (2024)

Mystery Booster 2 was revealed at Gen Con in 2024 and sold through Secret Lair and convention Festival-in-a-Box offers rather than a normal retail release. It is bigger than the original, with more than 1,800 cards, and its packs added two new slots: a **white-bordered** reprint (121 possible cards, a nod to the old white-bordered core sets) and a **Future Sight-frame** card printed in the futuristic frame from 2007's Future Sight. Each pack also had a brand-new **playtest card**.

The Mystery Booster 2 playtest cards kept the joke going. Wrenn and One is a land planeswalker that makes Squirrels, Subgoyf counts subtypes in graveyards ("Plains, Equipment, Aura, Arcane..."), and Kozilek, Compleated has the made-up keyword annihinfect. The set also gave a few digital-only cards their first paper printings; Velukan Dragon, a formerly digital-only card that rolls a die when it attacks or blocks, is now legal in Commander and Legacy. Seven Alchemy reprints carry an acorn-style stamp and aren't tournament legal.

The white-bordered sheet is where the strong reprints were: Teferi, Time Raveler, Wasteland, Path to Exile, Goblin Guide, Mystical Dispute, Leyline of Sanctity, Narset, Parter of Veils and Deathrite Shaman all appear, along with Commander favorites like Sakura-Tribe Elder and Reliquary Tower. The set also includes Underworld Breach, Bolas's Citadel, Natural Order and Thassa's Oracle, all of which are on the Commander Game Changers list as of October 2026.

## Mystery Booster Commander Edition (2026)

Mystery Booster Commander Edition is a Commander Draft product, meaning you draft a pool and then build a Commander deck from it, much like Commander Masters. It was announced in 2026 and revealed at Gen Con in late July. Its paper launch is at MagicCon: Atlanta on November 13, 2026, with a Secret Lair Festival-in-a-Box offer for players who can't attend; preorders for that offer opened in October 2026.

The set has over 2,000 cards, almost all reprints, but it adds a batch of new legendary creatures and planeswalkers designed as commanders. Every pack is guaranteed one of these new rare commanders, plus a reprinted rare or mythic commander, three commons or uncommons in each color, a wildcard, a Future Sight-frame or Alchemy card, and a playtest card (the playtest sheet reprints the Mystery Booster 2 cards).

Its one new mechanic is **rulebreaker**, which appears on eight commanders and bends Commander's deck-building rules:

- **Whtz, the Bibliophile** removes the maximum deck size, and its card-draw ability gets cheaper if your deck started with 200 or more cards.
- **Grizzlegom, Hurloon Hero** lets your deck include any land cards, then rewards you for every basic land type you control when it attacks.
- **Tolabow, Loch Rascal** lets your instants and sorceries include one extra color.
- **The Everforger**, **Seluma, Light of Aysen**, **Valko Indorian**, **The Unluckiest Planeswalker** and **Maular, the Next Evolution** each let a specific group of cards (artifact creatures and Equipment, Angels, Phyrexians, Auras, or creatures with mana value 7 or more) ignore your color identity.

Other new commanders lean into Magic's history, like Joven and Chandler, a pair of rogues from the Homelands era that steal artifacts, and Dyfed, the Guiding Hand, a planeswalker that can be your commander. Because of supply concerns, Wizards said in August 2026 that the set will be legal only in Commander at launch, with Legacy, Vintage and Pauper legality following on January 1, 2027. As of October 2026, its new cards aren't legal anywhere yet.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

None of these products created a Constructed deck, because the reprints were already legal wherever they could be played and the new cards are either joke playtest cards or Commander-focused designs. Their real effect is supply. Mystery Booster 2 put more copies of Teferi, Time Raveler, Wasteland and Leyline of Sanctity into circulation, and The List dribbled out older staples in every set for four years.

In Commander, the rulebreaker commanders are the most interesting development. They're the first commanders that explicitly change deck-building rules, and they are likely to spawn a wave of unusual decks once the set is legal in November 2026. Because they're so new, how they'll interact with the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) and the Game Changers list is still to be seen. Playtest and acorn-stamped cards can be used only with your playgroup's agreement under Rule 0.

## Cards from Mystery Booster worth owning

- **Teferi, Time Raveler (white-bordered):** a competitive staple in an old-school frame.
- **Wasteland (white-bordered):** a Legacy and Commander land in a collector-favorite style.
- **Velukan Dragon:** a former digital card that is now legal in paper Commander.
- **Chira, All In:** a strong commander for random-effects decks once it's legal.
- **Grizzlegom, Hurloon Hero:** a fun rulebreaker commander that rewards a varied mana base.
- **A handful of playtest cards:** Bear with Set's Mechanic or Five Kids in a Trenchcoat make great additions to a casual cube.

Mystery Booster is best enjoyed as an event: a chaos draft at a convention, a box shared with friends, or a cube full of playtest cards.
