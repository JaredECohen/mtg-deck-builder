Dragons of Tarkir is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in March 2015, the third and final set of the Khans of Tarkir block. After Sarkhan Vol changed history in Fate Reforged, the plane of Tarkir is now ruled by five dragonlords instead of the five khans, and every clan has been remade in its dragon's image. The headline is dragons everywhere, a mechanic for each clan, and a set of Commands that are still played in Modern and Pioneer: Kolaghan's Command, Atarka's Command and friends, plus Collected Company.

## The story

To understand Dragons of Tarkir, you need the twist from the set before it. In the original timeline, the elder dragon Nicol Bolas killed Ugin, the spirit dragon, on Tarkir more than a thousand years ago. Ugin's death ended the dragonstorms that spawned new dragons, the dragons died out, and five clans (the Abzan, Jeskai, Sultai, Mardu and Temur) took over. Sarkhan Vol, a dragon-obsessed planeswalker, grew up in that dragonless world.

In Fate Reforged, Sarkhan traveled back in time to the moment of Ugin's death. He couldn't stop the fight, but he found the dying Ugin and wrapped him in a hedron, a stone device from Zendikar, which preserved the dragon's life. That one act changed everything. Sarkhan vanished from the past, and Dragons of Tarkir opens in a present that has been rewritten around his choice.

In the new timeline, the dragonstorms never stopped. Five dragon broods grew into five dragonlords, and each conquered or absorbed one of the old clans:

| Dragonlord | Clan it replaced | Colors | Aspect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dromoka | Abzan | Green-White | Endurance |
| Ojutai | Jeskai | White-Blue | Cunning |
| Silumgar | Sultai | Blue-Black | Ruthlessness |
| Kolaghan | Mardu | Black-Red | Speed |
| Atarka | Temur | Red-Green | Savagery |

The khans are gone and the word "khan" itself has been banned. Familiar characters live very different lives: Zurgo, once the brutal khan of the Mardu, is now a minor orc warrior of no importance. Narset, who died in the original timeline protecting Sarkhan, is alive. She grew up in the Ojutai clan and has been studying the forbidden history of the khans, and by the end of the story she has ignited her planeswalker spark.

Sarkhan, meanwhile, finds that he is a living paradox. Nobody remembers him, because in this timeline he was never born; the "Dragon Man" who saved Ugin simply appeared out of nowhere and vanished. His trip through time also left him able to use blue mana, which is why Sarkhan Unbroken is Temur-colored. He returns to the canyon where he saved Ugin and finds the spirit dragon alive, studying the new history carved into the canyon walls. Ugin has no memory of the old timeline. Sarkhan explains what he did, makes his peace with the dragon, and then meets Narset, who no longer knows him. The set ends with Sarkhan content for the first time in his life, setting off with Narset to explore the Tarkir he created, and with Ugin free to resume his long rivalry with Bolas.

## Mechanics

Each clan has its own mechanic, and megamorph runs across all five.

### Megamorph

Megamorph works like morph: you can cast the card face down as a 2/2 for three mana, then turn it face up any time by paying its megamorph cost. The twist is that it also gets a +1/+1 counter when it flips. Den Protector is a two-mana rare that, when turned face up, returns any card from your graveyard to your hand.

### Bolster (Dromoka)

Bolster N puts N +1/+1 counters on the creature you control with the least toughness. Dromoka Captain is a three-mana first striker that bolsters 1 whenever it attacks. Bolster rewards wide boards and makes combat math awkward for your opponent.

### Rebound (Ojutai)

Rebound returns from Rise of the Eldrazi. If you cast a rebound spell from your hand, it is exiled as it resolves, and you can cast it again for free at the start of your next upkeep. Ojutai's Summons makes a 2/2 flying Djinn Monk now and another one next turn.

### Exploit (Silumgar)

When a creature with exploit enters, you may sacrifice a creature, including itself, for a bonus. Silumgar Butcher gives a creature -3/-3 when it exploits. Exploit pairs with tokens and with creatures that have death triggers.

### Dash (Kolaghan)

Dash is an alternative cost: the creature gains haste and returns to your hand at the end of the turn. Zurgo Bellstriker is a one-mana 2/2 that you can dash for two mana. Dashing dodges sorcery-speed removal and lets aggressive decks attack the turn a creature arrives.

### Formidable (Atarka)

Formidable abilities work only if the creatures you control have total power 8 or greater. Dragon Whisperer, a two-mana red mythic, can make a 4/4 flying Dragon token each time you activate it while formidable is on.

### Dragon matters

Several cards check whether you control a Dragon or can reveal one from your hand. Draconic Roar deals 3 damage to a creature, plus 3 to its controller if you meet that condition. Silumgar's Scorn becomes a hard counterspell in the same situation.

## Limited

Dragons of Tarkir was drafted with two packs of itself followed by one pack of Fate Reforged, and it was designed around the five allied-color clans rather than ten pairs. Each clan had a gold uncommon Dragon as a signpost.

| Clan | Colors | Plan | Uncommon Dragon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dromoka | Green-White | Bolster and +1/+1 counters | Enduring Scalelord |
| Ojutai | White-Blue | Fliers and noncreature spells | Cunning Breezedancer |
| Silumgar | Blue-Black | Exploit and sacrifice value | Ruthless Deathfang |
| Kolaghan | Black-Red | Dash and fast attacks | Swift Warkite |
| Atarka | Red-Green | Formidable and big creatures | Savage Ventmaw |

Megamorph defined the format's feel. Every face-down 2/2 might be anything, so bluffing and reading your opponent's mana mattered. The best commons were removal and efficient bodies: Pacifism and Enduring Victory for white, Twin Bolt and Sarkhan's Rage for red, Epic Confrontation for green and Reduce in Stature for blue. Uncommons like Ultimate Price, Roast, Silkwrap and Draconic Roar were premium. Because Dragons rewarded you for having them, the uncommon Dragons were especially valuable. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

In its first months in Standard, Dragons of Tarkir slotted into a format built on the Khans of Tarkir fetchlands and tri-color decks. Decks that leaned on the set included:

- **Esper Dragons**, a control deck that used Dragonlord Ojutai, Silumgar's Scorn, Foul-Tongue Invocation and Icefall Regent, taking advantage of all the "if you control a Dragon" bonuses.
- **Abzan** midrange and aggro decks that picked up Dromoka's Command, Den Protector and Deathmist Raptor.
- **Atarka Red**, a go-wide aggro deck that used Atarka's Command as a finisher that also stopped lifegain.
- **Red Dragon** decks built around Thunderbreak Regent, plus ramp decks that topped out with Dragonlord Atarka.

By 2016, Collected Company had become a centerpiece of Standard creature decks, including the Bant Company deck of the Shadows over Innistrad season. No Dragons of Tarkir card was banned in Standard, and the set rotated out with Magic Origins when Kaladesh arrived in September 2016.

The set's longest legacy is in non-rotating formats. Kolaghan's Command became a Modern staple for black-red midrange decks, since it can kill a creature or an artifact while drawing value. Atarka's Command joined red aggro and burn decks, especially in Modern. Collected Company is a pillar of creature combo and value decks in Modern and Pioneer, where it can put two creatures onto the battlefield at instant speed.

In Commander, the five Dragonlords are popular commanders and inclusions, and Dragon Tempest, Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Sarkhan Unbroken and Secure the Wastes are all common in Dragon and token decks. As of October 2026, no Dragons of Tarkir card is banned in Pioneer, Modern, Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Dragons of Tarkir worth owning

- **Collected Company**: an instant-speed engine for creature decks in Modern and Pioneer.
- **Kolaghan's Command**: flexible two-for-one value for black-red decks in Modern and Pioneer.
- **Atarka's Command**: a burst of damage that fits red aggro and burn, especially in Modern.
- **Dromoka's Command**: flexible green-white interaction for Pioneer and Commander.
- **Dragon Tempest** and **Haven of the Spirit Dragon**: key pieces for Dragon tribal Commander decks.
- **Dragonlord Silumgar**: a strong Commander creature that steals your opponent's best threat.

## The takeaway

Dragons of Tarkir is the payoff for the whole Khans block, both in story and in cards. The time-travel plot gave each clan a dragon-shaped identity, and the Commands and Collected Company have outlived the set's Standard season by a decade. If you're building a Pioneer deck with it, start with Collected Company and cheap creatures, or with Kolaghan's Command in a black-red midrange shell.
