Aetherdrift is the Magic: The Gathering expansion built around a death race across three planes, released in February 2025. Ten racing teams tear from Avishkar (the plane once called Kaladesh) through Amonkhet and the prehistoric jungles of Muraganda, chasing a prize that could restore a lost planeswalker spark. The headline is speed: Vehicles everywhere, a new "Start your engines!" mechanic that rewards you for hitting your opponent early, and a set of one-shot exhaust abilities.

## The story

The race is called the Ghirapur Grand Prix. After the Phyrexian Invasion, the Omenpaths (stable portals between planes) made cross-planar travel possible for ordinary people, and Avishkar's inventors turned that into a spectacle. The route starts in Ghirapur, crosses Amonkhet's deserts, plunges into Muraganda, and loops back to the Avishkar finish line. The prize is the Aetherspark, an engineered planeswalker spark built around a captured piece of the Blind Eternities.

The racers all have something to win. Chandra Nalaar drives for the Cloudspire team because she wants the Aetherspark for Nissa, who lost her spark in the invasion and can no longer travel home. Chandra's mother, Pia Nalaar, co-captains a rival Avishkar team with the young inventor Sita Varma, who races in secret under the alias "Spitfire." Basri Ket rides for the Champions of Amonkhet alongside Zahur, racing for the gods of a plane still recovering from Nicol Bolas. Daretti, a goblin planeswalker who lost his spark, leads the Goblin Rocketeers. And Winter, who appears in Duskmourn's story, drives for the Speed Demons because winning is his only way out of the House that holds him.

The race doubles as a heist. Loot, a small telepathic creature who has wandered through several recent stories, keeps steering racers toward the lead, and it turns out that Jace Beleren and Vraska want Loot badly. Jace stirs up political unrest in Ghirapur, manipulating Sita's father into a failed coup, all as cover for a trap. Near the finish, Jace takes over racers with mind magic and turns them against Chandra. She refuses to fight people who aren't in control of themselves, Vraska corners her, and Chandra gives Loot up rather than let anyone get hurt.

The Champions of Amonkhet win. Basri and Zahur hide from an attacking dragon in a cloud of sand and cross the line first, then claim the Aetherspark at the ceremony. The dragon assault is the real cliffhanger: Elspeth arrives to fight it and warns that dragons are appearing across the Multiverse. Jace and Vraska vanish to Tarkir with Loot, which leads directly into Tarkir: Dragonstorm.

## Mechanics

### Start your engines! and speed

A card with "Start your engines!" gives you a speed value if you don't have one, starting at 1. Once per turn, on your own turn, your speed goes up by one if an opponent lost life. It caps at 4, which is called max speed, and it never goes down. Speed belongs to you, not the card, so every engine card you control shares the same counter.

Cards with the mechanic get stronger at max speed. Burnout Bashtronaut is a one-mana 1/1 with menace that gains double strike at max speed. The Speed Demon, a five-mana flying demon, draws you cards equal to your speed at your end step (and costs that much life). The practical lesson is to start dealing damage on turn two or three, because it takes three separate turns of damage to reach max speed.

### Exhaust

Exhaust abilities can each be activated only once per permanent. They're usually a big payoff for a later turn. Stampeding Scurryfoot is a one-mana 1/1 that can later pay `{3}{G}` to grow and make a 3/3 Elephant. Loot, the Pathfinder carries three separate exhaust abilities: big mana, three cards, or 3 damage to any target. Because exhaust is a one-time cost, these cards are flexible early and still useful late.

### Vehicles, Mounts and cycling

Vehicles return in force. A Vehicle is an artifact that becomes a creature when you crew it, tapping creatures with enough total power. Mounts, from Outlaws of Thunder Junction, also come back: you saddle a Mount by tapping other creatures, and it gets a bonus when it attacks while saddled. Guardian Sunmare, for example, finds a cheap permanent from your library and puts it onto the battlefield when it attacks while saddled. Cycling returns on cards like Spectacular Pileup and Valor's Flagship, letting expensive spells trade themselves in for a fresh card when you don't need them.

## Limited

Aetherdrift drafts as a ten-team format: each two-color pair has signpost uncommons, and one of the two is always a Vehicle or a Mount.

| Pair | Theme | Signpost uncommons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Artifacts | Guidelight Pathmaker, Voyage Home |
| Blue-Black | Artifact drain | Haunt the Network, Haunted Hellride |
| Black-Red | Speed aggro | Gastal Thrillseeker, Apocalypse Runner |
| Red-Green | Exhaust midrange | Rocketeer Boostbuggy, Boom Scholar |
| Green-White | Mounts and Vehicles | Lagorin, Soul of Alacria, Veteran Beastrider |
| White-Black | Speed and recursion | Embalmed Ascendant, Dune Drifter |
| Blue-Red | Discard | Boosted Sloop, Broadside Barrage |
| Black-Green | Graveyard | Broodheart Engine, Thundering Broodwagon |
| Red-White | Vehicle aggro | Cloudspire Coordinator, Cloudspire Skycycle |
| Green-Blue | Exhaust ramp | Rangers' Aetherhive, Skyserpent Seeker |

The big story of the format was green. 17Lands data covered at the time showed green decks, and green paired with black or blue, at the top of the win-rate table, while Boros lagged. Green's commons were deep: Stampeding Scurryfoot, Hazard of the Dunes, Pothole Mole and the cheap removal spell Run Over all made decks better. Elsewhere, Lightning Strike, Spin Out and Ride's End were the premium commons.

The format rewarded tempo, but not reckless speed. Speed cards need three turns of damage to hit their best mode, so cheap creatures and Vehicles that attack early matter more than in a typical set. At the same time, many Vehicles crew poorly if your board is thin, so count your crew-capable creatures during the draft. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Aetherdrift's biggest Standard contributions were efficient support cards rather than one dominant deck. At Pro Tour Aetherdrift in February 2025, Matt Nass won with Domain Overlords, a ramp-control deck from earlier sets that picked up four Ride's End and Wastewood Verge from the new set. Wizards' coverage named Ride's End the most played new card of the event, and Stock Up, Momentum Breaker, Grim Bauble and Riptide Gearhulk also showed up across Azorius control, Orzhov midrange and other top decks. An Izzet Artifacts deck at the same event ran Repurposing Bay, Radiant Lotus and Boommobile.

No Aetherdrift card has been banned in Standard, Pioneer or Modern as of October 2026. Over the following year, Ride's End and Stock Up became the set's most durable Standard cards, the kind of flexible interaction and card selection that fit into many decks rather than defining one.

Outside Standard, Aetherdrift's notable effect was reprinting Bloodghast, which made the recursive vampire legal in Standard and Pioneer. In Commander, The Aetherspark and Radiant Lotus (a six-mana artifact that sacrifices artifacts for big bursts of mana) drew the most interest, along with legends like Loot, the Pathfinder and Mendicant Core, Guidelight. Aetherdrift also had two Commander precons themed around the race.

## Cards from Aetherdrift worth owning

- **Ride's End**: cheap exile removal for tapped targets, useful in any white deck while it's legal.
- **Stock Up**: efficient card selection that blue Standard decks reach for again and again.
- **Bloodghast**: a long-standing graveyard creature, now legal in Pioneer and Standard.
- **The Aetherspark**: a unique equipment planeswalker for Commander voltron and Vehicle decks.
- **Radiant Lotus**: an artifact mana engine for Commander artifact decks.
- **The Verge lands**: good dual lands for Standard and Pioneer decks in their colors.

## The takeaway

Aetherdrift is a fast, aggressive set that rewards attacking early, but its most lasting cards are its quiet ones: removal, card selection and lands. If you're building a Standard deck today, start with Ride's End and Stock Up, then decide whether you want to lean into speed with cheap attackers or play a slower Vehicle deck.
