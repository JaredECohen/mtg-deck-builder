Mirage is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 1996 as the first set of the Mirage block, the game's first true block of one large set followed by two smaller ones. It's set in Jamuraa, an African-inspired continent on Dominaria, and it introduced phasing, flanking and reminder text. Mirage also printed a group of cards still banned or restricted today, including Lion's Eye Diamond, Mystical Tutor and Flash.

## The story

Mirage was a deliberate change of scenery after the frozen north of Ice Age. Jamuraa is a tropical continent of jungles, deserts and coastal cities, home to three nations: **Zhalfir**, an old kingdom of knights and mages; **Femeref**, a wealthy theocracy that split from Zhalfir centuries earlier; and **Suq'Ata**, a mercantile empire. Creative lead Pete Venters wanted a sophisticated society rather than the Eurocentric medieval look of earlier sets.

The trouble starts with **Teferi**, an ancient Zhalfirin mage and planeswalker. On his private island, he experimented with "phasing," dropping things in and out of time. The experiments damaged the time stream, and when he tried to repair it with a massive burst of energy, the entire island phased out of existence, leaving bare rock. Three powerful spellcasters sensed the disturbance and came to investigate: **Mangara of Corondor**, a diplomat; **Jolrael**, who ruled a jungle empire of beasts; and **Kaervek**, a sorcerer allied with dark spirits from the Burning Isles.

Mangara settled in and brokered a long peace among the three nations. Kaervek, who wanted a kingdom of his own, couldn't stand it. He worked on Jolrael's fears of human ambition, raised covert armies, stirred up border disputes and then ambushed Mangara, sealing him inside an **Amber Prison**. With Mangara gone, Jamuraa slid into war: dragonfire, armies of the dead and a demon called the Spirit of the Night that slaughtered Femeref's assembly.

Then Teferi's island came back, nearly two hundred years after it vanished. Jolrael, now regretting her alliance, begged him for help. Teferi refused to fight directly, since his experiments still needed him, but he offered to guide Jamuraa's leaders through dreams to free Mangara. Mirage covers the beginning of this Mirage War, and the story resolves in Visions: the warrior Asmira gives her life to free Mangara, and Kaervek ends up imprisoned in amber himself. Teferi goes on to play a major role in the Weatherlight Saga and beyond.

## Mechanics

Mirage was the first set developed with Limited play in mind, and it also brought in reminder text and clearer card frames. It shipped with Fifth Edition rules ahead of that core set's release.

### Phasing

A permanent with phasing phases out on its controller's untap step every other turn, and while phased out it's treated as though it doesn't exist. Then it phases back in. In Mirage, phasing was mostly a drawback that let creatures be cheap. **Sandbar Crocodile** (common) is a 6/5 for `{4}{U}` that's only around half the time. Phasing is famous for the rules headaches it caused, but it later became a useful protection effect on cards like Teferi's Protection.

### Flanking

When a creature without flanking blocks a creature with flanking, the blocker gets -1/-1 until end of turn. It made three-mana 2/2 knights good on offense. **Zhalfirin Knight** (common, `{2}{W}`) is a 2/2 flanker that can gain first strike for `{W}{W}`, so most small creatures can't block it profitably.

### Cycles: charms, tutors, Diamonds and fetch lands

- **Charms**: one-mana instants with three modes. **Ivory Charm** (common) can shrink every creature's power, tap a creature or prevent 1 damage.
- **Tutors**: **Enlightened Tutor** (artifact or enchantment), **Mystical Tutor** (instant or sorcery) and **Worldly Tutor** (creature) each cost one mana and put the card on top of your library. Visions added Vampiric Tutor.
- **Diamonds**: two-mana artifacts that enter tapped and make one color, like **Sky Diamond**.
- **Sacrifice lands**: **Bad River**, **Flood Plain**, **Grasslands**, **Mountain Valley** and **Rocky Tar Pit** enter tapped and sacrifice to fetch a basic of either of two types, an early version of later fetch lands.

## Limited

Mirage was the first Magic set designed with sealed deck and booster draft in mind, which shows in how deep its commons are compared with earlier expansions. Records of how the format was drafted in 1996 are sparse, so there's no reliable list of archetypes to point to. What we can say from the card file: flanking knights in white and red rewarded attacking, **Pacifism** (which debuted here) and **Kaervek's Torch** were premium removal, phasing creatures were cheap but unreliable blockers, and the set's many enemy-color gold cards made two-color decks outside the usual friendly pairs possible. Charms gave every color a flexible instant. If you're new to drafting, our [draft vs. sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide) explains how Limited works.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Type II (1996-1998)

When Mirage arrived, Type II was dominated by Ice Age's Necropotence, and many of the period's top decks were Necro variants: big-creature Necro, weenie Necro and black-red Necro. Mirage added pieces to those decks and also fed new ones. **Hammer of Bogardan** gave red-blue control a recurring burn spell, a deck known as Counter Hammer, and **Painful Memories** found a home in black land destruction.

Mirage's most famous Constructed contribution is **Prosperous Bloom**, or ProsBloom. Mike Long won Pro Tour Paris in 1997 with it, and it's often called the first true combo deck in tournament Magic. It used Mirage's **Cadaverous Bloom** to turn cards in hand into mana, Visions' Prosperity to draw a pile of cards, Squandered Resources to sacrifice lands for more mana and Drain Life to finish. **Natural Balance** and **Infernal Contract** helped too. The Mirage block left Type II when Urza's Saga arrived in November 1998.

### Legacy and Vintage

Mirage predates Modern but remains vital in older formats.

- **Lion's Eye Diamond** was restricted in Vintage in 2003 and is the engine of Legacy storm and graveyard combo decks that use Infernal Tutor or flashback and dredge effects to cash in a discarded hand.
- **Mystical Tutor** was restricted in Vintage in 1999 and banned in Legacy in 2010.
- **Flash** was banned in Legacy in 2007 because of its combo with Protean Hulk, and it's restricted in Vintage.
- **Enlightened Tutor** was taken off Vintage's restricted list in 2009 and is legal in Legacy.
- **Phyrexian Dreadnought** shows up in Legacy combo decks that counter or ignore its sacrifice trigger.

For a full picture of these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

### Commander

In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), the three tutors are staples of competitive decks, and Commander banned **Flash** in April 2020 for making cEDH games feel predetermined. **Lion's Eye Diamond** was banned from 2008 to 2011 and is legal now. **Celestial Dawn**, **Grinning Totem**, **Cursed Totem** and **Shallow Grave** are popular niche picks, and the sacrifice lands are budget fixing.

## Cards from Mirage worth owning

- **Enlightened Tutor**: finds the artifact or enchantment that wins the game, in Legacy and Commander.
- **Worldly Tutor**: a creature tutor for green Commander decks.
- **Mystical Tutor**: a Commander staple and restricted Vintage card.
- **Lion's Eye Diamond**: the iconic Legacy storm enabler.
- **Shallow Grave**: a classic reanimation spell.
- **Cursed Totem**: a cheap hate piece against creature-based engines.
- **Wall of Roots**: a sturdy ramp creature in Pauper and casual decks.
