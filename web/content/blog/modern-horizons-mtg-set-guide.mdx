Modern Horizons is a Magic: The Gathering supplemental set released in June 2019, and it was the first set ever designed to go directly into the Modern format without passing through Standard. It mixed brand-new cards with older cards that had never been legal in Modern, and it changed that format more than any regular expansion of its era. Its headline themes were snow, Slivers, Ninjas and a long list of returning keywords from across Magic's history.

## What the set was for

For most of Modern's history, the only way a card entered the format was by being printed in a Standard-legal set. That put a ceiling on what Wizards of the Coast could do: anything strong enough to shake up Modern also had to be safe in a much smaller Standard environment. Modern Horizons removed that constraint. Every card in it was designed with Modern, and the older eternal formats, as the target.

The set had two jobs. The first was to add new tools to Modern that couldn't have been printed in a normal set. The second was to reprint older cards that had never been legal in the format, such as Flusterstorm, Fact or Fiction, Regrowth, Eladamri's Call, Genesis and Future Sight, so that Modern players could finally use them.

There is no storyline in the usual sense. Instead, the flavor jumps around the Multiverse and Magic's history. Urza, Lord High Artificer and Yawgmoth, Thran Physician revisit the Dominaria of the late 1990s, Serra the Benevolent is a planeswalker version of the angel who created her own plane, and Kess, Dissident Mage and Sisay, Weatherlight Captain bring back characters from the Weatherlight saga. The set also introduced a new planeswalker pair, Wrenn and Six, a dryad bonded to a treefolk. The common thread is nostalgia: many cards nod to old blocks, from Ice Age snow to Kamigawa Ninjas.

## Mechanics

### Snow

Snow is a supertype, not a color. A snow permanent that taps for mana produces snow mana, and some cards ask for it with the snow symbol in their costs. Modern Horizons printed snow-covered basic lands and a stack of payoffs that count your snow permanents. Ice-Fang Coatl is a flash flier that draws a card and gains deathtouch once you control three other snow permanents. Abominable Treefolk's power and toughness equal your snow permanent count. Arcum's Astrolabe, a one-mana snow artifact that fixes your colors and replaces itself, turned out to be the most important snow card in the set, and also the most controversial.

### Slivers

Slivers returned with new members in every color, focused on red and white. Each Sliver grants its ability to every Sliver you control, so the creatures get better the more of them you have. Cloudshredder Sliver gives all your Slivers flying and haste for `{R}{W}`, Lavabelly Sliver adds a small drain on each Sliver's arrival, and The First Sliver is a five-color legend that gives every Sliver spell cascade.

### Ninjutsu

Ninjutsu returned from Kamigawa. When one of your attackers goes unblocked, you can pay the ninjutsu cost and return that attacker to your hand to put the Ninja onto the battlefield tapped and attacking. Ingenious Infiltrator draws a card whenever a Ninja deals combat damage to a player, while Mist-Syndicate Naga copies itself each time it connects. Ninjutsu rewards cheap evasive creatures and turns them into a stream of card advantage.

### Changeling and the keyword grab bag

Changelings, which have every creature type, helped glue the tribal themes together; Changeling Outcast is a one-mana creature that can't block or be blocked. Beyond that, the set deliberately reached back for around 45 older mechanics: cycling, suspend, flashback, storm, entwine, level up, evoke, convoke, delve, persist and more. Hexdrinker uses level up, Echo of Eons uses flashback, and Mox Tantalite uses suspend. For a quick refresher on what all those words mean, our [keyword glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers the common ones.

## Limited

Modern Horizons was a high-powered, complex draft format. Because so many mechanics were packed into one set, the ten two-color pairs each pointed at a different idea, and the gold uncommons were the clearest signposts:

- **Green-blue snow:** Abominable Treefolk and Ice-Fang Coatl, with snow lands and snow creatures as the engine. Reviewers rated it one of the strongest decks in the format.
- **Blue-black Ninjas:** Ingenious Infiltrator plus evasive cheap creatures to enable ninjutsu.
- **Red-white Slivers:** Lavabelly Sliver and friends. Powerful when it came together, but it needed a critical mass of Slivers.
- **Blue-red draw-two:** Thundering Djinn and cards that reward drawing a second card each turn.
- **Black-red Goblins:** Munitions Expert and Goblin token makers like Sling-Gang Lieutenant.
- **White-black tribal and changelings:** Etchings of the Chosen anthems a creature type, and changelings count for every type.
- **White-blue blink:** Soulherder rewards exiling and returning your own creatures, with Ephemerate as the ideal partner.
- **Black-green graveyard:** Rotwidow Pack exiles creature cards from your graveyard to build a Spider army.
- **Red-green lands in the graveyard:** Ruination Rioter scales with land cards in your graveyard.
- **Green-white counters:** Good-Fortune Unicorn adds a +1/+1 counter to each creature that enters.

The format was slower than many premier sets because snow and cycling decks could grind, but aggressive Goblin and Sliver decks punished stumbles. Removal was decent at common, and the snow basic lands mattered more than in a normal set, so drafters learned to take them when they were in snow colors. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Modern

Modern Horizons hit Modern hard and fast. Within weeks of release, graveyard decks built around Hogaak, Arisen Necropolis took over. Hogaak can't be cast with mana at all, but convoke and delve made it a turn-two or turn-three 8/8 that could be cast again from the graveyard. Wizards first banned Bridge from Below in July 2019, and when Hogaak decks stayed dominant, the August 26, 2019 announcement banned Hogaak itself along with Faithless Looting. Faithless Looting was later unbanned and is legal in Modern as of October 2026, but Hogaak remains banned.

The second wave was snow. Arcum's Astrolabe let multicolor decks play snow basics, fix every color and draw a card for one mana, which made four-color midrange decks with Ice-Fang Coatl and Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath unusually consistent. Wizards banned Astrolabe in Modern on July 13, 2020, citing unhealthy win rates.

Plenty of Modern Horizons cards stayed legal and became long-term staples. Wrenn and Six anchored Jund and Temur midrange decks for years. Urza, Lord High Artificer powered an artifact combo deck. Force of Negation became a standard sideboard card in blue decks. Plague Engineer, Seasoned Pyromancer, Giver of Runes, Ranger-Captain of Eos, Collector Ouphe and Prismatic Vista all saw regular Modern play. For context on how Modern works, see our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

### Legacy, Vintage and Pauper

The set reached well beyond Modern. In Legacy, Wrenn and Six was banned on November 18, 2019 after Temur Delver decks built around it dominated the format, and Arcum's Astrolabe followed in the February 15, 2021 announcement. Force of Vigor, Force of Negation and Plague Engineer found Legacy homes as well. In Pauper, where only commons are allowed, Arcum's Astrolabe was also banned. Our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) covers how Wizards decides on bans like these.

### Commander

In Commander, Modern Horizons mostly added strong legends and staples rather than anything that needed a ban. Yawgmoth, Thran Physician became a popular sacrifice and -1/-1 counter commander, Urza leads artifact decks, Morophon, the Boundless is a favorite tribal commander, and The First Sliver gives Sliver decks a five-color leader. Generous Gift, Ephemerate, Prismatic Vista and the Talismans show up in a wide range of decks. None of the set's cards are on the Commander ban list or the Game Changers list as of October 2026.

## Cards from Modern Horizons worth owning

- **Wrenn and Six:** a defining two-mana planeswalker in Modern and a strong Commander card for land-recursion decks.
- **Force of Negation:** a free counterspell that remains a reliable sideboard card in blue decks.
- **Prismatic Vista:** a fetch land that finds any basic, useful in nearly every eternal deck.
- **Urza, Lord High Artificer:** an artifact engine in both Modern combo and Commander.
- **Yawgmoth, Thran Physician:** a top-tier sacrifice commander and a Modern combo piece.
- **Plague Engineer:** a flexible creature that answers tokens and one-toughness creatures.
- **Generous Gift:** catch-all removal that fits any white Commander deck.
- **The Horizon lands:** dual lands that cost a little life but cash in for a card late in the game.

Modern Horizons proved that a set built for an older format could work, and it opened the door to Modern Horizons 2 and 3. If you want to play its cards today, the most accessible paths are a Jund or Temur deck with Wrenn and Six in Modern or a Yawgmoth or Urza deck in Commander.
