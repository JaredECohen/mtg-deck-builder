Battle for Zendikar is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2015 that sent players back to Zendikar to fight the Eldrazi, the world-eating titans freed five years earlier in Rise of the Eldrazi. The set introduced colorless "devoid" spells, ingest and processors, and landfall's return, and it printed Gideon, Ally of Zendikar, one of the strongest planeswalkers of its era. It also started the story that formed the Gatewatch.

## The story

When the Eldrazi titans broke free in Rise of the Eldrazi, they began stripping Zendikar bare. By the time of Battle for Zendikar, the titan Ulamog and its brood have overrun much of the plane, and the great port city of Sea Gate has fallen. The planeswalker Gideon Jura has been trying to defend both Ravnica and Zendikar at once, and he realizes he needs help. The plane's hedrons, the strange floating stones the Eldrazi were once imprisoned with, are linked by leylines, so he recruits Jace Beleren, an expert on exactly that kind of puzzle.

While Jace and the merfolk scholar Jori En travel to study the hedron network, Gideon gathers refugees at a camp called Sky Rock and decides to go on the offensive. Soldiers, Kor, the vampires of Malakir under Drana and a group of Roil-mages join him. The merfolk planeswalker Kiora arrives with an army of sea monsters, Nissa Revane brings Zendikar's own elemental power, and together they retake Sea Gate.

Then Ulamog arrives. The planeswalkers' plan works at first: Nissa raises sunken hedrons into a ring around the titan, Jace activates them, and Ulamog is trapped. That's when the demon Ob Nixilis appears. He'd lost his planeswalker spark long ago and had already tried to reclaim it from the Khalni Heart before Nissa stopped him. Now he drains the hedron trap's power into himself, gets his spark back, and destroys the trap. In doing so he awakens the second titan, Kozilek, which surfaces in the middle of the battle. With two titans loose, Kiora's giant octopus Lorthos is cut apart. Ob Nixilis beats Gideon, Jace and Nissa and takes them prisoner. Chandra Nalaar arrives just in time to free them, and the four drive him off. The story continues in Oath of the Gatewatch, where these planeswalkers swear their oaths and finally destroy the titans.

## Mechanics

### Devoid and colorless matters

Devoid cards have colored mana costs but no color. That's how the set shows the Eldrazi draining color from Zendikar. Many cards then reward you for casting or controlling colorless spells. **Silent Skimmer** (common) is a devoid flier that drains 2 life whenever it attacks, and **Herald of Kozilek** (uncommon) makes colorless spells cost one less.

### Ingest and processors

Ingest creatures make a player exile the top card of their library whenever they deal combat damage. Processors, a new Eldrazi creature type, then put an opponent's exiled card into their graveyard to get an effect. **Fathom Feeder** (rare) is a two-mana deathtouch ingester, and **Wasteland Strangler** (rare) is a processor that gives a creature -3/-3 when it enters.

### Awaken

Awaken is an alternate cost on spells. If you pay the awaken cost, the spell also turns one of your lands into a 0/0 Elemental creature with haste and some +1/+1 counters. **Ruinous Path** (rare) destroys a creature or planeswalker, and if you pay more, it also makes a 4/4 land creature.

### Rally

Rally is an ability word on Allies, the Zendikari resistance. Whenever an Ally you control enters, each rally card gives your team a bonus. **Resolute Blademaster** (uncommon) gives your creatures double strike for the turn whenever an Ally enters.

### Converge and landfall

Converge counts how many colors of mana you spent on a spell. **Painful Truths** (rare) draws a card and costs a life for each color spent, and **Radiant Flames** (rare) deals damage to every creature equal to the colors spent. Landfall returns from the first Zendikar block and triggers whenever a land enters under your control. **Retreat to Coralhelm** (uncommon) taps or untaps a creature, or scries, on every land drop. ## Limited

Battle for Zendikar split its draft archetypes between the Zendikari and the Eldrazi. The gold uncommons show the plan for each pair:

- **White-blue: awaken.** Roil Spout and other spells that turn lands into creatures.
- **Blue-black: ingest and processors.** Ulamog's Nullifier counters spells and rewards exiling cards.
- **Black-red: devoid aggro.** Forerunner of Slaughter gives your colorless creatures haste.
- **Red-green: landfall.** Grove Rumbler grows on every land drop.
- **Green-white: Allies and tokens.** Grovetender Druids makes Plants with rally.
- **White-black: Allies and drain.** Drana's Emissary drains every upkeep.
- **Blue-red: colorless ramp.** Herald of Kozilek makes colorless spells cheaper.
- **Black-green: Eldrazi Scions.** Catacomb Sifter makes Scion tokens to sacrifice for mana.
- **Red-white: Ally aggro.** Resolute Blademaster gives double strike with rally.
- **Green-blue: converge.** Skyrider Elf grows with every color spent.

The Ally decks were fast and swingy, while the Eldrazi decks used Scion tokens to ramp into big colorless threats. Ingest and processors worked best when you drafted plenty of both. Strong commons included Silent Skimmer, Smite the Monstrous, Gideon's Reproach and the cheap bounce spell Clutch of Currents. If two-color archetypes are new to you, our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains how they work.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Battle for Zendikar entered a Standard full of Khans of Tarkir fetchlands, and its five battle lands fit right in. Because they enter untapped when you control two basic lands, and because they have basic land types that fetchlands can find, they made three-color decks consistent. At Pro Tour Battle for Zendikar in Milwaukee in October 2015, the most-played decks were Atarka Red, Jeskai Black and Green-White Megamorph, and Kazuyuki Takimura won the event with Abzan Aggro.

Over the next two years, **Gideon, Ally of Zendikar** became one of the format's defining cards. A four-mana planeswalker that made a 2/2 every turn was hard for most decks to answer, and it stayed central to white decks through the Shadows over Innistrad and Kaladesh seasons until rotation. **Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger** headlined Eldrazi Ramp decks, and Ob Nixilis Reignited and Painful Truths were reliable card-advantage tools in black. No Battle for Zendikar card was ever banned in Standard.

Outside Standard, the set's cards have aged unevenly. As of October 2026, Bring to Light is a combo enabler in Pioneer and Modern, Radiant Flames is a Pioneer sideboard sweeper, and Gideon, Ally of Zendikar has seen play in Pioneer white decks. In Modern, Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger is a common top end for Tron decks, Retreat to Coralhelm combos with Knight of the Reliquary, and Wasteland Strangler has played a removal role in Eldrazi creature decks. None of the set's cards are banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy. The battle lands themselves are rarely played outside Standard, since they need basics to enter untapped. For how these formats differ, see our [formats overview](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

In Commander, Zulaport Cutthroat is a staple of aristocrats decks, Omnath, Locus of Rage leads popular landfall decks, and Greenwarden of Murasa, Oblivion Sower, Hedron Archive and the landfall Retreats all show up often. The set's box toppers, the Zendikar Expeditions, were premium foil versions of fetchlands and other strong lands, an early version of the special-treatment chase cards in modern sets.

## Cards from Battle for Zendikar worth owning

- **Gideon, Ally of Zendikar**: a strong white planeswalker in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Ulamog, the Ceaseless Hunger**: a Modern Tron finisher and a Commander bomb.
- **Bring to Light**: a tutor that powers combo decks in Pioneer and Modern.
- **Zulaport Cutthroat**: a cheap drain effect for every black Commander sacrifice deck.
- **Omnath, Locus of Rage**: one of the best landfall commanders.
- **Painful Truths**: efficient card draw for multicolor decks.
- **Retreat to Coralhelm**: a combo piece and landfall engine.

Battle for Zendikar is a good set to know if you like colorless Eldrazi or landfall. Its Standard days are long gone, but in Pioneer you can still build around Gideon and its converge cards.
