Commander Legends: Battle for Baldur's Gate is a Magic: The Gathering set released in June 2022, built to be drafted as Commander and set in the Dungeons & Dragons city of Baldur's Gate on the Forgotten Realms. It was the second Commander Legends set, and it brought two mechanics that are still in heavy Commander use: Backgrounds, a new way to have two commanders, and the initiative, which sends players racing through a dungeon called the Undercity.

## The story

The set draws from three layers of Baldur's Gate fiction. The first is the classic Baldur's Gate video games, where the dead god of murder, Bhaal, left behind mortal children called Bhaalspawn. Abdel Adrian, Gorion's Ward is the protagonist of the novels based on those games, raised by the wizard Gorion; his half-brother Sarevok, Deathbringer schemes to take their father's power; and Bhaal, Lord of Murder himself appears as a three-color God. The ranger Minsc and his "miniature giant space hamster" Boo, fan favorites from the games, became the set's most famous card.

The second layer is the tabletop adventure Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus. In it, the holy city of Elturel is dragged into Avernus, the first layer of the Nine Hells, and heroes from Baldur's Gate follow it down to bargain with devils and the fallen angel who rules there. Cards like Descent into Avernus, Elturel Survivors and Raphael, Fiendish Savior come from that plot, and the angels of Tyr and the city's soldiers, the Flaming Fist, fill out the white cards.

The third layer was new at the time: the companions from Baldur's Gate 3, which was still in early access. Lae'zel, Shadowheart, Astarion, Gale, Wyll and Karlach all appear, along with the mind flayers and their nautiloid ship from the game's opening. Since the cards came out well before the game's full release, they're a snapshot of the characters as they were first introduced.

Rather than one plot, the set is a tour of the city and its people: the Patriars who run it, the Guild that runs its underworld, Candlekeep's library, the dragons who circle it and the adventurers passing through. It mirrors a D&D campaign: you pick a character, give them a backstory, and send them into a dungeon.

## Mechanics

### Backgrounds

Many legendary creatures in the set have "Choose a Background." In Commander, a creature with that ability can have a legendary Background enchantment as a second commander. Backgrounds grant an ability to the commander creatures you own. Agent of the Iron Throne makes each opponent lose 1 life whenever one of your artifacts or creatures dies, and Candlekeep Sage, a common, gives your commander "draw a card when it enters or leaves the battlefield." It's like partner, but split into a character and a backstory, and it lets a mono-colored creature pick up a second color. Our [Commander overview](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers how two commanders work with color identity.

### The initiative and the Undercity

When you "take the initiative," you venture into the Undercity, a five-room dungeon card, and you venture again at the start of each of your upkeeps while you keep it. Any player who deals combat damage to you steals it and ventures in turn. Because it keeps paying out and can be stolen, the initiative creates a running fight over one prize. White Plume Adventurer is a 3/3 that takes the initiative when it enters and untaps your creatures on each opponent's upkeep. Stirring Bard is a common 0/4 defender that takes the initiative.

### Dice, myriad and returning mechanics

D20 rolls return from Adventures in the Forgotten Realms: the Ancient Dragon cycle rolls a d20 when it hits a player, and Ancient Copper Dragon makes that many Treasures. Myriad, first printed in the Commander 2015 decks, makes a token copy of an attacker for each other opponent, which suits multiplayer; Battle Angels of Tyr and Elturel Survivors have it. Party, from Zendikar Rising, appears on cards like Nalia de'Arnise. Gates return too, led by the legendary land Baldur's Gate.

## The Commander decks

The four preconstructed decks shipped with new face commanders:

- **Party Time** (white-black): Nalia de'Arnise lets you cast Clerics, Rogues, Warriors and Wizards from the top of your library and pumps your team when you have a full party.
- **Mind Flayarrrs** (blue-black): Captain N'ghathrod gives Horrors menace, mills players when they connect, and steals artifacts or creatures milled that turn.
- **Draconic Dissent** (blue-red): Firkraag, Cunning Instigator goads enemy creatures when your Dragons attack and draws when forced attackers connect.
- **Exit from Exile** (red-green): Faldorn, Dread Wolf Herald makes a 2/2 Wolf whenever you cast a spell or play a land from exile.

They also carried useful reprints like Black Market Connections, Jeska's Will and Skullclamp. For upgrade advice, see [how to upgrade a Commander precon](/blog/how-to-upgrade-a-commander-precon).

## Limited

Like the first Commander Legends, CLB was drafted to build 60-card Commander-style decks. Each 20-card Draft Booster held a legendary creature or planeswalker, a Background, a non-legendary rare or mythic, three uncommons, thirteen commons and a foil, so there were plenty of commanders to go around. Rather than ten fixed two-color archetypes, the format revolved around picking a commander (often a creature plus a Background) and drafting toward its plan, with Backgrounds at common giving every deck a second-color option.

The initiative cards dominated. Taking the initiative early generated so much value that games often became a fight over who held it, and evasive creatures and cheap attackers rose in value because they could steal it back. Removal and fliers were the next priority, and color-fixing commons like the set's Gates kept three-color decks viable. Because decks were 60 cards with a commander, sideboarding and deck size worked differently from a normal draft; our [draft vs sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide) covers the standard format if you want the contrast.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Almost all of CLB's new cards are legal only in Commander, Legacy and Vintage, so its Constructed impact was concentrated in two places.

- **Legacy.** White Plume Adventurer powered a mono-white initiative deck that, together with Izzet Delver, took up a large share of top Legacy tables. Wizards banned White Plume Adventurer in Legacy on March 6, 2023, in the same announcement as Expressive Iteration.
- **Pauper.** CLB's common initiative creatures created a "Turbo Initiative" deck and spread into existing decks. In September 2022 the Pauper panel banned the four four-mana ones: Aarakocra Sneak, Stirring Bard, Underdark Explorer and Vicious Battlerager.

In Commander, the set's influence is broad. Backgrounds gave a new way to build two-commander decks, Minsc & Boo became one of the most popular red-green commanders, and Karlach, Lae'zel and Shadowheart are common picks. As of October 2026, Jeska's Will, reprinted in the precons, is on the official Commander Game Changers list, and no CLB card is banned in Commander. See [Commander brackets explained](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) for how Game Changers affect deck power.

## Cards from Battle for Baldur's Gate worth owning

- **Minsc & Boo, Timeless Heroes**: a powerful planeswalker and a beloved commander.
- **Karlach, Fury of Avernus**: extra combats in any red deck, as a commander or in the 99.
- **Lae'zel, Vlaakith's Champion**: a cheap counters engine.
- **Ancient Copper Dragon**: one of the most explosive Treasure makers in Commander.
- **Black Market Connections**: a flexible card advantage engine for any black deck.
- **Jeska's Will**: a Game Changer that turns a commander into a burst of mana and cards.
- **The cycle of "two or more opponents" duals** (Sea of Clouds, Luxury Suite and friends): untapped dual lands for multiplayer.

Battle for Baldur's Gate is best understood as a Commander toolbox wrapped in a D&D campaign. If you play Commander, start with a Background commander you like and pair it with the Background that fits your plan, then add the multiplayer duals.
