War of the Spark is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2019 that ended a story arc years in the making: the elder dragon Nicol Bolas invades Ravnica, and dozens of planeswalkers fight to stop him. The set put 36 planeswalker cards into one release and a planeswalker in every booster, and several of those walkers went on to be banned or restricted in multiple formats. It's one of the most important story sets Magic has printed, and one of the most influential for Constructed.

## The story

War of the Spark is the payoff for everything Nicol Bolas had been building since Amonkhet. In the sets leading up to it, he quietly took control of Ravnica's guilds by installing planeswalkers who owed him favors as their leaders: Ral Zarek for the Izzet, Vraska for the Golgari, Dovin Baan for the Azorius and others. When Ral, Kaya and Vraska turned against him, their plan fell apart, and Ral activated the Interplanar Beacon as a last resort, a signal that called planeswalkers from across the multiverse to Ravnica.

That was exactly what Bolas wanted. Once the planeswalkers arrived, Bolas switched on the Immortal Sun, an artifact that stops planeswalkers from leaving a plane. Tezzeret opened a bridge to Amonkhet, and Liliana Vess led the Dreadhorde, Bolas's army of undead Eternals, into the city. Then Bolas cast the Elderspell, which let his Eternals tear the sparks out of planeswalkers and feed them to him. Domri Rade, who had sided with Bolas, was the first to die. The planeswalker Dack Fayden was later killed too.

The defenders split into teams. Gideon and the Boros Legion took down the God-Eternal Rhonas. Chandra and the shapeshifter Lazav shut off the Immortal Sun, and many planeswalkers fled while they could. Karn and a small group crossed to Amonkhet and closed the bridge. Nissa animated the Selesnya guildhall, Vitu-Ghazi, to fight the Dreadhorde, and in the end all ten guilds united to restore Niv-Mizzet as Ravnica's Living Guildpact.

The final assault on Bolas's citadel turns on Liliana. Bolas had made her a deal years earlier, and when she finally turns on him, he cancels it and she begins to disintegrate. Gideon uses his magic to take the contract on himself and dies in her place. Liliana frees the God-Eternals, Niv-Mizzet stabs Bolas from behind with a spear from the god Hazoret, and the God-Eternal Bontu tears the stolen sparks out of the dragon. Ugin, Bolas's twin, carries the body to the Meditation Realm and later confirms that Bolas lives on there, powerless and imprisoned forever. Kaya joins the Gatewatch, and Ral, Kaya and Vraska are given missions to hunt down Bolas's remaining allies.

## Mechanics

### Planeswalkers everywhere

Every booster contained a planeswalker, and the set has 36 of them. To make that work, the uncommon and rare walkers break the usual mold. Many have a static ability that is always on, plus only minus loyalty abilities, so they act like a spell that sticks around. **Narset, Parter of Veils** (uncommon) stops opponents from drawing more than one card each turn and can minus to find a noncreature, nonland card. **Kiora, Behemoth Beckoner** (uncommon) draws a card whenever a big creature enters. If you're new to loyalty, our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) covers the basics.

### Amass

Amass N puts N +1/+1 counters on an Army creature you control, and creates a 0/0 Zombie Army token first if you don't have one. Many spells come with an amass bonus, so your Army grows as you cast removal and tricks. **Callous Dismissal** (common) bounces a nonland permanent and amasses 1. Because you only ever have one Army to grow, amass rewards stacking several small effects on the same token.

### Proliferate

Proliferate lets you choose any number of permanents and players and give each another counter of each kind it already has. In a set full of planeswalkers and Armies, that means extra loyalty and bigger tokens. **Grateful Apparition** (uncommon) is a 1/1 flier that proliferates whenever it hits a player or planeswalker. **Karn's Bastion** (rare) is a colorless land that proliferates for four mana.

## Limited

War of the Spark drafted fast and interactively, and the planeswalker in every pack meant you'd always have a few game-changing cards. Each color pair had a clear plan:

- **White-blue: stall and evasion.** Hold the ground and win in the air with Dovin's Veto backup.
- **Blue-black: build up amass.** Grow one big Army; Gleaming Overseer helps it along.
- **Black-red: sacrifice amass.** Mayhem Devil and Angrath's Rampage punish every sacrifice.
- **Red-green: power brutes.** Big creatures and fight effects like Domri's Ambush.
- **Green-white: go-wide proliferate.** Tokens and counters, with Pledge of Unity as a finisher.
- **White-black: small utility creatures.** Cruel Celebrant drains when your creatures die; Despark answers big threats.
- **Blue-red: spells-matter amass.** Instants and sorceries that grow an Army.
- **Black-green: multicolor ramp.** Deathsprout kills a creature and fetches a land.
- **Red-white: aggro tricks.** Combat tricks and fast creatures.
- **Green-blue: go-tall counters.** +1/+1 counters and proliferate on a few large creatures.

Strong commons included Ob Nixilis's Cruelty, Callous Dismissal and Charity Extractor, while the uncommon planeswalkers were often first picks because they could take over a board alone. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

War of the Spark reshaped Standard immediately. In its first months, Esper decks built around **Teferi, Time Raveler**, Esper Control and Esper Hero, were among the most popular in the format. Bant Ramp used mana creatures to cast **Nissa, Who Shakes the World** early, Mono-Red Aggro picked up Chandra, Fire Artisan, and Azorius aggro played Gideon Blackblade. Through 2019 and into 2020, Teferi kept slow decks in check by stopping instant-speed tricks and protection spells, but it also pushed many middle-of-the-road decks out of the format. On August 3, 2020, Wizards banned Teferi, Time Raveler in Standard, along with Wilderness Reclamation, Growth Spiral and Cauldron Familiar, a few weeks before the set rotated out.

The ban list kept growing in other formats. Karn, the Great Creator was restricted in Vintage in 2019, where so much of the format's speed comes from artifacts. Narset, Parter of Veils was restricted in Vintage too, since it shuts down that format's draw-heavy decks. Dreadhorde Arcanist was banned in Legacy in February 2021 because recasting one-mana spells every turn was too strong there. Teferi, Time Raveler was banned in Pioneer in February 2021.

In Pioneer, Karn and Nissa later became the core of **Mono-Green Devotion**, a ramp deck that used Karn to find the right artifact for every situation and to set up combos. Wizards decided the deck had warped the format for too long and banned Karn, the Great Creator in Pioneer on December 4, 2023. As of October 2026, Nissa remains legal there, and Teferi and Narset have long been played in Modern's blue control decks. For more on why cards like these get banned, see our explainer on the [ban list](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

In Commander, Finale of Devastation, Spark Double, Liliana, Dreadhorde General, Ugin, the Ineffable and Krenko, Tin Street Kingpin are all widely played. Karn's Bastion and Blast Zone are popular utility lands too. Before you sit down with Narset or Teferi, it's worth checking where they sit in your group's [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained), since both shut down other players' plans.

## Cards from War of the Spark worth owning

- **Teferi, Time Raveler**: a long-time Modern control card, even though it's banned in Pioneer.
- **Narset, Parter of Veils**: a cheap hate piece against draw-heavy decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Nissa, Who Shakes the World**: the mana engine for Pioneer green devotion decks.
- **Finale of Devastation**: a creature tutor that can also end a Commander game.
- **Spark Double**: copies your best creature or planeswalker in Commander.
- **Liliana, Dreadhorde General**: a game-winning six-drop and a favorite in black Commander decks.
- **Karn's Bastion**: a land that proliferates, good in counters and superfriends decks.

War of the Spark is the set to pick up if you like planeswalkers. Its walkers changed what a planeswalker card could be, and many of them still matter years later.
