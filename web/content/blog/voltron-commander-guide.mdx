## Short answer

A voltron deck needs enough auras or equipment that the creature is lethal in one or two attacks, and enough extra creatures or protection that one removal spell does not end your game. In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), voltron means one creature wearing the rest of the deck. The math is harsh: every aura in your hand does nothing until a creature is on the battlefield, and every aura on a creature that dies goes to the graveyard with it. You do not need twenty auras if six of them already kill. You need the creature to stay in play, or another creature to wear the next wave.

Orzhov Auras, the 2021 Pioneer Challenger product, is the 60-card version. Thirty-one nonlands cost one mana. The curve is the strategy: the creature and the auras have to come down immediately. The goldfish average win is turn 8.09, slower than burn, because auras spend cards to make one body larger instead of adding more attackers. Lurrus of the Dream-Den is in the printed list and is not legal in Pioneer on the Scryfall bulk dated 2026-11-01. The companion was the card that made dying auras easy to replay. Without it, the printed deck is a historical object, not a registration sheet. Thoughtseize and Sram are the other price centers, inside a $46.84 total.

Virtue and Valor, Ellivere of the Wild Court, is the Commander version at $127.24. Bear Umbra, Retether, and Hall of Heliod's Generosity are the top nonbasics, 30.1% of the product. Retether and the Hall are cards that get auras back. That is the Commander answer to the same problem Lurrus used to solve in Pioneer: auras in the graveyard are not on a creature. The [tempo page](/blog/mtg-tempo-decks-explained) is the small-creature comparison when you would rather have many bodies than one enchanted one.

## When to choose this

| Situation | Lean in | Walk away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60-card auras | 31 one-drops, turn 8.09, and a banned companion | Do not register Lurrus |
| Commander auras | Ellivere, with Retether and the Hall as the recursion | $127.24, 30.1% in three cards |
| One removal spell ends your turn | You need a second creature or a way to replay auras | A single creature and a hand of enchantments is a mulligan |
| You want many attackers | Voltron is the wrong shape | Tokens or a normal aggro curve |

### Orzhov Auras

Orzhov Auras is an official Pioneer Challenger Deck released 2021-10-15. The decklist and the Wizards source link come from MTGJSON file OrzhovAuras_Q06. Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/news/pioneer-challenger-decks-2021-08-24.

Read this as a deck that bets the game on one creature wearing several enchantments. The curve is full of one-mana auras because the creature has to attack early and often.

The nonland mana curve is 2 at mana value 0, 31 at mana value 1, 8 at mana value 2. The list runs 19 lands in the library.

Colored sources, using Scryfall produced_mana and counting basic lands as the color on their type line, restricted to this deck's colors: 15 white, 12 black.

MTGJSON AllPricesToday (2026-11-01) prices the exact printing in the product at $46.84 for main deck, sideboard, and commander, using TCGplayer nonfoil retail when it exists. The three most expensive nonbasic names are 4 Sram, Senior Edificer at $7.84 total, 1 Thoughtseize at $6.63 total, 4 Concealed Courtyard at $5.64 total, which is 42.9% of the priced total. Basic lands in the product add $2.45 on their own, because the price is the printing Wizards put in the deck, not the cheapest Plains on the market.

Across 120 goldfish games (seed 1729, aggro policy, opponent at 20 life) it won 93.3% and the average winning game ended on turn 8.09 (median 8.0). There is no opponent interaction in that number. A goldfish clock is a ceiling on speed, not a match win rate.

Those stand-ins do not do the same job. The gap is how much you are paying for the specific card, not a sleeve-in recommendation.

Scryfall's oracle-cards bulk dated 2026-11-01 marks Lurrus of the Dream-Den as not legal in pioneer. The product was printed before that change. Copying the list into a current tournament deck without checking the ban list is how a legal product becomes an illegal deck.

## Common mistakes

- Registering the Pioneer auras product as printed. Lurrus is not legal. The goldfish was run on the printed list anyway so you can see the curve, not so you can copy it.
- Drawing seven auras and no creature and keeping the hand. The one-drop count is high so this happens less often. It still happens, and the hand does nothing.
- Spending Thoughtseize on a random card and then losing the creature you needed the life total to race with. Thoughtseize is $6.63 of a $46.84 product. It has to take the removal spell.
- Buying Ellivere's expensive recursion and not playing enough auras to recur. Retether is a blank if the graveyard is empty.
- Equating auras and equipment without checking what happens when the creature dies. Some equipment stays. Auras do not. The Pioneer list is auras.

## FAQ

### How many auras is enough?

Enough that one creature becomes lethal, which on these curves means a lot of one-mana auras in the 60-card list (31 one-drops, not all of them auras) and a Commander list that can get the auras back. Count the auras that actually grant evasion or power, not every enchantment.

### Can I play Lurrus in Pioneer?

No. The 2026-11-01 Scryfall oracle-cards bulk lists Lurrus of the Dream-Den as not legal in Pioneer. The 2021 product included it as a companion.

### Why is the Commander aura deck almost three times the price?

Virtue and Valor is $127.24 against the Pioneer product's $46.84. Bear Umbra alone is $14.51, and the recursion pieces are the rest of the top three. Different formats, different expensive cards.

### Is voltron the same as tempo?

Tempo uses several small creatures and disruption. Voltron uses one creature and a stack of auras. Spirits goldfishes faster than the auras product because the creatures are the threats, not the costumes.
