A Voltron deck in Commander wins by making one creature, usually your commander, enormous and hard to answer, then attacking until opponents fall to 21 commander damage. You stack Equipment and Auras on that creature, give it evasion and protection, and turn it into a threat that can take out a player in a few hits. The name comes from an old cartoon robot assembled from smaller pieces, which is exactly what you're doing to your commander.

This guide covers how Voltron wins, which kinds of cards it runs, how to keep your commander alive, and the weaknesses you need to plan around.

## How Voltron wins

Commander has a special loss condition: a player who has been dealt 21 or more combat damage by the same commander over the course of the game loses, no matter what their life total is. That's the rule Voltron exploits.

Most Commander games start each player at 40 life. Dealing 40 damage to each of three opponents is a lot of work. Dealing 21 damage with one creature is far less. A commander with ten or more power can take a player out in two or three swings, and double strike or extra combat effects shorten that even further.

If commander damage, the command zone or the commander tax are unfamiliar, [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains them. The short version: your commander starts in the command zone, you can cast it from there, and each time you cast it again it costs `{2}` more.

### Why commander damage matters so much

Commander damage is tracked separately from life. A player at 50 life who has taken 21 commander damage from your general is out of the game. That means lifegain, which is normally a good defense against aggressive decks, does very little against Voltron. It's also why many Voltron decks focus on one opponent at a time: damage only matters if it's piling up on the same player.

## Choosing a Voltron commander

Not every commander is a good Voltron target. The best ones share a few traits:

- **They're cheap enough to cast early.** You want to start attacking before opponents are set up, and a cheaper commander is easier to recast after removal.
- **They have built-in protection or evasion.** Hexproof, flying or a way to dodge blockers saves deck slots.
- **They reward you for suiting up.** Some commanders get bigger or draw cards for each Aura or Equipment.

Some long-standing examples:

- **Uril, the Miststalker** has hexproof and gets +2/+2 for each Aura attached to it, making Aura-heavy builds natural.
- **Rafiq of the Many** gives a creature that attacks alone double strike, which doubles your commander damage per hit.
- **Sram, Senior Edificer** draws you a card whenever you cast an Aura, Equipment or Vehicle spell, which keeps your hand full.
- **Sigarda, Host of Herons** has flying and hexproof, and stops opponents' effects from making you sacrifice permanents.

## Equipment versus Auras

Voltron decks lean on two kinds of cards to grow their commander, and each has strengths and drawbacks.

### Equipment

Equipment is an artifact that you attach to a creature you control by paying its equip cost. The key advantage is that Equipment stays on the battlefield if the creature dies. You can move it to a new creature, or reattach it when your commander comes back.

Long-standing examples:

- **Lightning Greaves** gives haste and shroud with an equip cost of `{0}`, and you may attach it to a creature as that creature enters under your control, without paying the equip cost. It lets a freshly cast commander attack immediately while being protected from targeted removal.
- **Swiftfoot Boots** gives hexproof and haste for an equip cost of `{1}`. Hexproof is often better than shroud because you can still target your own creature with Auras and pump spells.
- **Sword of Fire and Ice** grants +2/+2 and protection from red and from blue, and deals damage and draws a card whenever the equipped creature deals combat damage to a player.
- **Darksteel Plate** makes the equipped creature indestructible.
- **Colossus Hammer** gives +10/+10, which can make even a small commander lethal, but its equip cost is huge, so decks that run it usually have ways to cheat on that cost.

The drawback of Equipment is mana. Casting the Equipment and then paying to equip it costs two separate payments, which is slow.

### Auras

An Aura is an enchantment you cast directly onto a creature. Auras are usually cheaper and more efficient than Equipment: one payment, and the bonus is immediate.

Long-standing examples:

- **Rancor** gives +2/+0 and trample, and returns to your hand if it goes to the graveyard from the battlefield.
- **Ethereal Armor** gives +1/+1 for each enchantment you control and first strike, for a single white mana.

The drawback is that Auras are fragile. If the enchanted creature leaves the battlefield, the Auras attached to it go to the graveyard. One removal spell or bounce effect can wipe out several cards' worth of investment, which is called a two-for-one or worse. Aura-heavy Voltron decks rely heavily on protecting the commander for that reason.

### Finding your pieces

Most Voltron decks also run tutors that find Equipment or Auras. Steelshaper's Gift, for example, searches your library for an Equipment card. Cards like these make the deck more consistent, since you'll often want one specific piece, like protection, at a specific time.

## Protecting your commander

Voltron puts a lot of eggs in one basket. Protecting that basket is the most important part of building the deck.

### Kinds of protection

- **Hexproof and shroud.** Stops targeted removal like Swords to Plowshares. Swiftfoot Boots and Lightning Greaves are the usual sources.
- **Indestructible.** Stops "destroy" effects, including many board wipes like Wrath of God.
- **Protection from colors.** Stops damage, enchanting, blocking and targeting from sources of those colors.
- **Instant-speed protection.** Spells like Heroic Intervention, which gives your permanents hexproof and indestructible until end of turn, let you respond to removal or a board wipe.
- **Creatures that protect.** Mother of Runes can give a creature you control protection from a color of your choice every turn.

A good Voltron deck runs several layers of protection, because no single kind stops everything.

## Voltron's weaknesses

Every Voltron player should know how the deck loses, because opponents will try these lines.

### Sacrifice effects

Edicts are cards that make a player sacrifice a creature, like Diabolic Edict. Hexproof and indestructible don't stop them, since nothing targets your creature and nothing is destroyed. If your commander is your only creature, you lose it. The fix is to keep a few other creatures on the battlefield, or play a commander like Sigarda that stops the effect.

### Exile and bounce

Removal that exiles or returns a creature to its owner's hand gets around indestructible. Bouncing an Aura-covered commander is especially brutal, since all the Auras go to the graveyard.

### Board wipes

Mass removal is a constant threat. Indestructible helps against "destroy all" effects but not against effects that exile, return to hand or shrink creatures. Instant-speed protection is your best defense.

### Chump blockers and fog effects

Even a giant commander can be blocked by a single 1/1 token each turn. Trample, flying and unblockability matter a lot for this reason. Effects that prevent all combat damage for a turn can also delay you.

### Being the obvious threat

At a multiplayer table, a huge commander painted with Equipment is easy to spot. Opponents will work together to remove it. Learning how opponents judge threats, which [commander threat assessment](/blog/commander-threat-assessment) covers, helps you know when to attack and when to stay quiet.

### All-in risk

When your commander is your entire plan, losing it repeatedly is a problem. Each time you recast it, the tax goes up by `{2}`. A Voltron deck that can't recover from its commander being exiled twice tends to stall out.

## Building a Voltron deck

A rough outline of priorities:

1. **Choose a commander** that's cheap, evasive or self-protecting.
2. **Include protection first.** Several hexproof, indestructible and instant-speed protection effects.
3. **Add evasion.** Trample, flying, unblockability or double strike.
4. **Add damage boosts.** Equipment and Auras that add power.
5. **Keep some backup creatures.** They protect against edicts and give you a plan B.
6. **Keep ramp and card draw.** Voltron still needs to cast its pieces on time.

For the general structure of land counts and categories, [how to build your first Commander deck](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) is a good template, and the [Deck Lab builder](/) can put together a first draft around the commander you pick.

### Table etiquette

Voltron can feel punishing to opponents if it kills a player early, so talk about power level before the game. The [Commander brackets guide](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) explains the official framework for matching decks at similar levels.

## The takeaway

Voltron wins by pouring Equipment and Auras into one commander and dealing 21 commander damage, which ignores life totals. Pick a commander that's cheap or self-protecting, protect it in several different ways, give it evasion, and keep backup creatures for sacrifice effects. Plan for exile, bounce and board wipes, and you'll have a deck that can take over a table fast.
