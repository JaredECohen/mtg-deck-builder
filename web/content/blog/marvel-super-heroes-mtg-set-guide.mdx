Marvel Super Heroes is the Universes Beyond expansion that brought the Avengers and their rivals to Magic: The Gathering, released in June 2026 as the follow-up to Marvel's Spider-Man. It's built around a Hero-versus-Villain split, with heroes pooling their strength through **teamwork** and **power-up** while villains scheme with **Plan** enchantments.

## The story

Unlike a Magic-original set, Marvel Super Heroes doesn't tell one new story. It works more like a greatest-hits tour of decades of Avengers comics. The main cast is the Avengers and the circle around them: the Fantastic Four, S.H.I.E.L.D., Wakanda, street-level heroes like Daredevil and Misty Knight, and younger teams such as the Young Avengers. The X-Men as a team sit this one out, though individual mutants tied to the Avengers, such as Quicksilver, The Scarlet Witch, Storm and Wolverine, still appear.

You can read the set's story beats straight off its card names. Origin of the Avengers is the team's founding. Armor Wars, World War Hulk, Secret Invasion, The Super Hero Civil War and Avengers Disassembled each name a famous comic event, and many of them are Sagas, so the event plays out over a few turns the way it did over a few issues. The Coming of Galactus is a four-chapter Saga that ends with a 16/16 Galactus token arriving to eat the world, a fitting payoff for one of Marvel's oldest cosmic threats.

The villains get just as much attention. Doctor Doom is the set's centerpiece antagonist: his card arrives with two Doombot tokens, his castle is a land (Castle Doom), and his scheme gets its own Plan enchantment, Doom Reigns Supreme. Kang the Conqueror bends time with an extra turn, Ultron copies your artifacts, Loki and Taskmaster deal in trickery and imitation, and Thanos shows up with a one-shot ability that wipes out half the board by choosing odd or even mana values, a neat nod to his snap.

The set also continues a thread that runs across Magic's Marvel releases: the Infinity Stones. Marvel's Spider-Man introduced The Soul Stone, and Marvel Super Heroes adds The Mind Stone, an artifact you can "harness" to switch on a powerful end-step ability. Wizards has framed the stones as a collection spread across multiple Marvel products, so this set is one chapter of that hunt rather than its conclusion.

## Mechanics

### Power-up

Power-up is an activated ability you can use only once per creature, and it usually adds +1/+1 counters plus a bonus. The twist is the discount: on the turn the creature enters, the power-up costs less by an amount equal to the creature's mana cost. Human Torch, Johnny Storm has a power-up that normally costs `{6}{R}`, but if you activate it the turn he comes down, you pay only `{4}`. That creates a real choice every game: cast the creature late and power it up immediately, or play it on curve and save the big activation for later. Kang the Conqueror's power-up is the most dramatic, granting an extra turn.

### Teamwork

Teamwork appears on instants and sorceries. "Teamwork N" is an optional extra cost: you tap any number of your untapped creatures whose total power is N or more, and the spell gets better. HULK SMASH! normally makes you pick one of two modes (destroy a noncreature artifact, or have your creature deal damage equal to its power to another creature), but with teamwork you get both. We Say Thee Nay! is a soft counterspell that becomes much harder to pay for when cast with teamwork. Since tapping creatures costs you attackers or blockers, teamwork rewards casting these spells at the right moment, often after combat or on your opponent's turn.

### Plan enchantments

Plan is a new enchantment subtype tied to villains. Each Plan says what advances it, such as a Villain entering under your control. Every step gives a small reward and adds a plan counter, and once you hit the threshold, the Plan sacrifices itself for a big finale. Doom Reigns Supreme drains for 1 each time a Villain enters, and on the fifth counter you flip over the top five cards of an opponent's library and cast up to two of them for free. Plans give villain decks a clock that pays off if left alone.

### Connive and double-faced Heroes

Connive returns: draw a card, then discard one, and if you discarded a nonland card, the conniving creature gets a +1/+1 counter. It powers the blue-black Villain deck through cards like Kang, Temporal Tyrant, which connives whenever he attacks.

Double-faced cards also return from Marvel's Spider-Man, now with a new trick: these cards can transform when an ability tells them to. Bruce Banner, Jennifer Walters, Tony Stark and King T'Challa each start as a cheaper human form and pay a hefty sorcery-speed cost to flip into their super-powered side.

### Smaller set pieces

Mjölnir, Hammer of Thor introduces "worthy": only a legendary, non-Villain creature that's red and/or white can use its cheap equip cost. The Mind Stone introduces harnessing for Infinity Stones. Elektra, Daughter of the Hand also brings the sneak ability, letting her enter tapped and attacking by returning an unblocked attacker to your hand.

## Limited

Marvel Super Heroes drafts as a two-color format with ten archetypes, each with a pair of signpost uncommons:

- **White-blue, teamwork:** Captain America, Living Legend untaps your creatures the first time they're tapped on your turn, which makes teamwork costs nearly free. Agent Maria Hill draws a card whenever she's tapped for teamwork.
- **Blue-black, connive:** Kang, Temporal Tyrant and Leader-style card filtering.
- **Black-red, Villains:** Madame Hydra makes a 2/1 menace token whenever you cast a Villain.
- **Red-green, power-up:** Hulk, Gamma Goliath cuts the cost of your other power-ups by `{3}`.
- **Green-white, Heroes:** Black Panther, Vanguard rewards you for every nontoken Hero you play.
- **White-black, attacking alone:** Agent 13, Sharon Carter investigates when a creature attacks by itself.
- **Blue-red, artifacts:** Iron Man, Master of Machines grows with your artifact count.
- **Black-green, creatures in the graveyard.**
- **Red-white, noncreature spells:** Thor Odinson has prowess twice.
- **Green-blue, +1/+1 counters.**

The consensus from drafters was that white and blue were the deepest colors, and white-blue teamwork was the archetype to be in when it was open. Hero in Training, Web Up and Take Up the Shield made white's commons strong, while blue had Frozen in Ice and We Say Thee Nay! The format leaned tempo-heavy: creatures were mostly ordinary, a handful of legendary heroes and villains decided games, and removal like Web Up and Super Villain Lockup mattered a lot. Several reviewers also felt the colors weren't evenly balanced, so reading which colors are open (see [drafting signals](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals)) paid off.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

As of October 2026, Marvel Super Heroes has had about four months in Standard, so its place is still settling, but some patterns showed up early. In Wizards' first metagame review after release (early July 2026), the most-played cards from the set were:

- **Namor the Sub-Mariner**, in Izzet spell decks (both prowess and Elemental-based builds). Cheap blue instants turn into Merfolk tokens, and Namor himself grows with them.
- **Jennifer Walters**, in green-white aggro. Stopping opponents from casting spells on your turn shuts off combat tricks and instant-speed removal when you attack.
- **Avengers Disassembled**, mostly from sideboards, as a sweeper against wide boards that can also hit a creature land.
- **Hidden Lair and M.O.D.O.K.** in Dimir midrange, **Castle Doom and Iron Man Armor** in artifact decks, and **Shang-Chi, Master of Kung Fu** in green creature-ability decks.

Later in the summer, Izzet spell decks were still described as among the most consistent options in the format, and Namor was a key reason. Players also experimented with The Mind Stone in blink decks, Doctor Doom and The Coming of Galactus in black-green midrange, and teamwork aggro built on Agent Maria Hill. Treat those as brews rather than proven archetypes. As of October 2026, no Marvel Super Heroes card is banned in Standard.

Outside Standard, the set's biggest home is Commander, where almost every legendary hero or villain doubles as a commander. Thanos, Doctor Doom, Kang the Conqueror and The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl all lead obvious decks. The set is also legal in Pioneer, Modern and Legacy since it's a Standard-legal premier set.

## Cards from Marvel Super Heroes worth owning

- **Namor the Sub-Mariner:** the set's clearest Standard staple, and a strong token engine in any blue spells deck.
- **Jennifer Walters // The Sensational She-Hulk:** silencing opponents on your turn is valuable in every aggressive deck.
- **The rare dual lands:** five easy two-color lands that work in any deck with basics.
- **Doctor Doom:** card advantage plus two bodies, and a natural Commander leader.
- **The Mind Stone:** a cheap indestructible mana rock that turns into a repeatable blink engine.
- **Thanos, the Mad Titan:** a fun Commander card with a one-sided board wipe built in.
- **Captain America's Shield:** an indestructible Equipment that taps a blocker every attack.

If you're opening Marvel Super Heroes for Standard, start with Namor or Jennifer Walters and build around them, and keep a copy of Avengers Disassembled for your sideboard.
