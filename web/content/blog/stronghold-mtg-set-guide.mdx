Stronghold is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in March 1998 as the second set of the Tempest block. It takes the crew of the skyship *Weatherlight* inside the fortress of Volrath, the shapeshifting ruler of the Phyrexian plane Rath, to rescue their captured captain. The set brought back buyback, shadow, Slivers and Spikes, introduced Mana Leak and Wall of Blossoms, and printed Reserved List cards like Mox Diamond, Dream Halls and Sliver Queen.

## The story

Stronghold is the second chapter of the Tempest block's part of the Weatherlight Saga, the multi-year storyline that ends with the Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria. In the first set, Tempest, the *Weatherlight* followed its kidnapped captain, Sisay, to the plane of Rath. The ship was ambushed by Volrath's warship *Predator*; Tahngarth, the golem Karn and the Legacy artifacts were captured; and Gerrard Capashen, the ship's reluctant leader, fell into the Skyshroud forest. Gerrard survived, allied with the elves of Skyshroud and their leader Eladamri, and regrouped with the crew.

Stronghold is the rescue. Gerrard, the cat warrior Mirri, the Urborgan noble Crovax and Starke il-Vec, a schemer who knew the fortress from the inside, infiltrate Volrath's Stronghold, a living fortress built of flowstone that Volrath can reshape at will. Volrath's minions pick off the party one by one as they go deeper.

The set's darkest story belongs to Crovax. Years before, he had shattered the artifact that bound the angel Selenia to his family, which cursed him to destroy whatever he loved. In the dungeons he finally meets Selenia, now serving Volrath. She attacks the party and badly wounds Mirri, and Crovax strikes her down. Her shattering transforms him into a vampire, the card Crovax the Cursed, and Mirri doesn't survive the Stronghold.

At the heart of the fortress, Gerrard learns who Volrath really is: Vuel, the son of the warlord who adopted Gerrard as a child. Vuel was cast out of his clan after a failed rite of passage, blamed Gerrard for it, and later gave himself to Phyrexia to become the Evincar of Rath. Gerrard frees Sisay and Starke's daughter Takara and kills "Volrath," but it's only a shapeshifter double. The real Volrath slips aboard the *Weatherlight* in Takara's form. As the ship escapes through a portal, the young mage Ertai, who had been holding the portal open, is left behind on Rath. Both threads come back later: Volrath in the Mercadian Masques story, and Ertai as the Phyrexian Ertai, the Corrupted in Planeshift.

## Mechanics

### Buyback

Buyback, returning from Tempest, is an optional extra cost. If you pay it, the spell goes back to your hand as it resolves instead of the graveyard. Fanning the Flames deals X damage for `{X}{R}{R}`, and paying 3 more lets you cast it again next turn. Constant Mists prevents all combat damage and can be bought back by sacrificing a land, which made it a famous stalling tool. Buyback turns ordinary spells into repeatable engines in long games.

### Shadow

A creature with shadow can only block or be blocked by other creatures with shadow, so it's effectively unblockable against most decks. Soltari Champion is a 2/2 shadow creature that pumps your other creatures when it attacks. Dauthi Trapper can even give shadow to another creature for a turn.

### Slivers, Spikes and Licids

Slivers, introduced in Tempest, share their abilities with every Sliver on the battlefield, including opponents' Slivers in this era. Stronghold added two-color Slivers like Crystalline Sliver (shroud for all Slivers) and Spined Sliver, and the five-color Sliver Queen, which makes 1/1 Sliver tokens for `{2}` each.

Spikes enter with +1/+1 counters and can move them to other creatures. Spike Feeder can also remove a counter to gain 2 life, which made it a Constructed staple. Licids are creatures that can turn themselves into Auras: Tempting Licid becomes an Aura that forces creatures to block the enchanted creature.

### Flowstone and en-Kor

Flowstone creatures like Flowstone Mauler pump power at the cost of toughness, reflecting Volrath's shape-shifting fortress. The en-Kor creatures, such as Warrior en-Kor, can redirect damage dealt to them onto another creature you control, which combos well with Spikes. Our guide to [reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) is a good refresher on activated abilities like these.

## Limited

Limited in 1998 was far less studied than it is today. Tempest block was drafted and played as Sealed, but there's no reliable record of a list of "Stronghold archetypes" the way modern sets have, and it would be inventing history to give one. What players did know was that the set rewarded a few things:

- **Shadow aggression.** White and black shadow creatures were hard to block and won races.
- **Buyback card advantage.** Fanning the Flames and Mind Peel could take over long games.
- **Removal.** Death Stroke, Smite, Stronghold Assassin and Fanning the Flames were the cards you wanted to take early.

If you're coming to old sets from modern Limited, our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) covers the general principles that applied then as now.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (April 1998 to October 1999)

Stronghold joined a Standard (still often called Type II at the time) with Fifth Edition, Mirage and Visions, Weatherlight and Tempest. Its biggest Constructed contribution was **Dream Halls**. Zvi Mowshowitz built "TurboZvi," a blue combo deck that used Dream Halls to cast huge spells for free by discarding cards, and Dream Halls combo decks were a real part of that 1998 Standard metagame.

As Urza's Saga arrived later that year, Standard entered the period remembered as "Combo Winter." Wizards issued a wave of bans, and on April 1, 1999, Dream Halls was banned in Standard along with Earthcraft, Fluctuator, Lotus Petal, Recurring Nightmare, Time Spiral and Memory Jar. Other Stronghold cards saw steady play through the period, including Shard Phoenix in the blue-red "Counter Phoenix" deck, and Volrath's Stronghold, Spike Feeder, Wall of Blossoms, Mana Leak and Mox Diamond. The set left Standard in November 1999 when Mercadian Masques arrived and the Tempest block rotated out. Our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) covers how and why cards get banned.

### Legacy, Vintage, Modern and Commander (as of October 2026)

Stronghold predates Modern, but several of its cards have been reprinted in Modern-legal sets, most notably Mana Leak and Ensnaring Bridge, which became a key piece of Modern's Lantern Control deck. In older formats:

- **Mox Diamond** is a Legacy staple in decks built around lands, such as Lands and other land-heavy strategies.
- **Hermit Druid** is banned in Legacy.
- **Burgeoning** and Ensnaring Bridge show up in fringe Legacy builds.
- **Commander** players prize Grave Pact, Sliver Queen (a popular Sliver commander), Volrath's Stronghold, Wall of Blossoms and Evacuation.

Because Mox Diamond, Dream Halls, Sliver Queen and Volrath's Stronghold are on the Reserved List, they will never be reprinted in a tournament-legal form. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains where these cards fit today.

## Cards from Stronghold worth owning

- **Mox Diamond**: free acceleration for Legacy decks with lots of lands.
- **Grave Pact**: a defining black enchantment in Commander.
- **Sliver Queen**: the classic five-color Sliver commander.
- **Volrath's Stronghold**: repeatable creature recursion on a land.
- **Ensnaring Bridge**: a prison card that still matters in Modern and Legacy.
- **Wall of Blossoms**: a cheap cantrip blocker for Commander.
