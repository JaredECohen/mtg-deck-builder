New Phyrexia is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2011 that ended the Scars of Mirrodin block with the villains winning. The metal plane of Mirrodin falls to the Phyrexians and is renamed, and the set shows that victory through Phyrexian mana, five terrifying praetors and some of the most format-warping cards of its era, including Karn Liberated, Batterskull and Birthing Pod.

## The story

The Scars of Mirrodin block told the story of an invasion from the inside. Mirrodin was an artificial metal world built by the golem planeswalker Karn, but Karn had unknowingly carried a trace of Phyrexian oil there. Over centuries that oil spread through the plane's core and grew a new Phyrexia. Scars of Mirrodin showed the Mirran natives realizing what was happening, Mirrodin Besieged showed open war between the Mirran resistance and the Phyrexians, and New Phyrexia shows the outcome. Wizards even kept the name secret until late in development, teasing that the third set would be either "Mirrodin Pure" or "New Phyrexia."

Phyrexia won. The set's cards are watermarked with either the Mirran or the Phyrexian symbol, and this time the split heavily favors the Phyrexians. Familiar Mirran creatures show up "compleated," their flesh replaced with metal and sinew. Phyrexia itself is ruled by five **praetors**, one per color, each leading a faction with its own vision of perfection: Elesh Norn's white orthodoxy, Jin-Gitaxias's blue pursuit of progress, Sheoldred's black cult of death, Urabrask's quiet red rebels and Vorinclex's green predators. They agree on almost nothing except that Karn should sit on their throne as a puppet "Father of Machines."

Three planeswalkers drive the plot. Elspeth Tirel, Koth of the Hammer and the Dominarian artificer Venser fight their way down into the plane's core to find Karn, who has been corrupted by the oil. Along the way they free Melira, a Sylvok woman born without any metal in her body who can heal the Phyrexian infection. Melira cleanses Karn's body, but his heart is already lost. Venser, already dying, gives his life and his planeswalker spark to Karn, which frees the golem from Phyrexian control. Karn, free again, turns to undoing what he unwittingly created, while the surviving Mirrans fight on as a hidden resistance.

That ending set up more than a decade of story. Karn's guilt over New Phyrexia runs through Dominaria, and the Phyrexians returned as the main villains in Phyrexia: All Will Be One and March of the Machine.

## Mechanics

### Phyrexian mana (new)

A Phyrexian mana symbol, written like `{B/P}`, can be paid with one mana of its color or with 2 life. That means any deck can cast these spells, at a cost. **Dismember** (uncommon, `{1}{B/P}{B/P}`) gives a creature -5/-5, and a red or blue deck can cast it for one mana by paying 4 life. **Gitaxian Probe** (common) was effectively a free card: pay 2 life, look at an opponent's hand and draw a card. The mechanic was so flexible that several of these cards were later banned, because they let decks run "colorless" spells without the usual mana restrictions.

### Infect

Returning from Scars of Mirrodin, a creature with infect deals damage to creatures as -1/-1 counters and to players as poison counters. Ten poison counters and you lose. **Glistener Elf** (common, one mana) and **Blighted Agent** (common, unblockable) are the poster children, and both became Constructed staples. New Phyrexia also spread infect into white with cards like **Shriek Raptor**.

### Proliferate

To proliferate, you choose any number of permanents and players and add one more of each kind of counter already there. That works with poison counters, +1/+1 counters and loyalty counters alike. **Tezzeret's Gambit** (uncommon) draws two cards and proliferates, and **Volt Charge** (common) deals 3 damage and proliferates.

### Living weapon

An Equipment with living weapon creates a 0/0 Germ token as it enters and attaches itself to it, so it's a creature and an Equipment in one card. **Batterskull** (mythic) is the most famous: it arrives as a 4/4 with vigilance and lifelink, and you can pay `{3}` to return it to your hand if it's in danger.

### Metalcraft and Splicers

Metalcraft rewards you for controlling three or more artifacts. **Puresteel Paladin** (rare) draws a card whenever an Equipment enters and, with metalcraft, makes every equip cost zero. The Splicers are a smaller theme: creatures like **Blade Splicer** (rare) come with a 3/3 Golem token and grant Golems an ability, first strike in its case.

### Chancellors

The rare Chancellor cycle can be revealed from your opening hand for a small bonus on the first turn. **Chancellor of the Annex** taxes an opponent's first spell, and **Chancellor of the Tangle** gives you an extra green mana on turn one.

## Limited

New Phyrexia was drafted with the earlier sets of the block, New Phyrexia first, followed by Mirrodin Besieged and Scars of Mirrodin. It was a fast, aggressive format, because Phyrexian mana made many cards castable in any color and life totals dropped quickly from both combat and self-inflicted payments.

The main strategies were:

- **Infect.** Poison counters only need to reach ten, so a dedicated infect deck could win quickly, especially with pump spells like **Mutagenic Growth** and **Triumph of the Hordes**. Splitting your plan between poison and regular damage was the classic mistake.
- **Artifact aggro and metalcraft.** White and red artifacts, cheap Equipment and Phyrexian mana creatures such as **Porcelain Legionnaire**, which many drafters rated the best common in the set.
- **Splicers and Golems.** The Splicer creatures give you two bodies for one card and turn every Golem into a real threat.
- **Removal-heavy midrange.** **Dismember**, **Geth's Verdict**, **Volt Charge** and **Vapor Snag** were all strong picks at common and uncommon, and with Phyrexian mana you could often splash them without the matching color.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2011-2012)

New Phyrexia arrived into one of the most dominant Standard decks ever: **Caw-Blade**, a white-blue deck built on Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Stoneforge Mystic and Squadron Hawk. New Phyrexia handed it two more weapons, Batterskull and Sword of War and Peace, which Stoneforge Mystic could fetch and put onto the battlefield early. On June 20, 2011, Wizards banned Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic in Standard, the first Standard bans in six years. No New Phyrexia card was banned, though Batterskull was part of why the deck was so hard to beat.

After the ban, the format opened up. Caw-Blade variants carried on without their best cards, Tempered Steel artifact aggro used Vault Skirge, Splinter Twin combo and Birthing Pod decks rose, and Valakut ramp became one of the most popular decks. Through the rest of its Standard life, Dismember and Gitaxian Probe showed up across many archetypes because nearly any deck could cast them. The set left Standard in October 2012. For more on how that cycle works, see our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### Modern, Legacy and Vintage

New Phyrexia's impact grew in non-rotating formats, and several of its cards ran into bans:

- **Mental Misstep** was on Modern's original banned list in August 2011 and was banned in Legacy in September 2011, after it made one-mana spells far worse across the format. It has been restricted in Vintage since August 2019.
- **Birthing Pod** powered the Kiki Pod and Melira Pod combo decks in Modern until it was banned in January 2015. Melira, Sylvok Outcast was a combo piece in that deck.
- **Gitaxian Probe** was banned in Modern in January 2017, restricted in Vintage in April 2017, banned in Legacy in July 2018 and banned in Pauper in May 2019.

Plenty of cards remained legal and became fixtures. **Karn Liberated** is the signature threat of Modern Tron, which assembles the three Urza lands to cast it as early as turn three. Modern Infect used Glistener Elf, Blighted Agent and Mutagenic Growth for years. Dismember became one of Modern's most played removal spells, and Surgical Extraction and Spellskite were sideboard regulars. In Legacy, Batterskull is the standard target for Stoneforge Mystic, and Modern Stoneforge decks picked it back up after Mystic was unbanned there in 2019. Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite became a top reanimation target in Legacy and Modern.

As of October 2026, Karn Liberated, Batterskull, Dismember and Surgical Extraction are the set's most enduring Constructed cards, and Mental Misstep remains a Vintage restriction. Our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) explains where these decks fit.

### Commander and Pauper

In Commander, the praetors are some of the most famous creatures in the format. Elesh Norn and Jin-Gitaxias are powerful enough that they shape whole tables, so check how they fit your group's [Commander bracket](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). Beast Within, Phyrexian Metamorph, Sword of War and Peace and Caged Sun also see wide play. In Pauper, commons like Mutagenic Growth and Vapor Snag have been played in competitive Stompy and Delver decks.

## Cards from New Phyrexia worth owning

- **Karn Liberated**: the centerpiece of Modern Tron and a strong colorless card in Commander.
- **Batterskull**: a long-time Legacy and Modern Stoneforge package card.
- **Dismember**: removal that any Modern deck can cast.
- **Surgical Extraction**: a flexible graveyard-hate sideboard card.
- **Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite**: a game-ending reanimation target and Commander bomb.
- **Beast Within**: answers anything in green Commander decks.
- **Phyrexian Metamorph**: a clone that fits in any Commander deck because of Phyrexian mana.

New Phyrexia is the set where the villains won, and its cards still feel that way. Phyrexian mana showed how dangerous it is to let any deck cast any spell, and the survivors of the ban list, from Karn Liberated to Dismember, remain staples.
