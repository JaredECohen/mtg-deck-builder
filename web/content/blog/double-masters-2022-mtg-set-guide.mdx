Double Masters 2022 is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in July 2022, the second Masters set built around the idea of "double": two rares and two foils in every draft booster. It drew on almost three decades of cards for Modern, Legacy and Commander players, and it was the first draft format designed so that all ten three-color combinations were viable. Its headline reprints included Cavern of Souls, Mana Drain, Mana Vault, Imperial Seal and Dockside Extortionist.

## What the set was for

The original Double Masters in 2020 proved that players liked opening two rares per pack, and the sequel doubled down on the same promise. Masters sets exist to reprint cards that have become scarce, and Double Masters 2022 spread that net wide. Some cards were here for Modern (Cavern of Souls, Force of Negation, Wrenn and Six), some for Legacy and Vintage (Mana Drain, Imperial Seal, Sensei's Divining Top), and a huge share for Commander (Smothering Tithe, Teferi's Protection, Chaos Warp, Dockside Extortionist and a long list of legendary creatures).

The set has no story. Its only new card was **Cryptic Spires**, a common land printed in every draft booster. When you build your deck you circle two colors on it, and from then on it's a tapped dual land of those colors. It let drafters fix for three-color decks without making four- and five-color piles too easy. It's a common land, so it's legal in Commander, Legacy and Pauper, though in practice it's mostly a Limited tool.

## The reprints that mattered

The set's value came almost entirely from its rare and mythic slots.

- **Cavern of Souls**: the uncounterable creature land, a staple of Modern tribal decks and Commander.
- **Mana Drain** and **Mana Vault**: old-school Vintage and Commander icons (Mana Vault dates back to Alpha).
- **Imperial Seal**: a one-mana tutor, which had previously been printed in paper only in Portal Three Kingdoms and as a judge promo.
- **Dockside Extortionist**: a goblin that made Treasure for every artifact and enchantment opponents controlled, one of Commander's most powerful cards at the time.
- **Force of Negation**: a free counterspell on your opponent's turn, a Modern sideboard staple.
- **Wrenn and Six**: a two-mana planeswalker that became a fixture of Modern and Legacy.
- **Bitterblossom**: the Faerie token engine played in Modern.
- **Crucible of Worlds**: replay lands from your graveyard.
- **Consecrated Sphinx**, **Muldrotha, the Gravetide** and **Elenda, the Dusk Rose**: Commander favorites.
- **Kozilek, Butcher of Truth**, **Ulamog, the Infinite Gyre** and **Emrakul, the Aeons Torn**: the original Eldrazi titans, back at mythic rare.

Lower down, the set carried a deep bench of Commander and cube staples at rare and uncommon: Teferi's Protection, Chaos Warp, Smothering Tithe, Panharmonicon, Hardened Scales, Green Sun's Zenith, Damnation, Supreme Verdict, Path to Exile, Lightning Bolt, Terminate and every Ravnica bounce land.

### Collecting Double Masters 2022

Like most premium sets of the era, Double Masters 2022 had several versions of its best cards. Draft Boosters held regular cards and traditional foils, while Collector Boosters were built around borderless rares and mythics and foil-etched cards. Each Collector Booster held two borderless rare or mythic cards, and they were the only place to find the textured foil mythics, the set's rarest treatment. For players who just wanted the cards, the draft boosters' two rares per pack made opening them for play unusually rewarding.

## Limited

Double Masters 2022 was drafted like a normal set but felt nothing like one. Every pack had two rares or mythics and two foils of any rarity, so power levels were sky-high, and as in the original Double Masters, the first pick of each pack took two cards.

The defining design choice was three colors. Mark Rosewater described the set as the first built so that every three-color combination had a home, with heavily overlapping themes so that most cards fit several decks. A green card might go into any of six three-color archetypes, and a two-color gold card fit three. The bounce lands at uncommon, Cryptic Spires in every pack and cheap fixers kept three-color mana workable. The ten archetypes were:

- **White-blue-black:** flicker and enter-the-battlefield value
- **Blue-black-red:** graveyard recursion midrange
- **Black-red-green:** removal, sturdy threats and light sacrifice
- **Red-green-white:** Auras and combat tricks on heroic-style creatures
- **Green-white-blue:** big ramp
- **White-black-green:** +1/+1 counters, going wide and tall
- **Blue-red-white:** prowess spells
- **Black-green-blue:** self-mill and graveyard growth
- **Red-white-black:** tokens and sacrifice
- **Green-blue-red:** ramp with flexible spells

The format rewarded taking powerful cards early and settling on two colors before adding a third. Removal like Terminate, Go for the Throat and Path to Exile was premium, and the high rare count meant bombs showed up in almost every game. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

As an all-reprint set, Double Masters 2022 didn't create new decks. Its effect was on supply: it made existing staples easier to find.

- **Modern:** Cavern of Souls, Force of Negation, Wrenn and Six and Bitterblossom are all Modern-legal cards with long Modern track records. Cavern in particular remains a key land for creature-based decks. Our [Modern guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) covers where these cards fit.
- **Legacy and Vintage:** Mana Drain is banned in Legacy, as it has been since the format began, but restricted-list staples like Mana Vault, Imperial Seal and Sensei's Divining Top saw Vintage play. As of October 2026, Wrenn and Six is banned in Legacy but legal in Modern.
- **Commander:** this is where the set mattered most. Dockside Extortionist was banned in Commander on September 23, 2024, alongside Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus and Nadu, Winged Wisdom. Several other reprints are on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026, including Smothering Tithe, Teferi's Protection, Mana Vault, Imperial Seal, Consecrated Sphinx, Gifts Ungiven and Grand Arbiter Augustin IV. Emrakul, the Aeons Torn has long been banned in Commander. For how the format works, see our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

## Cards from Double Masters 2022 worth owning

- **Cavern of Souls**: playable in every format with tribal or creature decks.
- **Force of Negation**: a long-running Modern and Legacy sideboard card.
- **Smothering Tithe** and **Teferi's Protection**: two of white's best Commander cards.
- **Chaos Warp**: red's flexible answer to anything.
- **Mana Vault** and **Imperial Seal**: high-power Commander and Vintage staples.
- **Bitterblossom**: a Modern-legal token engine.
- **Muldrotha, the Gravetide**: a popular graveyard commander.
