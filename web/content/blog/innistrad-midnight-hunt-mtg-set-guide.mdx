Innistrad: Midnight Hunt is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2021 that returned to Innistrad, the gothic-horror plane of werewolves, vampires, zombies and ghosts, at a moment when the nights were growing longer every day. It's the werewolf half of a two-set return (Innistrad: Crimson Vow followed with vampires), and it introduced day/night, disturb, coven and decayed, along with removal like Fateful Absence and Infernal Grasp that Pioneer still plays.

## The story

Innistrad has been without its protector for a while. Avacyn, the archangel who kept humanity safe, was destroyed years earlier, and the Eldrazi titan Emrakul has been sealed inside the plane's moon since the events of Eldritch Moon. Something is now wrong with the sky. The sun rises later and sets earlier each day, and the werewolves, who grow stronger at night, are becoming bolder and more savage. If the trend continues, Innistrad will fall into an eternal night, and humanity won't survive it.

The humans turn to an older kind of magic. The Dawnhart Coven, a circle of witches led by Katilda, proposes reviving Harvesttide, an ancient festival that hasn't been held in centuries. If enough people gather beneath the Celestus, a huge astronomical device in the Kessig countryside, the coven can draw on their combined hope and perform a ritual to restore the balance between day and night. The ritual needs a powerful artifact, the Moonsilver Key, which the planeswalker Kaya is sent to recover.

Other planeswalkers arrive to help. Arlinn Kord, a planeswalker who is also a werewolf, is torn between her human allies and the wolves. Teferi uses his time magic to slow the sunset, and Chandra helps the cathar Adeline organize the defense. The werewolf alpha Tovolar leads his howlpack against the festival and spends the battle trying to lure Arlinn into his Wild Hunt. When night finally falls, Arlinn transforms and fights Tovolar. She pretends to give in, then overpowers him, and the pack retreats.

The victory is incomplete. As the coven resumes the ritual at the heart of the Celestus, the ancient vampire Olivia Voldaren appears, steals the Moonsilver Key and leaves with it. The ritual stays unfinished, the endless night becomes a real threat, and the story continues directly into Crimson Vow, where Olivia's plans for a vampire wedding take center stage.

## Mechanics

### Day and night

Day/night is a game-wide state, like the monarch. Once a daybound card appears, it becomes day. If the active player casts no spells during their own turn, it becomes night on the next turn. If a player casts two or more spells in a single turn, it becomes day again on the next turn. Werewolves with daybound sit on their human side during the day and transform into their nightbound side at night. Brutal Cathar exiles an opposing creature whenever it enters or transforms back to its human face, so a day-night swing can give you a second removal trigger. The rules are simpler than they sound: cast two spells to bring back day, skip a turn's spells and night falls. Our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) covers how transforming works.

### Disturb

Disturb lets you cast a creature card from your graveyard transformed, as its Spirit back face, for the disturb cost. If that Spirit would leave the battlefield, it's exiled instead. Malevolent Hermit is a counterspell on a creature. Once it's in your graveyard, you can bring it back as Benevolent Geist, which makes your noncreature spells uncounterable.

### Coven

Coven checks whether you control three or more creatures with different powers. It rewards building a board with a range of creature sizes, such as a 1/1 token, a 2/2 and a 3/3. Augur of Autumn lets you play lands from the top of your library, and with coven active, creature spells too.

### Decayed

Decayed creatures can't block, and when one attacks, it's sacrificed at end of combat. Most decayed creatures are Zombie tokens, so the mechanic gives you cheap bodies to sacrifice or attack with once. Jadar, Ghoulcaller of Nephalia makes a decayed Zombie at each of your end steps if you don't already have one.

### Flashback

Flashback returns from the original Innistrad: you can cast the card a second time from your graveyard for its flashback cost, then it's exiled. Memory Deluge is an instant that looks at a number of cards equal to the mana you spent and keeps two, with an expensive flashback for a second dig.

## Limited

Midnight Hunt's ten archetypes, each with a signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue Spirits** (Faithful Mending).
- **Blue-black Zombies and self-mill** (Bladestitched Skaab).
- **Black-red Vampire aggression** (Vampire Socialite).
- **Red-green Werewolves** (Unnatural Moonrise).
- **Green-white coven Humans** (Dawnhart Wardens).
- **White-black sacrifice** (Fleshtaker).
- **Blue-red spells and flashback** (Arcane Infusion).
- **Black-green creatures dying and coming back** (Grizzly Ghoul).
- **Red-white day/night aggro** (Sunrise Cavalier).
- **Green-blue flashback** (Rootcoil Creeper).

The graveyard mattered in almost every color pair, thanks to flashback, disturb and self-mill. Day/night created a real tempo decision each turn: holding up a single instant could flip night on, strengthening your werewolves, while a two-spell turn could flip it back. Cheap interaction at common, like the green fight spell Duel for Dominance and the white Thraben Exorcism (which answers Spirits and disturb creatures), was valuable. For more on the fundamentals, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Midnight Hunt arrived with the fall 2021 rotation, and its first big test was the World Championship XXVII in October 2021. That field was split mostly among Izzet and Grixis Epiphany decks (built around Kaldheim's Alrund's Epiphany), Mono-Green Aggro and Mono-White Aggro, and the title went to Yuta Takahashi with Izzet Dragons. Cheap spells like Consider and Play with Fire filled out those Izzet lists, while Adeline, Intrepid Adversary and Brutal Cathar powered the white aggro decks.

Over the next three years, the set's cards stayed in Standard far longer than usual, because Wizards moved Standard to a three-year rotation in 2023 and Midnight Hunt didn't leave until August 2024. Its most important cards in that period:

- **The Meathook Massacre** became a staple of black midrange decks. On October 10, 2022, Wizards banned it in Standard, saying black was too dominant and Meathook was one of its strongest cards against decks of small creatures.
- **Infernal Grasp and Fateful Absence** were the default cheap removal for black and white decks.
- **Graveyard Trespasser** showed up in Rakdos Midrange, the deck that won Pro Tour March of the Machine in 2023.
- **The slowlands** (Deserted Beach, Shipwreck Marsh, Haunted Ridge, Rockfall Vale, Overgrown Farmland) made two-color mana bases work.

Midnight Hunt cards remain common in Pioneer as of October 2026. Infernal Grasp and Fateful Absence are standard removal there, Memory Deluge fits control decks, and Consider appears in blue spell decks. The Meathook Massacre is legal in Pioneer and shows up in black midrange. In Modern and Legacy, Consider is the set's most-played card, a cheap cantrip for blue tempo decks. In Commander, Infernal Grasp, Wrenn and Seven, Lier and Old Stickfingers are popular, and Midnight Hunt's two Commander decks added more. To compare these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Midnight Hunt worth owning

- **Infernal Grasp**: two-mana instant removal for any creature, played in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Fateful Absence**: white's flexible two-mana answer in Pioneer.
- **Consider**: a one-mana cantrip for blue decks in Modern, Legacy and Pioneer.
- **The Meathook Massacre**: a sweeper and win condition rolled into one, still legal in Pioneer.
- **Memory Deluge**: the card-advantage instant of Pioneer control.
- **Wrenn and Seven**: a strong lands-matter planeswalker in Commander.
- **The slowlands**: affordable dual lands that see play in every two-color deck.

Innistrad: Midnight Hunt is a good example of a set whose quiet uncommons and rares outlived its flashy mythics. If you're picking it up today, focus on the removal and the lands, which work in almost any deck.
