Core Set 2019 is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2018, the first core set since Magic Origins three years earlier. It mixes new cards with reprints, aims squarely at newer players, and builds its story around the origins of the dragon Nicol Bolas, who appears on its only double-faced card. It also reprinted Crucible of Worlds, Scapeshift and Omniscience, and gave Standard the extra-turn spell Nexus of Fate.

## What the set was for

Wizards stopped making core sets after Magic Origins in 2015, then brought them back with Core Set 2019. The return let Wizards move to a new release schedule of three large standalone sets a year plus one core set, and it gave newcomers a set that was easy to pick up. The design team started from the products new players would buy first, the Welcome Decks and Planeswalker Decks, then built the set around the cards those products needed.

The result has a lot of familiar effects. There are more vanilla creatures and tribal lords than usual, keywords are limited to the evergreen ones (flying, deathtouch, trample and so on), and a good chunk of the set is reprints. Even so, the set is seeded with combos and strong rares for experienced players. If you're new to the game, our guide to [how to play Magic: The Gathering](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers the basics this set was built to teach.

## The story

Core Set 2019's story isn't part of the main storyline of the time. It's a flashback that fills in the past of Nicol Bolas, the villain the multiverse was heading toward a showdown with. The tale was told in Kate Elliott's *Chronicle of Bolas* series on the Wizards site.

Long ago on Dominaria, the Elder Dragons hatched from egg-stones shed by an ancient being called the Ur-Dragon. Every egg held one dragon except one, which held twins: Nicol and Ugin. They were smaller than their siblings, and their very first sight was humans killing their newly hatched sister. Nicol wanted to fight; Ugin persuaded him to hide and survive. Nicol took the second name Bolas so he'd stand equal with his larger siblings, and the twins learned to hunt together.

Living for a time with the Elder Dragon Arcades Sabboth, who ruled a human settlement, Bolas learned how easily people could be manipulated. He used that lesson to turn dragon hunters against each other in a bloody war and made himself their master. When Ugin realized his brother had never truly cared for him, the shock ignited Ugin's planeswalker spark. Later, during the long Elder Dragon War, Bolas came close to ruling half of Dominaria. In the final clash, Ugin intervened to save Arcades, and Bolas, furious and envious, had his own spark ignite and left the plane. The rivalry between the twins runs through much of Magic's story afterward, from Ugin's fall on Tarkir (revisited in Fate Reforged) to War of the Spark.

The set's cards reflect this. **Nicol Bolas, the Ravager** is a mythic double-faced card that transforms into the planeswalker Nicol Bolas, the Arisen, showing the moment his spark ignites. The four other surviving Elder Dragons, **Arcades, the Strategist**, **Chromium, the Mutable**, **Palladia-Mors, the Ruiner** and **Vaevictis Asmadi, the Dire**, appear as three-color legends. A short follow-up story, *Unbowed*, follows the planeswalker Vivien Reid as she hunts Bolas on Ixalan.

## Mechanics

Core Set 2019 has no new keyword mechanics, which was the point. Instead, it leans on a few structural themes.

### Dragons and Elder Dragons

Dragons are the set's flagship creature type. Besides the Elder Dragon legends, there's **Lathliss, Dragon Queen**, who makes 5/5 Dragon tokens when other Dragons enter, **Sarkhan, Fireblood**, a planeswalker that ramps into Dragons, and **Dragon's Hoard**, a mana rock that draws cards off them. At common and uncommon, **Dragon Egg** blocks early and leaves a flying 2/2 behind.

### Tribal lords

Several rares pump a creature type: **Death Baron** for Zombies and Skeletons, **Elvish Clancaller** for Elves, **Goblin Trashmaster** for Goblins, **Valiant Knight** for Knights and **Supreme Phantom** for Spirits. Lords are an easy way for newer players to see synergy in action.

### Planeswalkers and their decks

Five planeswalkers are in the main set: **Ajani, Adversary of Tyrants**, **Tezzeret, Artifice Master**, **Sarkhan, Fireblood**, **Vivien Reid** and **Liliana, Untouched by Death**, plus the transforming Nicol Bolas. Ajani and Tezzeret also headlined the Planeswalker Decks, which came with exclusive cards that are part of the set for legality.

### Seeded combos

M19 hides a few combo pieces. **Sai, Master Thopterist** makes a Thopter for each artifact spell you cast. **Isareth the Awakener** brings creatures back from the graveyard when it attacks. **Liliana's Contract** draws four cards and wins the game if you control four Demons with different names. These cards gave enfranchised players something to explore.

## Limited

M19 drafted as a classic core set, with each two-color pair getting a gold signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue (Aerial Engineer): artifacts.** A creature that flies as long as you control an artifact.
- **Blue-black (Psychic Symbiont): control.** Card advantage and evasive finishers.
- **Black-red (Brawl-Bash Ogre): sacrifice.** Trade fodder for a bigger attack.
- **Red-green (Draconic Disciple): ramp.** Fix and accelerate into Dragons and fatties.
- **Green-white (Satyr Enchanter): enchantments.** Draw cards off Auras and enchantments.
- **White-black (Regal Bloodlord): life gain.** Make Bat tokens whenever you gain life.
- **Blue-red (Enigma Drake): spells.** A flier that grows with instants and sorceries in your graveyard.
- **Black-green (Poison-Tip Archer): attrition.** Deathtouch blockers and drain on every death.
- **Red-white (Heroic Reinforcements): go wide.** Tokens and a one-shot team pump.
- **Green-blue (Skyrider Patrol): evasion.** Flying creatures that pass out +1/+1 counters.

Removal at common was simple and familiar: **Luminous Bonds** in white, **Shock** in red, **Rabid Bite** and **Plummet** in green, with Murder and Lightning Strike at uncommon. The common dual taplands sometimes replaced a basic land in packs, which made light splashes easier. Because the set was built for newcomers, it's also a good format to practice fundamentals like [curving out](/blog/mana-curve-explained) and trading creatures well.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2018 and 2019

Core Set 2019 joined a Standard full of Dominaria and Ixalan cards. Its splashiest early moment came at the Pro Tour 25th Anniversary in August 2018, where several players did well with Turbo Fog decks running four copies of **Nexus of Fate**, a Buy-a-Box promo that's part of the set. The card stayed relevant after Ravnica Allegiance arrived, as Nexus decks used it with Wilderness Reclamation to take turn after turn. On MTG Arena, Nexus of Fate became the first card banned only in best-of-one Standard, because the shuffle-back clause meant it could be looped endlessly in a fog deck.

Elsewhere, the set's cards played supporting roles. **Militia Bugler** helped white aggro decks find their threats, **Ajani, Adversary of Tyrants** appeared in mono-white aggro alongside Benalish Marshal, and **Vivien Reid** gave green decks card advantage and a way to deal with fliers. Murder and Lightning Strike were staple removal for most of the set's time in Standard. M19 left Standard in October 2019, when Throne of Eldraine's release rotated it out with Ixalan, Rivals of Ixalan and Dominaria.

### Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander

M19 is Pioneer-legal, and its reprints matter there: Crucible of Worlds, Scapeshift and Omniscience are all legal in the format because of this printing. Nexus of Fate is banned in Pioneer. In Modern and Legacy the set is mostly a source of reprints rather than new staples.

As of October 2026, Commander is where M19 lives on. Crucible of Worlds, Omniscience and Scapeshift are popular, the Elder Dragons work as three-color commanders (Arcades, the Strategist leads defender decks that attack with walls), and Lathliss, Dragon Queen and Dragon's Hoard show up in Dragon decks. Our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains how those decks are put together, and the [formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) covers which cards are legal where.

## Cards from Core Set 2019 worth owning

- **Crucible of Worlds**: a Commander staple for land recursion and combo.
- **Omniscience**: a big-mana Commander finisher.
- **Scapeshift**: a land-combo engine in Commander and Modern.
- **Arcades, the Strategist**: a unique commander for defender decks.
- **Death Baron**: a key lord for Zombie and Skeleton decks.
- **Resplendent Angel**: a strong payoff in life-gain decks.
- **Lathliss, Dragon Queen** and **Dragon's Hoard**: two common pieces of Dragon decks.
- **Murder**: simple, reliable removal that's legal almost everywhere.
