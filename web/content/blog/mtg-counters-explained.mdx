A counter in Magic: The Gathering is a marker placed on a permanent or a player to track a lasting change. The most common is the +1/+1 counter, which permanently makes a creature bigger, but there are many kinds: loyalty counters on planeswalkers, poison counters on players, charge counters on artifacts and more. Counters stay until something removes them or the permanent leaves the battlefield.

Counters are also completely different from countering a spell, even though the words look alike. This guide covers the main kinds of counters, the rules that apply to all of them and how they differ from counterspells.

## How counters work

Any small object works as a counter: dice, beads, glass stones or printed counter cards. What matters is that everyone can see how many counters of each kind are on each permanent or player.

The general rules:

- **Counters are named by kind.** A card tells you what kind of counter to place, like "put a +1/+1 counter" or "put a charge counter." Counters of different kinds are tracked separately.
- **Counters last.** Unlike "until end of turn" effects, counters don't wear off. A +1/+1 counter stays on a creature for as long as it's on the battlefield.
- **Counters disappear when the permanent leaves.** If a creature with counters dies, is bounced or is exiled, its counters are gone. If it comes back, it's a new object with no counters, unless the effect that returns it says otherwise.
- **Copies don't copy counters.** A copy of a creature copies its printed characteristics, not the counters on it.

Counters on a permanent are part of what you need to check when [reading a card on the battlefield](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card), since they change its power, toughness or abilities.

## +1/+1 counters

A +1/+1 counter adds 1 to a creature's power and 1 to its toughness. A 2/2 with two +1/+1 counters is a 4/4. These are the most common counters in the game, and entire decks are built around them.

Some cards care about them directly. Walking Ballista enters with a number of +1/+1 counters equal to the X paid to cast it, and you can remove a +1/+1 counter from it to deal 1 damage to any target. Hardened Scales adds one extra +1/+1 counter whenever one or more would be put on a creature you control.

Because they're permanent, +1/+1 counters are often better than temporary pump spells. The downside is that a creature loaded with counters is a big target for removal, and all those counters are lost if it dies.

## -1/-1 counters and how they cancel

A -1/-1 counter does the opposite: it subtracts 1 from power and 1 from toughness. A creature whose toughness drops to 0 or less is put into its owner's graveyard. That's not destruction, so indestructible creatures still die from it, and regeneration doesn't help.

### +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters annihilate

If a permanent has both +1/+1 counters and -1/-1 counters on it, they cancel out. The game removes pairs of them, one of each, until only one kind remains. This happens automatically as a state-based action, meaning the game does it whenever a player would receive priority.

For example, a creature with three +1/+1 counters that gets two -1/-1 counters ends up with one +1/+1 counter. The two pairs are removed.

That rule creates real interactions:

- **Persist** returns a creature that dies to the battlefield with a -1/-1 counter on it, but only if it had no -1/-1 counters when it died. Kitchen Finks is a classic persist creature. If something puts a +1/+1 counter on a returned Kitchen Finks, the counters cancel, and it can persist again.
- **Undying** is the mirror: it returns a creature with a +1/+1 counter, but only if it had no +1/+1 counters. A -1/-1 counter cancels it out and resets undying.

### Infect and wither

Creatures with **wither** deal damage to creatures in the form of -1/-1 counters. Creatures with **infect** do the same to creatures, and deal damage to players as poison counters. Because those counters are permanent, a creature damaged by infect or wither stays shrunk after the turn ends, unlike normal damage.

## Loyalty counters

Loyalty counters belong to planeswalkers. A planeswalker enters the battlefield with loyalty counters equal to the loyalty number printed in its lower right corner.

- **Loyalty abilities** cost counters. A "+1" ability adds a loyalty counter, and a "-3" ability removes three.
- **You can activate only one loyalty ability per planeswalker each turn,** and only at sorcery speed, during your main phase when the stack is empty.
- **Damage dealt to a planeswalker removes that many loyalty counters.**
- **A planeswalker with 0 loyalty** is put into its owner's graveyard.

The [planeswalkers guide](/blog/mtg-planeswalkers-explained) covers how to use and attack them in detail.

## Poison counters

Poison counters go on players, not permanents. A player with 10 or more poison counters loses the game, no matter their life total.

Poison usually comes from infect creatures, which give players poison counters instead of dealing normal damage, and from **toxic**, which gives a player poison counters when a creature with toxic deals combat damage to them, in addition to the regular damage. Poison is a separate path to victory, so a player at 20 life can still be one hit away from losing.

## Other common counters

Many other counters exist, each tracking something specific. These are the ones you'll see most.

| Counter | Where it goes | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charge | Artifacts and other permanents | A generic counter; the card says what it's for |
| Lore | Sagas | Each one triggers a chapter ability |
| Time | Suspended cards and others | Counts down until something happens |
| Energy | Players | A resource you gain and spend on certain cards |
| Stun | Permanents | Stops the permanent from untapping once |
| Shield | Permanents | Prevents the next damage or destruction |
| Keyword | Creatures | Gives an ability like flying or deathtouch |

### Charge counters

Charge counters have no meaning on their own. The card that uses them tells you what they do. Everflowing Chalice enters with a charge counter for each time it was kicked, and taps for one colorless mana for each charge counter on it.

### Lore counters

Sagas are enchantments that tell a story over several turns. You put a lore counter on a Saga as it enters and again as your first main phase begins on each of your turns. Each time you add one, the chapter ability with that number triggers. After the last chapter resolves, you sacrifice the Saga.

### Time counters

Suspend uses time counters. You exile a suspended card with a number of time counters on it, remove one at the beginning of each of your upkeeps, and cast it without paying its mana cost when the last one is removed.

### Keyword counters

Some modern cards put counters that grant abilities, like a flying counter or a deathtouch counter. The creature has that ability as long as the counter stays on it.

## Effects that add more counters

A few effects change how many counters you place:

- **Proliferate** lets you choose any number of permanents and players that already have counters, then give each of them another counter of each kind already there. It's great with +1/+1 counters, loyalty counters and poison counters on opponents.
- **Doubling Season** doubles the counters placed on permanents you control. Because planeswalkers enter with loyalty counters, it also makes them enter with double loyalty.
- **Hardened Scales** adds one extra +1/+1 counter each time one or more are placed on your creatures.

Doubling Season also doubles tokens, which is why it appears in [token strategies](/blog/mtg-tokens-explained) as well.

## Counters vs. counterspells

"Counter" has two unrelated meanings in Magic.

- **A counter** is a marker placed on a permanent or player, as described above.
- **Countering a spell** means canceling it while it's on the stack. Counterspell, a classic two-blue-mana instant, says "Counter target spell." A countered spell doesn't resolve and goes to its owner's graveyard.

Cards that counter spells or abilities have nothing to do with placing counters. The overlap is only in the word. To understand how countering works, including responding to a counterspell with another one, read [how the stack works](/blog/how-the-stack-works-mtg).

## The takeaway

Counters are lasting markers on permanents or players. +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters change a creature's size and cancel each other out in pairs, loyalty counters fuel planeswalkers, and 10 poison counters make a player lose. Charge, lore, time and other counters each do what their cards say. Counters vanish when a permanent leaves the battlefield and aren't copied, and they have nothing to do with countering spells.
