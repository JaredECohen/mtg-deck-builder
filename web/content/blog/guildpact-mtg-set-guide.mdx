Guildpact is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2006, the second set of the original Ravnica block. It introduced three more of Ravnica's ten guilds, the Orzhov Syndicate, the Izzet League and the Gruul Clans, each with its own mechanic, and it printed some of the most enduring cards of the era: Steam Vents, Stomping Ground, Godless Shrine, Electrolyze, Mortify, Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind and a full cycle of Leylines.

## The story

Ravnica is a plane that is one endless city, held together for ten thousand years by the Guildpact, a magical treaty binding its ten guilds to their roles. The guilds compete, scheme and cheat, but the pact keeps any of them from destroying the others. Guildpact's story, told in Cory J. Herndon's novel of the same name, moves away from the city center to Utvara, a ruined valley being reclaimed on the city's edge.

Decades earlier, the Izzet magelord **Zomaj Hauc** dropped an experimental mana bomb on Utvara to wipe out a spreading plague. The blast killed everything in the valley, mutated the plague into an airborne form and tore open the **Schism**, a rift in the sky that pulls in the ghosts of everyone who dies there. Now the Orzhov send **Teysa Karlov**, a young advocate, to take over the valley as its baroness. She is supposed to make it habitable before a deadline, or the land reverts to the Selesnya.

The book's hero is the old Boros lawman **Agrus Kos**, now working as a bouncer in the town's tavern. Teysa discovers that her own guild's ghostly leadership, the Obzedat, has been manipulating her, erasing her memories and backing Hauc's secret project. Hauc has three dragon eggs and plans to use the ghost energy gathered by the Schism to hatch them and overthrow the guild founder **Niv-Mizzet**.

It ends in a fight over Utvara. Teysa takes control of a squad of ghost assassins, breaks with the Obzedat, and spreads a cure for the plague so that the dragons, which can only breathe the infected air, will suffocate. Hauc dies, the dragons kill each other, and Agrus Kos dies of his wounds after glimpsing his long-lost friend, the angel Feather, emerging from the Schism. The book closes on Hauc's ghost stirring, setting up Dissension, where the Guildpact itself is broken.

## Mechanics

### Haunt (Orzhov, white-black)

When a creature with haunt dies, or a haunt spell resolves, you exile it "haunting" a target creature. When the haunted creature dies, the haunt card's effect happens again. **Orzhov Pontiff** gives your team +1/+1 or the opponent's team -1/-1 when it enters and again when the haunted creature dies. **Blind Hunter** drains 2 twice in the same way. It plays like a slow two-for-one: best aimed at your opponent's creature you plan to kill anyway.

### Replicate (Izzet, blue-red)

Replicate lets you pay an extra cost any number of times as you cast an instant or sorcery and copy it once for each payment. **Gigadrowse** taps one permanent for `{U}`, or a whole board if you have the mana, and **Train of Thought** draws as many cards as you can pay for. **Djinn Illuminatus** gives every instant and sorcery you cast replicate.

### Bloodthirst (Gruul, red-green)

A bloodthirst creature enters with extra +1/+1 counters if an opponent was dealt damage this turn. **Scab-Clan Mauler** costs `{R}{G}` and is a 3/3 trampler if you hit first. It pushes you to attack early with cheap creatures and burn.

### Hybrid, gold and the Nephilim

Like all Ravnica sets, Guildpact features guild-colored gold cards and hybrid cards such as the three Guildmages and **Giant Solifuge**, a 4/1 trample, haste, shroud creature payable with red or green. It also introduced the first creatures with exactly four colors, the five **Nephilim**, ancient beings that predate the Guildpact; **Yore-Tiller Nephilim** reanimates a creature tapped and attacking each time it attacks.

### The Leylines

Guildpact printed five rare Leylines that you can put onto the battlefield for free if they are in your opening hand. **Leyline of the Void** exiles any card that would go to an opponent's graveyard, and **Leyline of Lifeforce** makes creature spells uncounterable.

## Limited

Guildpact was drafted with Ravnica: City of Guilds packs in the block format. Because each Ravnica set only supported its own guilds, a drafter usually committed to one guild from a set, or bridged two guilds that shared a color, such as Orzhov and Selesnya through white. Guildpact as a set was strongly guild-based: each guild got common gold creatures, a bounce land and a Signet, so fixing was plentiful for two- and three-color decks.

Orzhov was a slow, grinding guild built on haunt and drain; **Blind Hunter**, **Pillory of the Sleepless** and **Absolver Thrull** were strong commons. Izzet was the trickiest, rewarding instants and sorceries with **Wee Dragonauts** and **Izzet Chronarch**. Gruul was the most straightforward beatdown guild, with bloodthirst creatures like **Ghor-Clan Savage** and big bodies like **Streetbreaker Wurm**. Commons like **Castigate** and **Repeal** also made the cut in many decks.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Guildpact arrived in a Standard of Kamigawa block, Ninth Edition and Ravnica. The MTG Wiki's Standard timeline lists the main decks after its release as Zoo, Izzetron, Owling Mine, Heartbeat, Orzhov aggro and Gruul Beats. **Izzetron** was a blue-red big-mana control deck built on the Urza lands and Izzet cards. **Ghost Dad**, a white-black deck named for Ghost Council of Orzhova, used the Council alongside Plagued Rusalka and Dark Confidant, and coverage of that season's Pro Tour reported more players on Orzhov decks than any other guild on day two. Gruul decks used cheap bloodthirst creatures like Scab-Clan Mauler and threats like Giant Solifuge.

After Time Spiral arrived in October 2006, Gruul beatdown stayed a fixture of Standard, and Guildpact's shock lands and Electrolyze kept seeing play until the set rotated out in October 2007. No Guildpact card was banned in Standard.

As of October 2026:

- **Modern**: Steam Vents, Stomping Ground and Godless Shrine are staples of their colors. Leyline of the Void is a well-known sideboard card against graveyard decks, and Electrolyze still shows up in blue-red decks.
- **Legacy**: the shock lands support multicolor decks, and Leyline of the Void is a sideboard option.
- **Commander**: Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind, Teysa, Orzhov Scion, Ghost Council, the Signets, Mortify and Debtors' Knell are all popular.
- **Pauper**: the bounce lands and common Signets are Pauper-legal.

No Guildpact card is banned or restricted in any of these formats. ## Cards from Guildpact worth owning

- **Steam Vents, Stomping Ground, Godless Shrine**: the dual lands Modern and Commander decks reach for first.
- **Leyline of the Void**: free, game-winning graveyard hate.
- **Electrolyze**: card advantage and removal in one.
- **Mortify**: flexible removal for white-black decks.
- **Niv-Mizzet, the Firemind**: a classic blue-red commander and combo piece.
- **Teysa, Orzhov Scion**: a strong aristocrats engine.
- **Izzet, Gruul and Orzhov Signets**: cheap color-fixing rocks for Commander.
