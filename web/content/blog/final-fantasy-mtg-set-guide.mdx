Magic: The Gathering—FINAL FANTASY is the June 2025 Universes Beyond expansion that put Square Enix's role-playing series into Magic, with every mainline game from Final Fantasy I through XVI represented. It was the first Universes Beyond set designed to be legal in Standard from day one, and its headline card, Vivi Ornitier, ended up warping Standard enough to be banned.

## The story

Final Fantasy has no single shared world, so the set doesn't adapt one plot. Instead, it's an anthology: each mainline game gets its heroes, villains, summons, towns and signature weapons, and the cards are designed so that a character's abilities echo how they play or what they do in their own story.

A few examples show how that works. From Final Fantasy VII, Cloud, Midgar Mercenary fetches an Equipment when he enters and doubles the triggers from whatever he's carrying, a nod to the Buster Sword (which is its own mythic Equipment). His nemesis, Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER, feeds on creatures dying and transforms into Sephiroth, One-Winged Angel after enough of them fall in one turn, mirroring the character's final form. Aerith Gainsborough grows from life gain and, when she dies, passes her counters to your legendary creatures, a quiet reference to her fate in the game.

Final Fantasy VI brings Terra, Magical Adept and the clown-turned-god Kefka, Court Mage, who transforms into Kefka, Ruler of Ruin by forcing each opponent to sacrifice a permanent. Final Fantasy IX gives us Vivi Ornitier, Garnet and the villain Kuja. Final Fantasy X has Yuna, Hope of Spira returning enchantments from the graveyard, echoing her role as a summoner. Final Fantasy XIV is represented by Y'shtola Rhul, Venat, Emet-Selch and Zodiark, Umbral God, while Final Fantasy XVI's Dominants, such as Clive, Ifrit's Dominant and Jill, Shiva's Dominant, transform into the summons they carry. Older games get love too: Firion and The Emperor of Palamecia from II, Cecil, Golbez and Rydia from IV, Gilgamesh from V.

The five Crystal artifacts (Earth, Fire, Water, Wind and Darkness) tie back to the original game's quest to restore the elemental crystals, and the Summon cards, from Shiva to Bahamut to the Knights of Round, recreate the series' famous summon animations as multi-turn Sagas. The result is less a story and more a museum tour of a 35-plus-year series, organized so that fans of any one game can find their favorites.

## Mechanics

### Job select

Job select appears on Equipment. When the Equipment enters, you create a 1/1 colorless Hero creature token and attach the Equipment to it for free. Black Mage's Rod, a common, gives its wearer +1/+0 and pings each opponent whenever you cast a noncreature spell. Job select solves Equipment's classic Limited problem: it's never a dead card, because it brings its own body. Later, you can move it to a better creature.

### Tiered

Tiered spells make you choose exactly one of several additional costs, and paying more gets a bigger effect. Fire Magic costs `{R}` and deals 1 damage to each creature, or you can pay `{2}` more for 2 damage, or `{5}` more for 3. The same spell is a cheap answer to tokens early and a full sweeper late. The tiered spells are named after the games' spell tiers (Fire, Fira, Firaga; Cure, Cura).

### Summons

Summon cards are enchantment creatures that are also Sagas. They fight like creatures while their chapters tick up each turn, then they're sacrificed after the final chapter. Summon: Shiva taps and stuns opposing creatures for two chapters before its finale. Summon: Bahamut costs `{9}`, destroys two nonland permanents, draws two cards, and finally hits each opponent for the total mana value of your other permanents. Because they're creatures, Summons can be removed early, so timing matters.

### Towns

Town is a land subtype. Many dual lands, like Baron, Airship Kingdom, are Towns, and several rares, such as Midgar, City of Mako, pair a Town with an Adventure spell you can cast first before playing the land from exile. Cards like PuPu UFO and The Wandering Minstrel reward you for controlling Towns, and Starting Town is a fast-mana land that taps for any color for 1 life.

### Returning tools

Flashback, landfall, transforming double-faced cards and lots of Equipment return. Many legendary creatures transform when you meet a condition, which plays like the "Trance" or limit-break moments of the games.

## Limited

Final Fantasy was a two-color draft format with ten archetypes, and Play Booster boxes even included an insert explaining them:

- **White-blue:** artifacts matter, with Cid, Timeless Artificer pumping artifact creatures and Heroes.
- **Blue-black:** control.
- **Black-red:** Black Mage aggro, built on the 0/1 Wizard tokens that ping each opponent whenever you cast a noncreature spell (Black Waltz No. 3 is the signpost).
- **Red-green:** landfall aggro.
- **Green-white:** go wide, with Garnet, Princess of Alexandria turning Saga lore counters into +1/+1 counters.
- **White-black:** artifact and creature sacrifice.
- **Blue-red:** big noncreature spells, with payoffs like Ultros, Obnoxious Octopus and Prompto Argentum for spells that cost four or more.
- **Black-green:** graveyard value, led by Cloud of Darkness.
- **Red-white:** Equipment aggro.
- **Green-blue:** Town ramp.

Drafters generally rated blue-red big spells and black-green graveyard as the strongest pairs, with the field otherwise well balanced. The format wasn't especially fast; damage came from incidental pings, job select bodies and snowballing payoffs rather than from fast curves. Removal like Sephiroth's Intervention, Slash of Light and Suplex was worth taking early, and Summons were valuable at every rarity because they're card advantage on a body. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Final Fantasy hit Standard right before Pro Tour Final Fantasy in June 2025, where Izzet decks were the story of the event, and Vivi Ornitier was the set's most-played card. Vivi's appeal is simple: a three-mana creature that makes a pile of mana on your turn and grows with every cheap spell turns any Izzet hand into an explosive one.

Over the summer and fall of 2025, Vivi found its strongest home in **Izzet Cauldron**, which paired it with Agatha's Soul Cauldron so other creatures could borrow its mana ability. The deck put up dominant results across major events. Wizards responded on **November 10, 2025**, banning Vivi Ornitier in Standard, alongside Screaming Nemesis and Proft's Eidetic Memory. Wizards said Vivi was the card most likely to cause future balance problems. As of October 2026, Vivi remains banned in Standard, and it's the only Final Fantasy card on that list.

The rest of the set has played a supporting role in Standard. Dark Confidant, reprinted here, is legal in Standard for the first time in years, and cards like Starting Town and Fire Magic show up where their decks need them.

### Modern, Pioneer and Commander

In Modern, Final Fantasy's early additions included Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER in Mardu Energy, Vivi Ornitier in Izzet artifact and spell decks, Joshua, Phoenix's Dominant in Boros Energy, and Fire Magic as a sideboard sweeper. Vivi is still legal in Pioneer and Modern.

Commander is where the set made the deepest mark. Between the main set and the four Commander decks, there are dozens of legendary characters, and cards like Traveling Chocobo, Summon: Bahamut and the Crystals are easy includes in their themes. If you're new to the format, our guide to [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers the basics.

## Cards from Final Fantasy worth owning

- **Vivi Ornitier:** banned in Standard but still a strong engine in Modern and Pioneer Izzet decks.
- **Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER:** an aristocrats payoff that works in Modern and is a popular Commander.
- **Starting Town:** a flexible land for any multicolor deck.
- **Dark Confidant:** a long-standing card-advantage creature in older formats.
- **Traveling Chocobo:** a high-impact landfall engine for Commander.
- **Fire Magic:** a cheap, scalable sweeper that earns sideboard slots.
- **Cloud, Midgar Mercenary:** a natural leader for an Equipment-themed Commander deck.

Final Fantasy is easiest to enjoy as a Commander set, with a few cards that matter in Constructed. For Standard, build around Starting Town and the set's two-color legends rather than Vivi.
