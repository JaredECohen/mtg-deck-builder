Tenth Edition is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2007, the last of the numbered core sets before Magic 2010. It brought black borders back to a core set for the first time since Beta, let fans vote on which cards made the cut, and put legendary creatures in a core set for the first time. It is also a deep box of staples: Wrath of God, Birds of Paradise, Crucible of Worlds, Seedborn Muse, Grave Pact and all ten painlands.

## What the set was for

A core set in 2007 was a "greatest hits" collection. Almost every card in Tenth Edition was a reprint, chosen to give new players a clean introduction to the game and to give Standard a stable base of simple, powerful cards. Tenth Edition changed how that job was done in a few ways.

### Black borders

Since Unlimited in 1993, core sets had been printed with white borders, which made them easy to tell apart from expansions. Many players saw white borders as the "reprint look." Tenth Edition returned to black borders, the first core set to use them since Alpha and Beta. Every core set since has kept them.

### Selecting Tenth Edition

Wizards ran a public vote, "Selecting Tenth Edition," over about ten weeks starting in mid-2006. Players chose between pairs of cards, art and flavor text for a number of slots. It was a marketing stunt, but it also got a lot of players arguing about which classic cards deserved to come back.

### Legendary creatures and new keywords

Core sets had avoided legendary creatures because the legend rule was considered too complicated for beginners. Tenth Edition included two per color, such as Squee, Goblin Nabob, Kamahl, Pit Fighter, Arcanis the Omnipotent, Ascendant Evincar, Mirri, Cat Warrior and Reya Dawnbringer, many of them pulled from the Weatherlight and Odyssey storylines. It was also the first core set with defender, double strike, flash, lifelink, reach and shroud. Like Ninth Edition before it, it kept reminder text on its keywords, a choice aimed squarely at new players.

There is no plot in Tenth Edition, but the flavor text and art reach back across Magic's history: Squee and Phage the Untouchable come from Dominaria's past, and Kamahl, Pit Fighter is the barbarian hero of the Odyssey block.

## Mechanics

Tenth Edition had no new mechanics, but it collected a lot of the game's evergreen keywords in one place. Our [keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers each of them. A few themes stand out:

- **Creature lands at uncommon.** Treetop Village (a 3/3 trampling Ape), Faerie Conclave (a 2/1 flier), Spawning Pool (a regenerating 1/1), Ghitu Encampment (a 2/1 first striker) and Forbidding Watchtower (a 1/5) are lands that become creatures for a turn. They give mana-light decks extra threats that don't die to sorcery-speed removal.
- **Painlands.** All ten "painlands" were in the set, like Adarkar Wastes and Sulfurous Springs. Each taps for colorless mana for free, or for one of two colors at the cost of 1 damage.
- **Tribal lords.** Goblin King, Elvish Champion, Lord of the Undead and Field Marshal give their creature types a bonus, and Coat of Arms rewards any tribal deck.
- **Muses.** A rare cycle of Muses, including Seedborn Muse (untap your permanents during every other player's untap step) and Graveborn Muse.

## Limited

Tenth Edition was drafted as a full three packs of the same set, the way core sets were in that era. It was a slow, fair format by modern standards. Most of the commons were vanilla or lightly-skilled creatures, combat tricks were plentiful, and removal was the scarcest resource.

What mattered:

- **Removal at common.** Pacifism in white, Terror and Assassinate in black, Shock and Incinerate in red. Prodigal Pyromancer, a common that pings for 1 damage every turn, was among the best commons because it wiped out x/1 creatures and finished off blockers.
- **Fliers.** Aven Fisher, the Skyhunter cards and Sengir Vampire in the rare slot could win board stalls.
- **Card advantage creatures.** Civic Wayfinder (finds a land), Aven Fisher (draws a card when it dies) and Merfolk Looter helped smooth draws.

There weren't scripted color-pair archetypes like modern sets have. Drafters generally took removal and fliers first and built two-color decks with a sensible curve. If you'd like to understand why that approach works, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) breaks it down.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2007-2009

Tenth Edition spent its time in Standard next to Time Spiral block, Lorwyn and Shadowmoor block, and eventually Shards of Alara. It wasn't the source of the format's headline decks, which came from those blocks (Faeries with Bitterblossom from Morningtide was the most famous), but it supplied a lot of the glue:

- **Wrath of God** was the go-to board wipe for white control decks, and aggro and Faeries players planned around it.
- **The painlands** filled out two-color mana bases.
- **Faerie Conclave** gave Faeries decks another flier that didn't die to sorcery-speed removal.
- **Birds of Paradise, Llanowar Elves, Incinerate, Shock and Mogg Fanatic** were the cheap, efficient cards that green and red decks relied on.

No Tenth Edition card was banned in Standard. The set rotated out in July 2009 when Magic 2010 took its place.

### Eternal formats and Commander

Because Modern begins with Eighth Edition, every Tenth Edition card is legal there, though most of them have been reprinted many times. Its lasting value is in the staples it carried:

- **Birds of Paradise** and **Llanowar Elves** are the classic mana creatures.
- **Crucible of Worlds** is a long-time Legacy and Vintage engine with fetchlands and land destruction like Strip Mine.
- **Seedborn Muse**, **Grave Pact**, **Doubling Cube** and **Coat of Arms** are Commander favorites.
- **Pithing Needle** is a sideboard card in nearly every format.
- **Treetop Village** has been a green aggro threat in Legacy.
- **Mind Stone**, **Incinerate** and **Mogg Fanatic** are legal in Pauper, since they had common printings.

As of October 2026, no Tenth Edition card is banned in Modern, Legacy or Commander. Our [formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) guide covers where each of these formats draws its cards from.

## Cards from Tenth Edition worth owning

- **Birds of Paradise**: the most famous mana creature in the game, playable from Modern to Commander.
- **Wrath of God**: the classic board wipe.
- **Crucible of Worlds**: a land engine for Legacy, Vintage and Commander.
- **Seedborn Muse**: a Commander bomb that gives you every activated ability on every turn.
- **Grave Pact**: a key piece of Commander sacrifice decks.
- **Pithing Needle**: a cheap, flexible sideboard answer.

## The takeaway

Tenth Edition is where core sets started to look like the ones we open today: black borders, legendary creatures and reminder text for new players. If you pull out a box of old cards, its staples still slot into Modern and Commander decks. A classic white control deck with Wrath of God, Condemn and a creature land or two is a fun way to try the set in Modern.
