Oath of the Gatewatch is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2016 that finished the Battle for Zendikar story and founded the Gatewatch, the team of planeswalkers at the center of Magic's story for years afterward. It's the second, smaller set of the Battle for Zendikar block, and its headline is a brand-new mana symbol for colorless mana, the first basic land with no land type, and a pair of Eldrazi so efficient they warped Modern within weeks.

The set was also designed with team play in mind: its surge mechanic counts spells your teammate cast, making it one of the best sets ever for Two-Headed Giant.

## The story

### Zendikar on the brink

When Battle for Zendikar ended, the Zendikari had won a rare victory against Ulamog, one of the colossal Eldrazi titans, at the coastal fortress of Sea Gate. Oath of the Gatewatch opens with that hope shattered. A second titan, Kozilek, erupts from the ground, warping reality around it. The defenders are scattered, and the planeswalkers on Zendikar realize that holding ground isn't enough. The titans have to be destroyed.

### Four planeswalkers

Four planeswalkers end up on Zendikar at the same time. Gideon Jura, the soldier who has been fighting alongside the Zendikari, and Jace Beleren, who came at Gideon's request and has been studying the hedrons, are already there. Nissa Revane, the elf animist tied to Zendikar's living soul, is fighting for her home. Chandra Nalaar arrives later to join the fight. Along the way, the demon planeswalker Ob Nixilis tries to turn the chaos to his own advantage and has to be dealt with too.

### Binding the titans

Jace works out that the ancient hedrons, the stone structures floating over Zendikar, can be arranged into a pattern that pins the titans in place. Nissa reaches through the plane's leylines to hold Ulamog and Kozilek bound, and with both titans trapped, Chandra pours everything she has into a firestorm that burns them to ash. Zendikar is saved, and the set's final story cards, like Zendikar Resurgent, show the land itself recovering.

### The oath

Before the final battle, Gideon proposes a promise: if a threat arises that endangers the Multiverse, they'll stand together to stop it. Gideon swears first, then Jace, and eventually Chandra and Nissa make their own oaths. That team becomes the Gatewatch. The third Eldrazi titan, Emrakul, was missing from the battle, which sets up its reappearance in Eldritch Moon later in 2016.

## Mechanics

### Colorless mana and Wastes

Oath of the Gatewatch introduced the colorless mana symbol, shown in costs as `{C}`. Unlike generic mana, a `{C}` cost must be paid with actual colorless mana, from lands like Wastes or Sea Gate Wreckage, or from Eldrazi Scion tokens. Thought-Knot Seer costs `{3}{C}`, so a deck needs colorless sources to cast it. Wastes, the first basic land without a land type, taps for `{C}`. The symbol also became the standard way to print colorless mana production on every card from then on.

### Surge

Surge is an alternative, cheaper cost you can pay if you or a teammate has already cast another spell this turn. Reckless Bushwhacker is a hasty 2/1 for three, or for `{1}{R}` with surge, and when you surge it your other creatures get +1/+0 and haste. Surge rewards chaining cheap spells in one turn.

### Support

Support N puts a +1/+1 counter on each of up to N other target creatures (on a creature, it can't target itself). Relief Captain, a four-mana 3/2, supports 3 when it enters. Support turns a board of small creatures into a real threat.

### Cohort

Cohort is an Ally ability word for activated abilities that require tapping the creature and another untapped Ally. Stoneforge Acolyte taps with another Ally to dig four cards deep for an Equipment. Cohort only works if you play enough Allies, which made it a draft-only mechanic in practice.

### Devoid and Eldrazi Scions

Devoid returns from Battle for Zendikar: a card with devoid has no color even though it has colored mana symbols in its cost. Many Eldrazi and Eldrazi-themed spells also create 1/1 Eldrazi Scion tokens that you can sacrifice for `{C}`, which help cast the colorless-cost cards.

## Limited

Oath of the Gatewatch was drafted with two packs of Oath and one of Battle for Zendikar, so the archetypes were a continuation of the block. The main themes were:

- **Colorless Eldrazi** in any color pair, using Wastes and Scion tokens to cast `{C}` cards like Warping Wail and Spatial Contortion, two strong uncommon removal spells.
- **Allies**, especially green-white and red-white, with cohort cards and support creatures like Joraga Auxiliary.
- **Surge** decks in blue and red that chained cheap spells, with Comparative Analysis drawing two cards for `{2}{U}`.
- **Support and +1/+1 counters** in green and white, with Saddleback Lagac and Relief Captain.

Drafters had to decide early how much colorless mana to take: Wastes and colorless-producing lands had real pick value if you planned on playing several `{C}` cards. Removal like Grasp of Darkness and Immolating Glare (both uncommon) was premium, and big devoid creatures gave ramp decks a strong late game. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### In Standard (January 2016 to September 2017)

Oath of the Gatewatch fed several of Standard's best decks during its run. Reflector Mage was a centerpiece of Bant Company and later of white-based Humans decks, and it was strong enough that Wizards banned it in Standard in January 2017, along with Emrakul, the Promised End and Smuggler's Copter. Nissa, Voice of Zendikar powered green-white tokens strategies, Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet was a premier threat for black control, and Kozilek's Return and Chandra, Flamecaller saw play in red ramp decks. Oath rotated out when Ixalan released on September 29, 2017. For background on rotation, see our [rotation explainer](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### The Eldrazi Winter in Modern

Oath's biggest impact was in Modern. Thought-Knot Seer and Reality Smasher, combined with Eldrazi Temple and Eye of Ugin (older lands that make extra colorless mana for Eldrazi), let decks cast four- and five-power threats on turns two and three. Eldrazi decks dominated the Modern Pro Tour for Oath of the Gatewatch in February 2016 and the weeks around it, a stretch players still call the "Eldrazi Winter." On April 4, 2016, Wizards banned Eye of Ugin in Modern. The OGW creatures themselves were never banned, and as of October 2026 Thought-Knot Seer, Reality Smasher and Matter Reshaper still appear in Modern Eldrazi decks and in Legacy and Vintage Eldrazi aggro.

### In Pioneer

When Pioneer launched, Oath of Nissa was banned on November 4, 2019, among the format's first bans, then unbanned on July 13, 2020. A second Oath card went the other way: Inverter of Truth, a four-mana flying 6/6 that exiles your library face down and shuffles your graveyard back in, anchored a Pioneer combo deck with Thassa's Oracle and was banned in Pioneer in August 2020. The creature lands Hissing Quagmire, Needle Spires and Wandering Fumarole are reliable mana for their color pairs, and Reflector Mage is a tempo option for Azorius-based decks.

### In Commander

Kozilek, the Great Distortion is a popular top end for colorless and Eldrazi decks, and Mirrorpool and Sea Gate Wreckage are common colorless utility lands. Linvala, the Preserver is a strong catch-up card, General Tazri leads Ally decks, and Ayli, Eternal Pilgrim and Mina and Denn, Wildborn have their own followings.

## Cards from Oath of the Gatewatch worth owning

- **Thought-Knot Seer** and **Reality Smasher**: the core of Eldrazi decks in Modern, Legacy and Vintage.
- **Kozilek's Return**: an instant-speed sweeper for red decks and colorless ramp.
- **Reflector Mage**: a tempo creature that still fits Azorius decks in Pioneer.
- **Kozilek, the Great Distortion**: a top-end Eldrazi for Commander.
- **Mirrorpool**: a colorless land with two powerful copy effects for Commander.
- **Hissing Quagmire, Needle Spires, Wandering Fumarole**: creature lands that remain useful in Pioneer and Commander.

## The takeaway

Oath of the Gatewatch is small, but it introduced colorless mana, founded the Gatewatch and created one of Modern's most memorable eras. Its Eldrazi are still the most played cards from the set in eternal formats, and in Pioneer its creature lands and Reflector Mage give midrange decks solid tools.
