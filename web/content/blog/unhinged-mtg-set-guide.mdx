Unhinged is a Magic: The Gathering humor set released in November 2004, the second "Un-set" after 1998's Unglued. Its silver-bordered cards break the game's rules on purpose: they cost half a mana, care about the artist's name, make you say "Gotcha!" or ask you to stand up, sing or touch your face. It's best known for jokes that became legends, like Mox Lotus, Gleemax and a card whose name is a whole sentence.

## What the set was for

Unhinged had no story. It was a comedy album for people who already loved Magic, and almost every card is a joke about the game, its players or the people who make it. Mark Rosewater, who led the design, had wanted a follow-up to Unglued for years, and Unhinged arrived with a much more confident sense of what an Un-set could do.

Three ideas run through the set. First, it breaks rules that normal cards never could: fractional mana costs, cards that count words in a name, cards that care who drew the art. Second, it makes the game physical and social, with cards that trigger when an opponent laughs, says a number or touches the table. Third, it parodies Magic culture, from the player profiles R&D uses (Johnny, Combo Player is a real legendary creature here) to the constant power creep of rare cards.

The silver border is the warning label. It marks the cards as outside the normal rules, which is why they're not legal in tournaments or official Commander games. Since 2004 Wizards has added acorn-stamped cards to later Un-sets for the same purpose, and black-bordered Unfinity cards are tournament-legal, but every Unhinged card except the basics stays casual-only. If you're not sure how legality works across formats, start with [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

### Where Unhinged sits among the Un-sets

Unglued (1998) proved the idea, Unhinged (2004) sharpened it, and Unstable (2017) and Unfinity (2022) followed. Each new Un-set borrowed from Unhinged: Unstable kept its love of physical and social jokes, while Unfinity introduced the acorn stamp so that some cards could be black-bordered and tournament-legal while the wildest ones stayed casual. Unhinged was the last Un-set for 13 years, which is part of why its jokes are so well remembered.

## Mechanics

### Half-point mana and damage

Unhinged introduced half mana symbols. Little Girl costs half a white mana and is a half-power, half-toughness creature, while Sauté deals 3½ damage and Wet Willie of the Damned deals 2½ and gains you 2½ life. Cheap Ass makes your spells cost half a mana less, and Mons's Goblin Waiters sacrifice a creature or land for half a red mana. Life totals with fractions became a real thing at kitchen tables.

### Gotcha

Gotcha cards return from your graveyard to your hand when an opponent does something specific and you catch them by saying "Gotcha!" Laughing Hyena comes back when an opponent laughs, Number Crunch when they say a number, Cardpecker when they touch the table with their hand, and Kill! Destroy! when they say either word. It turned the whole table into part of the game.

### Artist matters

Several cards care who painted the art. Drawn Together gives +2/+2 to creatures by an artist you choose, Graphic Violence pumps all creatures by one artist, Persecute Artist strips a hand of every nonland card by a named artist (except Rebecca Guay), and Aesthetic Consultation tutors for a card by your chosen artist. Bursting Beebles and Erase (Not the Urza's Legacy One) reward controlling permanents that share an artist.

### Names, words and letters

Wordmail gives +1/+1 per word in a creature's name, which makes **Our Market Research Shows That Players Like Really Long Card Names So We Made this Card to Have the Absolute Longest Card Name Ever Elemental** a very large creature. When Fluffy Bunnies Attack shrinks a creature for each time a chosen letter appears in its name, Stone-Cold Basilisk destroys creatures with fewer letters in their names, and Zzzyxas's Abyss destroys the nonland permanent that comes first alphabetically.

### Physical and social jokes

Standing Army has vigilance as long as you're standing. Moniker Mage asks you to say your middle name, Six-y Beast makes your opponent guess a hidden number, Eye to Eye has you win a staring contest, and Enter the Dungeon has players play a subgame under the table. Fat Ass gets bigger while you're eating (the card notes that gum doesn't count).

## Limited

Unhinged was drafted, mostly at parties, prereleases and late-night store events. It plays as a normal Limited set underneath the jokes: removal like Kill! Destroy! and Duh, efficient creatures, and ordinary curves.

The difference is the table. Gotcha cards reward paying attention to what your opponents say and do, so players learned to keep a straight face and avoid saying numbers. Artist-matters cards turned drafts into a hunt for cards by the same painter. Fractional mana meant half-point life totals and some arguments about rounding. If you want to draft it today, treat it as a cube: our [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide) explains how to set one up, and the core lessons of [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) still apply, even when the card asks you to growl.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Unhinged has never had a Constructed meta, because silver-bordered cards were never legal in sanctioned play. The only exception is its full-art basic lands, which are legal anywhere and became popular with players who wanted their decks to look unusual.

The set still shaped the game. Its fractional mana, Gotcha and rules-breaking designs set the template for Unstable (2017) and Unfinity (2022). The Commander Rules Committee has looked at letting some silver-bordered cards into the format, but as of October 2026 every Unhinged card except the basics is listed as not legal in Commander, so they're only playable in games where everyone agrees beforehand. In that "Rule 0" space, Johnny, Combo Player, Richard Garfield, Ph.D. and Mox Lotus are popular for silly decks. Our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains how house rules work in the format.

## Cards from Unhinged worth owning

- **The full-art basic lands**: tournament-legal and still distinctive.
- **Mox Lotus**: the most famous Un-card joke.
- **Who // What // When // Where // Why**: a design curiosity.
- **Richard Garfield, Ph.D.**: a tribute to Magic's creator.
- **Blast from the Past** and **Old Fogey**: love letters to old keywords.
- **Little Girl**: the half-mana icon.
- **Super Secret Tech**: the foil-only collector's card.
