Magic 2010, usually called M10, is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2009, and it changed the game more than almost any set before it. It launched the yearly, year-named core set, was the first core set since the earliest days of the game to include brand-new cards, and arrived with the biggest rules update since Sixth Edition: combat damage stopped using the stack, mana burn disappeared and "in play" became "the battlefield." It also gave the world Baneslayer Angel, Doom Blade and the checklands.

## What the set was for

Before Magic 2010, core sets were numbered editions (Ninth Edition, Tenth Edition) made entirely of reprints, released every two years. They were the "basic" product, and experienced players mostly ignored them. Magic 2010 changed that plan in several ways at once.

- **A yearly core set, named for the next year.** Releasing in summer 2009 and named "2010," M10 began an annual schedule: three expansions and one core set every year. That replaced the occasional fourth sets of earlier years, and Standard moved to a single rotation each fall to match.
- **New cards.** M10 was the first core set since Beta to contain cards that had never been printed before. Many were top-down designs drawn from fantasy archetypes, like Ant Queen, Vampire Nocturnus and Xathrid Demon, and some were deliberate answers to strong cards in the format at the time.
- **A smaller, expansion-shaped set.** At 249 cards, M10 matched the size and rarity breakdown of a large expansion, including mythic rares, which core sets had never had. The five planeswalkers from Lorwyn (Ajani Goldmane, Jace Beleren, Liliana Vess, Chandra Nalaar and Garruk Wildspeaker) were reprinted as mythics, the first planeswalkers in a core set.
- **Teaching the new rules.** Booster packs included tips cards explaining the updated rules, and the intro packs were two-colored decks for the first time in a core set.

There is no plot inside the set itself. The tie-in story was a webcomic, The Veil's Curse, in which Garruk hunts Jace after being cursed by Liliana, but the cards don't tell a story so much as present classic fantasy tropes: knights, angels, dragons, goblins, elves and vampires.

### The rules changes

These changes, effective with M10's release, are still how the game works today. The most important ones:

- **Combat damage no longer uses the stack.** Previously, combat damage was put on the stack and players could respond before it resolved. The classic trick was to assign a creature's damage, then sacrifice it in response and still have the damage happen. After M10, damage is dealt immediately once assigned, with no window in between.
- **Damage assignment order.** When a creature is blocked by several creatures, the attacker now orders the blockers and must assign lethal damage to the first before moving on. (Later rules changes simplified this again.)
- **Mana burn was removed.** Unspent mana used to cost you 1 life per point. Now it simply empties, and mana pools empty at the end of every step and phase, not just every phase.
- **New words.** "In play" became the battlefield, "removed from the game" became exile, "at end of turn" became "at the beginning of the end step," and spells are now "cast" while abilities are "activated."
- **Deathtouch and lifelink became static abilities,** so lifelink gains life at the same time damage is dealt, and multiple instances don't stack.
- **Simultaneous mulligans** and **token ownership** (a token is owned by whoever created it) were cleaned up too.

Competitive players reacted badly at first, especially to the end of damage on the stack, but the changes made the game far easier to learn. Our guide to [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) uses the terms that came from this update.

## Mechanics

Magic 2010 had no new keywords. It used the evergreen ones (flying, first strike, deathtouch, lifelink and so on) and a handful of themes.

### Tribal lords

Each color got a rare lord: Captain of the Watch for Soldiers, Merfolk Sovereign for Merfolk, Cemetery Reaper for Zombies, Goblin Chieftain for Goblins and Elvish Archdruid for Elves. Each gives its tribe +1/+1 and adds something extra; Elvish Archdruid, for example, taps for one green mana for each Elf you control.

### Checklands

The rare dual lands Glacial Fortress, Drowned Catacomb, Dragonskull Summit, Rootbound Crag and Sunpetal Grove enter untapped as long as you already control a land of one of the matching basic types. They were the set's most important new design for Constructed, and they've been reprinted many times since.

### Enemy-color hosers

A cycle of cheap uncommons punishes enemy colors: Celestial Purge, Flashfreeze, Deathmark, Ignite Disorder and Mold Adder. They were sideboard cards in Standard and Limited.

## Limited

Magic 2010 was drafted with three packs of the set, and it's remembered as a clean, traditional core set format: evergreen keywords, creatures with clear jobs and enough removal at common to reward good picks. There were no set-specific archetypes in the modern sense, so most decks were two colors built around removal plus a good curve of creatures.

The best commons were removal spells. Lightning Bolt, Doom Blade, Assassinate, Pacifism, Ice Cage, Tendrils of Corruption, Excommunicate, Divine Verdict and Seismic Strike all answered creatures, and Fireball at uncommon was a game-ending finisher. Evasion mattered a lot: fliers like Stormfront Pegasus, Serra Angel and the blue Sphinxes and Djinns ended games, while Giant Spider and Wall of Frost held the air back. Lifelink and deathtouch, with their new rules, were easy to understand in combat. At rare, Baneslayer Angel, Earthquake, Siege-Gang Commander and Royal Assassin could take over a game on their own.

The format played at a medium pace. Aggressive white and red decks could punish slow starts, but bombs and card advantage usually decided games that went long. Because the cards are so readable, it's a good model for learning how to [evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

M10's Standard season ran from July 2009 to October 2010, alongside Shards of Alara block and then Zendikar block.

- **Jund** was the deck to beat for most of that stretch. It combined Alara cards like Bloodbraid Elf and Sprouting Thrinax with M10's Lightning Bolt, and Siege-Gang Commander showed up in some lists.
- **Baneslayer Angel** was the format's premier creature. It was played in white midrange decks like Naya and in control decks, and as a sideboard threat in aggressive Boros decks.
- **Great Sable Stag** was designed as an answer to blue and black decks. Players used it as a sideboard card against Jund, which had black, though its record in that job was mixed.
- **White aggro** used Honor of the Pure and Elite Vanguard, and Celestial Purge was a common sideboard card for red and white decks.
- The **checklands** were in nearly every two-color deck.

No Magic 2010 card was banned in Standard. The set rotated out in October 2010.

M10's lasting legacy is mostly in older formats. Lightning Bolt has been a Modern and Legacy staple ever since, and M10 was its first appearance in a core set since Fourth Edition in 1995. The checklands are mana-base staples in Modern and casual play (later reprints made them legal in Pioneer, but M10 itself isn't). Ponder became a mainstay of Legacy and Pauper blue decks, though it has been on the Modern banned list since that format began. Hive Mind found a home in a Modern combo deck that copies a Pact like Pact of Negation for the opponent, who then loses when they can't pay for it on their next upkeep. Burning Inquiry fuels madness and Hollow One style decks, and Elvish Archdruid remains a payoff in Modern Elves. In Pauper, M10 commons like Lightning Bolt, Ponder, Doom Blade and Sign in Blood are still format staples; our [Pauper guide](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg) covers that format.

In Commander, Elvish Archdruid, Lurking Predators, Coat of Arms, Howling Mine and Time Warp are long-running favorites. As of October 2026, Ponder is the only Magic 2010 card on a major banned list (Modern); nothing from M10 is banned in Legacy, Vintage, Pauper or Commander.

## Cards from Magic 2010 worth owning

- **Lightning Bolt**: the gold standard of burn, legal in Modern, Legacy and Pauper.
- **The checklands**: untapped dual lands for two-color decks.
- **Ponder**: a premium cantrip for Legacy and Pauper.
- **Doom Blade**: cheap, flexible removal for Commander and Pauper.
- **Baneslayer Angel**: a classic casual and Commander threat.
- **Elvish Archdruid**: a mana engine for Elves decks.
- **Hive Mind**: the heart of a unique Modern combo.

## The takeaway

Magic 2010 is the core set that made core sets matter. It modernized the rules and the language, introduced new cards and mythic rares to core sets, and left behind staples like Lightning Bolt and the checklands.
