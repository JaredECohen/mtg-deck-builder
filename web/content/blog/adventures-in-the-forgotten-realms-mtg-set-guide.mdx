Adventures in the Forgotten Realms is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in July 2021 that brought Dungeons & Dragons into a Standard-legal set for the first time. Instead of a Magic plane, it visits the Forgotten Realms, D&D's most famous campaign setting, and turns tabletop roleplaying staples like dungeon crawls, twenty-sided dice and character classes into game mechanics. It's still the go-to set for D&D monsters, heroes and villains in Commander.

## The story

Adventures in the Forgotten Realms doesn't tell a single plot the way a Magic story set does. It adapts a setting rather than one adventure, and the cards play like a tour through the best-known corners of D&D's Forgotten Realms: the Sword Coast cities of Waterdeep and Baldur's Gate, the frozen north of Icewind Dale, the jungles of Chult and the Underdark beneath them all.

The heroes are the characters D&D fans know from novels and campaigns. **Drizzt Do'Urden**, the drow ranger who left the Underdark, arrives with his panther Guenhwyvar as a token. His friend **Bruenor Battlehammer**, the dwarf king of Mithral Hall, is an Equipment payoff. **Minsc, Beloved Ranger** brings his miniature giant space hamster Boo. Wizards like Mordenkainen and the traveling author Volo appear too, and many commons show ordinary adventuring parties of clerics, rogues and fighters at work.

The villains are just as recognizable. **Tiamat**, the five-headed queen of evil dragons, is a five-color legend that tutors up other Dragons. **Lolth, Spider Queen** rules the drow from the Demonweb Pits, **Acererak the Archlich** waits at the bottom of the Tomb of Annihilation, and Xanathar, the beholder crime lord of Waterdeep, runs the city's underworld. Devils and demons such as Zariel, Asmodeus and Orcus represent the Nine Hells and the Abyss.

The set leans into the feel of a D&D session. Three dungeons from famous published adventures (Lost Mine of Phandelver, Dungeon of the Mad Mage and Tomb of Annihilation) exist as cards outside your deck that you explore room by room. Spells named "You Come to a River" or "You See a Pair of Goblins" read like a Dungeon Master offering choices. Flavor text often quotes characters or rules jokes, and creature types use D&D classes and races. It was sold as a regular premier set, and Wizards later grouped licensed crossover sets under the Universes Beyond name.

## Mechanics

### Venture into the dungeon

When a card tells you to venture into the dungeon, you either start one of the three dungeons or move your marker to the next room of the one you're in. Each room has its own small effect, like scrying, drawing a card or making a token, and finishing a dungeon unlocks bonuses on cards like **Nadaar, Selfless Paladin**, which venture whenever it enters or attacks and pump your team once you've completed a dungeon. Lost Mine of Phandelver is a short, friendly route, Tomb of Annihilation is the quickest but makes everyone pay, and Dungeon of the Mad Mage is long but rewarding.

### Roll a d20

Many cards roll a twenty-sided die and give a better result for a higher roll, usually in three bands such as 1-9, 10-19 and 20. **Hoarding Ogre** makes one to three Treasures when it attacks depending on the roll. **Barbarian Class** lets you roll an extra die and drop the lowest, which is how D&D handles advantage.

### Class enchantments

Classes are enchantments with three levels. You get the first level when the card enters, then pay mana at sorcery speed to unlock each later level, so one card gives you an early effect and a late-game mana sink. **Wizard Class** (`{U}`) removes your maximum hand size, draws two cards at level 2, and grows a creature every time you draw at level 3. **Ranger Class** makes a 2/2 Wolf, then adds +1/+1 counters whenever you attack, then lets you cast creatures from the top of your library.

### Pack tactics and Treasure

Pack tactics triggers when you attack with creatures whose total power is 6 or more. **Werewolf Pack Leader** draws you a card off it. Treasure tokens, artifacts you sacrifice for one mana of any color, show up across the set as the loot from adventuring. If keyword terms like ward or menace are new to you, our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) explains the card frame.

## Limited

Each two-color pair has a gold uncommon that sets its direction:

- **White-blue (Hama Pashar, Ruin Seeker): dungeons.** Venture often and double up on room effects.
- **Blue-black (Krydle of Baldur's Gate): Rogues and evasion.** Chip in with hard-to-block creatures.
- **Black-red (Kalain, Reclusive Painter): Treasure.** Make Treasure and spend it on bigger threats.
- **Red-green (Targ Nar, Demon-Fang Gnoll): pack tactics.** Attack with big creatures to hit the power-6 threshold.
- **Green-white (Trelasarra, Moon Dancer): life gain.** Gain small amounts of life each turn and grow your creatures.
- **White-black (Barrowin of Clan Undurr): dungeons and recursion.** Venture and bring back cheap creatures.
- **Blue-red (Farideh, Devil's Chosen): dice rolling.** Collect cards that roll and turn good rolls into card draw.
- **Black-green (Shessra, Death's Whisper): creatures dying.** Trade creatures and draw from deaths.
- **Red-white (Bruenor Battlehammer): Equipment.** Load up creatures with cheap Equipment and attack.
- **Green-blue (Gretchen Titchwillow): ramp.** Extra land drops and card flow into big spells.

The format was slower than many recent sets, which suited it. Dungeons and Classes reward long games, and the Dragons at the top of each color were real game-enders. Black's removal was deep, with Power Word Kill and the venture Aura **Precipitous Drop**, and value commons like **Dawnbringer Cleric** and **Plundering Barbarian** made almost every deck. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Adventures in the Forgotten Realms arrived in a crowded Standard. Coverage at the time expected little from it, because the format still held Throne of Eldraine and other strong 2019-2020 sets, but a few cards made a real dent. In the summer of 2021, Werewolf Pack Leader helped push Mono-Green Aggro toward the top of Standard, with Ranger Class and Ochre Jelly as other green options.

After the September 2021 rotation, the set's cards became more important because the older sets were gone. Portable Hole was a cheap answer in white aggro decks, including as a sideboard card against green aggro, and Hall of Storm Giants gave Izzet spell decks a late-game threat that cost no spell slot. The creature-land cycle in general improved mana bases in a format with few good dual lands. No cards from the set were banned in Standard, and it rotated out with Zendikar Rising, Kaldheim and Strixhaven in September 2022, when Dominaria United arrived. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains the yearly cycle.

In Pioneer, the set's cards are role-players rather than format-defining staples: the creature lands, Portable Hole and Werewolf Pack Leader are the kind of cards you'll find in aggro and midrange lists. In Pauper, Deadly Dispute became an important black card-advantage spell in artifact decks, and it was banned there on March 31, 2025, after Wizards judged it too strong alongside cards like Ichor Wellspring.

Commander is where the set lives on most. Old Gnawbone, Tiamat, Lolth, Acererak, Treasure Vault, **Xorn** and **Prosperous Innkeeper** are long-time favorites, and the set's legends gave D&D fans a way to build their favorite characters as commanders. If you're starting there, our guide to [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format. As of October 2026, Old Gnawbone and Treasure Vault remain among the set's most-played Commander cards.

## Cards from Adventures in the Forgotten Realms worth owning

- **Old Gnawbone**: a top Treasure engine and a popular green finisher in Commander.
- **Treasure Vault**: a colorless land that converts spare mana into Treasure for any deck.
- **Werewolf Pack Leader**: an efficient two-drop for green aggro in Pioneer.
- **The creature-land cycle**: Den of the Bugbear, Hive of the Eye Tyrant, Lair of the Hydra, Hall of Storm Giants and Cave of the Frost Dragon make lands do double duty.
- **Deadly Dispute**: card draw and mana in sacrifice decks, though not in Pauper.
- **Tiamat**: the centerpiece of five-color Dragon decks in Commander.
- **Wizard Class and Druid Class**: cheap value enchantments for Commander.

Adventures in the Forgotten Realms is best enjoyed as a flavor set with a few sharp Constructed cards. If you're building with it today, Pioneer green aggro and Commander are the two places its cards still matter most, and you can test a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
