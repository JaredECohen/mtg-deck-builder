Dominaria Remastered is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in January 2023 that collects cards set on Dominaria, the game's oldest and most storied plane, from Alpha through 2019. It was built to be drafted, with old-school mechanics like kicker, cycling, flashback, madness, threshold and storm, and it brought back the classic card frame for half the set. Its headline reprints were Force of Will, Sneak Attack, Vampiric Tutor, Dark Depths and Urza, Lord High Artificer.

## What the set was for

Dominaria Remastered was released the same season as The Brothers' War, which revisited Dominaria's ancient history. The idea was a "greatest hits" tour of the plane: cards from Urza's Saga, Invasion, Odyssey, Onslaught, Time Spiral, Dominaria (2018), Modern Horizons and many more, drawn from 27 different sets.

Remastered sets differ from Masters sets in one key way: they focus on a single world, and they aim to recreate how those old formats felt to play. So alongside the chase reprints, Dominaria Remastered brought back commons like Terror, Counterspell, Werebear and Battle Screech that defined Limited in their original sets. Around 150 of the cards were available in the retro frame, the look Magic used before 2003, which you'll recognize from cards like the original Shivan Dragon.

There's no single story here, but the card selection walks through Dominaria's eras: Urza and the Thran (Urza, Lord High Artificer, Yawgmoth, Thran Physician), the Phyrexian invasion (Spiritmonger, Phyrexian Scuta), the Otarian tales of Odyssey and Onslaught (Kamahl, Fist of Krosa, Chainer, Dementia Master), and the modern Dominaria (Lyra Dawnbringer).

### Retro frames and borderless art

Dominaria Remastered leaned hard on nostalgia in its treatments. Retro-frame cards showed up in Draft and Collector Boosters, every draft booster held at least one retro card plus a retro basic land, and in about a quarter of packs a retro rare or mythic replaced the regular one. The 45 borderless cards, mostly rares and mythics with new art, were exclusive to Collector Boosters. If you're building a Commander deck with an old-school look, the retro versions of cards like Birds of Paradise and Swords to Plowshares fit right in.

## Mechanics

Every mechanic here is a returning one, but many hadn't been seen in years.

### Kicker

Pay an optional extra cost as you cast a spell for a bigger effect. Kavu Primarch is a common 3/3 that gets four +1/+1 counters if kicked (and has convoke, so your creatures can help pay). Phyrexian Scuta kicks by paying 3 life instead of mana.

### Cycling

Pay a cost and discard the card to draw a new one. Twenty-plus cards cycle, from Street Wraith and the cycling lands like Drifting Meadow to Gempalm Incinerator, which deals damage when cycled. Lightning Rift rewards every cycle with a shock.

### Flashback

Cast the card a second time from your graveyard for its flashback cost. Momentary Blink, Deep Analysis, Call of the Herd and Battle Screech give two spells for one card. Battle Screech can even be flashed back by tapping three white creatures, which you get from the first cast.

### Madness

When you discard a madness card, you can cast it for its madness cost instead of putting it in the graveyard. Ichor Slick and Circular Logic pair well with discard outlets and cycling.

### Threshold

Cards get stronger once you have seven or more cards in your graveyard. Werebear becomes a 4/4, and Mystic Enforcer becomes a flying 6/6. Threshold ties neatly into cycling, flashback and self-mill.

### Storm

Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens copy themselves for each spell cast before them that turn. They're the payoffs for the blue-red spells deck.

### Older keywords

Echo (Mogg War Marshal), flanking (Suq'Ata Lancer), vanishing (Deadwood Treefolk) and cumulative upkeep (Mystic Remora) round out the set, the kind of reminder text that our guide to [reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) helps decode.

## Limited

Dominaria Remastered drafted like a trip back to the early 2000s. Packs held one rare or mythic, three uncommons and plenty of classic commons, and the format was slower and less synergy-driven than a modern set. Games often came down to card advantage, removal and a few strong late-game threats rather than fast curves.

The ten two-color archetypes, as described in contemporary draft guides:

- **White-blue:** flicker and bounce with Momentary Blink, Sawtooth Loon and Cloud of Faeries
- **White-black:** cards that cost life, such as Phyrexian Scuta and Urborg Syphon-Mage, paired with lifegain
- **Red-white:** Auras and enchantments, led by Mesa Enchantress
- **Green-white:** threshold with Werebear, Mystic Zealot and Mystic Enforcer
- **Blue-black:** classic control with Counterspell, Terror, Recoil and Thieving Magpie
- **Blue-red:** storm, with Frantic Search and Peregrine Drake setting up Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens
- **Blue-green:** ramp into big spells with Tatyova, Benthic Druid and Kavu Primarch
- **Black-red:** Goblins and Zombies sacrifice with Festering Goblin and Deadapult
- **Black-green:** "The Rock," removal plus big threats like Spiritmonger
- **Red-green:** cycling, with Lightning Rift as a payoff

Premium commons included Terror, Ichor Slick and Counterspell, and uncommons like Spiritmonger and Recoil were among the strongest cards you could take. For the general approach to a slower format like this, see our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

As an all-reprint set, Dominaria Remastered didn't change any format's rules or create new decks. Its value was in supply, and it reached every eternal format:

- **Legacy:** Force of Will is the format's defining counterspell, and Sneak Attack, Dark Depths and Sylvan Library are long-running Legacy cards.
- **Modern:** Urza, Lord High Artificer and Yawgmoth, Thran Physician (both originally from Modern Horizons) are Modern-legal engines. Dark Depths stays banned in Modern as of October 2026.
- **Pioneer:** the check lands, such as Isolated Chapel, Woodland Cemetery and Sulfur Falls, are Pioneer-legal and play a role in two-color mana bases there.
- **Pauper:** Grapeshot, Empty the Warrens and Peregrine Drake are banned in Pauper as of October 2026, while Counterspell and Terror remain common staples.
- **Commander:** the set reprinted seven cards on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026: Vampiric Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, Mystical Tutor, Worldly Tutor, Force of Will, Crop Rotation and Gamble.

For a refresher on how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained), and our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains why tutors are so valued there.

## Cards from Dominaria Remastered worth owning

- **Force of Will**: the most important card in Legacy.
- **Vampiric Tutor** and **Enlightened Tutor**: top-tier Commander tutors.
- **Urza, Lord High Artificer**: a Modern and Commander artifact engine.
- **Yawgmoth, Thran Physician**: a Modern combo piece and Commander favorite.
- **Sylvan Library**: card selection for any green deck in Legacy or Commander.
- **Birds of Paradise**: the classic mana creature.
- **The check lands**: cheap, reliable dual lands for Pioneer and Commander.
