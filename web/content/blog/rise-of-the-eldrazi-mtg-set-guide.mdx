Rise of the Eldrazi is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2010 as the third and final set of the Zendikar block. It's the set where the three Eldrazi titans, Emrakul, Ulamog and Kozilek, break out of their ancient prison on Zendikar, and it threw out the block's land theme to build a new world of colorless monsters, level-up creatures and enormous mana. It also produced one of the most loved draft formats in the game's history and a handful of cards that still define eternal formats.

## The story

For thousands of years, Zendikar had a reputation as a deathtrap: shifting land, violent storms called the Roil and ruins full of traps. Rise of the Eldrazi reveals why. The plane was a prison. Long ago, three planeswalkers, the vampire Sorin Markov, the dragon Ugin and the kor stonemage Nahiri, lured the Eldrazi titans to Zendikar and bound them inside a chamber called the Eye of Ugin, held in place by a network of floating stone hedrons.

The seal had one weakness. It could be loosened by three planeswalker sparks in the Eye at once, combined with Ugin's ghostfire. Nicol Bolas, who wanted planeswalkers drawn to Zendikar for his own reasons, set that up without anyone realizing. Chandra Nalaar followed a trail she'd learned from a stolen scroll into the Eye. Jace Beleren came after her, and Sarkhan Vol, already drifting under Bolas's influence, joined them. Their fight inside the chamber, and the ghostfire Chandra unleashed, cracked the prison open.

The titans' broods started appearing across the plane almost immediately. Sorin returned with the elf animist Nissa Revane and the vampire scholar Anowon, intending to reseal the prison. Nissa, who had learned enough on the journey to make her own call, decided Zendikar would be better off if the Eldrazi simply left. She broke the keystone hedron that Sorin needed for his ritual, and the titans rose. They didn't leave. Instead they began to drain the plane's life, turning land to chalky dust. The story of the novel *Zendikar: In the Teeth of Akoum* covers Nissa's half of this, and the webcomic *Enter the Eldrazi* follows Jace and Sarkhan.

Rise of the Eldrazi ends with the titans loose and Zendikar losing. The fight to save it comes years later in [Battle for Zendikar](/blog/battle-for-zendikar-mtg-set-guide), and Emrakul's own path leads to Innistrad.

## Mechanics

The set was a deliberate reboot of the block. None of Zendikar's landfall or Worldwake's mechanics came back. Instead, the design slowed the game down, gave players tools to reach eight, nine and ten mana, and made the payoff for getting there enormous.

### Eldrazi and Eldrazi Spawn

The Eldrazi are colorless spells with translucent frames. They aren't artifacts, which surprised players at the time, and several of the biggest have a "when you cast" trigger that happens even if the creature is countered. **Kozilek, Butcher of Truth** (mythic, `{10}`) draws four cards on cast. **Ulamog, the Infinite Gyre** (mythic, `{11}`) destroys a permanent on cast and is indestructible. **Emrakul, the Aeons Torn** (mythic, `{15}`) gives you an extra turn on cast, can't be countered and has protection from colored spells. All three shuffle your graveyard back into your library if they hit the graveyard.

To help cast them, many colored cards create **Eldrazi Spawn**: 0/1 colorless tokens you can sacrifice for `{C}`. **Nest Invader** (common) makes one for `{1}{G}`, and **Kozilek's Predator** (common) is a 3/3 that brings two. Spawn chump block, ramp and feed sacrifice effects all at once.

### Annihilator

Annihilator N means that whenever the creature attacks, the defending player sacrifices N permanents. It's the reason the Eldrazi feel apocalyptic: **Ulamog's Crusher** (common) is an 8/8 for `{8}` with annihilator 2, and one attack can take away two lands. Once an annihilator creature starts swinging, the opponent usually has a turn or two left.

### Level up

Level up creatures let you pay mana, at sorcery speed, to add level counters, and they gain new stats and abilities at set thresholds. **Student of Warfare** (rare) costs `{W}` and levels for `{W}`: a 3/3 first striker at level 2 and a 4/4 double striker at level 7. Leveling turns spare mana into a growing threat, which made white and blue level-up decks one of the stories of the draft format.

### Rebound

An instant or sorcery with rebound, cast from your hand, is exiled as it resolves, and on your next upkeep you can cast it again for free. **Staggershock** (common) deals 2 damage twice over two turns, which often meant two dead creatures for one card.

### Totem armor

Totem armor (renamed umbra armor in 2023) appears on Auras. If the enchanted creature would be destroyed, you remove its damage and destroy the Aura instead. **Hyena Umbra** (common, `{W}`) gives +1/+1, first strike and that protection, so Auras became much less risky to play.

## Limited

Rise of the Eldrazi draft is still cited by players and designers as one of the best Limited formats ever. It was built to be slower than Zendikar, where landfall aggro decks often ended games by turn five. Walls and defenders were common, removal was solid, and almost every deck could go big: an Ulamog's Crusher or a Hand of Emrakul at common meant any drafter could land a game-ending threat. (Those two were printed more often than other commons, using spare sheet space.)

Writers at the time described a format where almost every color pair was viable. A few plans stood out:

- **Eldrazi ramp (green-based).** Spawn makers like Nest Invader, Kozilek's Predator and Growth Spasm into Pathrazer of Ulamog, Artisan of Kozilek or the commons Crusher and Hand. Players often took a ninth or tenth land.
- **Blue-white levelers.** Knight of Cliffhaven, Ikiral Outrider and Enclave Cryptologist, with Venerated Teacher to jump them up two levels, was praised as one of the strongest archetypes and often underdrafted.
- **Auras with totem armor.** Green-white built around Hyena Umbra, Snake Umbra and Mammoth Umbra, with Totem-Guide Hartebeest to find them.
- **Aggressive red and black.** Vampires like Bloodthrone Vampire, burn like Staggershock and Flame Slash, and black removal such as Vendetta and Corpsehatch punished slow ramp decks.

The general lesson was patience: games went long, card quality mattered, and the drafter who planned for turn eight usually beat the one who planned for turn four. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2010-2011)

Rise of the Eldrazi arrived into a Standard dominated by Jund and blue-white control. It changed both. **Gideon Jura** gave control a planeswalker that protected the others, and "Super Friends" decks with Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Elspeth, Gideon and Wall of Omens became a top archetype. **Eldrazi Conscription** became the finisher in **Mythic Conscription**, a green-white-blue ramp deck that used Sovereigns of Lost Alara (from Alara Reborn) to fetch the Aura and attach it to an attacker. It was one of the most successful decks of the 2010 Nationals qualifier season. **Vengevine** slotted into Naya and Jund aggro decks.

After the October 2010 rotation, the set kept contributing. **Gideon Jura** appeared in many Caw-Blade lists in early 2011, before Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic were banned in Standard that summer, and the "Eldrazi Green" ramp deck used the titans and spawn makers. In summer 2011, a red-blue **Splinter Twin** combo deck emerged, pairing the Aura with Deceiver Exarch and Pestermite. The set left Standard with the Innistrad rotation on September 30, 2011.

### Modern

- **Splinter Twin** became one of Modern's defining combo decks, and Wizards banned it in January 2016 for squeezing out other decks. It was unbanned in December 2024 along with three other cards.
- **Eldrazi Temple** powered the "Eldrazi Winter" of early 2016, when Battle for Zendikar's cheap Eldrazi took over Modern; Eye of Ugin (from Worldwake) was banned to end it, and Temple stayed legal.
- **Inquisition of Kozilek**, **Ancient Stirrings** and **Kor Spiritdancer** (the engine of some Bogles builds) are long-running Modern cards.

### Legacy and Vintage

**Emrakul, the Aeons Torn** became the classic finisher for Sneak and Show and other Sneak Attack and Show and Tell decks, since putting it onto the battlefield usually ends the game a turn later. **Vengevine** combined so well with Survival of the Fittest that Survival was banned in Legacy (announced December 2010, effective January 1, 2011). **Eldrazi Temple** and **Inquisition of Kozilek** remain common in Eldrazi and black midrange decks. For a refresher on how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

### Pauper and Commander

In Pauper, **Ulamog's Crusher** is a favorite reanimation target, **Kiln Fiend** names a spell-based aggro deck, and **Overgrown Battlement** with **Mnemonic Wall** and **Vent Sentinel** form the Walls combo deck. In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), Kozilek and Ulamog are popular top-end threats, while Emrakul, the Aeons Torn is banned.

## Cards from Rise of the Eldrazi worth owning

- **Inquisition of Kozilek**: one of the best one-mana discard spells, played in Modern and Legacy.
- **Eldrazi Temple**: the mana engine for colorless Eldrazi decks in three formats.
- **Emrakul, the Aeons Torn**: the iconic Sneak and Show finisher in Legacy.
- **Splinter Twin**: back in Modern since the December 2024 unban.
- **All Is Dust**: a one-sided wipe in colorless decks and Commander.
- **Ulamog's Crusher**: still a Pauper staple.
- **Gideon Jura**: a strong, unusual planeswalker for casual and Commander control decks.
