Avacyn Restored is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2012 that closed out the original Innistrad block. It brings back the archangel Avacyn, who returns from her silver prison to drive the demons out of Innistrad, and it introduced two mechanics, miracle and soulbond, alongside a deep pool of angels, humans and some of the most-played cards of its era, from Griselbrand to Cavern of Souls.

## The story

Innistrad is a gothic horror plane where humans survive behind church walls while vampires, werewolves, zombies, ghosts and demons hunt them. Their protector was Avacyn, an archangel created centuries earlier by the vampire planeswalker Sorin Markov, who needed humanity to survive so his own kind would not starve. Before the Innistrad block begins, Avacyn vanished. She had fought the demon lord Griselbrand and sealed him inside the **Helvault**, a huge prison of moon silver, but his last blow caught her and the binding pulled her in too. With its angel gone, the Church of Avacyn lost its holy magic, and the monsters grew bolder every night.

Liliana Vess came to Innistrad for her own reasons. Griselbrand was one of the four demons holding her soul under contract, and she wanted him dead. Unable to break the Helvault herself, she went after its guardian, the cathar Thalia, raising zombies around the people Thalia protected and forcing her to choose between them and the prison. Thalia opened the Helvault, and everything inside poured out at once: hundreds of demons, Griselbrand among them, and Avacyn herself.

Avacyn Restored is the aftermath. Liliana hunts Griselbrand down and kills him, which frees her from one of her four contracts. Avacyn, restored to power, leads her flights of angels against the demons released from the Helvault. The set's legendary angels, **Gisela, Blade of Goldnight**, **Bruna, Light of Alabaster** and **Sigarda, Host of Herons**, lead those flights, and the humans who spent two sets hiding behind walls now fight back with angels overhead. Two new planeswalkers also debut: **Tamiyo, the Moon Sage**, a moonfolk scholar from Kamigawa studying Innistrad's moon, and **Tibalt, the Fiend-Blooded**, a cruel human who claimed devilish power.

The ending is a rare happy one for Magic. By the end of the set, Avacyn has cleaned up most of the demon threat and restored the balance between humans and monsters. That balance holds until Shadows over Innistrad, years later, when it breaks in a much darker way.

## Mechanics

### Miracle

A miracle card has an expensive normal cost and a much cheaper miracle cost. If it is the first card you draw in a turn, you can reveal it and cast it right then for the miracle cost. **Terminus** normally costs `{4}{W}{W}` to put every creature on the bottom of its owner's library, but as a miracle it costs only `{W}`, and since you can draw on your opponent's turn, it can wipe their attack mid-combat. **Bonfire of the Damned** works the same way: a one-sided board wipe that becomes absurdly efficient as a miracle. Miracles reward card-selection tricks that set up the top of your library, which is why they later found a home in Legacy.

### Soulbond

When a soulbond creature enters, or when another creature enters while it is unpaired, you can pair the two. While both stay on the battlefield, they share a bonus. **Silverblade Paladin** gives itself and its partner double strike, and **Wolfir Silverheart** turns itself and its partner into huge threats with +4/+4 each. Soulbond made creature combat swingy and rewarded keeping creatures on the battlefield, but it also meant one removal spell could break up two creatures' worth of value.

### Undying

Undying returned from Dark Ascension: when a creature with undying dies, if it had no +1/+1 counter on it, it comes back with one. **Butcher Ghoul** is the common example, and **Hound of Griselbrand** is a double striker that has to be killed twice.

### Angels and humans

Beyond the keywords, the set leans on two creature types. Angels are the big flying payoff, and humans, especially in white and red, are the aggressive core. **Angel of Glory's Rise** exiles every Zombie and returns all your Human creature cards from your graveyard to the battlefield, which shows how the two themes connect. Some notes on the groups behind that table:

- **White and the angels.** Restoration Angel was the breakout rare, a four-mana instant-speed flier that reused enter-the-battlefield effects. Angel of Jubilation, Herald of War and Cathars' Crusade fed angel and human decks, and Seraph of Dawn was a common that took over Limited games.
- **Blue.** Tamiyo was the set's control planeswalker. Temporal Mastery and Devastation Tide were the blue miracles, and Deadeye Navigator, which lets a paired creature blink itself repeatedly, became a Commander combo piece.
- **Black.** Griselbrand is the headline. Harvester of Souls drew a card whenever another nontoken creature died, and Blood Artist plus Exquisite Blood became a famous Commander pairing.
- **Red.** Bonfire of the Damned and Zealous Conscripts were the Constructed stars. Conscripts steals any permanent for a turn, which made it a combo piece with Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker.
- **Green.** Craterhoof Behemoth, Wolfir Silverheart and Soul of the Harvest made green a creature-heavy color. Somberwald Sage and Primal Surge are Commander favorites.
- **Lands.** Cavern of Souls is the most important land in the set, and Alchemist's Refuge, Desolate Lighthouse and Slayers' Stronghold are two-color utility lands.

## Limited

Avacyn Restored broke from the usual block structure for draft: instead of mixing in Innistrad and Dark Ascension packs, players drafted three packs of Avacyn Restored alone. That made it a self-contained format, which was unusual in 2012 and became the norm later.

The format was built around soulbond and the human/angel split. White was deep, with Seraph of Dawn, a common 2/4 flying lifelinker that was very hard to race, and white decks paired well with red humans that attacked early. Blue-white fliers used spirits and soulbond cards like Wingcrafter, which gives itself and its partner flying. Green soulbond creatures such as Trusted Forcemage and Nightshade Peddler (which hands out deathtouch) made green-based midrange decks strong in combat. Black offered removal like Death Wind and value creatures like Undead Executioner, and red had Pillar of Flame and the miracle Thunderous Wrath.

The format was also known for its rares. Rares and mythics like Bonfire of the Damned, Champion of Lambholt and Restoration Angel could take over a game on their own, so the format often rewarded opening a strong rare. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Block Constructed.** Pro Tour Avacyn Restored in Barcelona (May 2012) used Innistrad Block Constructed, and it was won by Alexander Hayne with a blue-white Miracles deck built to set up and cast cheap miracle spells.

**Standard.** Adoption in Standard was slow at first, with Delver of Secrets decks from the previous season holding on. Over the following year, Restoration Angel became a fixture in blue-white and Bant flash and midrange decks, both as a flash threat and as a way to reuse creatures like Thragtusk from Magic 2013. Bonfire of the Damned saw play in red-based decks, Tamiyo appeared in control, and Cavern of Souls let creature decks ignore countermagic. Avacyn Restored rotated out of Standard with the rest of the Innistrad block when Theros arrived in September 2013. None of its cards were banned in Standard.

**Modern.** Avacyn Restored is a Modern staple supplier. Cavern of Souls is a key land for Humans and other tribal decks. Restoration Angel has been played in many midrange and combo decks, including Kiki-Jiki combo builds, because a token copy of Restoration Angel can blink Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker and let it copy the Angel again. Griselbrand has shown up as a reanimation or cheat target. No Avacyn Restored card is banned in Modern as of October 2026.

**Legacy.** Griselbrand became one of the best targets for reanimator and Sneak and Show decks. Craterhoof Behemoth is the finisher for Elves. Terminus anchored the Miracles control deck, which used top-of-library manipulation to make miracles reliable.

**Commander.** The set is a Commander favorite: Avacyn, Angel of Hope, Craterhoof Behemoth, Cathars' Crusade, Blood Artist, Exquisite Blood, Deadeye Navigator and Gisela, Blade of Goldnight are all common sights. Griselbrand is banned in Commander. For how power levels are handled at the casual table, see [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

**Pauper.** Ghostly Flicker, a common, has been a staple of Pauper's blink and combo decks.

For the rest of the block, see our [Innistrad set guide](/blog/innistrad-mtg-set-guide).

## Cards from Avacyn Restored worth owning

- **Cavern of Souls**: still the defining tribal land in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Craterhoof Behemoth**: the go-wide finisher in Legacy Elves and Commander.
- **Griselbrand**: a premier reanimator target in Legacy.
- **Restoration Angel**: a flexible Modern and casual staple.
- **Blood Artist**: one of the defining aristocrats cards in Commander.
- **Avacyn, Angel of Hope** and **Cathars' Crusade**: core pieces of angel and counters decks.
- **Ghostly Flicker**: an important Pauper common.

## The takeaway

Avacyn Restored is one of the strongest sets of its era. It gave Modern and Legacy several lasting staples, gave Commander a long list of favorites, and miracle remains one of the most memorable mechanics Wizards has printed.
