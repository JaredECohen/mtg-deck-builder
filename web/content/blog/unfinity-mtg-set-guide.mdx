Unfinity is the fifth Magic: The Gathering "Un-set," the line of humor sets that break the game's usual rules, released on October 7, 2022 after a delay from its planned April 1 date. Set aboard a traveling space carnival, it introduced Attractions, stickers and tickets, and it was the first Un-set printed with black borders, mixing tournament-legal cards with "acorn" cards meant only for casual play.

## What the set was for

Un-sets exist to let designers make the jokes and wild mechanics that would never work in a normal set. Unfinity's theme mixes retro science fiction with circus, amusement park and game show tropes. Its employees are Goblins, Elves, Vampires and Zombies working the rides, and its guests are aliens, robots and squirrels. There's no storyline to follow; the flavor lives in the card names and art, and Magic itself is a running joke throughout, with cards that care about names, art, hats and even what you're wearing. Outside writers, including the comedy writer Seanbaby and the LoadingReadyRun group, helped with the creative work.

### Acorn cards and black borders

The big change was the border. Earlier Un-sets were silver-bordered, which over time came to mean "not legal anywhere," even in casual formats like Commander. Head designer Mark Rosewater wanted many of Unfinity's cards to be playable in Eternal formats, so the set went black-bordered and used the security stamp instead:

- **Acorn stamp:** a casual-only card, the modern equivalent of silver border. Use it only if everyone at the table agrees. **Pietra, Crafter of Clowns**, which asks you to tell a joke to someone outside the game, is an acorn card.
- **Oval stamp, or no stamp at common and uncommon:** a normal card, legal in Commander, Legacy and Vintage.

According to the MTG Wiki, 135 of the 244 cards are Eternal-legal, slightly over half the set, while most of the rares and mythics are acorn. The split was decided late in development, which is why some of the legal cards are as strange as they are. If you're unsure how to tell the two apart, the security stamp sits at the bottom of the card; see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Mechanics

### Attractions

Attractions are artifacts without a mana cost that live in a separate Attraction deck of at least ten cards, outside your main deck, with their own card back. When a card tells you to **open an Attraction**, you put the top card of that deck onto the battlefield. At the beginning of your first main phase each turn, you roll a six-sided die and **visit** every Attraction whose lit-up number you rolled, triggering its ability. Six is always lit; one never is.

Attraction makers include **Myra the Magnificent**, which opens an Attraction whenever you cast an instant or sorcery from your hand. Payoffs like **Black Hole** reward you for visits: it destroys a creature and one more for each Attraction you visited this turn.

### Stickers and tickets

Each Draft Booster includes a sticker sheet with name, art, ability and power/toughness stickers. Cards tell you to put stickers on cards you own, and the adhesive is meant to come off without damaging the card (in practice, players found stickers fell off more easily than intended). Name and art stickers are free; ability and power/toughness stickers cost tickets, a new counter type you earn from cards like those with **sticker kicker** (pay `{1}` extra on a creature spell to get a ticket and add a sticker).

The best-known sticker card is a common: **_____ Goblin**, a 2/2 for `{2}{R}` that lets you put a name sticker on it and adds `{R}` for each unique vowel on that sticker. It was the card that broke the system in Legacy (see the meta section).

### Die rolling and one-offs

Dice are everywhere. **Six-Sided Die** is a removal common with a different result for each roll, and **Clown Car** rolls X dice to make Clown Robots or grow itself. Unfinity also has one-off joke mechanics, among them **Parade!** on **Starlight Spectacular**, which lines up your creatures and gives each one +1/+1 for every creature ahead of it, and **space sculptor** on **Space Beleren**, which splits the battlefield into sectors.

## Limited

Unfinity was designed for draft, and its ten two-color archetypes are built around its gimmicks:

- **White-blue:** name stickers and evasion
- **Blue-black:** precise die-rolling control
- **Black-red:** high rolls
- **Red-green:** mass die rolling
- **Green-white:** ability stickers and going tall
- **White-black:** hats and recursion
- **Blue-red:** art stickers and tempo
- **Black-green:** Attractions and attrition
- **Red-white:** Clown Robot aggro
- **Green-blue:** power/toughness stickers and ramp

The format plays like a normal draft with extra steps. Every player needs a ten-card Attraction deck, so packs carry two Attractions each, and you can use any Attractions you open. Removal like **Six-Sided Die** and **Attempted Murder** (an X-cost spell that adds -1/-1 counters on even rolls and makes 1/2 flying Storm Crows on odd ones) carried a lot of weight. For a casual draft night, Unfinity is about as different from a normal set as Magic gets; the [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide) covers how groups run their own drafts.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Unfinity's legal cards were never part of Standard, Pioneer or Modern, so its competitive story happened in Legacy and Pauper, and it's short.

For about eighteen months, sticker and Attraction cards were legal in Eternal formats. **_____ Goblin** turned out to be a strong mana creature in Legacy, and that created an odd tournament problem: players had to bring sticker sheets to events, and decks with clone effects felt they needed sticker sheets too, in case they copied it. On May 13, 2024, Wizards banned every card that puts a sticker or an Attraction into the game in Legacy, Vintage and Pauper, and coverage counted dozens of cards hit in one announcement.

As of October 2026, those cards remain legal in Commander, according to Scryfall. Playing them means bringing sticker sheets or an Attraction deck to the table, so check with your group first; [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) are a good way to start that conversation.

Unfinity's legal non-sticker cards found real homes in Commander. **Saw in Half** became a popular way to copy your own creatures' enter-the-battlefield abilities, and **Clown Car** and **Comet, Stellar Pup** made it into dice and token decks.

## Cards from Unfinity worth owning

- **Saw in Half:** a flexible black instant that doubles as removal and a value engine.
- **Comet, Stellar Pup:** a fun, swingy Boros commander or planeswalker for dice decks.
- **Clown Car:** a scalable colorless Vehicle for any token deck.
- **Starlight Spectacular:** a strong anthem for wide white decks.
- **Magar of the Magic Strings:** a unique Rakdos spellslinger commander.
- **The full-art shocklands:** among the most striking versions of the Ravnica dual lands.

Unfinity is a set to play for fun first: build around dice or a goofy commander and keep the acorn cards for nights when everyone agrees. If dice-rolling sounds good, Comet, Stellar Pup is a ready-made commander to build around in the [MTG Deck Lab builder](/).
