Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles is a Universes Beyond premier set that brought Leonardo, Donatello, Michelangelo and Raphael into Magic: The Gathering in March 2026. It's set in the Turtles' New York City and pulls from four decades of comics, cartoons, toys and films. Its headline mechanic, sneak, is a new take on ninjutsu, and it made its way into Standard, Modern and Pauper decks within weeks.

## The story

Universes Beyond sets adapt an existing franchise rather than telling a new Magic storyline, and this one doesn't follow a single plot. The designers drew from several eras of the Turtles: Kevin Eastman and Peter Laird's original Mirage comics, the 1987 and 2003 animated series, the toy line and the live-action films. The set is a greatest-hits collection, and each brother appears on several cards, from commons up to mythics, in different eras and moods.

The core story is the one every version shares. Four baby turtles and a rat are exposed to a glowing mutagen ooze in the sewers beneath New York. The rat, Splinter, a master of ninjutsu, raises the turtles as his sons and trains them, and they grow into teenage ninjas who fight crime from the shadows. Their great enemy is Oroku Saki, the Shredder, who leads the Foot Clan. Their closest human allies are reporter April O'Neil and the hockey-masked vigilante Casey Jones. You'll find all of them here, including **Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising**, **April O'Neil, Kunoichi Trainee** and **Casey Jones, Vigilante**.

The rogues' gallery leans heavily on the 1987 cartoon and toy line. Krang, the brain-like alien warlord, shows up as **Krang, Master Mind** and **Krang, Utrom Warlord**, alongside the bumbling henchmen **Bebop & Rocksteady** and the rolling fortress **Technodrome**. Fan favorites from the comics and toys round out the cast: **Leatherhead, Swamp Stalker**, **Slash, Reptile Rampager**, **Mondo Gecko**, **Rat King, Verminister** and the time-traveling wizard Savanti Romero.

Several cards nod to specific stories. **The Last Ronin** is named after the grim future storyline in which one aging Turtle outlives his brothers. **Turtles in Time** shares its name with the classic arcade game. **Turtles Forever** shows several incarnations of the team at once, a reference to the multiverse crossover that unites Turtles from different eras. If you've seen any version of the Turtles, you'll recognize the setting: pizza, rooftops, sewer lairs and a lot of ninja tricks.

## Mechanics

### Sneak

Sneak is the headline mechanic. During the declare blockers step, after blocks are set, you can return one of your unblocked attackers to your hand and cast a card with sneak for its sneak cost. A creature cast this way enters tapped and attacking. It's close to ninjutsu, with two big differences: you actually cast the card, so it can be countered, and sneak also appears on instants and sorceries.

Example: **Leonardo, Big Brother** (common) normally costs `{2}{W}`, but you can sneak him in for `{W}`, and he gets +1/+0 for each other creature you control. Sneak makes evasive, cheap creatures valuable and makes blocking decisions much harder for your opponent.

### Mutagen tokens

Mutagen is a new predefined artifact token. You pay `{1}`, tap it and sacrifice it to put a +1/+1 counter on a target creature, at sorcery speed. Green-blue is the Mutagen color pair. **Mutagen Man, Living Ooze** makes X of them and makes your artifact tokens' abilities cheaper.

### Alliance and disappear

Alliance returns from Streets of New Capenna and triggers whenever another creature enters under your control. **Raphael, Tough Turtle** pings an opponent for 1 each time. Disappear is the new name for a revolt-style check: it rewards you if a permanent you controlled left the battlefield this turn. **Rat King, Verminister** makes a Rat and grows at your end step when disappear is on.

### Class enchantments and team-ups

Rare Class enchantments return. You level them up at sorcery speed to unlock new abilities. **Cool but Rude** turns attacks into loots, then into burn whenever you discard. Brother team-ups like **Raph & Mikey, Troublemakers** use hybrid mana costs, so they fit into more decks. ## Limited

Draft reviewers settled on a few clear winners.

- **White-black: sneak.** The strongest overall deck for many players. Cheap creatures and removal set up sneak plays, and Oroku Saki is the best sneak common. **Uneasy Alliance** handles big threats, and April O'Neil, Kunoichi Trainee slips past large blockers.
- **Blue-red: artifacts.** **Mouser Foundry** and **Sewer-veillance Cam** are premium commons, and **Brilliance Unleashed** is a strong gold uncommon.
- **Green-based midrange.** Green's depth supports bombs, removal like **Tenderize** and big bodies. **Frog Butler** fixes your mana so well that splashing is easy.
- **Red-white: alliance.** Go-wide with alliance payoffs like **Mighty Mutanimals** and removal such as **Bot Bashing Time**.
- **Black-green: disappear.** The official sac-and-recur deck, generally considered slower and clunkier. **Omni-Cheese Pizza** helps enable it.
- **Green-blue: Mutagen.** Counter synergies with **Return to the Sewers** as a key common, though reviewers found it inconsistent.

The format is generally on the slow side unless someone pulls off an early sneak, and an early Oroku Saki or The Last Ronin's Technique can snowball. Cheap deathtouch creatures like **Squirrelanoids** are unusually good, and the format has few sweepers. The Draft Night product uses a pick-two format for four players, which makes it a nice kitchen-table option.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

The set made a fast impression. Within its first weeks, **Leatherhead, Swamp Stalker** was showing up in most major green Standard decks, including Mono-Green Landfall. It gave aggressive green decks a hexproof threat that also answers artifacts and enchantments. By late July 2026, Frank Karsten's metagame breakdown listed Leatherhead as a key card in Selesnya Offense, the second-most-popular Standard deck at the time.

Other early sightings included **Cool but Rude**, **Casey Jones, Vigilante** and **Tokka & Rahzar, Terrible Twos** in red discard-aggro decks. The Last Ronin's Technique showed up in Boros token decks, and Michelangelo's Technique dug for combo pieces in creature-based shells. **North Wind Avatar**, which grabs a card from outside the game, appeared in an Omniscience list online, and Krang, Master Mind and Ravenous Robots fed an artifact deck that placed well at a Regional Championship. None of the set's cards had been banned in Standard as of October 2026. The Standard bans that August hit Badgermole Cub and other cards from other sets.

In Modern, **Leonardo, Cutting Edge** took a slot in the Selesnya life-gain combo deck built on Walking Ballista and Agatha's Soul Cauldron, since he costs one less than the creature he replaced and can be sneaked in for one mana. Pauper picked up the legendary commons: **Leonardo, Big Brother** in Mono-White Aggro and Oroku Saki in Orzhov decks. For Commander, the set comes with a five-color preconstructed deck featuring the Turtles and Splinter, and team-ups, Ninjas and artifact legends give deck builders plenty to work with. ## Cards from Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles worth owning

- **Leatherhead, Swamp Stalker**: a proven Standard threat for green aggro.
- **Leonardo, Cutting Edge**: a two-drop that found a home in Modern life gain.
- **Super Shredder**: a cheap, scaling threat for sacrifice decks in Constructed and Commander.
- **The Last Ronin**: a board wipe with a payoff built in, popular in black-green Commander decks.
- **Splinter, Radical Rat**: doubling Ninja triggers makes him a natural commander.
- **Leonardo, Big Brother** and **Oroku Saki, Shredder Rising**: commons that matter in Pauper, and easy to pick up for [budget decks](/blog/budget-mtg-decks-under-50).
