Magic: The Gathering | Star Trek is a Universes Beyond expansion that releases on November 13, 2026, with prereleases running November 6 through 12 and an MTG Arena launch on November 10. It celebrates the franchise's 60th anniversary by pulling captains, crews, starships and villains from nearly every era of Star Trek into one draftable, Standard-legal set. This is a preview guide written during prerelease week: everything below comes from the official reveals and the card file as previewed, and the Constructed picture is still a forecast.

| --- | --- |

Wizards lists the Commander decks (TRC) and the Stardates bonus sheet (SDS) as legal in Commander, Legacy and Vintage only, unless a card was already legal elsewhere. ## The story

Most Magic sets tell one new story across a single plane. Star Trek works differently: it's a tribute to six decades of television and film, so instead of one plot it gives you a sampler of the franchise's most famous moments. Wizards' product page lists the eras on the cards: The Original Series and its films, The Next Generation, Deep Space Nine, Voyager, Strange New Worlds, Lower Decks and the Kelvin-timeline films.

The legendary creatures are the set's cast list. On the bridge you'll find Captain James T. Kirk, Captain Jean-Luc Picard, Captain Kathryn Janeway, Captain Benjamin Sisko, Captain Jonathan Archer and Captain Pike, Fullness of Time. Their crews follow: Mr. Spock, Science Officer, Lieutenant Commander Data, Lt. Cmdr. Geordi La Forge, Nyota Uhura, Communications, Scotty, Miracle Worker, Dr. Beverly Crusher, Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy and Kira Nerys, Firebrand. Even the comedy of Lower Decks gets a seat, with Brad Boimler, Eager Ensign at uncommon.

The antagonists are just as well represented. The Borg arrive as an artifact-creature faction that absorbs other creatures, led by cards like Borg Cube and Locutus, the One Who Speaks. Khan, Vengeful Heart and the saga The Wrath of Khan retell the franchise's most famous film rivalry. Klingons like Gowron, Eager for Battle and General Chang, Cold Warrior bring a sacrifice-and-aggression theme, while Q, Stacking the Deck plays the trickster who bends the rules of the game itself.

Several sagas turn specific episodes into three-chapter cards. The City on the Edge of Forever phases out every artifact and creature, The Inner Light rebuilds a life from your graveyard, All Good Things... draws you through a four-chapter finale, and Year of Hell damages everyone every turn. Even the Kobayashi Maru gets a card, Kobayashi Maru Scenario, which gives you a small bonus on each of three upkeeps and then makes you lose the game on the fourth unless you get rid of it first. That's about as faithful to the no-win test as a card can be.

There's no single ending to spoil. The set's "conflict" is the franchise's ongoing tension between curiosity and threat: exploring strange worlds on one side, the Borg, Klingon warlords and mirror-universe tyrants on the other.

## Mechanics

Star Trek brings five new named mechanics and borrows two from Edge of Eternities. All the descriptions below are based on Wizards' mechanics article and the previewed oracle text.

### Federation

Federation is an ability word for effects that count the number of creature types among your non-Borg creatures. A board of a Human Officer, a Vulcan Scientist and a Klingon Warrior counts five types, not three creatures. Hoshi Sato, Exolinguist looks at that many cards when she enters, and Saavik, Stoic Student deals that much damage to an attacking or blocking creature. The Borg exclusion matters: Borg creatures add nothing to the count. It pushes you toward a diverse crew instead of a single tribe.

### Strange new worlds

This ability word counts the number of differently named lands you control. Each name counts once, so a Forest, an Island and two Plains count as three. The Traveler is a two-mana reach creature whose power equals that number, and Deep Space Scan draws that many cards. The set supports it with ten common Planet dual lands (Vulcan, Bajor, Qo'noS and friends) and rare lands like Quark's Bar. It rewards decks that play a wide mix of lands and let the game go long.

### Face a dilemma

You face a dilemma whenever you choose one or more modes for a modal spell or ability. That's the trigger; what happens to the spell afterward doesn't matter. Seven of Nine draws a card each time, Obsidian Order Operative drains for 1, and Elim Garak, Simple Tailor drains for 2. Modal commons like Command Decision (destroy a big creature, or draw a card) feed the payoffs naturally, and creatures with modal enter triggers count too.

### Beam me up

Beam me up is a graveyard-casting keyword on instants and sorceries. You can cast the card from your graveyard for its beam me up cost if you also return a creature you control to its owner's hand, and then it's exiled. Open Communications is a one-mana sorcery that draws a card, and later you can recast it for `{2}{U}` by bouncing a creature. Energize bounces an opposing creature for `{1}{U}`, then does it again from the graveyard. The cost is a real tempo loss unless the creature you return has a good enter-the-battlefield effect, which is exactly the point.

### Assimilate

Assimilate is a keyword action: you put a creature card onto the battlefield under your control with a +1/+1 counter on it, and it becomes a Borg artifact creature, losing its other creature types. If the creature is already on the battlefield, you gain control of it first. Borg Cube assimilates creatures it has exiled, and Death Is Irrelevant assimilates a creature card from any graveyard at each of your end steps while making your artifact creatures indestructible.

### Spacecraft and station (returning)

Spacecraft are artifacts that you power up by tapping your other creatures. Station taps a creature as a sorcery and adds charge counters equal to its power; at the printed threshold the ship gains abilities, and most become artifact creatures. U.S.S. Enterprise-D, Galaxy-Class costs three and lets you play an exiled card from the top of your library each turn you first add charge counters to it. Lt. Cmdr. Geordi La Forge doubles charge counters, which makes ships come online twice as fast.

The set also leans on red 1/1 Officer tokens. Captain Jean-Luc Picard makes one whenever you cast a creature or Spacecraft spell and pumps your team; Captain James T. Kirk can make one each time he enters or attacks.

## Limited

The gold uncommons map out ten two-color archetypes, which fits Mark Rosewater's comment, reported during previews, that the set has ten draft archetypes. Read the signposts below as a preview based on card text, not a verdict from play:

- **White-blue Spacecraft:** Hemmer, Fixing What Is Broken protects your ships on your turn and loots whenever he becomes tapped, including when you tap him to station a ship.
- **Blue-black dilemmas:** Seven of Nine draws a card each time you choose a mode.
- **Black-red Klingons and sacrifice:** L'Rell, Fierce Visionary makes a hasty 2/1 Warrior every turn and gives you a sacrifice outlet.
- **Red-green landfall:** Dal R'El, Augment Orphan and Cleveland Booker, Empath reward extra land drops.
- **Green-white counters:** Phlox, Unorthodox Doctor draws when creatures with counters connect, and The Doctor, EMH spreads counters.
- **White-black Borg artifacts:** Locutus, the One Who Speaks pumps your artifact creatures and doubles as removal from your hand.
- **Blue-red spells:** Spock, Always Your Friend digs for noncreature spells and Gray and Adira, Forever Joined copies one of your creatures each combat after you've cast a noncreature spell.
- **Black-green graveyard:** Vash, Freelance Archaeologist surveils every turn and draws when cards leave your graveyard.
- **Red-white big attacks:** Kirk, Commanding Officer and Worf, Honor-Bound Warrior care about attacking with 8 or more total power.
- **Green-blue land names:** The Traveler grows with each new land name, and the Planet duals help.

At common, cheap removal looks strong: He's Dead, Jim, Plasma Cascade and 70,000 Light-Years from Home will be early picks. The Planet duals are commons that both fix your mana and count toward strange new worlds, so they're better here than gain-lands usually are. Spacecraft reward decks with plenty of creatures, so prioritize bodies over tricks.

Speed is a guess until people play it. Station and strange new worlds both reward long games, while Klingon tokens and red-white attack triggers push the other way. Expect a midrange format with aggressive outliers. ## The meta: how it may play in Constructed

As of prerelease week, Star Trek hasn't been legal in a single sanctioned Constructed event, so there's no meta yet. What follows is what the card file suggests, not results.

The cards most often discussed as Standard candidates are cheap, efficient ones. Salt Vampire is a two-mana lifelink creature that strips a key card from the opponent's hand. Subcommander T'Pol offers flash interaction that can stop an activated ability or lock down a creature. Captain James T. Kirk is a three-mana engine for red aggro, and his "choose one or more" clause rewards emptying your hand. Captain Jean-Luc Picard is a three-mana anthem that keeps making bodies, a classic shape for go-wide white decks.

Bigger build-arounds will get tried too. Mr. Spock, Science Officer doubles all your draws. Captain Kathryn Janeway gives an extra land drop and explores with every creature, which slots naturally into the green landfall decks that were the most popular strategy in Standard RCQs in September 2026. Dr. Beverly Crusher doubles life-gain triggers, which could interest white life-gain decks.

For Commander, the set is already a favorite: legendary captains in every color, the four precon decks, and engines like Dr. Joseph M'Benga, which doubles counters on your creatures and gives them hexproof. The Stardates sheet is Commander-only, so check legality before you sleeve those cards for Standard.

No Star Trek card has been banned or restricted anywhere; the set isn't out yet. The first weeks after November 13 will show which of these ideas hold up.

## Cards from Star Trek worth owning

- **Captain Jean-Luc Picard:** an anthem plus token engine that fits Standard go-wide decks and Commander alike.
- **Mr. Spock, Science Officer:** card-draw doubling is a long-term Commander staple and a possible Constructed engine.
- **Dr. Joseph M'Benga:** counter doubling plus hexproof will be a Commander mainstay in +1/+1 counter decks.
- **Salt Vampire:** cheap disruption on a lifelink body, the kind of card Constructed decks pick up.
- **The shock lands:** every color pair's mana is better with them, in Standard and Commander.
- **Captain Kathryn Janeway:** a strong Commander for green landfall and counters builds.

## The takeaway

Star Trek is a franchise tribute with real gameplay hooks: Federation rewards a mixed crew, strange new worlds rewards a wide land mix, and dilemmas turn every modal card into value. At the prerelease, take cheap removal and Planet duals early, and build around creatures so your Spacecraft come online. For Standard, keep an eye on Salt Vampire, Kirk and Picard, and see how their deck types fit the [common archetypes](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) once real results arrive.
