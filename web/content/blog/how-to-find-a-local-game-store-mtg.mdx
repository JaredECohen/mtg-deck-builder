A local game store (players usually say LGS) is the easiest place to find people to play Magic: The Gathering with in person. Most stores that run Magic host regular casual nights, Friday Night Magic and prereleases, and nearly all of them welcome new players. You don't need a tournament deck or much experience to show up: bring a deck if you have one, ask the staff what's running, and you'll usually find a game.

This guide covers how to find a store, what the common events are like, and the etiquette that makes in-store games go smoothly. If you're brand new to the game itself, the [beginner's guide to playing Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) is a better first stop.

## How to find a store

There are several reliable ways to find a store near you.

- **The official store locator.** Wizards of the Coast runs a store and event locator that lists stores in its official play network, along with the events they host. Searching for "Wizards store locator" will turn it up.
- **Map searches.** Searching a maps app for "game store" or "card shop" finds most stores, including ones that focus on board games or other card games but still run Magic.
- **Ask around.** Friends who play, local community groups and online forums for your city often know which stores have the most active Magic scene.
- **Check the store's own pages.** Many stores post their weekly schedule on a website or social media page. Look for which nights run Magic and in which formats.

Not every store is the same. Some focus on competitive play, some on Commander, some on drafting. If the first store you visit isn't a great fit, try another. It's common for players to have a different favorite store for different formats.

### What to look for

A good store for a newer player has:

- **Regular casual events**, like a weekly Commander night or a beginner-friendly draft.
- **Enough space and tables** to sit and play comfortably.
- **Staff who'll answer questions** about events and formats.
- **A clean, welcoming atmosphere.** You'll get a feel for this within a visit or two.

## Friday Night Magic

**Friday Night Magic** (FNM) is a long-running weekly event that stores in Wizards' official play network can host. It's meant to be a social, entry-level way to play organized Magic, not a high-stakes tournament. Most stores run it on Friday evenings, as the name suggests.

The format depends on the store. Common choices include:

- **Draft**, where each player opens booster packs and picks cards one at a time to build a 40-card deck.
- **Constructed formats** like Standard, Pioneer or Modern, where you bring a 60-card deck you built in advance.
- **Commander**, usually in casual pods of four.

The [MTG formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) explains how the main formats differ. If you're not sure which FNM to try, a draft night is often the friendliest choice for a newer player, because everyone builds a new deck on the spot and nobody brought an expensive tuned list.

FNM is usually run at a relaxed level of rules enforcement. Mistakes are fixed and explained rather than heavily penalized, and the focus is on having fun and learning.

## Other events

Beyond FNM, stores commonly run:

- **Prereleases.** Held before a new set is released, prereleases let players open packs of the new cards and build a sealed deck. They're social and beginner-friendly. The [prerelease guide](/blog/mtg-sealed-prerelease-guide) explains what to bring and how to build your deck.
- **Commander nights.** Casual multiplayer Commander, often without prizes. These are among the most welcoming events for newer players.
- **Constructed events.** Weekly events for formats like Modern or Pioneer, sometimes more competitive than FNM.
- **Learn-to-play sessions.** Some stores run events specifically for new players, with demo decks and someone to teach.

Entry fees, prizes and formats vary from store to store, so check the schedule or ask the staff before you go.

## Store etiquette

Following a few habits makes you a player people are happy to sit down with.

### Before the game

- **Bring what you need.** Your deck in sleeves, a way to track life (dice or a notepad) and any tokens your deck makes.
- **Sleeve your cards.** Sleeves protect your cards and make shuffling easier. In events, sleeves should be uniform and unmarked.
- **Shuffle thoroughly and offer your deck to be cut.** Present your shuffled deck to your opponent so they can shuffle or cut it. This is standard practice at any table.
- **Introduce yourself.** A quick hello and handshake (or wave) starts things on the right foot.
- **In Commander, talk about power level.** A quick conversation about how strong your decks are, often called rule zero, makes for better games. The [rule zero guide](/blog/commander-rule-zero-guide) covers how to have that talk.

### During the game

- **Communicate clearly.** Say what you're casting, what you're targeting and when you're moving to combat or passing the turn. Clear communication avoids most disputes.
- **Keep your cards visible and tidy.** Keep lands and creatures in separate, easy-to-read areas, and make it clear which permanents are tapped.
- **Track life totals out loud.** Saying "I'm at 14, you're at 12" after combat keeps both players in sync.
- **Ask before touching.** Ask to read an opponent's card rather than picking it up.
- **Play at a reasonable pace.** Take time to think on big decisions, but don't stall on routine ones. In events, rounds have a time limit.
- **Call a judge or staff member when something goes wrong.** If you and your opponent disagree on a rule, ask for help rather than arguing. It's not an accusation, and nobody should take it as one.

### After the game

- **Shake hands and say "good game."** Win or lose, it's the expected end to a match.
- **Report results promptly** if it's an event, so the next round can start on time.
- **Clean up.** Throw away wrappers and empty cups, and push your chair in.

### Respect the store

The store gives you a place to play, often for free or a small fee. A few ways to return that:

- **Buy something when you can.** Packs, sleeves, snacks or singles all help keep the store open.
- **Treat the space well.** Don't eat messy food over other people's cards, and keep your belongings out of walkways.
- **Look after hygiene.** Long evenings in a crowded room are more pleasant for everyone when players arrive showered and in clean clothes.

## How to be welcoming

Every regular at a game store was once new. Making new players feel welcome is how a store's community grows.

- **Invite people in.** If someone is standing around with a deck, ask if they want a game.
- **Match their energy.** If a new player sits down with a simple deck, playing your strongest list against them isn't fun for either of you. If you have a gentler deck, use it.
- **Explain rather than correct.** When a newer player makes a rules mistake, explain what happens and why, without making a big deal of it.
- **Don't judge their deck or collection.** A pile of commons is a perfectly valid way to play. Advice is helpful when asked for and unwelcome when not.
- **Watch for people on the edges.** Players who sit alone or seem nervous are the ones a friendly word helps most.
- **Keep comments kind.** Trash talk that's fun between friends can feel hostile to someone you've just met.

If you've been playing a while and enjoy teaching, offering to show someone the basics is one of the best things you can do for your local scene. The [guide to teaching Magic](/blog/how-to-teach-someone-magic) has tips on doing it without overwhelming them.

## The takeaway

Use the official store locator, a maps search or local players to find stores near you, then check their schedules for casual nights, FNM and prereleases. A draft FNM or Commander night is a friendly place to start. Bring a sleeved deck and a way to track life, communicate clearly, ask a judge or staff member when you disagree on a rule, and treat the store and other players well. Once you're a regular, pay it forward by welcoming the next new player.
