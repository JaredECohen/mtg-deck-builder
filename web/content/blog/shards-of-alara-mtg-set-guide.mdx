Shards of Alara is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2008 that split a world into five three-color pieces and built a whole set around them. It introduced the mythic rare rarity, brought back planeswalkers with the first multicolored ones, and started the story of Nicol Bolas's plot to reunite the shattered plane of Alara.

## The story

Alara was once a single plane, rich in mana. Thousands of years ago it was broken apart, and each fragment, called a **shard**, kept only three of the five colors of mana. Over time, each shard developed into its own world, shaped by the colors it kept and the two it lost:

- **Bant** (green-white-blue, centered on white) is a land of knights, castles and strict social castes, where the angels watch over an orderly society that has forgotten what war really is.
- **Esper** (white-blue-black, centered on blue) is a world of mages obsessed with perfection, who graft a magical metal called etherium onto their bodies.
- **Grixis** (blue-black-red, centered on black) is a dying hellscape where life energy is so scarce that demons, necromancers and the undead fight over every scrap.
- **Jund** (black-red-green, centered on red) is a volcanic jungle where dragons sit at the top of the food chain and human clans survive by being as fierce as their predators.
- **Naya** (red-green-white, centered on green) is a lush jungle home to giant beasts called gargantuans, whom the elves and leonin worship.

The first set shows these worlds still separate, but the walls between them are weakening. Behind it all is the elder dragon **Nicol Bolas**, who lost much of his power when the multiverse changed during the Time Spiral block. He has engineered the **Conflux**, the violent reuniting of the shards, because the collision will release an enormous surge of mana that he plans to absorb.

The block's main hero is **Ajani Goldmane**, an albino leonin from Naya. When his brother Jazal, the leader of their pride, is murdered, Ajani's planeswalker spark ignites, and he sets out for revenge. That quest, told in Doug Beyer's novel *Alara Unbroken*, takes him across the shards and eventually leads him to Bolas. Other planeswalkers have their own roles: **Elspeth Tirel** has found a rare peace in Bant, **Tezzeret** is Bolas's agent from Esper, and **Sarkhan Vol** is a dragon-obsessed warrior who ends up serving Bolas.

The story continues through Conflux and Alara Reborn, as the shards crash together and their armies fight. At the end, Ajani learns that his brother's death was part of Bolas's manipulation. When Bolas arrives to drink the mana of the Conflux, Ajani steals the last of it and uses it to banish the dragon from Alara. The plane is whole again, though scarred, and Bolas left with more power than before, which set up later stories leading to War of the Spark.

## Mechanics

Each shard got its own mechanical identity, and the whole set pushed three-color cards.

### Exalted (Bant)

Whenever a creature you control attacks alone, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn for each instance of exalted you control. A board of exalted creatures turns one attacker into a big threat. **Sigiled Paladin** (uncommon) is a two-mana 2/2 first striker with exalted, and **Rafiq of the Many** (mythic) gives a lone attacker double strike on top of its exalted bonus.

### Artifacts matter (Esper)

Esper had no keyword, but nearly every Esper card is an artifact, many of them with colored mana costs. **Master of Etherium** (rare) gets bigger with every artifact you control and pumps your other artifact creatures, and **Etherium Sculptor** (common) makes your artifacts cheaper.

### Unearth (Grixis)

Unearth lets you pay a cost to return a creature card from your graveyard for one turn with haste, after which it's exiled. It's a second shot from cards that already traded away. **Vithian Stinger** (common) pings for 1 damage and can be unearthed to do it again.

### Devour (Jund)

A creature with devour N lets you sacrifice any number of your creatures as it enters, then it gets N +1/+1 counters for each one. Tokens make great devour fodder. **Predator Dragon** (rare) has flying, haste and devour 2, and **Mycoloth** (rare) turns its counters into Saproling tokens every upkeep.

### Power 5 or greater (Naya)

Naya cards care about creatures with 5 or more power. **Mayael the Anima** (mythic) digs those creatures out of your library and puts them onto the battlefield, and **Mosstodon** (common) gives them trample.

### Three-color cards and fixing

Each shard had a three-color charm, an Ultimatum rare with seven colored mana symbols, and a common tri-land, like **Savage Lands** for Jund. The Panoramas and Obelisks added more fixing. This fixing made three-color decks normal, which was new for Magic at the time. ## Limited

Shards of Alara was built for drafting a shard. Most strong decks picked one shard's three colors, leaned on its mechanic and used the tri-lands, Panoramas and Obelisks to make the mana work. The shards played like this:

- **Bant**: exalted creatures, often winning by attacking with one big creature each turn while the rest stayed back to block.
- **Esper**: artifact-heavy fliers and defensive cards, with removal like **Agony Warp**.
- **Grixis**: control and removal, plus unearth creatures that keep generating value.
- **Jund**: tokens and devour, backed by **Branching Bolt** and **Resounding Thunder**.
- **Naya**: big creatures, pump effects and **Naya Charm**.

Two-color decks could work, but the mana and the gold cards pushed drafters toward three colors, and the format was slower than most because of it. Good common removal, such as Agony Warp, Branching Bolt, Executioner's Capsule and **Oblivion Ring**, was always worth taking early. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2008-2010)

Shards of Alara entered a Standard ruled by **Faeries**, a blue-black Lorwyn-block tempo deck built around Bitterblossom. The biggest challenger the new set created was **Five-Color Control**, which used Vivid lands and Reflecting Pool to cast Cruel Ultimatum. Gabriel Nassif won Pro Tour Kyoto in March 2009 with a Five-Color Control deck, and Cruel Ultimatum's big swing became one of the iconic plays of the era. Elspeth, Knight-Errant and Ajani Vengeant were played in a range of white decks, from tokens to Boros aggro.

After the Lorwyn block rotated in October 2009, **Jund** became the deck to beat. It combined Shards of Alara cards like Blightning, Sprouting Thrinax and Broodmate Dragon with Alara Reborn's Bloodbraid Elf and Putrid Leech, and its cascade-fueled card advantage made it one of the most played decks of the 2009-2010 season, even as Jace, the Mind Sculptor control decks rose in 2010. No Shards of Alara cards were banned in Standard. Our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains the midrange style Jund made famous.

### Modern, Legacy and Pauper

In Modern, **Wild Nacatl** fueled Zoo decks enough to be banned in December 2011, then was unbanned in February 2014 when Wizards decided the format had lost its traditional aggressive decks. **Ad Nauseam** became the engine of Modern's Ad Nauseam combo deck, which uses cards like Angel's Grace or Phyrexian Unlife to survive drawing its whole library, and it was also a key card in Legacy storm. **Ethersworn Canonist** shows up in sideboards and artifact decks against combo, **Tidehollow Sculler** has seen play as a disruptive creature in white-black decks in Legacy and Modern, and **Relic of Progenitus** has been graveyard hate for years.

In Pauper, Blightning, Executioner's Capsule, Relic of Progenitus and Fleshbag Marauder have all been played in competitive decks.

### Commander

Shards of Alara's legends were some of the first three-color commanders, and they're still popular. **Sharuum the Hegemon** leads artifact combo decks, **Rafiq of the Many** builds Voltron decks around a single attacker, **Mayael the Anima** leads big-creature decks, and **Kresh the Bloodbraided** rewards sacrificing creatures. If you're building your first deck with one of them, start with our [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck). As of October 2026, Elspeth, Knight-Errant, Ranger of Eos, Tezzeret the Seeker and the shard charms are also common Commander picks.

## Cards from Shards of Alara worth owning

- **Elspeth, Knight-Errant**: one of the best four-mana planeswalkers ever made, and great in Commander.
- **Ad Nauseam**: the core of a Modern combo deck.
- **Ethersworn Canonist**: an evergreen anti-combo card.
- **Relic of Progenitus**: cheap graveyard hate for Modern and Pauper.
- **Wild Nacatl**: the one-drop that defined Zoo.
- **Sharuum the Hegemon**: a popular artifact commander.
- **Tidehollow Sculler**: disruption on a body for white-black decks.
