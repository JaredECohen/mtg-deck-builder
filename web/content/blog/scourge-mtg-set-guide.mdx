Scourge is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2003, the third and last set of the Onslaught block. It ends the story of Karona, the false god born on the continent of Otaria, and it debuted storm, one of the most powerful mechanics ever printed, along with landcycling. Mind's Desire, Tendrils of Agony, Brain Freeze, Stifle and Goblin Warchief all come from this set.

## The story

Otaria is the continent where the Mirari, a mysterious and corrupting artifact, surfaced in the Odyssey block. By the end of Legions, two warring champions had been fused into one being: **Phage**, the undead form of the barbarian **Jeska**, and **Akroma**, an angel created by the illusionist **Ixidor**. Their fusion produced **Karona**, an avatar of all five colors of mana and the "False God" of the set's most famous card.

Scourge is the story of Karona's War. Otaria's factions all want to claim the new god for themselves, while the Mirari's lingering mana keeps warping the people of the continent. Soldiers' armor merges into their bodies, wizards grow insubstantial, elves become tree-like, and zombies sprout extra limbs. The blast that created Karona also gave birth to the **Sliver Overlord**, a new hive leader for the slivers.

The barbarian **Kamahl**, Jeska's brother, ultimately frees his sister from Karona and from her Phage form. Then the golem **Karn** returns and takes Jeska to Argentum, the artificial plane he built, which we know as Mirrodin. Karn tells her she is a planeswalker and asks her to travel the multiverse with him, and he shows her **Memnarch**, the guardian golem he forged from the Mirari to watch over the plane. That sets up the Mirrodin block that followed. The source is J. Robert King's novel *Scourge*, published in May 2003. Scourge was also the last set printed with the original card frame and the last Dominaria story until Time Spiral.

## Mechanics

Scourge's lead designer, Brian Tinsman, built the set around mana value: reward players for either lots of cheap spells or a few huge ones. Storm handles the first half, landcycling the second.

### Storm (new)

When you cast a spell with storm, you copy it once for each other spell cast before it that turn, by any player. It rewards chaining many cheap spells together. **Tendrils of Agony** drains 2 life per copy, so a turn with nine spells before it drains 20. **Brain Freeze** mills three cards per copy, **Scattershot** deals 1 damage to a creature per copy, and **Mind's Desire** lets you play the top card of your library for free once per copy. Even Limited got storm commons, such as **Astral Steel** and **Sprouting Vines**.

### Landcycling (new)

Landcycling is a cycling variant: pay a small cost and discard the card to search for a land of a given type. **Eternal Dragon** has plainscycling `{2}`, **Twisted Abomination** has swampcycling `{2}`, and **Chartooth Cougar** has mountaincycling `{2}`. Expensive creatures stop being dead draws early, because they can fetch the lands you need to cast them later. The idea started as its own mechanic; Mark Rosewater suggested making it a form of cycling so it would work with the block's cycling payoffs.

### Mana value matters and the Decrees

Many cards care about "the greatest mana value among permanents you control," such as **Torrent of Fire** and **Cabal Conditioning**. The five rare Decrees each offer a big effect when cast and a smaller one when cycled; **Decree of Justice** makes 4/4 flying Angels when cast, or 1/1 Soldiers at instant speed when cycled.

### Morph, cycling and creature types

Morph and cycling return from Onslaught and Legions, as does the block's creature-type theme. There are Warchiefs for several tribes, including **Goblin Warchief**, and the set was marketed as the "Dragon set," with **Bladewing the Risen** as a Dragon lord and Dragon-themed Auras at common.

## Limited

Scourge was drafted with Onslaught and Legions in the block format, and it was the smallest of the three sets. In the drafts of the time, the newest set was opened last, so Scourge was usually the third pack. Its job was to support the block's creature-type decks (Goblins, Zombies, Elves, Birds, Clerics, Soldiers, Beasts, Wizards) and to add the high-mana-value theme.

Landcycling made it easier to play a curve that topped out at six or seven mana without stalling, so Scourge rewarded drafters who embraced big creatures. The common storm cards were hard to set up in Limited; one or two extra copies was a typical outcome. The best commons tended to be removal like **Torrent of Fire** and efficient creatures like **Twisted Abomination**. Morph mind games carried over from the earlier sets. For how drafting worked back then, compare with modern play boosters in our [draft vs sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Mind's Desire was too much for the eternal formats immediately. It was restricted in Vintage (then called Type 1) within about a week of Scourge's release, which also kept it out of Type 1.5, the format that later became Legacy. Mind's Desire remained banned in Legacy until Wizards unbanned it on August 7, 2023, and it is still restricted in Vintage as of October 2026.

In Standard, Scourge's biggest contributions came from creature cards. The MTG Wiki's Standard timeline lists Goblin Bidding, Goblin Vortex, Mirari's Wake, RW Astral Slide and "BW Eternal Dragon" among the decks of summer 2003. **Goblin Bidding**, popularized by Wolfgang Eder and played at the 2003 World Championships, ran four Goblin Warchief and four Siege-Gang Commander along with Patriarch's Bidding. Eternal Dragon and Decree of Justice gave control decks resilient threats into the Mirrodin era. Onslaught block rotated out in October 2004.

Dragonstorm had its biggest moment later: after its Time Spiral reprint, Dragonstorm combo decks were part of Standard in late 2006 and 2007.

As of October 2026:

- **Legacy**: Tendrils of Agony is the standard finisher for storm decks, and Brain Freeze has been a storm win condition too. Goblin Warchief and Siege-Gang Commander still feature in Goblins, Stifle in tempo decks, Sulfuric Vortex in Burn and Wirewood Symbiote in Elves.
- **Vintage**: Mind's Desire is restricted; Tendrils of Agony is a storm finisher.
- **Commander**: Siege-Gang Commander, Sliver Overlord, Karona, Decree of Justice and Temple of the False God see play.
- **Pauper**: Temporal Fissure, a common storm bounce spell, is banned in Pauper.
- **Modern**: Scourge is too old for Modern, but reprinted cards like Goblin Warchief, Siege-Gang Commander, Dragonstorm and Twisted Abomination are Modern-legal.

For how Legacy works, see the [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide), and for the formats in general, [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Scourge worth owning

- **Tendrils of Agony**: the defining storm finisher in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Stifle**: a cheap, flexible answer in Legacy.
- **Goblin Warchief**: the engine of Goblin decks.
- **Siege-Gang Commander**: a Goblin and Commander favorite.
- **Mind's Desire**: a combo card legal again in Legacy.
- **Sulfuric Vortex**: punishes lifegain and closes out games for Burn.
- **Decree of Justice**: a flexible token-maker for Commander.
