Ixalan is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2017 that sent the game to a plane of jungles, lost cities and open seas. Four factions (dinosaur-riding warriors, merfolk shamans, vampire conquistadors and pirates) race to find a golden city and the artifact at its heart. The headline is a typal set with new mechanics explore and enrage, the first Treasure tokens, and double-faced cards that flip into legendary lands.

Ixalan was the first set of a two-set block, followed by Rivals of Ixalan in January 2018. The plane returned years later in The Lost Caverns of Ixalan.

## The story

### Four factions and a city of gold

For centuries, the jungles of Ixalan have hidden Orazca, a city of gold, and the artifact inside it: the Immortal Sun. The secret is guarded by the River Heralds, merfolk shamans led by Tishana, who believe no one should hold the city's power. The Sun Empire, a dinosaur-riding civilization descended from the city's original builders, wants Orazca back. The vampires of the Legion of Dusk have sailed from the distant continent of Torrezon on a holy mission to claim the Immortal Sun, and the pirates of the Brazen Coalition, under Admiral Beckett Brass, want it because it's the biggest treasure in the world.

The Immortal Sun has one more effect that matters to the planeswalkers: while it's in place, no planeswalker can leave Ixalan.

### Jace and Vraska

That's a problem for Jace Beleren. After the disaster on Amonkhet, Jace planeswalked away and woke up on a tiny island on Ixalan with no memory of who he is. He's picked up by the pirate ship Belligerent, captained by the gorgon planeswalker Vraska, who is secretly on a mission for Nicol Bolas: find the Immortal Sun and bring it back. Jace joins her crew, and the two, who were enemies back on Ravnica, slowly become close as they hunt for the city together. Over the course of the voyage, Jace begins to piece his memories back together.

### The Sun Empire's poet

Huatli, a Sun Empire warrior who dreams of earning the title of warrior-poet, joins the hunt for Orazca on behalf of her people. Her journey through the jungle crosses paths with the other factions, and the legendary dinosaurs and lands of the set depict the places she, Jace and Vraska pass through.

By the end of Ixalan, all four factions are closing in on Orazca. The story concludes in Rivals of Ixalan, where the city rises from the jungle, the factions clash for the Immortal Sun, and Jace and Vraska make a fateful decision about their deal with Bolas. The Immortal Sun turns up again in War of the Spark.

## Mechanics

### Explore

When a creature explores, you reveal the top card of your library. If it's a land, it goes to your hand. If not, the creature gets a +1/+1 counter, and you choose whether to leave the card on top or put it into your graveyard. Merfolk Branchwalker is a two-mana 2/1 that explores when it enters. Explore always gives you something: a land drop or a bigger creature and some card selection.

### Enrage

Enrage is the Dinosaur ability word: it triggers whenever the creature is dealt damage. Ripjaw Raptor, a 4/5 for four, draws a card each time it's dealt damage, so blocking it or burning it is costly. Enrage rewards you for creatures that survive combat, and red's damage-to-each-creature effects can trigger your own Dinosaurs.

### Raid

Raid returns from Khans of Tarkir as the Pirate mechanic. Cards with raid get a bonus if you attacked with a creature this turn. Storm Fleet Aerialist is a two-mana 1/2 flier that enters with a +1/+1 counter with raid. Raid pushes you to attack first and cast creatures after combat.

### Treasure tokens

Ixalan introduced Treasure: an artifact token you sacrifice for one mana of any color. Pirates and black removal make them, such as Contract Killing, which destroys a creature and creates two Treasures. Treasure is now one of the most common token types in Magic.

### Transforming legendary lands

Ten rares are double-faced cards that start as a spell or permanent and transform into a legendary land. Search for Azcanta is a two-mana enchantment that surveils each upkeep and, with seven cards in your graveyard, flips into Azcanta, the Sunken Ruin, a land that digs for noncreature spells. Legion's Landing makes a lifelink Vampire and becomes Adanto, the First Fort once you attack with three creatures.

### Crew and typal synergies

Vehicles return from Kaladesh as pirate ships, such as Sleek Schooner. And each faction has a creature type: Dinosaurs (red, green, white), Pirates (blue, black, red), Merfolk (green, blue) and Vampires (white, black). Cards like Kinjalli's Caller make Dinosaurs cheaper, and Vanquisher's Banner rewards whatever type you choose.

## Limited

Ixalan's four tribes are spread unevenly (two three-color factions and two two-color ones), but every color pair still had a plan:

| Pair | Theme |
| --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Flyers |
| Blue-Black | Pirates and Treasure |
| Black-Red | Pirate aggro |
| Red-Green | Dinosaur enrage |
| Green-White | Dinosaur ramp |
| White-Black | Vampires |
| Blue-Red | Raid |
| Black-Green | Explore |
| Red-White | Dinosaur aggro |
| Green-Blue | Merfolk |

The two pairs without a faction (white-blue and black-green) had no gold signpost uncommon and instead leaned on flyers and explore. The format was aggressive and creature-heavy, and drafting a consistent tribe paid off, since many uncommons gave a bonus for controlling another creature of their type. Removal was solid but not plentiful: Contract Killing and Pounce at common and Lightning Strike at uncommon went early, and Fiery Cannonade, another uncommon, was a one-sided sweeper for Pirate decks. Big Dinosaurs like Shining Aerosaur gave slower decks a way to stabilize. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### In Standard (September 2017 to October 2019)

Ixalan entered a Standard dominated by Kaladesh's energy decks, and it filled specific roles. Rampaging Ferocidon joined Ramunap Red as a fast threat that also shut off lifegain. On January 15, 2018, Wizards banned it in Standard along with Attune with Aether, Rogue Refiner and Ramunap Ruins to slow down both energy and red aggro. Ferocidon was unbanned in August 2019, though it stayed banned in best-of-one play on MTG Arena.

Control decks loved the set. Vraska's Contempt was a premier answer in blue-black and Grixis control, Settle the Wreckage gave white control a way to punish all-in attacks, and Search for Azcanta was a slow but powerful engine. Hostage Taker became a defining four-drop for blue-black midrange decks.

Ixalan's best season came later. After Kaladesh and Amonkhet rotated in October 2018, Golgari Midrange built around explore creatures (Merfolk Branchwalker and Wildgrowth Walker, plus Rivals of Ixalan's Jadelight Ranger), Vraska's Contempt and Carnage Tyrant was one of the top decks of the Guilds of Ravnica era. Legion's Landing also showed up in white aggro. Ixalan rotated out when Throne of Eldraine released on October 4, 2019. For how rotation works, see our [rotation explainer](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### In Pioneer, Modern and Legacy

Ixalan is a quiet but steady Pioneer set. Vraska's Contempt is a reliable answer for black decks, Kitesail Freebooter is a disruptive option for black midrange, and Field of Ruin (a land from this set) helps control decks deal with utility lands. Carnage Tyrant remains a trump against blue control. In Modern, Search for Azcanta saw play in Azorius control. As of October 2026, no Ixalan card is banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy.

### In Commander

Ixalan's tribes are Commander favorites. Gishath, Sun's Avatar leads Dinosaur decks, Admiral Beckett Brass runs Pirates, and Vona, Butcher of Magan and Tishana, Voice of Thunder lead their own factions. Revel in Riches, Arguel's Blood Fast, Shapers' Sanctuary, Herald of Secret Streams and Vanquisher's Banner all show up regularly, and Treasure tokens are everywhere in the format now.

## Cards from Ixalan worth owning

- **Vraska's Contempt**: an instant-speed answer to creatures and planeswalkers for Pioneer and Commander.
- **Hostage Taker**: removal and card advantage on one body, great in Commander.
- **Gishath, Sun's Avatar**: the classic Dinosaur commander.
- **Search for Azcanta**: a value engine for blue control decks.
- **Vanquisher's Banner**: a lord and card-draw engine for any typal Commander deck.
- **Revel in Riches**: a Treasure engine and alternate win for black Commander decks.

## The takeaway

Ixalan is remembered for its flavor (pirates versus dinosaurs is hard to beat) and for giving Magic Treasure tokens and explore, two mechanics that keep coming back. In Pioneer, its best cards are interaction and midrange glue, so a Golgari deck built on explore creatures and Vraska's Contempt is a natural tribute to its strongest Standard season.
