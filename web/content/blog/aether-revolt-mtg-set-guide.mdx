Aether Revolt is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2017, the second and smaller set of the Kaladesh block. It picks up on the inventor plane of Kaladesh after the Inventors' Fair, as the Renegades rise up against a Consulate that the villain Tezzeret has taken over. The headline cards are some of the most-played of the era: Fatal Push, Walking Ballista and Heart of Kiran, plus Felidar Guardian, whose combo with Saheeli Rai got it banned.

## The story

Kaladesh is a plane powered by aether, a luminous energy that its inventors refine into fuel for flying ships, automatons and every kind of gadget. The Consulate controls who gets aether, and in the first Kaladesh set it used the Inventors' Fair as cover for seizing inventions. By the start of Aether Revolt, things have escalated. Tezzeret, the artificer planeswalker working for Nicol Bolas, has been named Head Judge and then granted emergency powers as Grand Consul. He is diverting much of the city's aether supply into the Aether Spire, where he is building a planar portal from the work of a brilliant elf inventor, Rashmi.

The Renegades fight back. Led by Pia Nalaar, Chandra's mother, they seize the city's central aether hub and large parts of the capital, Ghirapur. The Consulate answers with curfews, surveillance and confiscations. The Gatewatch, the team of planeswalkers that includes Gideon, Jace, Chandra, Nissa and Liliana, sides with the rebels, and Ajani Goldmane joins them while hunting Tezzeret. The Consulate's own planeswalker, the Vedalken Dovin Baan, advises Tezzeret on how to break the Renegades, and the counterspell expert Baral is sent to bait Chandra, who has an old grudge against him.

The climax is an assault on the Aether Spire. Liliana reaches Tezzeret first and overpowers him with the Chain Veil, forcing him to reveal that Bolas is on the plane of Amonkhet. Before she can finish him, Chandra and Gideon smash into the Planar Bridge and wreck it. Tezzeret escapes, but he takes the bridge's core with him, a detail that matters later in War of the Spark. Ajani takes the Gatewatch's oath, and the team leaves Kaladesh to confront Bolas, which leads straight into Amonkhet.

## Mechanics

### Revolt

Revolt checks whether a permanent you controlled left the battlefield this turn, and if so, the card gets a bonus. It is easy to trigger by cracking a fetchland or a Clue, sacrificing a token, or trading a creature in combat. Fatal Push is the famous example: it normally destroys a creature with mana value 2 or less, but with revolt it can kill one with mana value 4 or less.

### Improvise

Improvise lets you tap your untapped artifacts to help pay for a spell, with each artifact paying for one generic mana. It is similar to convoke, but for artifacts. Whir of Invention uses improvise to find an artifact from your library and put it onto the battlefield at instant speed, which made it a combo enabler in Constructed.

### Energy and Vehicles

Two mechanics carried over from Kaladesh. Energy is a resource you collect as counters and spend on abilities; Rogue Refiner draws a card and gives you two energy when it enters. Vehicles are artifacts that become creatures when you crew them by tapping creatures with enough total power. Heart of Kiran, a two-mana 4/4 flying Vehicle, can be crewed by tapping creatures or by removing a loyalty counter from a planeswalker.

### Expertise cycle

A cycle of rare "Expertise" spells lets you cast a cheaper spell from your hand for free after the main effect. Rishkar's Expertise draws cards equal to the greatest power among your creatures, then lets you cast a spell with mana value 5 or less for free.

## Limited

Aether Revolt was drafted with two packs of Aether Revolt and one pack of Kaladesh, so the archetypes blended both sets. Each color pair had a gold uncommon pointing at its plan.

| Pair | Theme | Signpost uncommon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Fliers and tempo | Spire Patrol |
| Blue-Black | Artifacts and improvise | Tezzeret's Touch |
| Black-Red | Artifact creatures and aggression | Weldfast Engineer |
| Red-Green | Big creatures | Outland Boar |
| Green-White | Revolt and counters | Renegade Rallier |
| White-Black | Revolt and Servo tokens | Hidden Stockpile |
| Blue-Red | Thopters and improvise | Maverick Thopterist |
| Black-Green | +1/+1 counters | Winding Constrictor |
| Red-White | Vehicles and aggro | Renegade Wheelsmith |
| Green-Blue | Energy | Rogue Refiner |

Vehicles shaped the format. A good Vehicle can dominate a board, so it pays to keep enough creatures to crew them and enough removal that hits them. Daring Demolition is one of the commons that can destroy a Vehicle even when it isn't crewed. Black had several strong removal commons, like Cruel Finality and Daring Demolition, while red had Chandra's Revolution at common and Hungry Flames at uncommon. Revolt rewarded decks with cheap artifacts and tokens to sacrifice, and improvise rewarded drafting Servos, Thopters and other cheap artifacts. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Aether Revolt entered a Standard that had just lost Smuggler's Copter, Reflector Mage and Emrakul, the Promised End to bans on January 9, 2017, days before the set's release. In its first months:

- **Mardu Vehicles** became the deck to beat, using Heart of Kiran alongside Kaladesh cards like Toolcraft Exemplar and planeswalkers that could crew the Heart.
- **Saheeli Rai combo** (often called Copycat) used Felidar Guardian with Saheeli Rai from Kaladesh. Saheeli copies the Guardian, the copy flickers Saheeli to reset her, and the loop makes as many hasty Cats as you want.
- **Black-green counters** used Winding Constrictor, Rishkar, Peema Renegade and Walking Ballista to grow a board quickly.
- **Temur Energy** later relied on Rogue Refiner and Kaladesh's Attune with Aether to fuel energy cards.

The Saheeli combo proved too strong. Wizards banned Felidar Guardian in Standard in late April 2017 in an emergency announcement, citing how much of the online metagame the combo had taken over. In January 2018, Wizards banned Rogue Refiner and Attune with Aether, along with two other cards, to weaken Temur Energy. Aether Revolt left Standard when Guilds of Ravnica released in October 2018.

Outside Standard, Fatal Push had the biggest impact. It gave black a one-mana instant removal spell that fetchlands make easy to upgrade with revolt, and it became a staple of Modern and Pioneer black decks. Walking Ballista found a home in Modern Hardened Scales, and in Pioneer it powered a combo with Heliod, Sun-Crowned until Wizards banned it there in August 2020. Felidar Guardian was banned in Pioneer in November 2019, among the format's first bans, while the Saheeli combo stayed legal in Modern.

In Commander, Paradox Engine was banned in July 2019 because it made infinite untap loops too easy. Sram, Senior Edificer, Rishkar's Expertise, Heroic Intervention and Yahenni, Undying Partisan remain popular. As of October 2026, Felidar Guardian and Walking Ballista are banned in Pioneer and Paradox Engine is banned in Commander; the rest of the set is legal in all the formats it's eligible for.

## Cards from Aether Revolt worth owning

- **Fatal Push**: one of the best removal spells in Pioneer and Modern.
- **Walking Ballista**: a flexible threat and removal piece in Modern and Commander.
- **Heroic Intervention**: a protective instant found in a huge number of green Commander decks.
- **Winding Constrictor**: the core of +1/+1 counters decks in Commander and casual Constructed.
- **Sram, Senior Edificer**: a card-draw engine for Equipment and Aura Commander decks.
- **Rishkar's Expertise**: a big card-draw spell for creature-heavy green Commander decks.

## The takeaway

Aether Revolt is a small set that punched far above its size. Its story wrapped up the Kaladesh arc and sent the Gatewatch to Amonkhet, and its cards include one of the best removal spells in non-rotating formats. If you're building a Pioneer deck with it, Fatal Push is the place to start, and Winding Constrictor anchors a counters plan.
