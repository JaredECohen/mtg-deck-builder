Dominaria is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2018 that returned to the game's original home plane for the first time in more than a decade. It's a celebration of Magic's history, packed with legendary characters, references to old stories and two new card styles, Sagas and legendary sorceries. It also gave Standard Teferi, Hero of Dominaria, one of the most dominant planeswalkers of its time.

## The story

The set takes place about sixty years after the Mending, the event that repaired Dominaria's fractured timeline at the end of the Time Spiral block. The plane is healing, and old names are back: Teferi, Jhoira, Jaya Ballard and Karn all return. The story starts right after Hour of Devastation, where the Gatewatch lost badly to Nicol Bolas on Amonkhet.

Gideon Jura and Liliana Vess arrive on Dominaria with a specific goal. Liliana made deals with four demons long ago in exchange for youth and power, and she has already killed three of them. The last is Belzenlok, who now leads the Cabal, a death cult that has spread across much of Dominaria. Belzenlok has also been rewriting history to credit himself with famous deeds and titles, which the set plays with on cards like Demonlord Belzenlok.

To reach him, they join the crew of the Weatherlight, the legendary skyship, rebuilt and captained by Jhoira. Her crew is a who's who of new and legacy characters: Shanna, a descendant of the original captain Sisay; the young wizard Raff Capashen; Tiana, an angel artificer; Arvad, a vampire knight; and Slimefoot, a fungus creature who stowed away. The crew gathers allies and artifacts around the plane. Teferi, who gave up his planeswalker spark long ago to seal a time rift, works with them too. Late in the story Jhoira gives him a Powerstone that holds his spark, and he becomes a planeswalker again.

The finale is an assault on the Cabal Stronghold. Gideon seizes the Blackblade, an ancient sword strong enough to kill an elder dragon or a demon, and Liliana uses it to slay Belzenlok, freeing herself from her last contract. With Belzenlok gone, the planeswalkers turn toward Nicol Bolas, which leads into the Ravnica block and War of the Spark. In a separate thread, Karn's search for his past turns up evidence that Phyrexia isn't finished with Dominaria, a hint that pays off years later in Dominaria United and March of the Machine.

## Mechanics

### Sagas

Sagas are enchantments that tell a story over several turns. A Saga enters with a lore counter and triggers its first chapter, gets another counter after your draw step each turn, and is sacrificed after its final chapter. History of Benalia makes a 2/2 vigilance Knight on each of its first two chapters, then pumps your Knights on the third. Sagas give you a predictable sequence of effects from a single card, and they've appeared in many sets since.

### Historic

Historic is a term for artifacts, legendary cards and Sagas. Many Dominaria cards trigger when you cast a historic spell. Jhoira, Weatherlight Captain draws a card each time, which turns a deck of artifacts and legends into a card-advantage engine.

### Legendary sorceries

A legendary sorcery can be cast only if you control a legendary creature or planeswalker. In exchange, the effect is very strong. Urza's Ruinous Blast exiles every nonland permanent that isn't legendary, so a deck of legends can wipe the board while keeping its own key pieces.

### Kicker

Kicker returns: you can pay an extra cost when casting the spell for a bigger effect. Shivan Fire is a one-mana 2 damage, or 4 damage when kicked for four more. Josu Vess, Lich Knight is a four-mana creature that, kicked for ten mana total, brings eight 2/2 Zombie Knights with it.

## Limited

Dominaria was widely praised as a draft format, slower than many recent sets and rewarding careful play. Its ten archetypes, each with a gold uncommon:

- **White-blue historic** (Raff Capashen, Ship's Mage).
- **Blue-black control** (Rona, Disciple of Gix).
- **Black-red sacrifice** (Garna, the Bloodflame).
- **Red-green kicker** (Hallar, the Firefletcher).
- **Green-white tokens** (Shanna, Sisay's Legacy).
- **White-black legends** (Arvad the Cursed).
- **Blue-red Wizards** (Adeliz, the Cinder Wind).
- **Black-green Saprolings** (Slimefoot, the Stowaway).
- **Red-white Auras and Equipment** (Tiana, Ship's Caretaker).
- **Green-blue ramp** (Tatyova, Benthic Druid).

Because games went long, kicker cards gave you flexibility early and power late, and card advantage mattered. Common removal like Shivan Fire, Eviscerate, Vicious Offering and Blessed Light was the backbone of most decks, and blue's Academy Journeymage was a strong tempo play in Wizards decks. For more on the skills that carry from set to set, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Dominaria hit Standard hard. In its first months, the format was shaped by a handful of its cards:

- **Teferi, Hero of Dominaria** anchored white-blue and Esper control. Its +1 drew a card and untapped two lands, so you could protect it with counterspells, and its -3 dealt with any threat.
- **Goblin Chainwhirler** wiped out X/1 creatures and tokens and gave red aggro decks a powerful three-drop.
- **History of Benalia** was the engine of white Knight aggro and later of Boros Angels decks, alongside Lyra Dawnbringer.
- **Steel Leaf Champion and Llanowar Elves** powered green aggro decks.
- **Karn, Scion of Urza** fit almost any deck because it's colorless.

These cards stayed relevant after the fall 2018 rotation, and Teferi remained a top card until Dominaria rotated out in October 2019. No Dominaria card was banned in Standard.

As of October 2026, Dominaria's long-term impact is spread across formats. Teferi was a staple of Modern white-blue control for years and also saw play in Pioneer. Karn, Scion of Urza became a regular in Modern Tron decks. Damping Sphere is a common Modern sideboard card against Tron and storm-style decks. Opt, Cast Down and Llanowar Elves are everyday cards in Pioneer. In Commander, Muldrotha, the Gravetide is one of the most popular graveyard commanders, Jhoira leads artifact and historic decks, and Gilded Lotus, Helm of the Host and Shalai are widely played. For a comparison of these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Dominaria worth owning

- **Teferi, Hero of Dominaria**: a control finisher and card engine in Pioneer and Modern.
- **Karn, Scion of Urza**: a colorless planeswalker for Modern Tron and artifact decks.
- **Damping Sphere**: a cheap sideboard answer to big mana and storm.
- **Muldrotha, the Gravetide**: one of Commander's most popular three-color commanders.
- **Helm of the Host**: copies your best creature every turn in Commander.
- **Llanowar Elves**: the classic one-mana ramp creature, a Pioneer green staple.
- **Shalai, Voice of Plenty**: protects you and your team from targeted spells in Commander.

Dominaria is a set worth revisiting for its story and its cards. Its most famous card, Teferi, Hero of Dominaria, still fits Pioneer control decks, and its legends fill Commander tables.
