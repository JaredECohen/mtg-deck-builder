An enchantress deck is built around cards that draw you a card whenever you cast an enchantment or whenever an enchantment enters the battlefield under your control. With a few of those engines in play, every enchantment you cast replaces itself, so the deck can deploy a wall of defensive and value enchantments without running out of cards. It wins by locking the opponent out of attacking, building one enormous creature with Auras, or simply burying the table in card advantage.

The archetype is named after its signature creatures, Argothian Enchantress and its relatives. It shows up in older constructed formats and is one of the most recognizable strategies in Commander. This guide covers the engines, the enchantments that fill out the deck, how to protect it, and how to build it as a Commander deck.

## How an enchantress deck works

The deck has three layers:

1. **Draw engines.** Cards that reward you for casting or playing enchantments by drawing cards.
2. **Payoff and defense enchantments.** Enchantments that do real work on their own: stopping attacks, removing threats, making mana, or growing a creature.
3. **A way to win.** Usually a single Aura-loaded creature, a creature that scales with enchantment count, or a lock piece that keeps the opponent from interacting while you finish them off.

The engines are what make it an archetype rather than a pile of enchantments. Without them, you're just a slow deck. With two in play, a cheap enchantment draws two cards, and the deck snowballs. That's [card advantage](/blog/card-advantage-explained) generated passively, every turn, without spending cards on draw spells.

## The draw engines

There are two families of enchantress effects, and the difference between them matters.

### Cast triggers

These trigger when you cast an enchantment spell:

- **Argothian Enchantress:** a two-mana green 0/1 with shroud that draws a card whenever you cast an enchantment spell. Shroud keeps it safe from targeted removal, including your opponent's.
- **Enchantress's Presence:** a three-mana green enchantment with the same draw trigger. As an enchantment itself, it dodges creature removal and board wipes that only hit creatures.
- **Verduran Enchantress** and **Mesa Enchantress:** the original green and white versions, both small creatures that let you draw when you cast an enchantment.
- **Sythis, Harvest's Hand:** a green-white legendary enchantment creature that gains you 1 life and draws a card whenever you cast an enchantment spell.

### Enters triggers

These trigger on an enchantment entering the battlefield under your control, an ability word called constellation on many of them:

- **Eidolon of Blossoms:** an enchantment creature that draws a card whenever it or another enchantment enters under your control.
- **Setessan Champion:** draws a card and gets a +1/+1 counter whenever an enchantment enters under your control.

### Why the difference matters

Cast triggers only care about spells. Enters triggers care about anything entering, so they also count enchantments you put onto the battlefield without casting them: enchantment tokens, enchantments returned from the graveyard, and enchantments put directly into play by a tutor effect. Replenish, a white sorcery that returns every enchantment card from your graveyard to the battlefield, triggers an enters engine for each card it brings back, and a cast engine not at all.

The reverse is also true. If an opponent counters your enchantment spell, a cast trigger has already gone on the stack and still draws you a card. An enters trigger gets nothing. A good list runs both types so neither weakness costs too much.

## The rest of the deck

Draw engines do nothing by themselves. They need enchantments that are worth casting even without the card draw.

### Defensive enchantments

Enchantress decks often want to stop creatures from attacking at all. Ghostly Prison and Propaganda make opponents pay two extra mana for each creature that attacks you, which slows down creature decks enormously, especially in multiplayer where they discourage attacks against you specifically. Solitary Confinement goes further, preventing all damage to you and giving you shroud, at the cost of skipping your draw step and discarding a card each upkeep to keep it around. In a deck that draws off every enchantment, losing the draw step hurts much less than it would anywhere else.

### Removal on an enchantment

Several of the most flexible removal spells in the game are enchantments, so they draw a card while answering a threat. Oblivion Ring exiles another nonland permanent until it leaves the battlefield. Journey to Nowhere exiles a creature the same way. Pacifism stops a creature from attacking or blocking. These count for your engines and do work against any opponent.

### Mana and value

Serra's Sanctum is a legendary land that taps for one white mana for each enchantment you control. In a deck full of enchantments, it can produce enormous amounts of mana. Sterling Grove gives your other enchantments shroud and can be sacrificed to search your library for an enchantment.

## Auras: the main win condition

Auras are enchantments that attach to a permanent, usually a creature. They're the classic risk in Magic: if the creature dies, the Auras attached to it go to the graveyard, and you've lost several cards at once. Enchantress decks can afford that risk better than any other deck, because each Aura already drew a card when it was cast.

Some Auras are especially good here:

- **Rancor:** gives +2/+0 and trample, and returns to your hand when it's put into a graveyard from the battlefield, so it's almost impossible to lose.
- **Ethereal Armor:** gives +1/+1 for each enchantment you control, plus first strike. In an enchantress deck, that's a huge number.
- **Ancestral Mask:** gives +2/+2 for each other enchantment on the battlefield, which counts your opponents' enchantments too.
- **Auras with umbra armor** (the ability formerly called totem armor): if the enchanted creature would be destroyed, the Aura is destroyed instead and the damage is removed. These are the best answer to removal that an Aura can offer.

Pick a creature that's hard to remove, such as one with hexproof or shroud, as the main Aura target. The [Voltron guide](/blog/voltron-commander-guide) goes deep on building around one heavily enchanted creature, including how Auras compare with Equipment.

### Creatures that scale with enchantments

Instead of stacking Auras on one creature, some builds use creatures whose size depends on how many enchantments you control. These reward the same game plan without putting all your cards on one target.

## Protecting the enchantress board

The deck's biggest weakness is that it puts everything into one card type. A single effect that destroys all enchantments, like Tranquility, can undo many turns of work. Plan for that:

- **Give your enchantments shroud or hexproof.** Sterling Grove and Greater Auramancy give your other enchantments shroud. That stops targeted removal but not board-wide effects.
- **Keep a recovery card.** Replenish, or any effect that returns enchantments from the graveyard, turns a board wipe into a temporary setback. If you're using enters engines, a recovery spell can even draw you a fresh hand.
- **Don't overextend.** Hold back a couple of enchantments, ideally an engine and a defensive piece, once you have enough on the battlefield to win. Having more on the board rarely helps more than having something to rebuild with.
- **Spread your Auras.** If you have a second creature that can carry them, splitting Auras means one removal spell can't take everything.

Against creature decks, enchantress can feel unbeatable once it's set up. Against decks that interact with enchantments directly, or combo decks that ignore the board, it has to be faster. The engines are cheap for a reason: get them down early.

## Building enchantress in Commander

Commander suits enchantress well. Games are long, 100-card singleton gives you room for many engines, and multiplayer means defensive enchantments like Ghostly Prison protect you from several opponents at once.

### Commander choices

Most enchantress commanders are green and white, often with a third color:

- **Sythis, Harvest's Hand** (green-white): a draw engine in the command zone, available every game.
- **Tuvasa the Sunlit** (green-white-blue): grows with each enchantment you control and draws a card the first time you cast an enchantment spell each turn.
- **Zur the Enchanter** (white-blue-black): whenever it attacks, it searches your library for an enchantment with mana value 3 or less and puts it onto the battlefield, which suits enters engines and powerful cheap enchantments.

Two-color green-white builds have the most dedicated engines. Adding blue brings card selection and counterspells; adding black brings removal and recovery.

### A rough Commander outline

These numbers are a starting point, not a rule:

| Category | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Lands (including Serra's Sanctum if you have it) | 36-38 |
| Enchantress draw engines | 8-10 |
| Defensive or lock enchantments | 6-8 |
| Enchantment-based removal | 6-8 |
| Auras and enchantment-scaling creatures | 10-14 |
| Ramp and mana | 8-10 |
| Protection and recovery | 4-6 |
| Flexible slots | Rest |

The [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) explains how to balance these categories in general. For enchantress, the main thing is engine density: you want to see an engine early in nearly every game.

### Playing it at the table

In multiplayer, enchantress decks look harmless for the first few turns, which is a real advantage. Avoid drawing attention by putting your defensive pieces down first. Once a lock or a big Aura creature is in place, the table will notice, so make sure you have protection ready before you commit your whole hand.

## Common mistakes

- **Too few engines.** Without engines, you're playing a slow deck with no card advantage. Count them, and run more than you think.
- **Too many weak enchantments.** Every enchantment should do something useful by itself, not just trigger a draw.
- **All Auras on an unprotected creature.** Pick a resilient target or spread them out.
- **No plan for a board wipe.** Include at least one recovery card and keep a few cards in hand.

## The takeaway

An enchantress deck turns every enchantment into a cantrip. Put down cheap cast and enters engines early, fill the deck with enchantments that defend, remove threats or make mana, then win with a big Aura-covered creature or a lock the opponent can't break. Protect against enchantment wipes with shroud effects and a recovery spell, and the deck will out-draw almost anything.
