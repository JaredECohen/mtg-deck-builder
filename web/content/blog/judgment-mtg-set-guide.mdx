Judgment is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2002 as the third and final set of the Odyssey block. It finishes the story of the Mirari, a mysterious orb of power on the continent of Otaria on Dominaria, and it deliberately tilts toward green and white after the black-heavy Torment. Judgment is best remembered for the Wish cycle, the graveyard-powered Incarnations like Anger and Wonder, and combo pieces that still show up in Legacy.

## The story

The Odyssey block takes place on Otaria, about a century after the Phyrexian invasion devastated Dominaria. Its story revolves around the **Mirari**, a silver orb that grants immense power and slowly poisons the mind of whoever holds it. In Odyssey and Torment, the barbarian **Kamahl** and the dementia caster **Chainer** fought over it, and the Cabal, a black-aligned criminal cult, built its empire on the pit fights it ran.

Judgment, told in Will McDermott's novel of the same name, picks up with Kamahl holding the orb. He had promised the dying Chainer that he'd keep it safe from everyone, including himself. That's harder than it sounds. Laquatus, the scheming merfolk ambassador, and the aven commander Eesha both want it. Kamahl's sister **Jeska** and the dwarf **Balthor** try to separate him from it, because they can see what it's doing to him.

After a long run of politics and war, most of the cast is dead and the Mirari's grip is broken. In the end, the orb, fused to Kamahl's sword, is driven through Laquatus's body into the ground of the Krosan Forest. The Mirari's magic floods into the land, and the forest begins to grow huge and wild. That overgrown Krosa is where the Onslaught block picks up, with a transformed Kamahl and a broken Jeska reborn as Phage. Judgment's set name fits the ending: everyone who reached for the Mirari got exactly what they deserved.

## Mechanics

Judgment kept the block's graveyard themes. Flashback (cast a card again from your graveyard for an alternate cost) and threshold (bonuses once you have seven or more cards in your graveyard) both return. **Battle Screech** (uncommon) makes two 1/1 white fliers, then flashes back by tapping three untapped white creatures, and the two new Birds help pay that cost. **Toxic Stench** (common) is a small -1/-1 until you reach threshold, then it destroys a creature.

### Wishes

The five Wishes fetch a card you own from outside the game, which in tournaments means your sideboard. **Burning Wish** (rare, `{1}{R}`) gets a sorcery, **Cunning Wish** an instant, **Living Wish** a creature or land, **Golden Wish** an artifact or enchantment, and **Death Wish** any card at the cost of half your life. Wishes changed how players built sideboards: instead of four copies of one answer, players began running single copies of many cards so a Wish could grab the right one. Our [sideboarding guide](/blog/sideboarding-101) covers how sideboards work today.

### Incarnations

Incarnations are creatures that also do something while they're in your graveyard. **Anger** (uncommon) is a 2/2 haste creature for `{3}{R}`, but its real job is in the graveyard: while it's there and you control a Mountain, all your creatures have haste. **Wonder** does the same with flying and an Island, **Valor** with first strike, **Brawn** with trample and **Filth** with swampwalk. **Genesis** (rare) lets you pay `{2}{G}` each upkeep to return a creature card to your hand. Discarding or milling an Incarnation is often better than casting it.

### Phantoms and Advocates

Phantoms are Spirits that enter with +1/+1 counters and prevent any damage to them by removing a counter instead. **Phantom Centaur** (uncommon, `{2}{G}{G}`) is a 2/0 that enters with three +1/+1 counters, so it starts as a 5/3 with protection from black that shrugs off most combat damage. Advocates are white and green creatures that return cards from an opponent's graveyard to their hand for a benefit, a fitting tool against a graveyard block. **Nullmage Advocate** (common) gives back two cards to destroy an artifact or enchantment.

## Limited

Judgment was drafted in the order of the time, oldest first: Odyssey, then Torment, then Judgment. Its color skew mattered a lot. The set has about twice as many green and white cards as black ones, and those green and white cards were stronger on average, so Judgment packs fed whoever was in green or white.

At common and uncommon, **Battle Screech** was one of the best cards in the block, effectively four evasive creatures for one card. **Phantom Centaur**, **Phantom Flock** and the other Phantoms were very hard to kill in combat, **Battlewise Aven** got better at threshold, and **Toxic Stench** gave black a cheap removal spell that scaled with the block's graveyard plans. The format rewarded drafters who tracked graveyards, since threshold, flashback and Incarnations all turned the discard pile into a resource. For general draft habits that still apply, see our [draft vs. sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2002-2003)

Judgment joined a Standard built around Odyssey block graveyards and Invasion block gold cards. The most successful decks of the period were **Psychatog** (the blue-black control deck Carlos Romão piloted to win the 2002 World Championships) and **UG Madness**, nicknamed "Wonder Dog." Madness used Torment's madness cards with Wild Mongrel and similar discard outlets, and Judgment's **Wonder** let a single discard give the whole team flying.

Judgment also created its own archetype. **Mirari's Wake** decks paired the enchantment with Cunning Wish, Moment's Peace and the Mirari artifact from Odyssey to stall, double their mana and win with huge spells; by mid-2003 "Cunning Wake" and "Mirari's Wake" were among the top archetypes. Green-white decks used **Glory** and the Phantoms. Judgment rotated out with the rest of Odyssey block when Mirrodin arrived in October 2003.

### Legacy and Vintage

Judgment's Wishes gave combo decks a way to reach past their main deck. **Burning Wish** was a centerpiece of Vintage's Long.dec era and spent years on the Vintage restricted list before coming off in 2012; it remains a storm staple in Legacy. **Living Wish** fetches creatures and utility lands in Legacy toolbox decks. **Worldgorger Dragon** combined with Animate Dead or Necromancy creates a loop that generates unlimited mana, and the Dragon combo is a long-standing Legacy deck. **Solitary Confinement** appears in enchantment prison builds. For how these formats differ from Standard, our [formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) has the overview.

### Pauper and Commander

In Pauper, **Prismatic Strands** is one of white's best tricks, and **Battle Screech** and **Lava Dart** have both been played in competitive decks. In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), **Anger** is a classic reanimator and discard-deck enabler, **Mirari's Wake** is a mana doubler for green-white, and **Genesis**, **Wonder** and **Glory** turn the graveyard into a permanent advantage.

## Cards from Judgment worth owning

- **Burning Wish**: a Legacy and Vintage storm staple.
- **Living Wish**: a flexible toolbox tutor for Legacy.
- **Anger**: the graveyard haste enabler every reanimator Commander deck wants.
- **Mirari's Wake**: a mana doubler and anthem in one.
- **Worldgorger Dragon**: the heart of a classic Legacy combo.
- **Genesis**: endless creature recursion from the graveyard.
- **Prismatic Strands**: a Pauper staple fog that can win combat outright.
