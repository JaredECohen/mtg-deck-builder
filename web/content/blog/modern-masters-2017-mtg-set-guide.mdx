Modern Masters 2017 is the Magic: The Gathering reprint set released on March 17, 2017, the third and final entry in the Modern Masters series. Its 249 cards came from sets between Eighth Edition and Magic 2014, and it is best remembered for reprinting the five Zendikar fetchlands alongside Tarmogoyf, Snapcaster Mage, Liliana of the Veil, Cavern of Souls and Death's Shadow, right as that last card was taking over Modern.

## What the set was for

The Modern Masters series existed to do two things: make the Modern format easier to get into (see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained)) by reprinting its staples, and give experienced drafters a high-powered Limited environment built from Modern-era cards. Modern Masters 2017 followed Modern Masters (2013) and Modern Masters 2015, and like them it was sold as a premium product. Booster boxes held 24 packs, exactly enough for one eight-player draft, because the set was designed to be drafted.

It was also a set of its moment. Modern in early 2017 was being reshaped by Death's Shadow decks: Wizards banned Gitaxian Probe in January 2017, but black-green Death's Shadow builds still put three copies in the Top 8 of Grand Prix Vancouver that February, a month before Modern Masters 2017 arrived with Death's Shadow in it. For a primer on the format itself, see the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

## The reprints that mattered

The set's value was concentrated in a handful of Constructed staples:

- **The Zendikar fetchlands:** **Arid Mesa**, **Marsh Flats**, **Misty Rainforest**, **Scalding Tarn** and **Verdant Catacombs**, each at rare. Together with the Onslaught fetches, they are the backbone of Modern mana bases.
- **Tarmogoyf:** the two-mana green creature that grows with each card type in all graveyards, a Modern and Legacy cornerstone for years.
- **Snapcaster Mage** and **Liliana of the Veil:** both mythic, both among the most-played cards in their colors at the time.
- **Cavern of Souls:** the land that makes a chosen creature type uncounterable, key to tribal decks.
- **Death's Shadow:** a `{B}` 13/13 that gets -X/-X equal to your life total, the heart of the deck dominating Modern when the set came out.
- **Voice of Resurgence**, **Restoration Angel**, **Scavenging Ooze**, **Blood Moon**, **Stony Silence**, **Grafdigger's Cage** and **Damnation** rounded out the Constructed list.

### Mechanics in the set

Modern Masters 2017 didn't introduce mechanics, but it pulled in several to support its draft archetypes:

- **Unearth:** return a creature from your graveyard for one attack, then exile it. **Kathari Bomber** and **Sedraxis Specter** support the black-red and Grixis graveyard decks.
- **Populate:** copy a creature token you control, as on **Rootborn Defenses** and **Druid's Deliverance**, for the green-white token deck.
- **Overload:** pay more to change "target" to "each." **Mizzium Mortars** deals 4 damage to one creature you don't control, or to all of them.
- **Miracle:** cast a card cheaply if it's the first you draw in a turn. **Bonfire of the Damned** and **Terminus** are the big ones.
- **Bloodrush:** discard a creature to pump an attacker, as on **Ghor-Clan Rampager**.
- **Soulbond:** pair two creatures for a shared bonus. **Wingcrafter** gives both flying; **Deadeye Navigator** lets them blink.

## Limited

Modern Masters 2017 was a multicolor draft, with five two-color guild archetypes and five three-color shard archetypes. Signets moved up to uncommon and gold cards were plentiful, so drafters were encouraged to play more than two colors:

- **White-blue:** flicker and enters-the-battlefield effects
- **Blue-black:** instant-speed control
- **Black-red:** unearth and sacrifice
- **Red-green:** go-wide tokens
- **Green-white:** populate
- **Esper (white-blue-black):** control with creatures such as **Wall of Denial**
- **Grixis (blue-black-red):** graveyard value with **Sedraxis Specter** and **Izzet Charm**
- **Jund (black-red-green):** sacrifice, with **Sprouting Thrinax**
- **Naya (red-green-white):** token aggro
- **Bant (green-white-blue):** Splicers like **Blade Splicer** and **Master Splicer**

The design team changed several cards' rarity to balance the draft. **Terminate** and the Signets moved up to uncommon, while **Burning-Tree Emissary**, **Magma Jet** and **Augur of Bolas** moved down to common. Guildgates and tri-lands made mana fixing easy, so the format rewarded bold multicolor decks. The usual Limited fundamentals apply; see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) for the basics of judging removal, creatures and fixing.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

As a reprint set, Modern Masters 2017 added no new cards to any format. Its impact was on availability: the fetchlands and mythic staples it reprinted were among the most-needed cards for Modern players in 2017, and the Zendikar fetchlands in particular had been hard to find since their original printing in Zendikar in 2009.

The timing with Death's Shadow was notable. In its first months after release, Death's Shadow decks remained at the top of Modern. Jund and black-green builds were followed by Grixis versions using delve threats and Snapcaster Mage, which eventually became the top variant. Modern Masters 2017 supplied both Death's Shadow and Snapcaster Mage to those decks.

None of its cards are banned in Modern as of October 2026. **Griselbrand** is banned in Commander, so in practice it's a Legacy and Vintage card. For the reasoning behind bans in general, see the [ban list guide](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

## Cards from Modern Masters 2017 worth owning

- **The five Zendikar fetchlands:** essential for Modern, Legacy and multicolor Commander mana.
- **Cavern of Souls:** a must for tribal decks in Commander and Constructed.
- **Cyclonic Rift:** one of Commander's most impactful blue cards.
- **Snapcaster Mage and Liliana of the Veil:** long-running staples for eternal formats and cubes.
- **Path to Exile:** cheap removal for any white deck.
- **Blood Moon:** a sideboard card that punishes greedy mana bases.
- **The Signets:** cheap, reliable mana rocks for Commander.

Modern Masters 2017 is worth knowing mainly for the cards it put back into circulation, especially lands that every Modern player needs.
