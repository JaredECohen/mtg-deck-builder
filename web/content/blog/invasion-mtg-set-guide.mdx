Invasion is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2000 that kicked off the climax of the Weatherlight Saga: the Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria. It was a multicolor set that introduced kicker, domain and split cards, gave the game Fact or Fiction and Phyrexian Altar, and is still remembered as one of the best-loved sets and Limited formats in Magic's history.

## The story

The Weatherlight Saga had been building for years toward one event. Yawgmoth, the god-like ruler of the artificial plane Phyrexia, had wanted to conquer Dominaria for thousands of years, ever since the Brothers' War. The ancient planeswalker Urza had spent that time preparing for the attack, building weapons, breeding armies and assembling the **Legacy**, a set of artifacts that included the skyship *Weatherlight* and its captain, Gerrard Capashen.

In Invasion, the attack finally comes. Portals open in the sky and Phyrexian armies pour into Dominaria under the command of the spider-like general **Tsabo Tavoc**. J. Robert King's novel *Invasion* follows the war across the world. The Weatherlight crew, including Gerrard, Sisay, Karn, Orim, Tahngarth, Squee and the navigator **Hanna**, fight to close portals while Benalia falls, Llanowar is bombarded with plague, and Yavimaya fights back with the newly awakened kavu. Teferi decides he can't save his homeland of Zhalfir and phases it out of reality instead, which infuriates Urza. Hanna is infected with the Phyrexian plague and dies, and her father, the mage Barrin, is devastated. Karn, a pacifist silver golem, finally fights back when his friends are threatened.

The set's card design matches the story's theme of unity. Its expansion symbol is the emblem of the **Coalition**, the alliance of Dominarian forces from all five colors, and its gold cards show nations of different colors fighting side by side. The war continues in Planeshift, where the Phyrexian plane of Rath is layered over Dominaria, and ends in Apocalypse, when Yawgmoth himself arrives. In the end, both Urza and Gerrard die, and Karn, fitted with Urza's powerstone eyes, activates the Legacy, releasing a wave of white mana that destroys Yawgmoth and the invaders. Karn becomes a planeswalker, which sets up much of his later story.

## Mechanics

### Kicker (new)

Kicker is an optional extra cost you can pay when you cast a spell to get a bigger effect. **Kavu Titan** (rare) is a two-mana 2/2, but if you pay its `{2}{G}` kicker it enters as a 5/5 trampler. **Urza's Rage** (rare) deals 3 uncounterable damage, or 10 if you pay its huge kicker. Kicker made cards flexible early and late, and Wizards has brought it back many times since.

### Domain (new)

Domain cards count how many basic land types you control, from zero to five. **Tribal Flames** (common) deals damage equal to that number, so it's a five-damage burn spell in a deck with all five types. **Collective Restraint** (rare) taxes attackers based on your domain count. Domain rewards five-color decks, and the Invasion block gave players the fixing to build them.

### Split cards (new)

A split card has two half-cards printed side by side, and you choose which half to cast. Invasion's five split cards each combine two colors, like **Assault // Battery** (2 damage or a 3/3 Elephant) and **Spite // Malice** (a counterspell or a kill spell). They were so unusual that Wizards didn't preview them before release, and they've returned in many later sets.

### Gold cards and the dragon legends

Invasion focused on allied-color pairs and three-color cards, with enemy-color cards held back until Apocalypse. Its legendary dragons, like **Rith, the Awakener** and **Darigaaz, the Igniter**, each cost three colors. The common **allied taplands**, such as **Coastal Tower**, and sacrifice lands like **Geothermal Crevice** provided fixing. If gold cards are new to you, our guide to [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) explains multicolor costs.

## Limited

Invasion was drafted at the highest level, and many veteran players still call Invasion block one of the best Limited environments ever made. Booster Draft at the time often used three packs of Invasion, and later Invasion, Planeshift and Apocalypse.

The format was built around multicolor:

- **Two-color allied decks** were the backbone, with gold commons like **Galina's Knight** and **Llanowar Knight**.
- **Three-color and domain decks** were very real, because the taplands, common lands like **Irrigation Ditch**, which can be sacrificed for two mana of other colors, and mana creatures made splashing easy. Decks that took good fixing early could play the best card in every pack.
- **Kicker** smoothed out draws: cards like **Agonizing Demise** were removal early and removal plus damage to the opponent late.
- **Removal was plentiful** across colors, including Agonizing Demise, **Zap**, **Exotic Curse** and **Recoil**, and **Armadillo Cloak** was a famously strong common for green-white decks.

Games were slower and more strategic than many earlier formats, with lots of interaction and card advantage. Our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) covers the fundamentals that made this format reward skilled drafters.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2000-2002)

Invasion arrived in a Standard alongside the Mercadian Masques block, Sixth Edition and later Seventh Edition. Its first big contribution was **Fires**, a red-green aggro deck built on Fires of Yavimaya, which gave every creature haste, alongside Saproling Burst, Blastoderm, Kavu Chameleon and Urza's Rage. It was the defining deck of the Masques-Invasion Standard season.

Blue control used **Fact or Fiction** and **Absorb** to keep up, and black-red decks found new weapons too. At the 2001 World Championship, Tom van de Logt won with a black-red deck nicknamed **Machine Head**, which used Invasion's Blazing Specter, Crypt Angel, Plague Spitter and Skizzik and was built to beat the decks that beat Fires. Invasion's cards stayed central until the block rotated in October 2002. No Invasion card was banned in Standard. For more on how sets enter and leave Standard, see our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### Vintage, Legacy and Pauper

**Fact or Fiction** was strong enough that it was restricted in Type 1 (now Vintage) in December 2001, and it stayed restricted until September 2011. Invasion's cards aren't Modern-legal from this set, since Modern begins with Eighth Edition, but reprints have made Fact or Fiction, Opt, Absorb and a few others legal there.

In Legacy, **Tribal Flames** was a key burn spell in Domain Zoo, and **Phyrexian Altar** is a premier combo piece, because it turns any loop of creatures dying and returning into infinite mana. In Pauper, commons like **Exclude**, **Recoil**, **Repulse** and **Armadillo Cloak** have all seen play. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains how these eternal decks work.

### Commander

Invasion is a quiet favorite in Commander. **Phyrexian Altar** is one of the strongest sacrifice outlets in the format, the five dragon legends are classic three-color commanders, and **Yawgmoth's Agenda**, **Restock**, **Sterling Grove** and **Teferi's Moat** see play in their own niches. If you're new, our guide to [deck archetypes](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains the combo and sacrifice strategies Altar enables. As of October 2026, Phyrexian Altar and Fact or Fiction remain the set's most played cards.

## Cards from Invasion worth owning

- **Phyrexian Altar**: a top-tier Commander and Legacy combo engine.
- **Fact or Fiction**: one of the most famous blue card-advantage spells, now legal in Modern through reprints.
- **Opt**: a simple cantrip played in many formats.
- **Urza's Rage**: an uncounterable burn spell with a big late-game mode.
- **Tribal Flames**: domain burn for Legacy Zoo.
- **Rith, the Awakener and the other dragon legends**: nostalgic three-color commanders.

Invasion is where the Phyrexian war finally arrives, and it's a model of how story, mechanics and Limited can all point the same way. Its card pool lives on in Legacy, Commander and Pauper, and you can build a Legacy deck with its cards in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
