Planeshift is the second set of Magic: The Gathering's Invasion block, released in February 2001. It continues the Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria, the climax of the long-running Weatherlight Saga, as the artificial plane of Rath crashes down over Dominaria and brings fresh Phyrexian armies with it. Mechanically it's a multicolor set built on kicker, domain and a new mechanic called gating, and it printed long-lived cards like Flametongue Kavu, Meddling Mage and Terminate.

## The story

The Weatherlight Saga ran from the Weatherlight set in 1997 to Apocalypse in 2001. It follows Gerrard Capashen, heir to a legacy of artifacts built by the planeswalker Urza, and the crew of the flying ship *Weatherlight* as they prepare for Phyrexia's invasion of Dominaria. Invasion, the first set of the block, opened the war. Planeshift is the second act, told in J. Robert King's novel of the same name.

As the book begins, the plane of Rath, a Phyrexian staging ground that Gerrard's crew had fought through in the Tempest block, begins to overlay Dominaria. Huge chunks of it materialize across the world, bringing millions of Phyrexians and the evil Stronghold fortress with them. Crovax, a former *Weatherlight* crew member who became the Evincar of Rath, now leads the invasion on the ground, and he kills his own general, Tsabo Tavoc, for her failures.

Urza scatters his allies across the world. Nine planeswalkers he recruited carry armies to the fronts; the elf leader Eladamri fights alongside the Keldons in Keld; and the ancient dragon Darigaaz goes looking for the Primevals, five dragon lords who once ruled Dominaria. The *Weatherlight* rescues a minotaur army and leads the push across Urborg toward the Stronghold. Along the way, the minotaur Tahngarth finally kills his old enemy Greven il-Vec, the brutal commander of the Phyrexian warship *Predator*.

Then things fall apart. The planeswalkers' assault on Phyrexia itself goes wrong when one of them, Tevesh Szat, betrays the others. Urza, seduced by the beauty of Phyrexia, bows to the dark god Yawgmoth. Ertai, a mage left behind on Rath years earlier and now remade as a Phyrexian (the card Ertai, the Corrupted), snatches Gerrard and Squee into the Stronghold, where Crovax kills Squee and Gerrard too swears loyalty to Yawgmoth. Darigaaz's resurrection of the Primevals goes out of control until Karn talks him down, and the dragon sacrifices himself. The book ends with Dominaria near collapse and Karn emerging with the Thran Tome and a plan, setting up the finale in Apocalypse.

## Mechanics

### Gating

Gating creatures are cheap for their size, but when one enters the battlefield you must return a creature of one of its two colors that you control to your hand. Silver Drake, a white-blue common, is a 3/3 flier for three that bounces a white or blue creature. The drawback can be an upside: bouncing a creature with an enters-the-battlefield effect lets you use it again. Doomsday Specter and Shivan Wurm are the rare versions, and Shivan Wurm's 7/7 trample body for five made it a popular big threat.

### Kicker with new costs

Kicker, from Invasion, is an optional extra cost that upgrades a spell. Planeshift kept mana kickers (Orim's Chant can be kicked for an extra `{W}` to also stop attacks) but added nonmana ones. Phyrexian Scuta's kicker is paying 3 life to enter with two +1/+1 counters, and a common cycle like Magma Burst asks you to sacrifice lands for a bigger effect. The five Battlemages have two different kickers each: Sunscape Battlemage can be kicked to kill a flier, to draw two cards, or both.

### Domain, Familiars, Lairs and Planeswalker enchantments

Domain counts your basic land types, as on Gaea's Might and the huge Draco, a sixteen-mana dragon that gets two cheaper per type. The Familiars are two-mana creatures that cut the cost of spells in two colors, like Stormscape Familiar (white and black spells). The Dragon Lairs, such as Crosis's Catacombs, are three-color lands that return another land to your hand when they enter. A cycle of rare "Planeswalker's" enchantments depicts the planeswalkers Urza recruited; each reveals a random card from an opponent's hand and punishes them based on its mana value. ## Limited

Invasion block Limited was usually drafted with two Invasion packs and one Planeshift pack. It's often cited by longtime players as one of the great multicolor draft formats. Invasion pushed you into three or more colors with gold cards, domain and fixers, and Planeshift added more of the same with gating creatures, Familiars and the three-color Lairs.

Archetypes were less about ten two-color pairs and more about which "wedge" of three allied colors you landed in, since Invasion's dragons, charms and Lairs are all allied-color triples. Planeshift's gating commons are among its best Limited cards: Silver Drake, Cavern Harpy and Lava Zombie give a lot of stats for their mana, and their bounce lets you reuse creatures with useful enter effects. Removal like Terminate, Strafe and Slay was prized, and Hobble shut down an attacker while drawing a card. The format was rarely fast, so players could take strong gold cards and fix their mana. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (March 2001 to October 2002)

Planeshift entered a Standard alongside Sixth Edition (later Seventh), Mercadian Masques block and Invasion. Its biggest card was **Flametongue Kavu**, which instantly became one of the best creatures in the format: a 4/2 body that also killed most opposing creatures. It went straight into red-green **Fires** decks built around Invasion's Fires of Yavimaya, and into black-red decks too.

At the 2001 World Championship, Tom van de Logt won with **Machine Head**, a black-red control deck whose creatures included Phyrexian Scuta and Flametongue Kavu. Meddling Mage saw play in white-blue decks as a way to stop an opponent's key spell, and Terminate became a standard black-red removal spell. No Planeshift card was banned in Standard, and the set rotated out along with the rest of Invasion block when Onslaught arrived in October 2002. Our guide to [deck archetypes](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains the aggro-control spectrum these decks sat on.

### Legacy, Vintage, Modern and Commander (as of October 2026)

Planeshift predates Modern's Eighth Edition cutoff, so the set itself isn't Modern-legal, but many of its cards have been reprinted in later sets and are legal there now, including Terminate and Meddling Mage. In older formats:

- **Flametongue Kavu** was a fixture of Extended creature decks for years and remains a Commander favorite.
- **Eladamri's Call** helps Legacy creature toolbox decks find a key piece at instant speed.
- **Meddling Mage** has been a Legacy option for shutting off a combo deck's key card.
- **Commander** players use Terminate, Diabolic Intent, Eladamri's Call, Orim's Chant, Lord of the Undead and Questing Phelddagrif.
- **Pauper**: Terminate is a common and is played in black-red decks.

Planeshift has no Reserved List cards, and none of its cards are banned or restricted in Legacy, Vintage or Commander. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) covers the format where most of these cards still live.

## Cards from Planeshift worth owning

- **Flametongue Kavu**: the classic removal-on-a-body creature.
- **Terminate**: clean two-mana removal for black-red decks in nearly every format.
- **Meddling Mage**: a flexible hate card for Legacy and Commander.
- **Eladamri's Call**: instant-speed creature tutoring for Legacy and Commander.
- **Diabolic Intent**: a strong tutor for black Commander decks.
- **Lord of the Undead**: a long-time pick for Zombie tribal.
