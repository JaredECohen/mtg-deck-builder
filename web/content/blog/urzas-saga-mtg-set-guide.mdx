Urza's Saga is the first set of the Urza block in Magic: The Gathering, released in October 1998, and it's often called the most powerful expansion ever printed. It told the story of the planeswalker Urza in the centuries after the Brothers' War, introduced cycling and echo, and unleashed so many broken cards that the following months became known as "Combo Winter," with ban after ban in Standard. Gaea's Cradle, Tolarian Academy, Show and Tell, Sneak Attack and Yawgmoth's Will all came from here.

## The story

The Urza block is a flashback. By 1998 the main Magic story was following Gerrard and the Weatherlight crew, and Urza's Saga jumped back thousands of years to explain where the Weatherlight, the Legacy and the whole plan to defeat Phyrexia came from. The block is tied to the Artifacts Cycle novels; the book paired with this set is Planeswalker by Lynn Abbey.

At the end of the Brothers' War, Urza set off the Golgothian Sylex, an artifact that devastated the continent of Terisiare and killed his brother Mishra. The blast also ignited Urza's planeswalker spark. Urza's Saga begins with him drifting through the planes, haunted by grief and by what he has learned: his brother had been corrupted by Phyrexia, the mechanical hell ruled by Yawgmoth.

Urza swears to destroy Phyrexia, and his journey shows up in the set's three main settings.

- **Phyrexia.** Urza attacks Phyrexia directly and is driven back. He is helped through this period by Xantcha, a Phyrexian sleeper agent who broke free of her programming, and his encounter with the demon Gix and Phyrexia's horrors leave him unhinged for a long time.
- **Serra's Realm.** Urza recovers in the artificial plane made by the planeswalker Serra, a world of angels and enchantments. That's where cards like Serra Avatar, Serra's Sanctum and the set's white enchantments come from.
- **Tolaria and Argoth.** Back on Dominaria, Urza founds the Tolarian Academy on the island of Tolaria to train artificers and wizards, including his longtime friend Barrin and a young student named Teferi. The Academy is where Urza experiments with time travel, which eventually goes catastrophically wrong in the later books. The forest of Argoth, home of Titania and the elves, gives the set its green cards, like Gaea's Cradle and Priest of Titania.

Out of all this comes the plan that drives the next five years of Magic's story: the Legacy, a set of artifacts (including the skyship Weatherlight and the silver golem Karn) designed to defeat Phyrexia. Karn, Silver Golem appears as a legendary creature in this set, a long way from the planeswalker he becomes in Apocalypse.

## Mechanics

### Cycling

Cycling debuted in Urza's Saga. A card with cycling lets you pay its cycling cost and discard it to draw a card, so a card that's wrong for the moment is never truly dead. The set had a cycle of common lands with cycling `{2}`, like Drifting Meadow and Remote Isle, plus spells such as Lull. Cycling became one of the most-reprinted mechanics in the game.

### Echo

A permanent with echo makes you pay its mana cost again at the start of your next upkeep, or sacrifice it. In return, echo creatures were big for their cost. Viashino Outrider is a typical example: a three-mana 4/2 that you have to pay for twice. Echo created hard decisions between developing your board and keeping your last threat.

### Free spells

Several cards untap your lands when they resolve, effectively costing nothing. Great Whale untaps up to seven lands when it enters, Peregrine Drake untaps five, and Time Spiral untaps six on top of giving everyone a new hand. These were meant to be fun value, but combined with the set's mana producers they became the engine of several combo decks.

### Enchantment themes

The set was originally designed around enchantments, and that shows. "Sleeping" enchantments like Opal Archangel turn into creatures when your opponent casts a creature spell, and "growing" enchantments like Midsummer Revel gather counters over time. Argothian Enchantress draws you a card every time you cast an enchantment, and Serra's Sanctum makes white mana for each enchantment you control.

### The legendary mana lands

Gaea's Cradle (green mana per creature), Serra's Sanctum (white per enchantment) and Tolarian Academy (blue per artifact) are three legendary lands that can make enormous amounts of mana. Academy, in particular, broke the game.

## Limited

Urza's Saga was the first set sold in 75-card tournament decks instead of 60-card starters, partly to make Sealed Deck events easier to run. It was drafted and played in Sealed at the time, but its Limited format is remembered far less than its Constructed chaos, and you won't find the color-pair archetype design that modern sets have.

What's well known is which cards dominated. Pestilence, a common, was a game-winning bomb in Limited: it can wipe out an opponent's small creatures while you keep a big one alive. Arc Lightning was premium common removal, Pacifism and Expunge answered threats, and echo creatures offered big bodies early at the cost of tempo later. The cycling lands and cycling spells smoothed out draws in a way players hadn't had before.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Combo Winter is the defining story of Urza's Saga. Within weeks of release, decks built around Tolarian Academy, cheap artifacts and card-drawing like Windfall and Stroke of Genius were winning in the first few turns. Wizards' Duelists' Convocation International (DCI) responded with an unprecedented series of bans in Standard, all verified on the official ban timeline:

- **December 1998:** Tolarian Academy and Windfall were banned in Standard, effective at the start of 1999.
- **March 1999:** Dream Halls, Earthcraft, Fluctuator, Lotus Petal, Recurring Nightmare and Time Spiral were banned in Standard, effective April 1, and an emergency announcement added Memory Jar from Urza's Legacy to the list.
- **Summer 1999:** Mind Over Matter was banned in Standard as well.

Of those, Tolarian Academy, Windfall, Fluctuator and Time Spiral are Urza's Saga cards. In Urza block Constructed, Gaea's Cradle, Serra's Sanctum and Tolarian Academy were all banned by June 1999.

Once the dust settled, the set still shaped Standard until Invasion arrived in late 2000. Morphling became the control finisher of the era, Phyrexian Processor turned life into enormous Minion tokens, and Wildfire gave red mana-ramp decks a way to wreck opposing lands while keeping their own big threats. Stroke of Genius was both card draw and a kill condition. In Extended, Goblin Lackey made Goblins so fast that it was banned there in 2003.

As of October 2026, Urza's Saga is one of the most important sets in eternal formats:

- **Legacy.** Show and Tell and Sneak Attack form the core of Sneak and Show, which cheats huge creatures like Emrakul, the Aeons Torn onto the battlefield. Gaea's Cradle and Priest of Titania power Elves, Serra's Sanctum and Argothian Enchantress power Enchantress, Exploration fuels Lands decks, Back to Basics punishes nonbasic mana, and Goblin Lackey still leads Goblins. Windfall, Yawgmoth's Will and Tolarian Academy are banned. Our [Legacy guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) covers these archetypes.
- **Vintage.** Tolarian Academy, Windfall and Yawgmoth's Will are restricted, and Smokestack and Show and Tell appear in prison and combo decks.
- **Pauper.** Peregrine Drake was banned in Pauper in 2016 for powering infinite-mana combos; Pestilence and Duress have long histories there.
- **Commander.** Tolarian Academy is banned. Gaea's Cradle is one of the most powerful lands in the format, and Exploration, Smokestack, Sneak Attack, Show and Tell and Argothian Enchantress all have dedicated decks. See our [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

Because they're on the Reserved List, Gaea's Cradle, Serra's Sanctum and Tolarian Academy are among the 18 Urza's Saga cards that Wizards has committed never to reprint.

## Cards from Urza's Saga worth owning

- **Show and Tell**: the engine of Legacy's Sneak and Show and a Commander favorite.
- **Sneak Attack**: puts your biggest creature into play for one red mana.
- **Gaea's Cradle**: the defining green Commander land (Reserved List).
- **Exploration**: extra land drops for Legacy Lands and Commander.
- **Back to Basics**: a blue prison piece against nonbasic mana bases.
- **Argothian Enchantress**: the card-draw engine for enchantment decks.
- **Duress**: cheap discard that's been reprinted many times.

## The takeaway

Urza's Saga is a cautionary tale and a treasure chest at once. It broke Standard so badly that Wizards rethought how sets were developed, yet many of its cards are what make Legacy and Commander fun today. If you want to play with it, Legacy's Sneak and Show deck is the most direct way to put Show and Tell and Sneak Attack to work.
