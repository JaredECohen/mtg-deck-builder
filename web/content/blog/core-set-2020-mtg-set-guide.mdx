Core Set 2020 is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2019, a mix of new cards and reprints with no new keywords that arrived right after the huge War of the Spark. It's built around Chandra Nalaar, a strong Elemental theme and a cycle of Leylines, and for a "simple" set it caused a remarkable amount of trouble: three of its cards were banned in Standard and several more in Pioneer.

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | M20 |
| Release date | July 12, 2019 |
| Setting | Multiplanar core set (no single plane), with a Chandra flavor theme |
| Main set size | 280 cards |
| Headline themes | Elementals, Leylines, three-color "wedge" draft, protection returns as evergreen |

Core sets are meant to be an on-ramp for newer players, so M20 used only evergreen mechanics. Its one rules note was that protection came back as an evergreen keyword after a few years away.

## What the set was for

Core sets don't carry a full plot the way a premier expansion does, and Core Set 2020 had no Story Spotlight cards or official story arc. What it did have was a face: Chandra Nalaar, the fire mage who had just helped win the War of the Spark. The set gave her three planeswalker cards at three rarities, each showing a different stage of her life: Chandra, Novice Pyromancer at uncommon, Chandra, Acolyte of Flame at rare, and the mythic Chandra, Awakened Inferno, which shows her at full power. A fourth Chandra appeared in the set's planeswalker deck, and support cards like Chandra's Regulator and Chandra's Embercat tied the theme together.

The Elemental theme follows the same idea. Fire elementals sit alongside sea, sky and earth elementals across the set, including a cycle of mythic Cavaliers (Cavalier of Flame, Cavalier of Thorns and friends) and the three-color legendary Omnath, Locus of the Roil. Other planeswalkers filled out the cast: Sorin, Imperious Bloodlord led a Vampire subtheme, Vivien, Arkbow Ranger and Ajani, Strength of the Pride returned, and Mu Yanling, Sky Dancer gave the wind mage from the Global Series her first main-set card.

Beyond the flavor, M20's job was to reset the format after War of the Spark: reprint useful staples, introduce a few powerful new rares, and give drafters a clean, approachable Limited format.

## Mechanics

### No new keywords, but strong themes

Core Set 2020 had no new or returning set mechanics beyond the evergreen ones. The themes did the work instead.

### Elementals

Elemental creature cards appear in every color, and green-blue and red-green reward you for playing several. Risen Reef, a three-mana green-blue Elemental, looks at the top card of your library each time it or another Elemental enters, putting a land onto the battlefield or into your hand. Creeping Trailblazer pumps other Elementals, and Omnath, Locus of the Roil deals damage equal to your Elemental count when it enters.

### The Leyline cycle

Leylines are enchantments you can start the game with on the battlefield if they're in your opening hand. M20 reprinted Leyline of the Void, Leyline of Sanctity and Leyline of Anticipation and added two new ones: Leyline of Combustion and Leyline of Abundance, which adds an extra `{G}` whenever you tap a creature for mana. Leylines are mostly sideboard cards, but Leyline of Abundance was a green mana engine in early Pioneer until it was banned.

### Protection

Protection returned as an evergreen keyword in M20 after a few years of being used only sparingly. Unchained Berserker has protection from white, and Shifting Ceratops has protection from blue and can't be countered.

### Color hate

M20 included a small cycle of cards that punish specific colors, like Fry (5 damage to a white or blue creature or planeswalker) and Veil of Summer (a green answer to blue and black spells). These are classic sideboard cards, and some of them turned out to be much stronger than intended.

## Limited

Core Set 2020 was designed around five three-color "wedges" (one color plus its two enemies), with each wedge pointing at an ally-colored pair and enemy pairs bridging between them:

| Pair | Theme | Signpost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green-White | Going wide | Ironroot Warlord |
| White-Blue | Flyers | Empyrean Eagle |
| Blue-Black | Control with enters-the-battlefield effects | Tomebound Lich |
| Black-Red | Aggro | Ogre Siegebreaker |
| Red-Green | Elementals | Creeping Trailblazer |
| White-Black | Go-wide drain | Corpse Knight |
| Blue-Red | Flying Elementals | Lightning Stormkin |
| Black-Green | Recursive value | Moldervine Reclamation |
| Red-White | Flying aggro | Skyknight Vanguard |
| Green-Blue | Elementals with enter effects | Risen Reef |

The format played like a classic core set: plenty of solid creatures, clean removal such as Disfigure and Pacifism, and few complicated interactions, which made it a good format for newer drafters. The wedge structure meant a two-color deck could splash a third color for a bomb without much strain. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### In Standard (July 2019 to September 2020)

For a core set, M20 left fingerprints all over Standard. Field of the Dead powered ramp decks (often with Golos, Tireless Pilgrim and Scapeshift before Scapeshift rotated) that produced an endless stream of Zombies, and Wizards banned it in Standard on October 21, 2019. Veil of Summer gave green decks a nearly free answer to counterspells and discard; it was banned in Standard on November 18, 2019, along with Oko, Thief of Crowns and Once Upon a Time from Throne of Eldraine. Later, Agent of Treachery became the payoff in Fires of Invention decks that stole the opponent's best permanents, and on June 1, 2020, Wizards banned both cards.

Other M20 cards were less notorious but just as present. Knight of the Ebon Legion was a staple of black aggro, Sorin, Imperious Bloodlord fueled Vampires, Kethis, the Hidden Hand anchored a legends combo deck, and Spectral Sailor showed up in Mono-Blue Tempo. M20 rotated out of Standard when Zendikar Rising released on September 25, 2020.

### In Pioneer

M20 was one of the most-banned sets in early Pioneer. Leyline of Abundance was banned on November 4, 2019, Veil of Summer on November 11, 2019, and Field of the Dead on December 2, 2019. Kethis, the Hidden Hand followed in August 2020, and Sorin, Imperious Bloodlord was banned on August 26, 2024, after years in Vampire and Rakdos aggro decks. Lotus Field, meanwhile, became the namesake of Lotus Field Combo, a long-running Pioneer deck. As of October 2026, Knight of the Ebon Legion and Lotus Field remain legal in Pioneer. For the bigger picture on why cards get banned, see our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

### In Modern, Legacy and Commander

Field of the Dead was banned in Modern on February 15, 2021, as part of a larger update, and Lotus Field followed it onto the Modern ban list in May 2026. Veil of Summer is a long-standing sideboard card in Modern and Legacy, and the reprinted Leyline of the Void remains a go-to graveyard hoser. In Commander, Yarok, the Desecrated, Kykar, Wind's Fury and Omnath, Locus of the Roil are popular commanders, and Mystic Forge is a common inclusion in artifact decks. Golos, Tireless Pilgrim, by contrast, is banned in Commander.

## Cards from Core Set 2020 worth owning

- **Veil of Summer**: a one-mana sideboard staple for green decks in eternal formats.
- **Knight of the Ebon Legion**: still one of the best one-drops for black aggro in Pioneer.
- **Leyline of the Void**: the classic graveyard hoser, reprinted here.
- **Lotus Field**: the engine of Pioneer's Lotus Field Combo, and a great land for mono-color Commander decks.
- **Yarok, the Desecrated**: a favorite commander for enter-the-battlefield value.
- **Mystic Forge**: card advantage for any colorless or artifact Commander deck.

## The takeaway

Core Set 2020 proves that "just a core set" can still reshape formats: Field of the Dead, Veil of Summer, Agent of Treachery and a Pioneer ban list full of M20 cards are its legacy. If you're building in Pioneer, Knight of the Ebon Legion is still a strong aggressive start for black decks.
