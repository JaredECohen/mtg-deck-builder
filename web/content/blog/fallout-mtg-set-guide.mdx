Fallout is the Magic: The Gathering Universes Beyond product built on Bethesda's post-apocalyptic video game series, released in March 2024 as four preconstructed Commander decks plus Collector Boosters. It brought the Wasteland, Vault-Tec and the Brotherhood of Steel to the card table, introduced rad counters and Junk tokens, and printed 154 new cards, several of which became fixtures in Commander. It landed about a month before the Fallout TV series premiered in April 2024.

## The story

Fallout is set in an alternate history where America's 1950s optimism about atomic power never faded, right up until a nuclear exchange called the Great War wrecked the country. Centuries later the survivors scrape by in "the Wasteland": irradiated ruins full of raiders, mutated wildlife, robots that kept running after their owners died, and the Vaults, underground shelters built by the Vault-Tec Corporation that were secretly social experiments. Bottle caps from Nuka-Cola are the currency. The player character in most games is a Vault Dweller who steps out into this world.

The product doesn't tell one story. Each deck is a slice of a different game and region:

- **Scrappy Survivors** follows Fallout 4's Commonwealth, the ruined Boston area. Dogmeat, the loyal dog companion, leads it, with Preston Garvey of the Minutemen as the alternate commander. Its cards are about scavenging and kitting yourself out.
- **Mutant Menace** goes to Appalachia from Fallout 76 and is fronted by the Wise Mothman, a cryptid worshipped by a cult. The alternate commander, the Master, is the villain of the original 1997 Fallout, who wanted to turn humanity into super mutants.
- **Hail, Caesar** is pure Fallout: New Vegas. Caesar commands his Legion of slave soldiers, and Mr. House, the reclusive owner of the Las Vegas Strip and his robot army, is the alternate.
- **Science!** centers on Dr. Madison Li, a researcher from Fallout 3 and Fallout 4, and Liberty Prime, the giant robot the Brotherhood of Steel uses as a war machine.

Fan-favorite companions and characters fill the rest: Codsworth the robot butler, Nick Valentine the synth detective, Piper Wright the reporter, Three Dog the radio DJ, Yes Man the Securitron, plus Vault Boy as the set symbol and on the bobblehead cards. The flavor is the selling point. Cards like Vault 101: Birthday Party turn the opening of Fallout 3 into a Saga, and Nuka-Cola Vending Machine makes Food and Treasure the way the games hand out drinks and caps.

## The decks

Each deck is three colors, which is how Wizards packed four very different playstyles into one release.

| Deck | Colors | Commander | Theme |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scrappy Survivors | Red, green, white | Dogmeat, Ever Loyal | Auras and Equipment, Junk tokens |
| Mutant Menace | Black, green, blue | The Wise Mothman | Rad counters, milling, +1/+1 counters and proliferate |
| Hail, Caesar | Red, white, black | Caesar, Legion's Emperor | Tokens, sacrifice, squad |
| Science! | Blue, red, white | Dr. Madison Li | Artifacts and energy |

**Dogmeat, Ever Loyal** mills five cards when it enters and gets back an Aura or Equipment, then makes a Junk token whenever one of your enchanted or equipped creatures attacks. It's a "voltron-lite" deck that stays wide enough to survive a removal spell.

**The Wise Mothman** gives every player a rad counter when it enters or attacks, and whenever nonland cards are milled it spreads +1/+1 counters around your board. It's the most unusual of the four: you poison the table with radiation and grow from the fallout.

**Caesar, Legion's Emperor** lets you sacrifice another creature whenever you attack, then pick two modes: two hasty Soldier tokens that come in attacking, a card for 1 life, or damage to an opponent equal to your token count. It's an aggressive aristocrats deck.

**Dr. Madison Li** gives you energy whenever you cast an artifact spell and spends it to pump an attacker, draw cards, or return an artifact from your graveyard. It plays like a value engine built on artifacts.

### New and returning mechanics

**Rad counters.** A new counter that goes on players. At the start of your precombat main phase, if you have rad counters, you mill that many cards. For each nonland card milled, you lose 1 life and remove one rad counter. It's a slow, self-correcting poison: lands you mill don't hurt you, so the counters can hang around. Glowing One, an uncommon deathtouch Zombie Mutant, gives four rad counters to any player it hits, and Nuclear Fallout is a board wipe that also hands every player X rad counters.

**Junk tokens.** A new predefined artifact token: tap and sacrifice it to exile the top card of your library, and you may play that card this turn, at sorcery speed only. Junk is card advantage that rewards having mana to spare. Mister Gutsy, Junktown and Veronica, Dissident Scribe all make it.

**Bobbleheads.** A new artifact subtype. Seven three-mana Bobbleheads, one for each S.P.E.C.I.A.L. stat, all tap for any color and each has a second ability that scales with how many Bobbleheads you control. Luck Bobblehead even wins the game if you roll exactly seven sixes, which is more joke than plan.

**Energy** returned from Kaladesh. You get `{E}` counters and spend them on abilities. Liberty Prime, Recharged is a huge 8/8 that you sacrifice unless you pay `{E}{E}` each time it attacks or blocks.

**Squad** came back from the Warhammer 40,000 decks: pay an extra cost any number of times and get that many token copies of the creature. Wasteland Raider, a four-mana 4/3 that makes each player sacrifice a creature when it enters, can become several edicts at once.

Smaller pieces include proliferate, Sagas representing the Vaults, and a cycle of five enemy-color filter lands (Desolate Mire, Ferrous Lake, Viridescent Bog, Sunscorched Divide, Overflowing Basin) that pay `{1}` for two colored mana, finishing a cycle Odyssey started in 2001.

Two other cards are worth calling out. **Inventory Management** is a two-mana split second instant that lets you reattach every Aura and Equipment you control, which can win a combat or save your Equipment from removal. **Strong Back** is an Aura that gives +2/+2 for each Aura and Equipment on the creature and makes further Auras and equip costs `{3}` cheaper, a big payoff in voltron decks.

The reprints were generous too: the decks included lands like Exotic Orchard and many checklands and battle lands, plus Commander standbys like Chaos Warp, Blasphemous Act, Heroic Intervention and Solemn Simulacrum, all in Fallout art. Collector Boosters added a few reprints that aren't in any deck, in a Pip-Boy screen frame: Wasteland, Crucible of Worlds, Vandalblast, Ravages of War and Farewell, plus nine reprints given Fallout names, such as Tarmogoyf printed as Scrounging Deathclaw.

## How it plays

Out of the box the four decks are balanced against each other and sit in the casual-to-focused range of the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). As of October 2026, none of the cards in the four decks is on the official Game Changers list (Farewell, which is, appears only in Collector Boosters), so the decks play like typical precons.

- **Scrappy Survivors** is the most straightforward: suit up one or two creatures and swing. It's a good first deck if you like attacking.
- **Hail, Caesar** is the fastest. It goes wide with tokens and keeps attacking, and it punishes players who leave themselves open.
- **Mutant Menace** is slow and interactive. Rad counters chip at everyone's life and libraries, so games feel like a war of attrition.
- **Science!** builds up artifacts and energy and can grind through long games, with Liberty Prime as a top end.

If you're upgrading one, the usual advice from our [precon upgrade guide](/blog/how-to-upgrade-a-commander-precon) applies: cut the clunkiest top-end cards, add cheap interaction and smooth the mana. The decks are built around their new commanders, so most upgrades come from adding more of what the commander wants, like extra Equipment for Dogmeat or more self-mill for the Mothman.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Fallout's new cards were never legal in Standard, Pioneer or Modern, so their competitive footprint is in Commander. Legacy and Vintage allow the new cards, but none of them became a regular in those formats.

In Commander the impact was real but quiet:

- **The filter lands** went straight into enemy-color mana bases. They don't enter tapped and they make two colors from one land, which three-color decks need.
- **Codsworth, Handy Helper** became a common pick in Aura and Equipment decks, since ward 2 on your commander is cheap protection.
- **Nuka-Cola Vending Machine, Mutational Advantage and Atomize** showed up in Food, counters and Golgari decks respectively.
- **Rad counters** gave mill decks a new angle. The Wise Mothman and the Master are both build-arounds that care about cards being milled.
- **Squad** and **Junk** are both repeatable value, and they fit Commander's long games better than one-on-one formats.

There have been no Commander bans involving Fallout's new cards as of October 2026. The bigger story was commercial and cultural: Fallout brought a lot of video game fans to Magic through Commander alone, with no draftable boosters at all.

## Cards from Fallout worth owning

- **The five filter lands**: untapped two-color mana for any deck in those colors.
- **Codsworth, Handy Helper**: protection and mana for every Aura or Equipment deck.
- **Mutational Advantage**: a one-card answer to a board wipe in counters decks.
- **Atomize**: flexible instant-speed removal with a small proliferate bonus.
- **Nuka-Cola Vending Machine**: a steady Food and Treasure engine.
- **Pip-Boy 3000**: a cheap Equipment that keeps paying off every attack.
- **Wasteland and Crucible of Worlds (Collector Boosters)**: long-standing Legacy and Commander staples, if you're opening packs.

## The takeaway

Fallout is a good place to start Commander if you love the games: each deck is playable out of the box, the themes are easy to read, and the strongest new cards, the filter lands and Codsworth, hold up in decks that have nothing to do with the Wasteland.
