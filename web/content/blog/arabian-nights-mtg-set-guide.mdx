Arabian Nights is the first expansion ever made for Magic: The Gathering, released in December 1993, only months after the original Alpha and Beta core sets. Designed by Richard Garfield, it borrowed its characters and places straight from *One Thousand and One Nights* instead of inventing a plane. With just 78 cards, it introduced ideas Magic still uses, from coin flips to utility lands, and printed some of the most powerful cards in the game, like Bazaar of Baghdad and Library of Alexandria.

## The setting and flavor

Arabian Nights doesn't have a plot in the modern sense. Garfield, inspired in part by the *Sandman* comic issue "Ramadan," built the set around characters, creatures and places from *One Thousand and One Nights*, keeping their original names. You'll find **Aladdin**, **Ali Baba**, **Sindbad**, **King Suleiman** and **Shahrazad** herself, along with djinn and efreet, flying carpets, magic lamps and cities like Baghdad and Alexandria. According to Magic lore, the set's entire flavor text was written in a single night.

Because these were real-world stories, later Magic canon had to fit them into the multiverse. The explanation is the plane of **Rabiah**, once ruled by djinn until a great spirit war weakened them and humans became the dominant people. The legendary figures of the tales lived long ago, and the plane was later copied a thousand times over, a nod to the thousand and one nights. A few stories set on Rabiah followed, including the comics about the planeswalker Taysir and short stories about the City of Brass and the origins of the Serendib Efreet. Arabian Nights stayed unique as a real-world setting until Portal Three Kingdoms in 1999.

The set is also part of Magic's production history. It was the first set with an expansion symbol, a scimitar. It was originally planned as a standalone game with its own card back, which was dropped late in development, and the basic lands that were meant to be removed left one stray **Mountain** on the common sheet. Packs held just eight cards: six commons and two uncommons.

## Mechanics

Arabian Nights has no keywords, but it's full of design firsts that later became Magic staples.

- **Stealing permanents.** **Old Man of the Sea** (`{1}{U}{U}`) taps to take control of a creature with power less than or equal to its own, and **Aladdin** steals artifacts. Control-changing became a blue staple.
- **Abilities opponents can use.** **Ifh-Bíff Efreet** is a 3/3 flier whose `{G}` ability, dealing 1 damage to each flier and each player, can be activated by any player.
- **Utility lands.** **Bazaar of Baghdad** taps to draw two cards and discard three, with no mana at all. **Library of Alexandria** draws a card if you have exactly seven in hand. **Desert** pings attacking creatures.
- **Coin flips.** **Bottle of Suleiman**, **Mijae Djinn** and **Ydwen Efreet** were the first cards to flip coins.
- **Early cumulative upkeep.** **Cyclone** grows a counter each turn and asks you to pay more to keep it, two years before Ice Age named the mechanic.
- **First -1/-1 counters and lifelink ancestors.** **Unstable Mutation** gives +3/+3 but adds a -1/-1 counter each upkeep, and **El-Hajjâj** gains you life whenever it deals damage, the idea that became lifelink.
- **Cards from outside the game.** **Ring of Ma'rûf** fetches a card from outside the game, which inspired the Wish cycle in [Judgment](/blog/judgment-mtg-set-guide).

Diacritics in card names, like the accents in Juzám Djinn and Ifh-Bíff Efreet, weren't printed in the titles because the font couldn't handle them, but they appear in the text box and in the official Oracle names.

## Limited

Arabian Nights was never drafted the way modern sets are. Booster draft hadn't become a standard format, the eight-card packs were sold as an add-on to the base game, and the set was on shelves for only about a month. There's no meaningful record of Arabian Nights Limited archetypes, so it's best understood as a collection of add-on cards. The commons make that clear: **Kird Ape**, **Flying Men** (a 1/1 flier for `{U}`), **Erg Raiders**, **Hasran Ogress** and **Moorish Cavalry** were efficient creatures for the time, while cards like **Camel** and **War Elephant** carried banding from the base set.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Early tournament Magic

In 1994, before Standard existed, tournaments used essentially every card printed, with a restricted list. Arabian Nights cards were part of that pool. **Juzám Djinn** was considered one of the most powerful creatures in the game, **Serendib Efreet** and **Erhnam Djinn** were efficient threats, and **Library of Alexandria** eventually landed on the restricted list. When Type II arrived in January 1995, Arabian Nights itself wasn't in it, but its cards returned through reprints. Erhnam Djinn's Chronicles printing made it the centerpiece of **Erhnamgeddon**, a 1996 Type II archetype that cast Armageddon to wipe out the lands the opponent needed to deal with the Djinn. Learn more about how these formats evolved in [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

### Vintage and Legacy today

**Bazaar of Baghdad** is one of the defining cards of Vintage, the engine of Dredge decks that discard cards on purpose and play them from the graveyard. It's banned in Legacy. **Library of Alexandria** is restricted in Vintage and banned in Legacy. **Shahrazad** was banned in both formats in September 2008 because subgames ate up tournament time. In Legacy, **Serendib Efreet** has seen play in Faerie Stompy, and **Kird Ape** has been a classic Zoo one-drop.

In 2020, Wizards banned **Jihad** and **Stone-Throwing Devils** in every format and removed their images from its card database for being racist or culturally offensive.

### Pauper, Commander and Old School

A few Arabian Nights commons are Pauper-legal, and **Oubliette** has seen Pauper play as removal. In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), **City of Brass** is a common five-color land and **Bazaar of Baghdad** is legal, while Library of Alexandria and Shahrazad are banned. Outside sanctioned play, Arabian Nights is a cornerstone of the community-run "Old School" format, which uses only cards from 1993 and 1994, and **Dandân** is the card at the heart of the casual Forgetful Fish variant.

## Cards from Arabian Nights worth owning

- **City of Brass**: still a practical five-color land in Commander and casual decks, with many reprints.
- **Kird Ape**: an efficient Zoo one-drop that's legal in Modern and Pauper through reprints.
- **Serendib Efreet**: a strong blue three-drop for Legacy tempo and casual decks.
- **Bazaar of Baghdad**: the Vintage Dredge engine.
- **Erhnam Djinn**: a piece of Type II history with several reprints.
- **Desert**: a cheap, playable utility land in Pauper and Commander.
