Commander Legends is the Magic: The Gathering set released on November 20, 2020, and it was the first set ever built to be drafted as Commander. Instead of a normal 40-card draft, you picked two cards at a time, chose a legendary commander from your pile and played a 60-card multiplayer game. It also became one of the most important Commander products of its era, with Jeweled Lotus, Opposition Agent, Jeska's Will, Hullbreacher and reprints such as Vampiric Tutor and Mana Drain.

## What the set was for

Commander Legends had no storyline. Lead designer Gavin Verhey's team set out to answer a question players had asked for years: what would a draft look like if the end product was a Commander deck? The answer was a supplemental "innovation product" stuffed with legends from every corner of the multiverse, from Lorwyn and Alara to the Weatherlight era, alongside reprints Commander players had wanted.

The structure of the booster did most of the work. Every Draft Booster held two legendary creatures in their own slot, on top of the usual rare, so drafters always had commanders to choose from. One in roughly six packs replaced a common with **The Prismatic Piper**, printed at a new "special" rarity, which works like a basic commander: any player may use it whether they drafted it or not, and it picks a color before the game begins.

The draft rules were also unusual. You opened three boosters but took two cards at every pick, which kept the draft moving and helped players stay inside their commander's color identity. Decks were 60 cards rather than 100, and the singleton rule was waived, though the set was big enough that duplicates rarely came up. If you are new to the format the set was built around, start with [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

The set wasn't a fixed print run, and it introduced foil-etched cards, a new foiling treatment with its own frame that Wizards went on to use in many later Commander products.

### The two Commander decks

Commander Legends also came with two 100-card preconstructed decks, each with three new cards and a foil commander. They were designed as entry points, with mostly reprints:

- **Arm for Battle** (red-white) is led by **Wyleth, Soul of Steel**, a trampler that draws a card for each Aura and Equipment attached to it when it attacks. The deck is a voltron and Equipment pile with reprints like **Sram, Senior Edificer**, **Blackblade Reforged** and **Swords to Plowshares**.
- **Reap the Tides** (green-blue) is led by **Aesi, Tyrant of Gyre Strait**, a 5/5 Serpent that gives you an extra land drop each turn and draws a card on landfall. It is a ramp and lands deck with staples such as **Three Visits** and **Kodama's Reach**.

Both decks upgrade well; see [how to upgrade a Commander precon](/blog/how-to-upgrade-a-commander-precon).

## Mechanics

### Partner

Partner returned from Commander 2016 at a much larger scale. If both of your legends have partner, you can play them together as your commanders, and your deck's color identity combines theirs. The set had 40 mono-colored legends with partner, plus a colorless one and the partner planeswalkers **Jeska, Thrice Reborn** and **Tevesh Szat, Doom of Fools**, so a drafter in two colors could almost always build a legal pair. **Kediss, Emberclaw Familiar**, a red partner that copies your commander's combat damage to every other opponent, shows the multiplayer focus.

Partner also made the set a goldmine for competitive Commander. **Rograkh, Son of Rohgahh** costs zero mana, so it lets a single-colored commander pick up red identity at almost no cost, and it became a common half of cEDH partner pairs. The set also reprinted the original Commander 2016 partners, including **Thrasios, Triton Hero**, **Tymna the Weaver**, **Kraum, Ludevic's Opus** and **Vial Smasher the Fierce**.

### Monarch

Monarch came back from Conspiracy: Take the Crown because it shines in multiplayer. When a card makes you the monarch, you draw an extra card at the beginning of your end step. Whenever a creature deals combat damage to the monarch, its controller takes the crown. The five Courts, such as **Court of Grace** (a 1/1 flying Spirit each upkeep, or a 4/4 Angel if you're the monarch), and **Archon of Coronation** (you don't lose life from damage while you're the monarch) were the headline monarch cards.

### Encore

Encore was the set's only new keyword. You pay the encore cost and exile the creature from your graveyard to make a token copy for each opponent; each copy has haste, must attack its assigned opponent, and is sacrificed at the end step. **Briarblade Adept**, a common that shrinks a defender with each attack, gives you three hasty attackers for `{3}{B}` in a four-player game. **Phyrexian Triniform** is the extreme version: a 9/9 whose encore for `{12}` leaves behind three Golems per copy when they die.

### Cascade

Cascade returned in the green-blue archetype. When you cast a spell with cascade, you exile cards from the top of your library until you hit a cheaper nonland card and may cast it for free. **Imoti, Celebrant of Bounty** gives cascade to every spell you cast with mana value 6 or greater.

## Limited

Commander Legends draft is closer to a four-player Commander night than to a typical 40-card draft. Games are longer, removal and card advantage matter more than a tight curve, and politics are real: the monarch passes around the table, and encore punishes everyone at once. The set officially supported ten two-color archetypes:

- **White-blue:** flying
- **Blue-black:** self-mill and encore
- **Black-red:** sacrifice
- **Red-green:** power matters
- **Green-white:** +1/+1 counters
- **White-black:** tokens
- **Blue-red:** Pirates and Treasure
- **Black-green:** Elves
- **Red-white:** Equipment and Auras
- **Green-blue:** cascade and big spells (mana value 6 or more)

Because most commanders were mono-colored partners, the usual plan was to grab two strong legends early and draft toward their combined colors, using **The Prismatic Piper** as a fallback if your second commander never arrived. Commanders that generate value on their own, like **Malcolm, Keen-Eyed Navigator** for Pirates or **Ich-Tekik, Salvage Splicer** for artifacts, were high picks. Commons such as **Sailor of Means** (a Treasure for color fixing) and **Dragon Egg** (a blocker that leaves a flying Dragon behind) did a lot of work in the long games. Cube designers still borrow the set's ideas; see the [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Commander Legends was never legal in Standard, Pioneer or Modern, so its biggest effect was on Commander and, to a lesser extent, Legacy and Pauper.

### Commander and bans

Two cards from the set were banned in Commander:

- **Hullbreacher** was banned on July 12, 2021. It was meant to punish extra card draw, but players paired it with wheel effects to empty opponents' hands while refilling their own, the same reason **Leovold, Emissary of Trest** had been banned.
- **Jeweled Lotus** was banned on September 23, 2024, as part of the Commander Rules Committee's move against fast mana; it made almost every deck faster at casting its commander.

As of October 2026, **Opposition Agent**, **Jeska's Will** and **Vampiric Tutor** are on the Game Changers list, which caps how many such cards a deck can run in the lower brackets. See the [Commander brackets guide](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) for how that works.

In competitive Commander, the set's partners mattered most. Rograkh became one of the most common partners in cEDH, and reprinting Thrasios, Tymna, Kraum and Vial Smasher made those established partners easier to get.

### Pauper and Legacy

The commons were legal in Pauper, and **Fall from Favor** proved too strong: Wizards banned it in Pauper on January 14, 2021, citing Magic Online data showing it was both removal and a card-advantage engine through the monarch. Its new cards are legal in Legacy and Vintage too, but the set's real home has always been the command zone. For Pauper itself, see [what is Pauper](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg).

## Cards from Commander Legends worth owning

- **Jeska's Will:** one of the best mana and card advantage spells in red Commander.
- **Opposition Agent:** a flash creature that turns every tutor and fetchland against its owner.
- **Akroma's Will:** a one-card finisher or fog for any creature deck with white.
- **Court of Grace:** steady flying tokens that also invite opponents to attack each other for the crown.
- **Commander's Plate:** turns a commander into a hard-to-block, hard-to-kill threat.
- **Kodama of the East Tree and Sakashima of a Thousand Faces:** popular commanders and combo pieces.
- **Rograkh, Son of Rohgahh:** the go-to free partner for competitive pairs.
- **Archon of Coronation:** a monarch payoff that protects your life total.

Commander Legends is the set to look at if you want to see how a Commander deck is put together from scratch: two partners, a theme and a pile of interaction.
