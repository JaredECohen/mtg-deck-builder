Born of the Gods is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2014, the second set of the Theros block, set on a plane inspired by Greek myth. Its story follows the satyr planeswalker Xenagos as he climbs into the heavens to become a god, and its cards added inspired, tribute and the five two-color Gods to the block's enchantment theme. It's a small set, but Courser of Kruphix and Brimaz, King of Oreskos made it matter well beyond its own season.

## The story

On Theros, the gods are real, and their power comes from the devotion of mortals. They live in Nyx, the starry night sky, and Nyxborn creatures made of that starlight sometimes cross into the mortal world. In the first Theros set, the planeswalker Elspeth Tirel found a home there as the champion of Heliod, the sun god.

Born of the Gods turns to Xenagos, a satyr who once led the endless revels of Skola Vale. When his planeswalker spark ignited, he saw how small the gods of Theros were compared to the wider multiverse, and the realization left him bitter and ambitious. He returns to Theros wanting to join, or replace, its pantheon. His monsters attack the cities of Meletis, Akros and Setessa, and the gods begin to lose sight of their oracles, which makes them suspicious of each other. Meanwhile Mogis, the god of slaughter, sends armies of minotaurs against the human cities, and the boundary between Nyx and the mortal world grows thin enough for Nyxborn creatures to spill through.

Elspeth leads an army of heroes to break the minotaur siege at Akros, and the humans win. The trouble is the celebration that follows. Xenagos has learned that the gods are fed by devotion, and he turns the victory revel into the ritual that launches him into Nyx as the god of revels. Elspeth is blamed for his ascension and driven into the wilderness in disgrace. Another planeswalker is on the plane too: Kiora, the merfolk planeswalker from Zendikar, has arrived to gather sea monsters for her own fight at home, and the tritons of Theros take her for an avatar of the sea god Thassa.

The set leaves Theros with a new god in Nyx and its heroine in exile. In the next set, Journey into Nyx, Elspeth and her friend Ajani travel into Nyx itself to confront Xenagos, and Elspeth kills him with the spear Godsend.

## Mechanics

### Inspired

Inspired is an ability word that triggers whenever the creature untaps. In practice, that means attacking with it, so it taps, and getting the bonus at your next untap step. **Pain Seer** (rare) is a two-mana 2/2 that puts the top card of your library into your hand each time it untaps, at the cost of life equal to that card's mana value. **Sphinx's Disciple** (common) draws a card each time it untaps. Inspired creatures reward attacking even into a board where they might trade.

### Tribute

Tribute gives your opponent a choice when the creature enters. They can put some +1/+1 counters on it, or decline and let a bonus effect happen. **Fanatic of Xenagos** (uncommon) is a 3/3 trampler with tribute 1. If the opponent doesn't add the counter, it gets +1/+1 and haste for the turn. Tribute is often weaker than it looks, because the opponent always picks the option that's worse for you.

### Bestow

Bestow returns from Theros. A bestow creature can be cast normally, or for its bestow cost as an Aura on another creature. If that creature leaves, the Aura falls off and becomes a creature again. **Eidolon of Countless Battles** (rare) grows with each creature and Aura you control, and **Herald of Torment** (rare) is a 3/3 flier that can instead give a creature +3/+3 and flying.

### Heroic and devotion

Heroic triggers whenever you cast a spell that targets the creature, which makes Auras, pump spells and bestow a natural fit. **Hero of Iroas** (rare) makes your Auras cheaper and gets a +1/+1 counter each time you target it. Devotion counts the colored mana symbols among the mana costs of permanents you control. The set's five Gods, one for each allied two-color pair, are indestructible enchantments that only become creatures once your devotion to their two colors reaches seven. **Xenagos, God of Revels** (mythic) gives one of your creatures haste and doubles its power each turn. For help reading devotion and mana symbols on a card, see our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Born of the Gods was drafted with one pack of itself followed by two packs of Theros, so it mostly added new tools to the Theros archetypes rather than replacing them. Heroic remained the backbone of white, blue and red, with cheap creatures like Akroan Skyguard and Elite Skirmisher growing off Auras and tricks. Black and red leaned into minotaurs, now with Ragemonger as a gold uncommon payoff. Green offered big tribute creatures like Nessian Demolok and Snake of the Golden Grove, and inspired gave every color a reason to attack.

Each two-color gold uncommon pointed at a theme. Kiora's Follower untaps permanents for green-blue ramp and inspired tricks, Siren of the Silent Song forces discards in blue-black, Ephara's Enlightenment adds a flying, counter-granting Aura for white-blue heroic, Reap What Is Sown spreads counters in green-white, and Fanatic of Xenagos is a fast red-green beater. Good commons included Bolt of Keranos, Sudden Storm and Weight of the Underworld. To learn how formats like this compare, see our [overview of Magic formats](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

When Born of the Gods released, Standard was shaped by Theros devotion decks and the Return to Ravnica block. The biggest winner was **Mono-Black Devotion**. Bile Blight gave it the cheap instant-speed removal it wanted, and right after release the deck was widely seen as one of the best in the format. Robert Berni won Grand Prix Phoenix in spring 2014 with it. Mono-Blue Devotion, the other top devotion deck, gained much less from the set.

Elsewhere, **Brimaz, King of Oreskos** became a powerhouse in white aggressive decks. **Courser of Kruphix** was a staple of green midrange, and its value only grew after Khans of Tarkir arrived in fall 2014, when it became a common sight in three-color midrange decks until the block rotated in October 2015. Searing Blood gave red aggro a removal spell that also hit the opponent. No Born of the Gods card was banned in Standard.

As of October 2026, the set's lasting impact is narrower but real. Courser of Kruphix has seen play in Modern and Pioneer midrange long after rotation. Satyr Wayfinder fills graveyards in Pioneer and Modern self-mill decks, and Retraction Helix is the core of a Pioneer combo deck that turns a creature into a repeatable bounce engine. Spirit of the Labyrinth has shown up in eternal-format hatebear decks as a way to punish extra card draw. None of the set's cards are banned in Pioneer or Modern.

In Commander, the five two-color Gods are the main draw. Xenagos, God of Revels, Karametra, God of Harvests, Phenax, God of Deception, Mogis, God of Slaughter and Ephara, God of the Polis all lead their own decks, and Courser of Kruphix and Brimaz are popular in the 99. Our [guide to Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains how to build around a legendary God.

## Cards from Born of the Gods worth owning

- **Courser of Kruphix**: card advantage and life gain in one green three-drop, playable in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Brimaz, King of Oreskos**: an efficient white threat that makes tokens on offense and defense.
- **Xenagos, God of Revels**: a Gruul commander that turns any creature into a hasty finisher.
- **Phenax, God of Deception**: a well-known mill commander.
- **Karametra, God of Harvests**: Selesnya ramp in a single card.
- **Satyr Wayfinder**: an efficient self-mill creature for graveyard decks.
- **Bile Blight**: two-mana instant removal that also answers token swarms.

Born of the Gods is a set to collect for its Gods and its green and white staples. Its Standard season was short, but in Pioneer you can still play Courser of Kruphix, Brimaz and the devotion shells that made Theros famous.
