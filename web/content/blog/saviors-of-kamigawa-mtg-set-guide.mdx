Saviors of Kamigawa is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 2005 as the third and final set of the original Kamigawa block. It closes the story of the Kami War on Kamigawa, a plane inspired by Japanese myth where mortals and spirits fight over a stolen god-child. Mechanically, it's the set that made the number of cards in your hand matter, and it introduced channel, sweep and epic, along with lasting cards like Pithing Needle.

## The story

The Kami War started with one man's greed. Takeshi Konda, the most powerful daimyo on Kamigawa, used a ritual to tear open the barrier between the mortal world and the spirit world and kidnapped a god: the child of O-Kagachi, the greatest of the kami. He sealed it inside a stone disk in his palace, a prize the spirits call That Which Was Taken, and from that day Konda stopped aging. The kami, enraged, turned on the physical world. Most mortals had no idea why their gods were killing them, and because many spirits return after being defeated, it was a war humanity could not win.

By the time of Saviors of Kamigawa, the war is in its twentieth year. The novel *Guardian*, the last of Scott McGough's Kamigawa trilogy, follows two people at its center. **Princess Michiko Konda**, Konda's daughter, has spent the block searching for the truth about her father's crime. **Toshiro Umezawa**, a thief and kanji mage who wanted nothing to do with any of it, keeps getting pulled back in, partly by his bargain with the Myojin of Night's Reach, partly by the ogre shaman Hidetsugu, whose vengeance has gone out of control.

The climax comes as O-Kagachi itself manifests above the Jukai forest. Toshi steals the disk through the shadows and realizes why Michiko matters: she and the stolen spirit are connected, essentially sisters. When Michiko touches the disk and Toshi writes a binding kanji in his own blood, the prison shatters. The freed spirit takes the name **Kyodai**, merges with Michiko, and together they destroy O-Kagachi and take its place. Kyodai punishes Konda by turning him to stone and shattering him while he's still aware.

Toshi doesn't get a happy ending, exactly. He's stabbed by a vengeful moonfolk and saved at the last moment by the Myojin of Night's Reach, who takes his sight and sends him to another world. He lands on Dominaria, where he founds Clan Umezawa, the family line that leads to Tetsuo Umezawa. Kamigawa itself got its peace, and more than a thousand years later the plane returned, transformed, in [Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty](/blog/kamigawa-neon-dynasty-mtg-set-guide).

## Mechanics

### Hand size matters

Saviors is full of cards that care how many cards are in your hand, often compared to an opponent's. The designers called this theme "wisdom." The rare "First to" legends are the headline: **Kiyomaro, First to Stand** (rare, `{3}{W}{W}`) has power and toughness equal to the cards in your hand, and **Masumaro, First to Live** gets twice that. Commons like **Akki Underling** get bigger with seven or more cards in hand. The design pushes you to hold cards instead of dumping them, which is a reversal of how most aggro decks want to play.

### Channel

Channel is an ability you activate by discarding the card from your hand, so it can't be countered like a spell. Most channel cards are Spirits that offer a choice: play the creature, or channel it for a one-time effect. **Ghost-Lit Raider** (uncommon) is a 2/1 that can ping creatures for 2, or you can pay `{3}{R}` and discard it to deal 4 damage to a creature. **Arashi, the Sky Asunder** (rare) can channel to deal X damage to every flier.

### Sweep

Sweep spells are Arcane spells that let you return any number of lands of one basic type to your hand, and the effect scales with how many you return. **Sink into Takenuma** (common) makes a player discard a card per Swamp returned. Sweep also refills your hand, which ties it to the hand-size theme.

### Epic

Epic is the set's strangest mechanic. After you cast an epic spell, you can't cast spells for the rest of the game, but at the start of each of your upkeeps you get a copy of it. **Enduring Ideal** (rare, `{5}{W}{W}`) puts an enchantment from your library onto the battlefield, then does it again every turn. **Endless Swarm** makes a Snake for each card in your hand, every turn. Epic decks need a plan that works without casting anything else.

### Flip cards and the block's mechanics

Kamigawa's flip cards return as the Ascendants, a rare cycle of monks that flip into legendary enchantments. **Erayo, Soratami Ascendant** (rare, `{1}{U}`) is a 1/1 flier that flips when the fourth spell of a turn is cast and then counters each opponent's first spell every turn. Soulshift, splice onto Arcane and bushido also continue from the earlier sets, and **Evermind** became the first card with no mana cost that you can only use through splice.

## Limited

Saviors was drafted in the order of the day, oldest set first: a pack of Champions, then Betrayers, then Saviors. That made it a 15-card supplement to the block's established Spirits, Arcane and samurai decks rather than a format of its own. Its hand-size cards asked you to hold cards, which pulled against the block's habit of chaining Spirit and Arcane spells, so many drafters treated Saviors packs as a source of removal and quality creatures rather than a new archetype.

Useful commons and uncommons included the channel Spirits such as **Ghost-Lit Raider** and **Ghost-Lit Warder**, sweep spells like **Barrel Down Sokenzan** (twice the Mountains returned in damage to a creature), **Kitsune Dawnblade** and **Torii Watchward** in white, and evasive moonfolk in blue. Hand size also rewarded slower, card-drawing decks, so blue was a natural home for the theme. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2005-2006)

Saviors entered a Standard still recovering from the Affinity era, with Mirrodin block and Kamigawa block both legal. Its biggest Standard contributions were combo and control pieces rather than aggressive creatures. **Enduring Ideal** decks, which used the epic spell to assemble a lock of enchantments one per turn, were a known archetype through late 2005 and 2006. **Owling Mine** combined Ebony Owl Netsuke with Howling Mine to bury opponents in cards and punish them for holding seven. **Pithing Needle** became a standard sideboard tool against equipment, activated-ability engines and combo pieces.

In Kamigawa Block Constructed, the set added finishers like **Kagemaro, First to Suffer**, **Maga, Traitor to Mortals** and **Arashi, the Sky Asunder**, sideboard cards such as **Manriki-Gusari** and **Kataki, War's Wage**, and efficient creatures like **Hand of Honor** and **Hand of Cruelty** that gave aggro decks a chance against the block's control decks. Saviors left Standard when Time Spiral arrived in October 2006.

### Modern, Legacy and Vintage

Because it released after Eighth Edition, Saviors is fully Modern-legal. **Pithing Needle** remains a common sideboard card in Modern and Legacy against fetch lands, planeswalkers and artifact engines, and you can learn how to use cards like it in our [sideboarding guide](/blog/sideboarding-101). **Enduring Ideal** survives as a fringe Modern combo deck. **Kataki, War's Wage** is still a cheap answer to artifact-heavy decks.

### Commander

**Erayo, Soratami Ascendant** was so oppressive in [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) that it was first banned as a commander and later banned outright. Other favorites include **Sakashima the Impostor**, **Kaho, Minamo Historian**, **Iname as One**, **Michiko Konda, Truth Seeker**, **Seed the Land**, **Twincast** and **Oboro, Palace in the Clouds**, a land that returns to your hand for `{1}` and combos with landfall.

## Cards from Saviors of Kamigawa worth owning

- **Pithing Needle**: a flexible sideboard card that has stayed relevant for two decades.
- **Enduring Ideal**: the centerpiece of every enchantment-epic deck.
- **Kataki, War's Wage**: a two-mana answer to artifact strategies.
- **Twincast**: a clean copy effect for spell decks and Commander.
- **Sakashima the Impostor**: a strong Commander clone.
- **Oboro, Palace in the Clouds**: a landfall and Commander combo piece.
- **Footsteps of the Goryo**: cheap reanimation for graveyard decks.
