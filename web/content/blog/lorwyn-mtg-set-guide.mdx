Lorwyn is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2007 that introduced planeswalkers as a card type and built a whole set around creature tribes. Its world is a sunlit fairy-tale plane of kithkin, elves, faeries, giants and elementals with no humans at all, inspired by Celtic folklore. Lorwyn also printed Thoughtseize, Cryptic Command and the cards that made Faeries one of the most dominant decks of its era.

## The story

Lorwyn is a world where the sun never fully sets and midsummer never ends. Its peoples know little of real evil, but they're consumed by rivalry. The kithkin are small, close-knit villagers who share a telepathic bond called the thoughtweft. The elves of the Gilt-Leaf rank everything by beauty and hunt anything they consider ugly, which they call "eyeblights." Boggarts (Lorwyn's goblins) live for feasts and new sensations, merrows run the rivers, giants wander and sleep for years, the flamekin walk a path of personal enlightenment, and treefolk keep long memories. Above all of them, unseen, the faerie queen Oona and her fae play their own games.

The set's story is told in the novel *Lorwyn* by Cory J. Herndon and Scott McGough. It centers on **Rhys**, a Gilt-Leaf elf hunter. Ordered by his superior Nath to wipe out a peaceful boggart festival, Rhys calls on the poison magic of his old treefolk mentor, the yew Colfenor, and the blast destroys the forest, his own horns and nearly everyone present. Disfigured and branded a kin-slayer, he's cast out of elf society.

Rhys falls in with an unlikely band. **Ashling**, a flamekin pilgrim, is carrying a message from Colfenor. **Brigid Baeli**, a kithkin archer and folk hero, is her guide. **Maralen**, a mysterious elf who appears right after the massacre, and the troublemaking faeries of the Vendilion clique tag along. Colfenor asks Rhys to plant his seed in a sacred grove so the yew's line survives. The journey goes wrong in all the expected fairy-tale ways: betrayals, ambushes and a duel in which Rhys finally kills Nath.

Lorwyn mostly sets things up. Maralen turns out to be a faerie carrying a piece of Oona's soul, Ashling's link to a great elemental grows, and Colfenor's plans run deeper than Rhys knew. The story continues in Morningtide and then turns dark when the Great Aurora flips the plane into Shadowmoor, its gloomy twilight face. Decades later, the plane returned in [Lorwyn Eclipsed](/blog/lorwyn-eclipsed-mtg-set-guide).

## Mechanics

### Planeswalkers

Planeswalkers had been planned for Future Sight but debuted here as a new card type: permanents with loyalty counters and abilities you activate once per turn, which opponents can attack. Lorwyn printed five, one per color, nicknamed the **Lorwyn Five**: Ajani Goldmane, Jace Beleren, Liliana Vess, Chandra Nalaar and Garruk Wildspeaker, all rares. **Garruk Wildspeaker** (`{2}{G}{G}`) could untap two lands, make a 3/3 Beast or pump your team with an Overrun effect, and it became the most-played of the group.

### Tribes and tribal spells

The set revolves around eight creature types, each spread over two or more colors: kithkin, merfolk, faeries, elves, goblins, giants, elementals and treefolk. Tribal spells (renamed kindred in 2024) are instants, sorceries and enchantments that also carry a creature type, so they count for tribe cards. **Eyeblight's Ending** (common) is an Elf instant that destroys any non-Elf creature.

### Changeling

A changeling card is every creature type at all times. **Mirror Entity** (rare) is a 1/1 changeling for `{2}{W}` that turns your whole team into X/X creatures for `{X}`, and **Crib Swap** (uncommon) exiles a creature and leaves its owner a 1/1 changeling.

### Evoke

Evoke is an alternate, cheaper cost for a creature: if you pay it, the creature enters, its "enters" ability triggers, and then you sacrifice it. It appears on Elementals. **Mulldrifter** (common) is a 2/2 flier that draws two cards for `{4}{U}`, or you can evoke it for `{2}{U}` as a sorcery-speed Divination. **Shriekmaw** (uncommon) kills a nonblack, nonartifact creature for an evoke cost of `{1}{B}`. Evoke gives you a choice every game: tempo now, or a body later.

### Champion, clash and hideaway

- **Champion** creatures make you exile another creature of a given type when they enter; when the champion leaves, the exiled creature returns. **Changeling Titan** (uncommon) is a 7/7 for `{4}{G}` that champions any creature.
- **Clash** has you and an opponent reveal the top card of your libraries; if yours has the higher mana value, you get a bonus. **Lash Out** (common) deals 3 to a creature and, if you win the clash, 3 more to its controller.
- **Hideaway** appears on a cycle of rare lands. When one enters (tapped), you exile one of your top four cards face down, and you can later play it for free once a condition is met. **Windbrisk Heights** lets you cast the hidden card after you attack with three or more creatures.

## Limited

Lorwyn was drafted as three packs of Lorwyn until Morningtide joined it in early 2008. The draft format pushed you toward a tribe, and since every tribe spans two colors, tribes worked like archetypes. Kithkin were the white aggro deck, merfolk sat in blue and white, faeries in blue and black, elves in green and black, boggarts in black and red, giants in red and white, elementals across red and green (and everywhere else), and treefolk in green with black or white.

The trap was going too deep. Tribal payoffs like Wizened Cenn or Imperious Perfect were strong, but a deck of mediocre tribe members lost to one with good removal. Strong commons and uncommons included **Mulldrifter**, **Nameless Inversion** (a changeling instant that gives +3/-3), **Eyeblight's Ending**, **Lash Out**, **Shriekmaw** and **Oblivion Ring**, and changelings were valuable because they fit every tribe. Clash and champion gave the format a lot of small decisions, and evoke Elementals kept removal flexible. Our guide to [deck archetypes](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) covers how tribal aggro and midrange plans differ.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (2007-2009)

Lorwyn's tribes reshaped Standard for two years. The headliner was **Faeries**. Lorwyn supplied Spellstutter Sprite, Scion of Oona, Pestermite, Sower of Temptation and Cryptic Command, and Morningtide added Bitterblossom, Mistbind Clique and Vendilion Clique. The deck played on the opponent's turn, countered their key spell with a Sprite and beat down with flying 1/1s. Coverage from Pro Tour Hollywood in May 2008 reported Faeries as the most-played deck by a wide margin, and Faeries stayed near the top until the block rotated.

Other Lorwyn-fed decks included **Kithkin** (Wizened Cenn, Knight of Meadowgrain, Goldmeadow Harrier, Cloudgoat Ranger, Militia's Pride and Windbrisk Heights), **Doran** midrange around Doran, the Siege Tower, **Merfolk**, **Elves**, **Mannequin** decks using Makeshift Mannequin with evoke creatures, and **Reveillark** control (the namesake card is from Morningtide). Lorwyn left Standard when Zendikar arrived in October 2009.

### Modern

Lorwyn is one of Modern's most important early sets. **Thoughtseize** is a format-wide staple. **Cryptic Command** anchored blue control for years. **Merrow Reejerey** has been in Modern Merfolk since the format began, **Primal Command** shows up in Ponza and Amulet Titan, and **Pestermite** was a key half of the Splinter Twin combo. **Ponder** was banned in Modern in September 2011 and remains banned. For more on the format, see our [Modern guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

### Legacy, Vintage and Pauper

In Legacy, **Thoughtseize** and **Ponder** are everyday cards, and **Spellstutter Sprite** turns up in tempo decks. In Vintage, **Ponder** was restricted in June 2008 and freed again in 2024, while **Thorn of Amethyst** remains restricted. In Pauper, **Spellstutter Sprite** is the backbone of Faeries, and **Mulldrifter**, **Pestermite** and **Springleaf Drum** remain playable.

### Commander

Lorwyn's legends are casual favorites: **Doran, the Siege Tower** for toughness matters, **Wort, Boggart Auntie** and **Brion Stoutarm** for goblins and giants, **Horde of Notions** for five-color Elementals, and **Sygg, River Guide** for Merfolk. **Rings of Brighthearth** and **Thousand-Year Elixir** are combo pieces, and Mulldrifter and Shriekmaw are value staples.

## Cards from Lorwyn worth owning

- **Thoughtseize**: the benchmark discard spell in Modern and Legacy.
- **Cryptic Command**: still a premier blue instant for control decks.
- **Spellstutter Sprite**: a Faeries staple from Pauper to Legacy.
- **Primal Command**: a flexible ramp and disruption spell in Modern.
- **Mulldrifter**: a perennial value creature in Pauper and Commander.
- **Doran, the Siege Tower**: a unique effect and a popular Commander.
- **Windbrisk Heights**: hideaway land for go-wide decks.
