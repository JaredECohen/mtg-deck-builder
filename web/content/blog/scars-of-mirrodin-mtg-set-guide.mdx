Scars of Mirrodin is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2010 that sent players back to Mirrodin, the artificial metal plane built by the golem Karn. The headline is the return of the Phyrexians: their oil has been spreading through Mirrodin for years, and this set is where the war for the plane begins. It gave the game infect, metalcraft and proliferate, plus several cards that still define Modern and Commander.

## The story

Mirrodin is a world of metal: silver plains, copper forests and a blinking artificial sun, all built by Karn as a home for himself. The plane's first story (the original Mirrodin block) ended with the defeat of the mad overseer Memnarch, but something older had been sleeping inside it. Karn had carried a trace of Phyrexian oil with him when he made Mirrodin, and that oil slowly corrupted the plane from its core outward. By the time of Scars of Mirrodin, the Phyrexians have rebuilt themselves in the plane's interior and are ready to come to the surface.

Three planeswalkers arrive to deal with it. **Koth of the Hammer** is a native Mirran, a vulshok geomancer from the red mountains who wants to save his home. On Dominaria he recruits **Elspeth Tirel**, a white planeswalker whose spark ignited when she was a child prisoner of Phyrexians on another world, and **Venser**, an artificer who knew Karn and was the last person to speak with him before he vanished to Mirrodin. Together they cross to the plane, link up with the Mirran resistance and start searching for Karn, who they hope can reverse the infection.

What they find is grim. The Mirran peoples, the leonin of the plains, the vedalken artificers, the elves and the vulshok, are fractured and slow to see the danger. Phyrexian creatures are already roaming the surface, and some Mirrans are being "compleated" and converted to the enemy's side. Elspeth is shaken by facing the creatures that haunted her childhood. Geth, an old villain resurrected as a Phyrexian lieutenant, appears as **Geth, Lord of the Vault**.

Scars of Mirrodin is the first chapter of a three-set war. In Mirrodin Besieged the fighting goes into the open, and in New Phyrexia the invaders win: the plane is renamed New Phyrexia, and Karn is rescued only when Venser gives his own spark to him, dying in the process. Koth stays behind to lead a doomed resistance, and Elspeth flees. That victory sets up the Phyrexian story that eventually ends years later in [Phyrexia: All Will Be One](/blog/phyrexia-all-will-be-one-mtg-set-guide) and March of the Machine. The tie-in novel is The Quest for Karn by Robert B. Wintermute, published in 2011, and Wizards also ran webcomics covering the planeswalkers' journey.

## Mechanics

The set is split between the Mirrans, who care about artifacts, and the Phyrexians, who care about poison and counters.

### Infect

A creature with infect deals damage to creatures as -1/-1 counters and to players as poison counters. Ten poison counters and a player loses, no matter their life total. **Plague Stinger** is the typical example: a two-mana 1/1 flier that ignores your opponent's life entirely. Infect plays like a separate race. Your infect creatures can't chip in normal damage, so a deck has to commit to it fully, and pump spells become as good as burn because each point of power is a poison counter.

### Metalcraft

Metalcraft abilities turn on as long as you control three or more artifacts. **Galvanic Blast** deals 2 damage for one red mana, or 4 with metalcraft. **Mox Opal** only taps for mana when you have metalcraft, which is why it lives in artifact-heavy decks. The tension is that your artifacts become targets: one well-timed Shatter or sweeper can switch your whole deck off.

### Proliferate

To proliferate, choose any number of permanents and players that already have counters, and give each one more of a kind it has. It adds a poison counter to an opponent, a -1/-1 counter to a weakened creature, or a loyalty counter to your planeswalker. **Inexorable Tide** proliferates every time you cast a spell, and **Contagion Clasp** does it repeatedly for four mana.

### Imprint

Imprint returns from the original Mirrodin. An imprint card exiles another card and uses it. **Mimic Vat** catches creatures as they die and turns them into hasty tokens, and **Prototype Portal** copies an artifact from your hand over and over.

The set also introduced the five "fast lands," such as **Blackcleave Cliffs**: dual lands that enter untapped if you control two or fewer other lands. They're excellent in fast decks, and our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) covers how to parse these conditional texts.

## Limited

Scars of Mirrodin was drafted as SOM-SOM-SOM in its first season and is still remembered as a skill-testing format. It splits into two broad camps rather than ten neat color pairs.

**Infect** decks, usually black-green or black-based, fill up on cards like Plague Stinger, **Blight Mamba** and **Ichorclaw Myr**, then use pump spells and **Tainted Strike** to finish. The catch is that infect cards don't help each other's damage: a deck with half infect and half normal creatures races toward two different goals and gets neither. The good infect drafters committed early and took every poison card they saw.

**Metalcraft** decks, often white-red or white-blue, play lots of cheap artifacts and creatures like Glint Hawk and Myr, then win with equipment and cards that get stronger once three artifacts are out. Proliferate rounded out blue-black and helped both camps, since it adds poison and -1/-1 counters alike.

Removal mattered more than usual because the format had big artifact bombs that any color could play, and colorless cards like **Tumble Magnet** and the Myr made splashing easy. Black's removal, such as Grasp of Darkness and **Skinrender**, made it a popular color to start in. If you want a refresher on how to judge cards in a format like this, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Scars of Mirrodin joined a Standard that already had Zendikar, Worldwake and Magic 2011. Its biggest Standard story is the deck it fed rather than one it created. **Caw-Blade**, a white-blue deck built around Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Stoneforge Mystic and Squadron Hawk, took over the format through early 2011, and Scars cards such as Venser, the Sojourner appeared in some builds alongside Mirrodin Besieged's Sword of Feast and Famine. Wizards banned Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic (both from Worldwake) in Standard on June 20, 2011, effective July 1. No Scars card was banned in Standard.

Scars also created decks of its own. Tempered Steel aggro, powered by Glint Hawk, Memnite, Mox Opal and Precursor Golem, was a real archetype after Mirrodin Besieged added more cheap artifacts. Mono-red players used Koth of the Hammer alongside Arc Trail. Infect saw some play but was more of a Limited phenomenon in Standard.

The longer life is in other formats:

- **Modern**: Mox Opal has a complicated history. It was banned in Modern in January 2020 for powering artifact decks and then unbanned in December 2024, and Scryfall lists it as Modern-legal as of October 2026. Galvanic Blast, Memnite and Etched Champion have been Affinity staples for years, Wurmcoil Engine is a classic Tron finisher, and Blackcleave Cliffs and the other fast lands are common in aggressive mana bases.
- **Legacy and Vintage**: Wurmcoil Engine appears in artifact "Stompy" and Workshop decks, and Mox Opal in artifact combo.
- **Commander**: Wurmcoil Engine, Myr Battlesphere, Exsanguinate, Mimic Vat, Contagion Clasp and the Myr mana creatures are long-time favorites. Infect and proliferate also gave birth to poison Commander decks, which are attractive because ten poison counters kill a player regardless of the format's 40 starting life.
- **Pauper**: Galvanic Blast is still Pauper-legal and played in artifact decks, while Kuldotha Rebirth is banned in Pauper.

For how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Scars of Mirrodin worth owning

- **Wurmcoil Engine**: a colorless threat that fits almost any Commander deck and many eternal decks.
- **Mox Opal**: a Modern and Legacy artifact staple.
- **The fast lands** (Blackcleave Cliffs, Copperline Gorge, Darkslick Shores, Razorverge Thicket, Seachrome Coast): reliable duals for aggressive decks and Commander.
- **Galvanic Blast**: Modern and Pauper Affinity's burn spell.
- **Mimic Vat**: a powerful Commander value engine.
- **Exsanguinate**: a classic Commander finisher for mono-black.
- **Venser, the Sojourner**: a blink planeswalker that still shows up in casual and Commander decks.

## The takeaway

Scars of Mirrodin is where the modern Phyrexian story starts, and it's one of the most influential sets of its era. If you're building today, start with its artifacts: Wurmcoil Engine and Mox Opal still matter, and metalcraft gives you a clear [archetype](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) to build around.
