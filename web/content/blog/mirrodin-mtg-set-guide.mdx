Mirrodin is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2003 that opened the Mirrodin block on an artificial plane made of metal. It introduced Equipment, affinity, imprint and artifact lands, and it was the first expansion printed in the modern card frame that debuted in Eighth Edition. It also fed Affinity, one of the most dominant Standard decks ever, and it's the first large expansion legal in Modern.

## The story

Mirrodin is a plane that shouldn't exist. It was built by the planeswalker Karn, the silver golem from the Weatherlight saga, who originally called it Argentum. Karn left a guardian behind, the golem Memnarch, to watch over it. By the time this set takes place, Memnarch had been warped by the power of the Mirari (the artifact at the heart of the Odyssey block, which ended up on Mirrodin) and by something darker that Karn had carried with him. Memnarch named the plane after the Mirari and began populating it with creatures snatched from other worlds, their memories wiped.

Mirrodin's landscape is metal grown wild: the Tangle, a forest of copper trees; the Glimmervoid, a plain of razor-sharp grass; the Mephidross, a necrotic swamp; the Oxidda Chain of mountains; and the Quicksilver Sea. Four moons circle the sky, matching white, blue, black and red. Green has no moon, which becomes a plot point later in the block.

The story, told in Will McDermott's novel *The Moons of Mirrodin*, follows Glissa Sunseeker, an elf hunter from the Tangle. She suffers strange visions of a forest made of something other than metal, and one night the monstrous levelers (like the Leveler card) attack her home and kill her parents. Glissa sets out to find out who sent them, picking up companions along the way: Slobad, a goblin tinkerer; Bosh, a broken-down iron golem Slobad repairs; and allies among the leonin of Taj-Nar and the human wizards of the Quicksilver Sea. Her path runs through Geth, the necromancer ruling the Mephidross, and then to the vedalken city of Lumengrid, where she kills Pontifex, the vedalken leader she thinks is behind the attacks. Only then is the real culprit revealed: Memnarch, who has been hunting her because she carries a latent planeswalker spark he wants for himself.

The rest of the block, across Darksteel and Fifth Dawn and their novels, continues the hunt. Glissa takes up the legendary Kaldra artifacts only to have Memnarch turn them against her, a fifth sun, a green one, erupts from the plane's core, and Glissa finally brings Memnarch down. Karn then leaves Glissa, Slobad and Geth as the plane's guardians.

## Mechanics

### Equipment

Equipment is the set's most important legacy. An Equipment is an artifact you attach to a creature you control by paying its equip cost, at sorcery speed. Unlike an Aura, it stays on the battlefield when the creature dies, so you can move it to the next creature. Bonesplitter (+2/+0, equip 1) is the classic example: cheap, simple and brutal in any deck. Lightning Greaves gives haste and shroud for an equip cost of zero, and Sword of Kaldra is the set's huge legendary Equipment. Equipment has been a permanent part of Magic ever since.

### Affinity for artifacts

A spell with affinity for artifacts costs `{1}` less for each artifact you control. Frogmite is a 2/2 that costs four, so with four artifacts out it's free. Myr Enforcer, Somber Hoverguard and Thoughtcast work the same way. It sounds modest, but combined with the set's cheap artifacts and artifact lands, affinity let players dump their hand in the first few turns.

### Artifact lands

Ancient Den, Seat of the Synod, Vault of Whispers, Great Furnace and Tree of Tales are lands that are also artifacts. They count for affinity and for cards like Disciple of the Vault, which drains an opponent whenever an artifact goes to the graveyard. They're also fragile, since anything that destroys artifacts destroys them.

### Imprint and entwine

Imprint cards exile another card and take on some of its traits. Chrome Mox exiles a nonartifact, nonland card from your hand and then taps for one of that card's colors. Isochron Scepter imprints a cheap instant and lets you cast a copy every turn, and Duplicant exiles a creature and takes on its size.

Entwine appears on modal spells with two options. Pay the entwine cost on top of the normal cost and you get both modes. Tooth and Nail is the best known: tutor two creatures, put two creatures onto the battlefield, or entwine to do both.

## Limited

Mirrodin was drafted as three packs of Mirrodin until Darksteel arrived. Artifacts changed how drafting worked. Because so many commons are colorless, almost any pick could end up in your deck, so you could stay flexible longer than in most sets. Bonesplitter, the Spellbombs and colorless creatures like Myr and Golems were playable in every color.

The recognizable plans were:

- **Affinity aggro (blue, often with red or black).** Cheap artifacts let Frogmite, Myr Enforcer and Somber Hoverguard come down early, and Thoughtcast refilled your hand.
- **Equipment beatdown (white and red especially).** Small creatures with Bonesplitter or Vulshok Gauntlets on them pressured opponents, and white's Skyhunter Patrol and Auriok creatures used Equipment well.
- **Artifact removal and big green.** Green had artifact hate like Deconstruct and Viridian Shaman, which was often maindeckable because so much of the format was made of artifacts.

Removal at common included Arrest, Electrostatic Bolt (extra strong against artifact creatures), Irradiate (which scales with your artifact count), Wail of the Nim and Pyrite Spellbomb. Equipment made even small creatures into real threats, so a low curve and a few good Equipment went a long way.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (October 2003 to October 2005)

Mirrodin's Standard run is famous for one deck: **Affinity**, an aggressive artifact deck (see our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) for how aggro, combo and control decks differ). Artifact lands, Frogmite, Myr Enforcer, Thoughtcast, Atog, Disciple of the Vault and Shrapnel Blast were all Mirrodin cards. Darksteel added Arcbound Ravager, Darksteel Citadel and Skullclamp, and Fifth Dawn added Cranial Plating. Together they made a deck that could deploy most of its hand in two or three turns and win out of nowhere.

Wizards responded twice. Skullclamp, from Darksteel, was banned in Standard effective June 20, 2004. When Affinity still dominated, Wizards banned Arcbound Ravager, Disciple of the Vault, Darksteel Citadel and all five Mirrodin artifact lands in Standard, effective March 20, 2005. It was one of the biggest single Standard bans ever, and it ended the Affinity era.

Other Mirrodin-fueled decks included Tooth and Nail ramp (sometimes called "Elf and Nail" when built with Onslaught's elves), March of the Machines and Big Red decks. The set rotated out when Ravnica: City of Guilds arrived in October 2005. Our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) covers how bans like these get decided.

### Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper and Commander (as of October 2026)

Because Modern starts at Eighth Edition, Mirrodin is the first large expansion in the format, and its power shows up in the ban list:

- **Modern bans:** all five artifact lands, Chrome Mox, Cloudpost, Seething Song and Second Sunrise are banned in Modern. Frogmite, Myr Enforcer and Thoughtcast were long-time members of Modern Affinity, and Oblivion Stone is a classic Tron card.
- **Legacy and Vintage:** Chalice of the Void is a key card in Legacy prison and Eldrazi decks and is restricted in Vintage. Chrome Mox and Goblin Charbelcher show up in Legacy fast-combo decks.
- **Pauper:** Atog, Disciple of the Vault and Cloudpost are banned. Affinity commons like Frogmite, Myr Enforcer and Thoughtcast have been central to Pauper's artifact decks.
- **Commander:** Lightning Greaves, Solemn Simulacrum, Gilded Lotus, Mindslaver, Duplicant, Sculpting Steel and the Talismans are common sights.

Mirrodin has no Reserved List cards. For more on the formats where these cards live today, see our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Mirrodin worth owning

- **Chalice of the Void**: a Legacy and Vintage staple that shuts off whole decks.
- **Chrome Mox**: free acceleration for Legacy and Vintage.
- **Lightning Greaves**: one of the most-played Equipment in Commander.
- **Solemn Simulacrum**: a colorless value creature for Commander.
- **Isochron Scepter**: the engine of several combo decks and a Commander favorite.
- **Oblivion Stone**: a colorless board wipe for Tron and Commander.
- **Gilded Lotus** and the **Talismans**: mana rocks that fit almost any Commander deck.

Mirrodin is one of the most influential sets ever printed. It gave Magic Equipment, it showed how dangerous cheap artifact synergies can be, and its cards still shape Legacy, Modern and Commander.
