Magic: The Gathering players speak in shorthand. "ETB" means a card "enters the battlefield," "bolt" means to deal 3 damage to something, "flood" means drawing too many lands, and "goldfishing" means testing a deck with no opponent. Most of these words come from famous cards or from years of players needing quick ways to describe common situations.

This guide explains the slang you're most likely to hear at a kitchen table, a game store or in a deck tech video, grouped by topic. If you're still learning the basic turn, start with [how to play Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) and come back when the words start flying.

## Card and rules shorthand

These terms describe what cards do. Some are official game terms, and some are just abbreviations players use because the full wording is long.

### ETB (enters the battlefield)

**ETB** stands for "enters the battlefield." An ETB effect is a triggered ability that happens when a permanent comes into play, like a creature that draws you a card when it arrives. Players say things like "it has a good ETB" or "that's an ETB trigger."

Older cards spell out "enters the battlefield" in full. More recent cards shorten the same wording to "enters," but the meaning hasn't changed, and players still say ETB.

### LTB and dies

**LTB** means "leaves the battlefield," the mirror image of ETB. **Dies** is an official term: a creature dies when it's put into a graveyard from the battlefield. A creature that's exiled didn't die, which matters for cards that trigger on death.

### CMC vs mana value

**CMC** stands for "converted mana cost," the old official name for the total amount of mana in a card's cost, ignoring color. A card that costs `{2}{U}{U}` has a CMC of 4. In 2021 Wizards renamed the term **mana value**. The math is the same; only the name changed. Players who started before the rename still say CMC, so you'll hear both.

A few edge cases worth knowing:

- Lands have a mana value of 0, because they have no mana cost.
- An `{X}` in a cost counts as 0 everywhere except on the stack, where it counts as whatever number was chosen.

### Mill

To **mill** is to put cards from the top of a library into its owner's graveyard. The name comes from an old artifact called Millstone, which did exactly that to a target player. For years "mill" was only slang, but it's now an official keyword action printed on cards: "mill three cards." A deck that tries to win by emptying the opponent's library is called a mill deck.

### Bolt

**Bolt** comes from Lightning Bolt, a one-mana red instant that deals 3 damage to any target. As a verb, "bolt it" means deal 3 damage to it, whether you're using Lightning Bolt or something similar. You'll also hear it as a measuring stick: a creature that "dies to bolt" has 3 or less toughness, and a card that's "worse than bolt" does less for more mana.

### Wrath and sweeper

**Wrath** comes from Wrath of God, a white sorcery that destroys all creatures. Any spell that clears the board of creatures gets called a wrath, and "to wrath" means to cast one. **Sweeper** and **board wipe** are broader terms for any effect that removes many things at once, including smaller ones that only deal a bit of damage to every creature.

### Cantrip

A **cantrip** is a cheap spell that replaces itself by drawing a card, usually alongside a small effect. Opt, a one-mana blue instant that lets you scry 1 and then draw a card, is a classic example. Cantrips smooth your draws without costing you a card, which is why blue decks love them.

### Tutor

A **tutor** is any card that searches your library for another card. The name comes from Demonic Tutor, a black sorcery that finds any card and puts it into your hand. Tutors that find only certain cards, like a land or a creature, still get the name.

### Blink and flicker

To **blink** or **flicker** a permanent is to exile it and then return it to the battlefield, usually right away. The names come from older cards, Flicker and Momentary Blink. Blinking a creature with a strong ETB makes that ability happen again. The returned permanent is a new object, so any counters, damage or Auras that were on it are gone.

## Game-state slang

These words describe how a game is going, especially when it's going badly.

### Flood and screw

**Mana flood** means drawing too many lands and not enough spells. **Mana screw** is the opposite: you're stuck on too few lands to cast what's in your hand. "I got flooded" and "I got screwed" are the most common complaints in Magic. The guide to [mana flood and screw](/blog/mana-flood-and-screw-explained) covers how deck building reduces both.

### Mulligan

A **mulligan** is choosing to shuffle your opening hand back and draw a new one, usually because it has too few or too many lands. Under the current rule, you draw seven new cards and then put cards from your hand on the bottom of your library, one for each mulligan you've taken. "Mull to six" means taking one mulligan. The [mulligan guide](/blog/mtg-mulligan-guide) explains when to keep and when to ship a hand back.

### Topdeck

A **topdeck** is the card you draw from the top of your library, especially when you're relying on it. "Living off the top" or being "in topdeck mode" means your hand is empty and you're playing whatever you draw each turn. A "lucky topdeck" is drawing exactly the card you needed.

### Curve out

Your **curve** is the spread of mana values in your deck. To **curve out** is to use all your mana every turn in order: a one-drop on turn one, a two-drop on turn two, a three-drop on turn three, and so on. Aggressive decks win by curving out.

### Ramp

**Ramp** means getting more mana than the normal one land per turn. Llanowar Elves, a one-mana green creature that taps for `{G}`, is a classic ramp card, and so is Rampant Growth, which puts an extra basic land onto the battlefield. "Ramping into" a big spell means using ramp to cast it early.

### Chump block

A **chump block** is blocking with a creature that will die without killing the attacker, just to keep the damage off you. Throwing a 1/1 in front of a 5/5 is a chump block. It's a trade of a card for time, and it's often right when you're low on life.

### Value

**Value** means getting more out of your cards than your opponent gets from theirs. A creature that draws a card when it enters and then trades with an enemy creature "got value." Players also use it as a joke, shouting "value!" when a play is greedy or cute. The idea behind it is card advantage.

### Tempo

**Tempo** is about time and mana efficiency rather than card count. If you spend one mana to bounce a creature your opponent spent four mana casting, you've gained tempo, even though nobody gained a card. Tempo decks try to stay a step ahead of the opponent on the board. The [tempo guide](/blog/tempo-explained-mtg) goes deeper.

## Deck and archetype slang

These words describe what kind of deck someone is playing or how they built it.

### Aggro, control, midrange and combo

- **Aggro** decks attack early and try to win before the opponent sets up.
- **Control** decks answer threats with removal, counterspells and sweepers, then win late.
- **Midrange** decks sit between the two, with efficient creatures and removal.
- **Combo** decks assemble a specific set of cards that win together.

Plenty of decks blend two of these, and you'll hear labels like "aggro-control" or "combo-control" for hybrids.

### Splash

To **splash** a color is to add a small amount of it to a deck that's mostly other colors, usually for a few powerful cards. A green-white deck with three black cards and a handful of black mana sources is "splashing black." Splashing adds power at the cost of a less reliable mana base.

### Goldfish

To **goldfish** is to play out hands of your deck with no opponent, or with an opponent who does nothing, like a pet goldfish. Goldfishing tells you how fast and consistent a deck is: "it goldfishes a turn-four kill" means that with no interference, it wins on turn four. It's one of the quickest ways to test a new deck before you play it against people.

### Staple, bomb and jank

- A **staple** is a card that shows up in many decks because it's efficient and flexible.
- A **bomb** is a card so powerful it can win a game on its own, a term used most in Limited.
- **Jank** describes a deck or card that's unusual, fragile or built for fun more than power. Most players use it affectionately.

### Netdeck and brew

A **netdeck** is a list copied from the internet, often from a tournament result. A **brew** is a deck you built yourself. Neither is better: netdecks are tested, while brewing teaches you more about why cards work.

## Social and table slang

A few more terms come up mostly in conversation.

- **Kitchen table:** casual Magic with friends, without tournament rules.
- **Rule zero:** a pregame talk in Commander where players agree on house rules and power level.
- **Pubstomp:** bringing a far stronger deck than everyone else at a casual table.
- **Scoop:** to concede, from scooping up your cards.
- **GG:** "good game," said at the end of a match.

## How to pick up the rest

New slang keeps appearing as new cards become famous, so no list is ever complete. When you hear a term you don't know, it's usually one of three things: a famous card's name used as a verb, an abbreviation of rules text, or a shorthand for a situation. Asking "what does that mean?" is normal at any table, and most players are happy to explain.

## The takeaway

Most Magic slang falls into three groups: shorthand for rules text (ETB, mana value, mill), famous cards turned into verbs (bolt, wrath, tutor, blink), and words for how a game is going (flood, screw, topdeck, tempo). Learn the dozen terms above and you'll follow almost any table conversation or deck tech. The fastest way to make them stick is to build a deck and play it.
