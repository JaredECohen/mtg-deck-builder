Innistrad Remastered is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released on January 24, 2025, collecting the best of Magic's gothic horror plane into one draftable package. It pulls from the original Innistrad block, the Shadows over Innistrad block, Innistrad: Midnight Hunt and Innistrad: Crimson Vow, and reprinted cards like Liliana of the Veil, Snapcaster Mage, Griselbrand, Wrenn and Seven and The Meathook Massacre.

## What the set was for

Innistrad Remastered is part of the Masters series, the line of reprint sets designed to bring sought-after cards back into circulation and to be drafted on their own. It was the fourth "Remastered" set in paper and the first to feature a Headliner: **Edgar Markov**, the Vampire commander from Commander 2017, which appears in several special treatments including a version serial-numbered to 500 in Collector Boosters.

The set had an unusual history. Wizards started work on it around 2020, planning to cover only the first two Innistrad blocks, then shelved it. By the time it came back with JC Tao as set lead, Midnight Hunt and Crimson Vow had both come out, so they joined the mix. The final file also includes Innistrad cards from Commander products and a handful of reprints from other sets, given new Innistrad art and flavor.

The setting is the same one players have visited four times: a plane of frightened Human villages beset by Vampires, Werewolves, Zombies and Spirits, watched over (sometimes badly) by the angel Avacyn and the Church of Avacyn. The cards cover everything from Avacyn's return and her later madness to Emrakul's arrival and the Werewolf-tilted day and night cycle of Midnight Hunt. For the original visits, start with the [Innistrad set guide](/blog/innistrad-mtg-set-guide) and the [Shadows over Innistrad set guide](/blog/shadows-over-innistrad-mtg-set-guide).

## Mechanics

Innistrad Remastered doesn't add mechanics. It gathers the best of four sets:

- **Transforming double-faced cards:** a card with two faces that flips when a condition is met. **Delver of Secrets** becomes a 3/2 flier when you reveal an instant or sorcery in your upkeep; **Huntmaster of the Fells** flips back and forth based on how many spells were cast.
- **Flashback:** cast the card a second time from your graveyard. **Lingering Souls** makes two 1/1 fliers, then two more for `{1}{B}`.
- **Madness:** if you discard the card, you can cast it for its madness cost. **Fiery Temper** deals 3 damage for `{R}` when discarded.
- **Disturb:** cast the card from your graveyard as its back face, a Spirit. **Lunarch Veteran** gains you life as a one-drop, then comes back as a flier.
- **Meld:** two specific cards combine into one huge creature. **Gisela, the Broken Blade** and **Bruna, the Fading Light** meld into **Brisela, Voice of Nightmares**, a 9/10 that stops opponents casting cheap spells.
- **Emerge:** sacrifice a creature to cast an Eldrazi for less. **Elder Deep-Fiend** has flash and taps up to four permanents when cast.
- **Escalate:** pay an extra cost for each additional mode. **Collective Brutality** discards cards to stack removal, discard and drain.
- **Investigate, Blood tokens, undying, miracle and soulbond** round things out, on cards like **Tireless Tracker**, **Bloodtithe Harvester**, **Young Wolf**, **Temporal Mastery** and **Deadeye Navigator**.

## Limited

Innistrad Remastered was built to be drafted, and its ten two-color archetypes map neatly onto the plane's monsters:

- **White-blue:** Spirits and fliers
- **Blue-black:** Zombies
- **Black-red:** Vampires and madness
- **Red-green:** Werewolves
- **Green-white:** Humans and go-wide tokens
- **White-black:** attrition and aristocrats (sacrifice payoffs; see [deck archetypes explained](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained))
- **Blue-red:** instants and sorceries
- **Black-green:** morbid (rewards for a creature dying this turn)
- **Red-white:** aggressive go-wide tokens
- **Green-blue:** self-mill

Like most Masters-style sets, the power level is high and the commons are deep. Flashback and disturb mean nearly every card offers two uses, so games tend to grind, and graveyard value is everywhere: **Moldgraf Millipede** fuels the green-blue and black-green decks, while **Tragic Slip**, **Fiery Temper** and **Imprisoned in the Moon** are cheap answers that any deck in those colors wants. **Lingering Souls** at uncommon is a draft-defining card for white-black. If you're coming to the format fresh, expect removal and card advantage to be stronger than in a typical new set.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Because Innistrad Remastered is reprints only, it didn't add anything new to any Constructed format. Its impact was on supply: cards that Modern, Legacy and Commander players already used became easier to find, many in new retro-frame and borderless treatments.

A few legality notes worth knowing, as of October 2026:

- **Griselbrand** is banned in Commander, so it's a Legacy and Vintage card in practice.
- **Hermit Druid** is banned in Legacy but remains a popular Commander combo piece.
- **Sorin, Imperious Bloodlord** was banned in Pioneer on August 26, 2024, after Rakdos Vampires became the format's dominant deck. Its INR reprint doesn't change that.

The Modern-relevant reprints are long-established cards rather than new threats. Liliana of the Veil and Snapcaster Mage have been Modern staples for over a decade, Thalia, Heretic Cathar and Restoration Angel have long homes in creature decks, and Collective Brutality is a familiar sideboard card. For how these fit together in the format, see the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

## Cards from Innistrad Remastered worth owning

- **Liliana of the Veil:** a three-mana planeswalker that still sees play in eternal formats.
- **Snapcaster Mage:** the classic blue value creature for Modern, Legacy and cube.
- **Wrenn and Seven:** one of the strongest green planeswalkers in Commander.
- **The Meathook Massacre:** a sweeper that keeps paying off for the rest of the game.
- **Thalia, Heretic Cathar:** a strong tempo creature for white aggro decks.
- **Collective Brutality:** a flexible sideboard card against aggro, burn and spell-heavy decks.
- **The slow dual lands:** easy, reliable two-color mana for Commander and casual decks.
- **Blood Artist and Village Rites:** cheap staples for any aristocrats deck.

Innistrad Remastered is the easiest way to pick up a decade of Innistrad's best cards at once, and it's a fun draft on its own. If you want to use the reprints, start with a Modern deck built around one of the format's long-standing pieces and see how it holds up in the [MTG Deck Lab builder](/).
