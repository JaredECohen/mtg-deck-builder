Masters 25 is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in March 2018 to celebrate the game's 25th anniversary. Instead of new cards, it collected 249 older cards from almost every era of Magic, from Alpha-era classics to recent staples, and packaged them for booster draft. Its headline reprints included Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Vendilion Clique, Chalice of the Void, Imperial Recruiter and Thalia, Guardian of Thraben.

| Fact | Details |

| Release date | March 16, 2018 |
| Setting | None: a reprint set drawing on 25 years of Magic sets |

| Headline mechanics | None new; older keywords such as morph and cycling return on reprinted cards |

## What the set was for

The Masters line exists to put older, hard-to-find cards back into circulation and to give experienced players a draft format full of familiar favorites. Modern Masters started it in 2013, and by 2018 the series had produced Modern Masters 2015, Eternal Masters, Modern Masters 2017 and Iconic Masters. Masters 25 was the anniversary edition, so its theme was Magic's history itself.

Wizards described the set as drawing from nearly every expansion across the game's first 25 years. That gave it a different feel from earlier Masters sets. Modern Masters sets were built around loud, linear themes like affinity or the Eldrazi, while Masters 25 emphasized classic archetypes that any long-time player would recognize: white weenie, red aggro, green ramp, blue control, tokens and tempo. The card file reads like a greatest-hits album, with Akroma, Angel of Wrath, Ball Lightning, Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, Swords to Plowshares, Brainstorm and Dark Ritual all making appearances.

There's no plot. The flavor is nostalgia, and the set is best understood as a tour of the game's past rather than a story.

Several reprints carried their old reputations with them. Flash and Protean Hulk both appear, a pairing infamous for a Legacy combo; as of October 2026, Flash is banned in Legacy and Commander. Sundering Titan is banned in Commander, and Simian Spirit Guide is banned in Modern, so check the ban list before building around them. ### What the reprints did

Masters sets are mainly supply events. Masters 25 put fresh copies of Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Vendilion Clique, Chalice of the Void and Ensnaring Bridge into the market, which helped Modern and Legacy players get into decks like Lantern Control, Eldrazi with Chalice of the Void, and blue Jace control. For Commander players, Animar, Soul of Elements, Imperial Recruiter, Courser of Kruphix, Phyrexian Obliterator, Akroma's Vengeance and Pernicious Deed were welcome reprints of cards that already appeared in a lot of decks.

Not every slot was a hit. Some of its mythic rares, such as Tree of Redemption, Armageddon and Akroma, Angel of Fury, had little Constructed demand, and some players felt that made the average pack less exciting than earlier Masters releases.

## Limited

Masters 25 was built to draft well, and it did. The format favored strategy shapes over specific mechanics. Wizards' own Limited preview made three points: the set was less linear than earlier Masters sets, it leaned on classic archetypes, and it hid a lot of small synergies at common and uncommon.

The ten gold uncommons hinted at the speed of each color pair rather than a mechanic:

- **Aggressive pairs:** Watchwolf (green-white), Boros Charm (red-white) and Blightning (black-red) pushed fast, low-curve decks.
- **Slower pairs:** Cloudblazer (white-blue) and Lorescale Coatl (green-blue) rewarded longer games and card advantage.
- **Value and control pairs:** Shadowmage Infiltrator (blue-black), Pillory of the Sleepless (white-black) and Baloth Null (black-green) leaned on removal and recursion.
- **Midrange and tempo:** Stangg (red-green) and Quicksilver Dagger (blue-red) supported creature-based or card-drawing decks.

Red aggro was one of the clearest archetypes, with commons and uncommons like Frenzied Goblin, Jackal Pup, Mogg Flunkies and Hordeling Outburst. It was strong but fragile, since its cards ran out of steam if the game went long. Green ramp used Arbor Elf, and white weenie had Savannah Lions. Blue offered tempo and control tools, including Counterspell, Arcane Denial and Brainstorm at common.

Removal was good and plentiful. Swords to Plowshares, Lightning Bolt, Murder and Ravenous Chupacabra were all in the commons and uncommons, which kept bombs in check and made Masters 25 a skill-testing format. Small two-card synergies, such as Whitemane Lion bouncing a creature with an enters-the-battlefield effect, were a reward for drafters who paid attention. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Because it contained only reprints, Masters 25 didn't create new decks. Its effect on Constructed was about access: more copies of key cards made existing decks easier to build.

In **Modern**, Vendilion Clique, Chalice of the Void, Ensnaring Bridge, Thalia, Guardian of Thraben, Blood Moon and Eidolon of the Great Revel all supported established decks of the time, including Burn, Death and Taxes, Lantern Control and prison-style Blood Moon decks. Jace, the Mind Sculptor, which had been banned in Modern until it was unbanned in February 2018, arrived in Masters 25 a month later, at exactly the moment Modern players wanted copies. Jace remains legal in Modern as of October 2026.

In **Legacy** and **Vintage**, Jace, Doomsday, Imperial Recruiter, Chalice of the Void and Swords to Plowshares were the important reprints. Chalice of the Void is restricted in Vintage as of October 2026.

In **Commander**, the set is a solid source of staples, including Swords to Plowshares, Counterspell, Lightning Bolt, Rancor, Cultivate, Swiftfoot Boots, Ash Barrens, Myriad Landscape, Zulaport Cutthroat and Vindicate. Notion Thief is on the Game Changers list as of October 2026.

## Cards from Masters 25 worth owning

- **Jace, the Mind Sculptor:** a famous planeswalker that is legal in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Vendilion Clique:** a flexible flash threat for blue decks in older formats.
- **Thalia, Guardian of Thraben:** a cheap hate bear for Death and Taxes and many Commander decks.
- **Chalice of the Void:** a prison staple that fits colorless and artifact decks.
- **Imperial Recruiter:** a creature tutor that makes any toolbox deck better.
- **Swords to Plowshares:** the cleanest one-mana removal spell for Legacy and Commander.
- **Courser of Kruphix:** card advantage and life gain for green decks.
- **Living Death:** a mass reanimation spell for graveyard Commander decks.

Masters 25 is best remembered as a fun, high-quality draft format and a source of iconic reprints, even if its rare slot disappointed some players. If you own its cards, the most direct way to use them in a single deck is a Modern prison or Death and Taxes list.
