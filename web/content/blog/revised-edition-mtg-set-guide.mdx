Revised Edition is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in April 1994, the third printing of the original game after Alpha/Beta and Unlimited. It was the first core set to change the card list: it dropped the Power Nine and other problem cards and added cards from Arabian Nights and Antiquities. Printed in huge numbers with white borders, it's the set that introduced a generation of players to the game, and its dual lands are still among the most important cards in Legacy and Vintage.

## What the set was for

In early 1994 Magic was selling faster than it could be printed. The original black-bordered Alpha and Beta printings had been followed by white-bordered Unlimited, and Revised was the next print run of the basic game, made to meet demand. Like every core set, it has no story of its own. Its flavor comes from the Dominaria of the earliest cards: Serra Angel, Shivan Dragon, Lord of the Pit and the rest of the original cast.

What made Revised different was that it was the first time Wizards changed the contents of the base set.

### What was cut

Revised removed 35 cards from Unlimited. The most famous are the **Power Nine**: Black Lotus, the five Moxen, Ancestral Recall, Time Walk and Timetwister. Other removals included Chaos Orb, Lich, Time Vault, Berserk, Psionic Blast, Sinkhole, Word of Command, Raging River and Icy Manipulator (which came back in Fourth Edition). Some were cut for raw power, others for being confusing or for physical-dexterity rules (Chaos Orb asked you to flip it onto the table).

### What came in

In their place came 39 cards from the first two expansions, Arabian Nights and Antiquities. These included **Serendib Efreet**, **Kird Ape**, **Ornithopter**, **Atog**, **Millstone**, **Hurkyl's Recall**, **The Rack**, **Desert Twister**, **Ivory Tower**, **Primal Clay** and **Mishra's War Machine**. Arabian Nights' Mountain was the only one of its cards that had been printed before, and Revised is the reason many players first saw cards from those small, hard-to-find sets.

### The look

Revised kept Unlimited's white borders but lost the faux-beveled edge around the art, and the art itself printed lighter and more washed out than earlier printings. It also had a famous error: **Serendib Efreet** was printed with the wrong art, showing the Arabian Nights card Ifh-Bíff Efreet instead. About half the original cards also received wording or formatting updates.

### Summer Magic (Edgar)

Wizards prepared a corrected print run in mid-1994, code-named "Edgar" and later nicknamed **Summer Magic**. It tried to fix Revised's washed-out colors and errors and to tone down some imagery, but it introduced new problems, including a Hurricane printed with a blue border and some cards printed too dark or with wrong artist credits. The run was recalled and almost all of it destroyed; only a small number of packs reached the public. Scryfall lists Summer Magic as its own set (code SUM) with the same 306-card list as Revised. It's a collector's curiosity rather than a playable set, and its cards are functionally identical to Revised's.

## Limited

Revised predates booster drafting as we know it. In 1994, Magic was played mostly as Constructed or casually out of starter decks and boosters, and there's no organized Revised draft format or set of archetypes to describe. Sealed and "pack wars" were common casual ways to play.

Opened today, a Revised Sealed or draft pool is a strange experience: creatures are small and inefficient by modern standards, removal like **Terror**, **Fireball**, **Lightning Bolt** and **Swords to Plowshares** is precious, and big rares such as **Shivan Dragon**, **Serra Angel** and **Lord of the Pit** dominate. If you want a modern view of Limited, read [draft vs sealed](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Revised's era was the very beginning of organized Constructed play. Decks in 1994 were defined by the cards in the base set and the first expansions: dual lands, Lightning Bolt, Swords to Plowshares, Serra Angel, Juggernaut and Sengir Vampire were staples, and powerful artifacts and restricted cards from older printings were still common. Players began separating "anything goes" Constructed (later Type I, today's Vintage) from a format limited to recent sets (Type II, which became Standard), and Revised was the core set in the background of that split until Fourth Edition replaced it in 1995. Detailed tournament metagame records from 1994 are sparse, so we won't name winning decks we can't verify.

Today Revised's cards live on mostly in eternal formats:

- **Legacy**: the dual lands are the backbone of nearly every multicolor Legacy deck. Swords to Plowshares, Counterspell, Dark Ritual, Animate Dead and Lightning Bolt are all played. Sol Ring, Demonic Tutor, Mana Vault, Wheel of Fortune, Mind Twist, Balance, Channel and Fastbond are banned in Legacy. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains why.
- **Vintage**: the dual lands are everywhere. Sol Ring, Demonic Tutor, Mana Vault, Wheel of Fortune, Balance and Channel are restricted, and the ante cards (Contract from Below, Darkpact, Demonic Attorney) are banned. Crusade is banned in all formats as part of Wizards' 2020 removal of cards with racist or culturally offensive depictions.
- **Commander**: Sol Ring is in almost every deck, and Demonic Tutor, Swords to Plowshares, the dual lands, Wheel of Fortune, Regrowth and Mana Vault are heavily played. Balance, Channel and Fastbond are banned in Commander. See [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).
- **Pauper**: Revised commons such as Lightning Bolt, Dark Ritual and Pestilence are Pauper-legal; Atog is banned there.
- **Modern**: Revised itself isn't Modern-legal, but cards like Lightning Bolt, Counterspell, Birds of Paradise and Wrath of God were reprinted in Modern-legal sets.

### The Reserved List

Revised contains 27 card names on the Reserved List, Wizards' policy that certain early cards won't be reprinted in tournament-legal form. They include all ten dual lands (Underground Sea, Volcanic Island, Tundra, Tropical Island, Badlands, Scrubland, Bayou, Taiga, Plateau and Savannah), plus Wheel of Fortune, Fork, Copy Artifact, Braingeyser, Vesuvan Doppelganger, Fastbond, Sedge Troll and others. That's why the duals are still printed only in their original runs.

## Cards from Revised Edition worth owning

- **The dual lands**: still the best two-color lands in Legacy, Vintage and Commander.
- **Sol Ring**: the most played Commander card.
- **Demonic Tutor**: a Commander staple.
- **Swords to Plowshares**: premier white removal.
- **Lightning Bolt**: the defining burn spell.
- **Counterspell** and **Dark Ritual**: classic commons for Legacy and Pauper.
- **Animate Dead**: a reanimation staple.

## The takeaway

Revised is less a set than a snapshot of early Magic, and its biggest legacy is the dual lands and staples like Sol Ring and Swords to Plowshares. If you want to play with its cards today, Legacy is the natural home, where Animate Dead, the duals and Dark Ritual still do real work.
