Every turn in Magic: The Gathering has five phases in the same order: the beginning phase, the first main phase, the combat phase, the second main phase, and the ending phase. Some phases are split into smaller steps. The beginning phase has untap, upkeep and draw steps; combat has five steps; and the ending phase has an end step and a cleanup step.

Knowing the order matters because cards refer to it constantly ("at the beginning of your upkeep," "until end of turn," "at the beginning of the next end step"), and because it decides when you're allowed to do things. This guide walks through each part of the turn in order, then answers the timing questions that trip up newer players. If you're brand new, the [beginner's guide to playing Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) gives the big picture first.

## Two terms you need first

**Active player.** The player whose turn it is. Most things in the turn happen for the active player, but both players can act during it.

**Priority.** The permission to cast an instant or activate an ability. In most steps, the active player gets priority first. They can do something or pass. Once both players pass priority in a row with nothing waiting on the stack, the game moves to the next step or phase. If something is on the stack when both pass, the top item resolves and the active player gets priority again.

That rule explains a lot: a step doesn't end until both players are done with it. It also means you always get a chance to respond during any step where players receive priority.

**Sorcery speed vs instant speed.** You can cast instants and activate most abilities whenever you have priority. Everything else (creatures, sorceries, artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers, and playing a land) can only happen during your own main phase, when the stack is empty. Players call that "sorcery speed."

## The beginning phase

### Untap step

You untap all your tapped permanents. This happens automatically, and it's the main reason tapping a land or creature only costs you for one turn cycle.

Nobody receives priority during the untap step, so no one can cast spells or activate abilities here. If a card would trigger during the untap step, it waits and goes on the stack at the start of the upkeep.

### Upkeep step

The upkeep is mostly a home for triggered abilities. Any card that says "at the beginning of your upkeep" triggers now. Those triggers go on the stack, and players get priority, so both players can cast instants and activate abilities in response.

Common upkeep effects include cards that make you pay a cost to keep them, cards that drain or deal damage each turn, and cards that give you a benefit every turn. Forgetting upkeep triggers is one of the most common mistakes in casual games, so get into the habit of checking your battlefield before you draw.

### Draw step

You draw a card. This turn-based draw happens first, before anyone gets priority. Then any "at the beginning of your draw step" triggers go on the stack, and players get priority.

In a two-player game, the player who takes the first turn skips the draw on that first turn. In multiplayer games, including Commander, the official rules have no player skip that first draw.

## The first main phase

The main phase is where you do most of your building. During your main phase, while the stack is empty, you can:

- Play a land, if you haven't played one this turn. You get one land per turn by default.
- Cast creatures, sorceries, artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers, and other noninstant spells.
- Activate planeswalker loyalty abilities, one per planeswalker per turn.
- Cast instants and activate abilities, as you can in any step where you have priority.

Some cards care about the first main phase specifically (also called the precombat main phase). For example, a Saga gets a lore counter as your first main phase begins, which triggers its next chapter.

**Should you cast spells before or after combat?** A useful default: cast creatures and other spells after combat unless they help you attack. Holding them until the second main phase keeps your opponent guessing about combat tricks and gives you more information from how combat went. Cast spells before combat when they make your attack better, like removing a blocker or giving your team a bonus.

## The combat phase

Combat has five steps. Here is what each one does at the level of the turn; for blocking, damage assignment and keywords in depth, see the combat section of the [beginner's guide](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering).

### Beginning of combat step

Combat starts here, before attackers are declared. Players get priority. This is the step to use if you want to stop a creature from attacking: on your opponent's turn, tapping one of their creatures during the beginning of combat removes it as an attacker. Tapping it during their main phase works too, but waiting until beginning of combat gives them less chance to adjust their plans.

### Declare attackers step

The active player chooses which creatures attack and taps them (unless they have vigilance). Creatures with summoning sickness can't attack. Attack triggers ("whenever this creature attacks") then go on the stack, and players get priority.

If no creatures attack, the declare blockers and combat damage steps are skipped entirely.

### Declare blockers step

The defending player chooses blockers. Each untapped creature can block one attacker, and several creatures can block the same attacker. Block triggers go on the stack, and players get priority again. This is the classic window for combat tricks, because both players now know exactly which creatures are fighting which.

An important detail: once a creature is blocked, it stays blocked even if the blocker leaves the battlefield. An attacker whose blocker was removed deals no combat damage unless it has trample or another ability that says otherwise.

### Combat damage step

Creatures deal their combat damage at the same time. If any creature has first strike or double strike, there are two combat damage steps: first-strike damage first, then regular damage. Players get priority after each.

### End of combat step

A last chance to act before the second main phase. Effects that last "until end of combat" end after this step. Players get priority here, which is occasionally useful for instants that care whether a creature attacked or blocked this turn.

## The second main phase

The second main phase works exactly like the first. If you haven't played a land yet, you can still play it now. This is where most experienced players cast the creatures and sorceries they held back, now that they know what survived combat and what their opponent spent.

## The ending phase

### End step

Abilities that trigger "at the beginning of the end step" or "at the beginning of the next end step" happen here. Players get priority. This is the step most famous for instant-speed play: casting an instant or flash creature at the end of your opponent's turn lets you use mana you held up for defense without tapping out on your own turn.

### Cleanup step

Three things happen, in this order:

1. The active player discards down to their maximum hand size, which is seven unless an effect changes it.
2. At the same time, all damage marked on creatures is removed, and all "until end of turn" and "this turn" effects end.
3. Normally, no one gets priority.

That last point has one exception. If something triggers during cleanup, or a state-based action needs to happen, players get priority, and then the game runs another cleanup step afterward.

## Common timing questions

**Does damage on a creature last until my next turn?** No. Damage is removed in the cleanup step of the turn it was dealt. A 3/3 that took 2 damage in combat is back to full health at the end of the turn. But within the same turn, damage adds up: 2 combat damage plus a 1-damage spell after combat kills that 3/3.

**Can I play a land during combat?** No. You can only play lands during your main phase, while the stack is empty.

**Can I cast a creature during my opponent's turn?** Only if it has flash, or an effect lets you cast it as though it had flash.

**When does summoning sickness wear off?** A creature can attack or use abilities with the tap symbol once you've controlled it continuously since the start of your most recent turn. In practice: it's ready on your next turn. Haste removes that restriction.

**Does mana carry over between steps?** No. Unused mana empties from your mana pool at the end of each step and phase. If you tap lands in your upkeep and don't spend the mana, it's gone before your draw step.

**What happens if I have eight cards at the end of my turn?** You discard one during your cleanup step. You only check hand size during your own cleanup, so your opponent's turn doesn't make you discard.

**Can I respond after my opponent draws?** Yes. The draw happens first, then players get priority during the draw step. That's a good time for instant-speed effects that care about the top of their library or that you want to cast before they can use their main phase.

**What does "at the beginning of the next end step" mean if it's already the end step?** It means the next one to begin, so it waits until the following turn's end step. Effects created during the end step can feel delayed for that reason.

**Is there a difference between my end step and "end of turn"?** In everyday play, people say "end of turn" to mean the end step. Technically, "until end of turn" effects end in the cleanup step, which comes after the end step. That's why you can still use a creature you pumped "until end of turn" during the end step.

## Putting it together

Here is the whole turn in one table.

| Phase | Steps | Priority? | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginning | Untap | No | Untap everything |
| | Upkeep | Yes | Upkeep triggers |
| | Draw | Yes | Draw first, then priority |
| First main | None | Yes | Lands and sorcery-speed spells |
| Combat | Beginning of combat | Yes | Tap down would-be attackers |
| | Declare attackers | Yes | Attackers tap; triggers |
| | Declare blockers | Yes | Combat tricks window |
| | Combat damage | Yes | First strike adds a step |
| | End of combat | Yes | "Until end of combat" ends |
| Second main | None | Yes | Cast what you held back |
| Ending | End step | Yes | End-step triggers, flash plays |
| | Cleanup | Usually no | Discard to seven, damage wears off |

## The takeaway

Learn the five phases first, then the steps inside beginning, combat and ending. When you're unsure if you can do something, ask two questions: does anyone have priority right now, and is this a sorcery-speed action that needs my main phase with an empty stack?
