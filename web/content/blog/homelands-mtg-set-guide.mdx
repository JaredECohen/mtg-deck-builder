Homelands is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 1995, set on the plane of Ulgrotha, home of Baron Sengir's vampires and the planeswalker Serra. It's best known today for two things: a rich setting that gave Magic its first real look at the Sengir vampires and Serra's angels, and a reputation as one of the weakest sets ever printed. Even so, a few of its cards, like Merchant Scroll and Memory Lapse, became tournament staples.

## The setting and flavor

Homelands doesn't tell one story from start to finish the way modern sets do. Instead, its cards, flavor text and tie-in comics sketch a world after a disaster. That world is **Ulgrotha**, a small plane with a long history of magical catastrophe.

Two famous names from Alpha come from here. The planeswalker **Serra**, creator of the Serra Angels, came to Ulgrotha and married another planeswalker, **Feroz**. Together they tried to heal the plane and protect it, and Feroz wove a great protective spell, **Feroz's Ban**, around Ulgrotha. The Ban did its job of keeping powerful outsiders away, but it also had consequences no one intended, and Feroz died. After his death, Serra left the plane, and without the two of them holding things together, Ulgrotha's peoples turned on one another.

The most dangerous of them is **Baron Sengir**, the vampire lord who rules from Castle Sengir and spreads his bloodline by turning his victims. His family includes **Irini Sengir**, the vampire dwarf, and **Veldrane of Sengir**, and the mysterious **Grandmother Sengir** works her own schemes. Opposing them are the people of the plane's other regions: the abbeys of **Aysen**, the townsfolk of **An-Havva**, the minotaurs of **Anaba**, the giants and rogues of the **Koskun** lands, merchants and pirates on the coasts, and **Autumn Willow**, the forest's guardian. The ghostly knight **Ihsan's Shade** is one of the set's best-known villains.

The comics add a darker note: **Apocalypse Chime**, a card that destroys every permanent with a name originally printed in Homelands, comes from a story about a bell that wipes out nearly everything on the plane. Ulgrotha returned decades later as one of the planes invaded in [March of the Machine](/blog/march-of-the-machine-mtg-set-guide).

Homelands was originally treated as part of the Ice Age block, but it had nothing to do with Ice Age's story, and when Coldsnap was released in 2006, it replaced Homelands as the third set of that block.

## Mechanics

Homelands introduced no new keywords. Its design relied on themes instead, and many of them were tied to the setting's peoples.

### Tribal themes

Several of Ulgrotha's peoples had their own small creature-type themes. Minotaurs had **Anaba Spirit Crafter**, which gives Minotaurs +1/+0, and **Didgeridoo**, an artifact that puts Minotaurs from your hand onto the battlefield. Faeries had **Faerie Noble** and **Willow Priestess**. The Sengir vampires had **Baron Sengir**, which can regenerate other Vampires, and **Sengir Bats**. These themes were too small to build around in their own set, but they foreshadowed the tribal designs Magic would print later.

### Delayed card draw

Several cheap spells replaced themselves at the start of the next turn's upkeep instead of right away. **Jinx** changes a land's type, **Headstone** exiles a card from a graveyard, and **Prophecy** reveals a card from an opponent's library, and each draws you a card in the following upkeep.

### World enchantments

Homelands printed several world enchantments, an old supertype where only one can be on the battlefield at a time. **Serra Aviary** gives fliers +1/+1, **Koskun Falls** makes creatures cost `{2}` each to attack you, and **Mystic Decree** strips flying and islandwalk from every creature.

### Lands with filtered mana

Five uncommon lands, **Castle Sengir**, **Wizards' School**, **Aysen Abbey**, **Koskun Keep** and **An-Havva Township**, tap for colorless mana or, for an extra cost, colored mana. They're slow, but they were early experiments in mana fixing.

### Ante

Like many early sets, Homelands included a card that played with ante, the old optional rule where players risked cards from their decks. **Timmerian Fiends** can steal an opponent's artifact through ante, and it's banned in every format that allows the set. For how to read old card text and types, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Homelands wasn't designed for Limited the way modern sets are, and booster draft as we know it hadn't become a standard tournament format in 1995. Most people opened Homelands in sealed packs or mixed it into casual play. The card pool shows why it didn't make much of a draft format: few commons were efficient, removal was scarce, and the tribal themes were too thin to support. There are no documented draft archetypes for the set, so treat any you see described online with suspicion.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Type II (1995-1997).** Homelands entered a Type II format that also included Ice Age, Alliances and, later, Mirage and Visions. Only a few of its cards made a mark. **Memory Lapse** was the most successful: a two-mana counterspell that makes the opponent redraw the countered card, which bought blue decks a full turn. Other cards had interesting ideas on paper, like **Autumn Willow**, a big green creature that targeted removal couldn't touch, and **Ihsan's Shade**, which dodged white removal like Swords to Plowshares, but they made far less of an impact. Timmerian Fiends was banned for ante. The rest of the set saw little tournament play, which is a big part of its reputation.

**Legacy.** **Merchant Scroll** has been used in blue combo and control decks to find a key instant like Force of Will or a combo piece. Most of the rest of the set is too slow for the format.

**Vintage.** Merchant Scroll was restricted in Vintage in June 2008 as part of a larger round of restrictions that also hit Brainstorm, Ponder and Gush. For more on how restrictions work, see our [Vintage format guide](/blog/mtg-vintage-format-guide).

**Commander.** Homelands is a niche Commander set, but a few cards appear: Merchant Scroll in blue decks, Primal Order in mono-green decks that punish nonbasic lands, Sengir Autocrat in sacrifice decks, and Baron Sengir in vampire decks. Its legends, including Baron Sengir, Grandmother Sengir, Autumn Willow and Soraya the Falconer, have a following among players who like building around old cards. For how the format works, see [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

**Pauper.** Serrated Arrows, Memory Lapse, Merchant Scroll and Sea Sprite are Pauper-legal commons from the set, and Serrated Arrows has seen play as repeatable removal.

## Cards from Homelands worth owning

- **Merchant Scroll**: restricted in Vintage and a useful blue tutor in Commander.
- **Memory Lapse**: the set's best Type II card and a Pauper option.
- **Serrated Arrows**: repeatable Pauper removal.
- **Autumn Willow** and **Baron Sengir**: Reserved List legends with a place in Magic's history.
- **Primal Order**: a punishing card for Commander tables full of nonbasic lands.

## The takeaway

Homelands is weak as a card pool but important as a piece of Magic's history. It gave the game Ulgrotha, Baron Sengir's family and Serra's backstory, and a handful of its cards, led by Merchant Scroll and Memory Lapse, still matter. If you want to play its cards in a competitive format, Legacy is the most realistic home, where a blue deck can use Merchant Scroll to find its best instants.
