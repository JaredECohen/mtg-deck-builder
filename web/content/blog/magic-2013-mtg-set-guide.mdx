Magic 2013, usually shortened to M13, is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2012. It cast the dragon Nicol Bolas as the villain of the summer, brought exalted back from Shards of Alara, and gave Standard its defining creature of the era, Thragtusk. It also printed Omniscience, Talrand, Sky Summoner and Krenko, Mob Boss, cards that are still busy in Legacy and Commander today.

## What the set was for

Core sets in the Magic 2010 to Magic 2015 run had a double job. They were the product a brand-new player was most likely to buy, so they leaned on clean, readable cards and classic reprints like Serra Angel, Clone and Murder. They also had to give Standard players something new, because each one spent about fourteen months next to two "real" blocks. In M13's case that meant Innistrad block first and Return to Ravnica block from October 2012.

M13 didn't bring a big rules overhaul the way Magic 2010 did or the way Magic 2014 later would with the new legend rule. Its firsts were design firsts instead:

- **The first multicolored card in a core set.** Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker, a reprint from Conflux, costs blue, black and red mana and was the face of the set.
- **Legendary creatures in a core set again.** Tenth Edition had tried them; M13 brought back one per color, each built around a simple theme: Odric, Master Tactician (white), Talrand, Sky Summoner (blue), Nefarox, Overlord of Grixis (black), Krenko, Mob Boss (red) and Yeva, Nature's Herald (green), plus the reprinted Akroma's Memorial.
- **Exalted and flash showed up in a core set for the first time.**

The set was lead-designed by Doug Beyer, and it launched alongside the video game Duels of the Planeswalkers 2013, which ran the same "face a greater challenge" campaign against Bolas.

### The Bolas angle

There is no full plot in M13, but there is a villain. Several cards reference Bolas directly: Augur of Bolas, Disciple of Bolas, and the Grixis demon Nefarox, which reads like one of Bolas's lieutenants. Ajani was the hero on the other side. His new card, Ajani, Caller of the Pride, came at the end of a long story thread in which Ajani tracked down Bolas after learning the dragon was behind the destruction of his home plane, Alara. The other planeswalkers were Jace, Memory Adept, Liliana of the Dark Realms, Chandra, the Firebrand and Garruk, Primal Hunter, one per color.

## Mechanics

### Exalted

Exalted is a triggered ability: whenever a creature you control attacks alone, it gets +1/+1 until end of turn for each instance of exalted you have. Three exalted permanents turn a lone 2/2 into a 5/5. The mechanic rewards a slower, one-attacker-per-turn style that's very different from the usual "swing with everything" plan.

M13 spread exalted across white and black. Knight of Infamy, a two-mana 2/1 with protection from white and exalted, was a strong uncommon. Sublime Archangel, the mythic, gives all your other creatures exalted, so a board of four creatures makes a single attacker enormous. Cathedral of War is a land with exalted, and Nefarox, Overlord of Grixis forces the defending player to sacrifice a creature whenever it attacks alone.

### Legendary creatures and simple build-arounds

The five legends were each a small deckbuilding prompt. Talrand, Sky Summoner makes a 2/2 flying Drake every time you cast an instant or sorcery. Krenko, Mob Boss taps to create a 1/1 Goblin for each Goblin you control, so it doubles your Goblin count each turn. Yeva lets you cast green creatures at instant speed. Odric lets you decide how your opponent's creatures block when you attack with a big enough team. They were teaching cards, and they became Commander favorites.

### Returning evergreen keywords

The rest of the set used familiar keywords: flying, deathtouch, lifelink, first strike, haste and flash. Vampire Nighthawk, reprinted at uncommon, packs three of them into one three-mana creature. Our [keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers each of these.

## Limited

M13 was a classic core-set draft format: lots of vanilla and simple creatures, a handful of efficient removal spells, and a few rares that could win alone. It was faster than many core sets, mostly because exalted and cheap evasive creatures punished slow starts.

Each color had a clear role:

- **White** was the aggressive color, with exalted creatures, Pacifism and cards like Captain's Call. White-black exalted decks could put one huge attacker into the red zone every turn.
- **Blue** offered fliers, Encrust as removal for a creature or artifact, and tempo spells. Talrand's Invocation, two 2/2 fliers for four mana, was a strong uncommon.
- **Black** had Murder at common, plus Mark of the Vampire, an Aura that gives +2/+2 and lifelink and could take over a board stall.
- **Red** had Searing Spear, the best common removal spell, and Turn to Slag for bigger threats.
- **Green** brought big creatures, Arbor Elf and Prey Upon.

Most strong decks were two colors with a clear plan: an exalted deck with a few big auras or removal spells, or a midrange deck that traded early and won with bigger creatures or fliers. The cards are simple enough that M13 is a good format for practicing fundamentals, and our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) walks through how to rank removal, evasion and tricks in a format like this.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2012-2013

M13 joined Standard while Innistrad block was still the backbone of the format, and from October 2012 it lined up with Return to Ravnica block. Thragtusk quickly became the card everything else had to deal with. A five-mana creature that gains 5 life and replaces itself with a 3/3 when it leaves the battlefield is miserable for aggro decks to beat and great against removal-heavy control decks.

Thragtusk showed up in nearly every green midrange and control deck of the season, including Jund Midrange, Bant Control and Junk Reanimator, which also discarded and returned big threats with Unburial Rites. Thundermaw Hellkite was the answer red decks reached for: it flies over a board of Thragtusks and Beast tokens, has haste, and taps down opposing fliers, which made it a strong card against Reanimator's Angels. Searing Spear gave red aggro a cheap burn spell, and Augur of Bolas helped the blue-white-red Flash decks find their instants.

No M13 card was banned in Standard. The set left Standard alongside Innistrad block when Theros released in September 2013.

### Eternal formats and Commander

M13 never became part of Pioneer, since that format starts at Return to Ravnica, and its cards rarely appear in Modern. Its long life is in Legacy and Commander.

- **Omniscience** became a Legacy combo payoff. Show and Tell lets you put it onto the battlefield for three mana, after which the rest of your hand is free. The "Omni-Tell" family of decks was a long-running Legacy archetype.
- **Talrand, Sky Summoner** and **Krenko, Mob Boss** are popular commanders and staple inclusions in spells and Goblin decks.
- **Gilded Lotus**, **Akroma's Memorial** and **Primordial Hydra** are casual and Commander favorites.
- **Augur of Bolas** is legal in Pauper thanks to a later common printing, so it can join blue spell decks there.

As of October 2026, no Magic 2013 card is banned in Modern, Legacy or Commander. Our [formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) guide covers where each of these formats draws its card pool from.

## Cards from Magic 2013 worth owning

- **Omniscience**: the payoff for Show and Tell combo in Legacy and a fun Commander finisher.
- **Thragtusk**: still a strong value creature for casual and Commander green decks.
- **Talrand, Sky Summoner**: a simple, popular commander for instants and sorceries.
- **Krenko, Mob Boss**: the face of Goblin tribal in Commander.
- **Thundermaw Hellkite**: a five-mana Dragon that still hits hard in casual play.
- **Gilded Lotus**: big mana acceleration for Commander.

## The takeaway

Magic 2013 was a teaching set that happened to print one of the most influential Standard creatures of its decade. If you want a taste of how its cards play today, Modern is the most accessible Constructed home for them, and Thragtusk plus Thundermaw Hellkite still make a fun midrange core.
