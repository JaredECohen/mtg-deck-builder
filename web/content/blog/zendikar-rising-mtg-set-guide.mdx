Zendikar Rising is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2020, which sent players back to Zendikar after the Eldrazi were gone. It's an adventure set about expeditions into floating ruins, and it introduced party and modal double-faced cards while bringing back landfall and kicker. It gave Standard one of its most-banned decks and gave Modern a set of spell-lands that are still everywhere.

## The story

Zendikar survived the Eldrazi, but it's still a dangerous place. The Roil, the plane's wild magical upheaval, keeps reshaping the land, and adventurers make their living exploring the floating ruins of the ancient kor, called Skyclaves. Two planeswalkers who call Zendikar home are back, and they want very different things.

**Nahiri**, the kor lithomancer, ages ago helped imprison the Eldrazi on Zendikar. She blames herself for the Roil and wants to stabilize the plane. She funds an expedition from the Sea Gate Expeditionary House to find the **Lithoform Core**, an artifact of the old kor empire that can reshape the land. **Nissa Revane**, an elf animist who can speak with the land's elementals, first helps her. Then she sees the cost. When Nahiri tests the Core's restoring power, it kills one of Nissa's elementals. Restoring Zendikar to its ancient, stable form would erase the wild nature living there now, and Nissa turns against her.

Nahiri hires a crew of adventurers: the kor warrior Akiri, the merfolk rogue Zareth San, the wizard Kaza and the cleric Orah. They reach the Core in the Murasa Skyclave, but elementals attack. Nahiri uses the Core to defend herself and kills Zareth in the process. When Akiri threatens to throw the artifact off the ruin, Nahiri pins her with a hedron and pushes her over the edge. Nissa, climbing up with Jace Beleren, catches her.

Nahiri heads for the ancient Singing City, where she can push the Core's power through Zendikar's leylines. Jace follows and strikes a deal: he won't stop her if she waits to study the artifact first. Nissa gets there first and destroys the focal point with the help of **Ashaya**, an elemental who is part of the land itself. When Jace tries to take the Core to Ravnica, an elemental snatches it and brings it to Nissa. She uses its power to heal the scars the Eldrazi left and restore the land's wild nature, and that drains the Core. Nahiri, beaten, leaves Zendikar and swears revenge on Jace.

## Mechanics

### Party

Your party is up to one each of Cleric, Rogue, Warrior and Wizard among the creatures you control, so a party ranges from zero to four. Cards scale with it. **Archpriest of Iona** has power equal to your party size, and removal like **Deadly Alliance** costs `{1}` less for each party member. Party rewards drafting a mix of creature types rather than going all in on one.

### Modal double-faced cards

Each draft booster had a modal double-faced card (MDFC). These cards have a spell on the front and a land on the back. You pick a face when you play it, and they don't transform. The mythic cycle is the most famous: **Shatterskull Smashing** is a damage spell or an untapped red land if you pay 3 life. These cards cut down mana flood and screw, which made them strong across formats. Our guide to [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) covers the basic card frame.

### Landfall (returning)

Landfall abilities trigger whenever a land enters under your control. **Akoum Hellhound** is a one-mana 0/1 that gets +2/+2 each time you play a land, and **Lotus Cobra** adds a mana of any color. Fetch lands and extra land drops trigger landfall twice in one turn.

### Kicker (returning)

Kicker is an optional extra cost for a bigger effect. **Into the Roil** returns a nonland permanent to its owner's hand, and draws a card if kicked. **Bloodchief's Thirst** kills something cheap for `{B}` or anything for four mana.

### Snap-on Equipment

Every Equipment in the set attaches itself to a creature you control when it enters, so you skip the first equip cost. **Maul of the Skyclaves** gives +2/+2, flying and first strike the turn it lands.

## Limited

Every color pair has a gold signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue: party control.** Fliers and a full party, rewarded by Spoils of Adventure, which draws three cards.
- **Blue-black: Rogues and mill.** Get eight cards in the opponent's graveyard to turn on Rogue bonuses (Soaring Thought-Thief).
- **Black-red: party aggro.** Attack with a mixed crew and Ravager's Mace.
- **Red-green: landfall aggro.** Big landfall swings from Brushfire Elemental.
- **Green-white: landfall midrange.** Murasa Rootgrazer lets you replay lands.
- **White-black: Clerics and life gain.** Cleric of Life's Bond grows as you gain life.
- **Blue-red: Wizards and spells.** Instants, sorceries and Wizard triggers (Umara Mystic).
- **Black-green: +1/+1 counters.** Moss-Pit Skeleton and other counter synergies.
- **Red-white: Warriors and Equipment.** Kargan Warleader pumps the team.
- **Green-blue: kicker ramp.** Ramp into kicked spells with cards like Vine Gecko.

Kicker cards give every deck flexible early and late plays, and the uncommon MDFCs, like **Kabira Takedown** and **Khalni Ambush**, are strong picks because they replace a land. Snap-on Equipment and landfall pushed many games toward early aggression, so cheap creatures and removal mattered. Good commons and uncommons included **Feed the Swarm**, **Into the Roil** and **Kor Blademaster**. If you're new to reading draft archetypes, our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains how signpost cards point the way.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Zendikar Rising's Standard debut was rough. **Omnath, Locus of Creation** combined with Lotus Cobra, extra land drops and fetch effects to gain life, add mana and deal damage in the same turn. Wizards first banned Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath from Theros Beyond Death a few days after release, hoping that would be enough. It wasn't. Omnath decks dominated the 2020 Season Grand Finals, and on October 12, 2020, Wizards banned Omnath in Standard along with Lucky Clover and Escape to the Wilds. Omnath was also suspended in Historic on MTG Arena.

After the bans, Standard opened up. Dimir Rogues used the set's mill-matters Rogues, Azorius Blink decks used Yorion, Sky Nomad, and Gruul Adventures and other aggro decks took advantage of the slower field. **Skyclave Apparition**, **Luminarch Aspirant** and the MDFCs stayed in decks for the rest of the set's time in Standard. It rotated out with Kaldheim, Strixhaven and Adventures in the Forgotten Realms when Dominaria United arrived in September 2022.

The longer story is in the eternal formats. **Skyclave Apparition** became one of the most-played white creatures in Modern, partly because it permanently answers threats like Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath and Kroxa, Titan of Death's Hunger. The MDFC lands, from Shatterskull Smashing to Agadeem's Awakening, gave Modern and Pioneer decks spells that double as lands. They also enabled decks that win with no lands left in the library. In Pioneer that led to the February 15, 2021 ban of Balustrade Spy and Undercity Informer, two cards from other sets that let such decks mill themselves and win. As of October 2026, Omnath isn't banned in Modern or Pioneer, and no Zendikar Rising card is on those formats' ban lists.

In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), the set's landfall cards are favorites. Ancient Greenwarden, Lotus Cobra, Scute Swarm and Felidar Retreat power lands decks, and Omnath, Locus of Creation leads four-color landfall builds.

## Cards from Zendikar Rising worth owning

- **Skyclave Apparition**: flexible white removal on a body in Modern and Pioneer.
- **The mythic MDFCs** (Shatterskull Smashing, Agadeem's Awakening, Emeria's Call, Sea Gate Restoration, Turntimber Symbiosis): spells that are also lands, for nearly any deck in their color.
- **Valakut Awakening and Spikefield Hazard**: cheap red MDFCs that show up in Modern and Pioneer.
- **The Pathway lands**: untapped dual lands for Pioneer and Commander mana bases.
- **Lotus Cobra**: a landfall ramp piece for Commander and combo decks.
- **Ancient Greenwarden**: a top payoff for lands-matter Commander decks.
- **Omnath, Locus of Creation**: banned in Standard but a strong four-color commander.

Zendikar Rising's lasting lesson is that lands that are also spells make every deck better. When you build with the set's cards today, count the MDFCs as part of your land total, about half a land each, and lean on landfall payoffs that reward an extra land drop.
