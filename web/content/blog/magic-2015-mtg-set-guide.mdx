Magic 2015 is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2014, a mix of new cards and reprints meant to welcome newer players while still feeding Standard. It doesn't visit a single plane, but it nods to many of them, follows a cursed Garruk Wildspeaker's hunt for other planeswalkers, and includes a run of cards designed by well-known video game creators. It also debuted the card frame Magic still uses, and it left behind Goblin Rabblemaster, Hornet Queen and The Chain Veil.

## What the set was for

Core sets in this era had a clear job. Each summer, Wizards released a set that could serve as someone's first booster pack: simpler cards, lots of basic keywords and classic reprints, sold alongside that year's Duels of the Planeswalkers video game. Magic 2015 kept that role but also tried to make experienced players care. About half the set was new, and many of its rares were built with Constructed in mind. If you're new to the game, our guide on [how to play Magic: The Gathering](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers the basics a core set like this was designed to teach.

The set's best-known experiment was its **guest designers**. Wizards invited a group of video game developers to each design a card with the Magic team. Among them, Plants vs. Zombies creator George Fan made **Genesis Hydra**, Minecraft creator Markus Persson made **Aggressive Mining**, Blizzard's Rob Pardo made **Xathrid Slyblade**, Brian Fargo of Interplay made **Yisan, the Wanderer Bard**, and Borderlands writer Mikey Neumann made **Chasm Skulker**. The results ranged from casual oddities to cards that still show up in Commander.

Magic 2015 also changed how every card looks. It introduced the updated card frame, with a holofoil stamp on rares and mythics and the collector information moved to the bottom left, which is the frame most modern cards still use. Our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) walks through it.

### The story hook

The set's planeswalkers carry a light storyline. Garruk Wildspeaker, corrupted by the Chain Veil curse that Liliana Vess left on him, has become **Garruk, Apex Predator**, a hunter who stalks other planeswalkers across the Multiverse. His card can destroy planeswalkers outright, and the sorcery **In Garruk's Wake** wipes out every creature and planeswalker you don't control. The artifact that cursed him appears as **The Chain Veil**. The other planeswalkers in the set, including **Jace, the Living Guildpact**, **Ajani Steadfast** and **Nissa, Worldwaker**, are the kind of targets Garruk is after, and his hunt continued into later stories. A cycle of **Souls**, like **Soul of Ravnica** and **Soul of New Phyrexia**, represents the spirit of famous planes, a reminder that a core set is a tour of the Multiverse rather than one world.

## Mechanics

### Convoke

Convoke returns from the original Ravnica block. When you cast a spell with convoke, you can tap your creatures to help pay for it, with each creature covering `{1}` or one mana of its color. **Stoke the Flames** (uncommon) costs `{2}{R}{R}` and deals 4 damage to any target, but a board of creatures can cast it for little or no mana. **Chief Engineer** gives every artifact spell you cast convoke, and **Chord of Calling** was reprinted as a convoke tutor.

### Slivers

Slivers return after Magic 2014. Each Sliver grants its ability to every Sliver you control, so the more you have, the better they get. **Sliver Hivelord** is a five-color legend that makes all your Slivers indestructible.

### The Soul cycle and guest cards

The Souls are big creatures you can cast from your hand, plus an ability you can activate from the graveyard by exiling them, so they give value even after they die. The guest-designed cards don't share a mechanic, but several, like Genesis Hydra and Yisan, became Commander favorites. Core sets lean on simple evergreen keywords like flying, deathtouch and trample, which makes them a good place to learn the game.

## Limited

Magic 2015 drafted like a classic core set, with simple, mostly two-color decks and a focus on creatures and removal rather than intricate archetypes. The main themes were:

- **White: go-wide tokens and convoke.** Cards like **Triplicate Spirits** and **Seraph of the Masses** reward a wide board.
- **Blue: fliers and artifacts.** Evasion, tempo and artifact synergies.
- **Black: removal and recursion.** Efficient kill spells and sticky creatures.
- **Red: aggression and burn.** Cheap creatures and direct damage, including **Heat Ray**.
- **Green: big creatures.** Large bodies, ramp and fight effects.
- **Slivers across colors.** Slivers share their abilities, so a few of them can carry a deck.

Convoke tied the colors together: white and green creature decks could cast expensive spells early by tapping their teams. Removal was the priority at every rarity, and simple card advantage like **Shaman of Spring** was valued. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Magic 2015 spent its Standard life alongside the Theros and Khans of Tarkir blocks, and a handful of its cards became staples. Goblin Rabblemaster was the standout, a must-answer three-drop for red aggro decks that also showed up in Boros and Mardu lists. Stoke the Flames was a common burn spell, including in the Atarka Red decks of 2015, where convoke let tokens pay for it. Hornet Queen was a powerful top end for green midrange and reanimation decks because its deathtouch fliers shut down attacks. Ensoul Artifact even gave blue a small aggressive artifact deck built around cheap artifacts and indestructible Darksteel Citadel.

No Magic 2015 cards were banned in Standard. The set rotated out in October 2015 with the Theros block, when Battle for Zendikar arrived. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains how that cycle worked.

Since Pioneer begins with Return to Ravnica, Magic 2015 is legal there, and its pain lands are a quiet but real contribution to Pioneer mana bases. Goblin Rabblemaster is an aggressive option in Pioneer red decks, and Card Kingdom's writers note it also jumped to Modern and Legacy at points in its life.

Commander is where the set gets the most play today. The Chain Veil is a famous combo piece and a superfriends staple, Hornet Queen is a popular top end in green, Chief Engineer and Waste Not anchor their archetypes, and Genesis Hydra, Yisan and Sliver Hivelord all lead or support decks. The pain lands are budget-friendly dual lands. As of October 2026, The Chain Veil and Hornet Queen remain the set's most-recognized Commander cards, and our guide to [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format.

## Cards from Magic 2015 worth owning

- **Hornet Queen**: a defensive and offensive top end for green Commander decks.
- **The Chain Veil**: a planeswalker engine and combo piece for Commander.
- **Goblin Rabblemaster**: an aggressive three-drop for Pioneer red decks.
- **The pain lands**: Battlefield Forge, Caves of Koilos, Llanowar Wastes, Shivan Reef and Yavimaya Coast fix mana in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Chief Engineer**: a cheap enabler for artifact Commander decks.
- **Waste Not**: a payoff for discard-themed decks.
- **Genesis Hydra**: a flexible two-for-one for green Commander decks.

Magic 2015 is a core set that punches above its weight, mostly through a few Constructed rares and a lot of long-lived Commander cards.
