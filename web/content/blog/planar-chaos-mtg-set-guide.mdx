Planar Chaos is the second set of the Time Spiral block in Magic: The Gathering, released in February 2007. Its theme was "the alternate present": rifts in time have splintered Dominaria into overlapping alternate histories, so the set is full of familiar cards in the wrong colors, like a black Wrath of God called Damnation. It's one of the strangest and most beloved experiments in the game's design history, and it introduced vanishing.

## The story

The Time Spiral block is the end of a long Dominarian saga. Centuries of catastrophes, from the Brothers' War through the Phyrexian invasion, have torn holes in time across the plane. These time rifts are pulling past and present together, draining the land and threatening to unravel the whole Multiverse.

Planar Chaos, told in the novel of the same name by Scott McGough and Timothy Sanders, picks up immediately after Time Spiral. The planeswalker Teferi has just sealed the rift over Shiv, letting the region he had phased out of reality return safely, but the effort cost him his planeswalker spark. Now mortal, he and his companions Jhoira and Venser try to close the remaining rifts. As the set's flavor puts it, the rifts have splintered the timeline into alternate histories, and those alternate Dominarias are bleeding through. That's the story reason behind the color-shifted cards: they are glimpses of a world where history went differently.

The group's travels take them to Urborg, the swamp-island protected by the planeswalker Lord Windgrace, and to the Skyshroud forest, guarded by the planeswalker Freyalise. Both regions are besieged by Phyrexians who aren't from this Dominaria at all; they are pouring in from an alternate reality through the rifts. Behind much of the chaos is the Weaver King, a psychic horror from Rath who seizes control of the Phyrexians and the local Slivers.

The planeswalkers of old pay for the rifts with their lives. Freyalise channels everything she has into the Skyshroud rift and is consumed sealing it. Windgrace places part of himself into Urborg itself and then crushes Urborg's rift, vanishing in the process. Venser, who has been discovering powers he doesn't understand, outwits and kills the Weaver King. And the silver golem Karn, now a planeswalker, travels to the ruins of Tolaria, where the oldest and worst rift began. The story continues in Future Sight, where the last rifts are closed.

## Mechanics

### Color-shifted ("timeshifted") cards

Forty-five cards in Planar Chaos are reprints of older cards from an alternate reality, functionally identical except for their color (and sometimes a new name and creature type). They had a distinct card frame with white title text. Damnation is the famous example: it's Wrath of God, but black. Others include Pongify, a blue creature removal spell that leaves behind a 3/3 Ape, Mire Boa, a green take on River Boa that regenerates and has swampwalk, and Prodigal Pyromancer, a red version of the blue Prodigal Sorcerer.

The rest of the set also played with the color pie. White got counterspells (Mana Tithe, a one-mana Force Spike) and shroud (Calciderm), blue got vigilance and creature destruction, black got first strike, red got reach and trample, and green got flying and card draw (Harmonize).

### Vanishing

A permanent with vanishing enters with a number of time counters. At the start of your upkeep you remove one, and when the last is removed you sacrifice it. It's a cleaner version of fading from Nemesis, and the time counters interact with suspend cards.

Keldon Marauders is the best-known vanishing card: a two-mana 3/3 with vanishing 2 that deals 1 damage to the opponent when it enters and again when it leaves, so it's often 5 or more damage for two mana. Calciderm is a four-mana 5/5 with shroud and vanishing 4, a big, untargetable clock for white decks.

### Returning mechanics

Suspend lets you exile a card with time counters on it for a cheap cost and cast it for free when the last counter comes off. Planar Chaos added a cycle of rare creatures with "suspend X," like Aeon Chronicler and Detritivore, that get stronger the longer you wait. Split second (spells that can't be responded to except with mana abilities) appears on cards like Extirpate and Sulfur Elemental. Madness, echo, morph, flash, kicker and flanking also returned. The set also had the first split cards with both halves in the same color, like Dead // Gone and Boom // Bust.

## Limited

Planar Chaos was drafted alongside Time Spiral, so most drafts mixed one pack of it with two packs of the large set. Time Spiral block Limited is remembered as one of the most complex formats ever: suspend, morph, madness, flash and split second all at once, plus nostalgia references that rewarded players who knew the game's history. Planar Chaos added color-shifted commons that blurred what each color could do, so you couldn't always predict a trick from the colors of the opponent's open mana.

What was strong was what's usually strong. Cheap removal like Dead // Gone, Sunlance, Cradle to Grave, Pongify and Midnight Charm was prized. Morph creatures such as Shaper Parasite (which shrinks or kills a creature when turned face up) gave you flexible plays. Fliers and resilient creatures, like Malach of the Dawn and Revered Dead, ground out games, and the rare Serra Sphinx could win alone. The format rewarded patience and planning, since suspended spells and vanishing creatures meant you were always counting turns.

The 2021 remastered set, Time Spiral Remastered, brought many of these cards back into a single draft environment for players who want to try the block today.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Planar Chaos was Standard-legal from late February 2007 until Shards of Alara rotated Time Spiral block out in October 2008, so it lived through both the Time Spiral and Lorwyn seasons.

- **Black control.** Damnation gave black a Wrath of God for the first time, and it showed up in blue-black control decks such as the Dralnu du Louvre lists in the months after release.
- **White aggro.** Calciderm and Mana Tithe pushed aggressive white decks, giving them a hard-to-answer threat and a cheap way to punish opponents who tapped out.
- **Red aggro.** Keldon Marauders gave red decks a two-drop that dealt a lot of damage on its own.
- **Blue control** sometimes used Aeon Chronicler as an uncounterable source of card advantage, and Detritivore found a home in land destruction decks next to Wildfire.

No Planar Chaos card was banned in Standard. In Extended, Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth was a key piece of the Hexmage Depths combo deck, which used Urborg so that Vampire Hexmage could pay for Dark Depths' counters.

The set's long tail is mostly in older formats and Commander. As of October 2026:

- **Modern.** Simian Spirit Guide is banned in Modern, since free mana is dangerous in a format full of fast combo. Damnation and Urborg see play in black midrange and control decks. Our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) covers how these fit in.
- **Legacy and Vintage.** Simian Spirit Guide fuels fast combo and Moon decks, and Extirpate is a split-second graveyard answer for sideboards.
- **Pauper.** Keldon Marauders, Mana Tithe and Mire Boa are commons and have all seen play in Pauper.
- **Commander.** Damnation is a staple board wipe, Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth turns on Cabal Coffers and fixes black mana for every deck, and Magus of the Coffers, Necrotic Sliver and the Dragons have loyal fans. Our [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format.

Planar Chaos is a 2007 set, so none of its cards are on the Reserved List; that list covers sets only through Urza's Destiny. That's why cards like Damnation and Urborg have been reprinted.

## Cards from Planar Chaos worth owning

- **Damnation**: a four-mana board wipe for any black Commander or control deck.
- **Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth**: mana fixing and a Cabal Coffers enabler in Commander.
- **Simian Spirit Guide**: free mana for Legacy combo decks.
- **Extirpate**: an uncounterable graveyard answer.
- **Keldon Marauders**: a cheap, damaging creature for red aggro and Pauper.
- **Magus of the Coffers**: big black mana in creature form.

## The takeaway

Planar Chaos asked a fun question, "what if the color pie were different?", and answered it with cards that still matter. Damnation and Urborg are Commander staples, Simian Spirit Guide shaped Legacy combo, and the story is a moving end for Dominaria's old planeswalkers. If you want to play its cards in Modern today, a black control deck built around Damnation is the natural fit.
