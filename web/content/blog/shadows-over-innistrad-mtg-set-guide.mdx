Shadows over Innistrad is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2016 that returned to Innistrad, the gothic horror plane of vampires, werewolves and zombies. This time the angels have gone mad, and Jace Beleren arrives to investigate a mystery that leads to an old grudge between two planeswalkers and, finally, to the Eldrazi titan Emrakul. The set added investigate and delirium, brought back madness, and printed some of the most enduring cards of its era.

## The story

When players last left Innistrad, the archangel Avacyn had been freed from the Helvault and the plane's humans were finally winning against the night. A few years later, something is wrong. People are going mad, strange stone pillars called cryptoliths have appeared across the countryside, and the angels who protect humanity have turned on the people they used to save. Avacyn herself has begun to purge whole villages she sees as corrupted.

Jace comes to Innistrad looking for Sorin Markov, the ancient vampire planeswalker who created Avacyn, because he believes Sorin can explain what's happening. His search plays out like a detective story, with Jace collecting clues, slowly feeling the madness press on his own mind, and visiting Liliana Vess, who has her own business on the plane. The answers point to **Nahiri**, a kor planeswalker from Zendikar.

Thousands of years earlier, Nahiri, Sorin and Ugin had sealed the Eldrazi titans on Zendikar together. When the Eldrazi broke free, Sorin never came to help. Worse, long before that, he had imprisoned Nahiri in the Helvault for a thousand years. Freed at last, she wants revenge. She has trapped Sorin in stone with her lithomancy, and her cryptoliths are reshaping Innistrad's ley lines to draw something terrible to the plane. The warped magic is what's driving the angels mad.

Avacyn's madness keeps growing until she becomes a threat to the whole plane. The story's climax comes when Sorin, once free, confronts the angel he made. Unable to reason with her, he unmakes Avacyn, and the light she cast over Innistrad goes out. Nahiri's real goal is revealed at the end: the cryptoliths were a beacon for **Emrakul**, the largest of the Eldrazi titans. Shadows over Innistrad closes with Emrakul on her way, and the story continues directly in Eldritch Moon, where the Gatewatch gathers to face her.

## Mechanics

### Investigate

To investigate, you create a Clue token, an artifact with "`{2}`, sacrifice this: draw a card." Clues let cheap creatures turn into card advantage later. **Thraben Inspector** (common, `{W}`) is a 1/2 that investigates on entry, and it became a staple in several formats. **Tireless Tracker** makes a Clue whenever a land enters under your control and grows each time you crack one.

### Delirium

Delirium checks whether there are four or more card types among cards in your graveyard. Land, creature, instant, sorcery, artifact, enchantment and planeswalker all count, and a double-faced card counts its types. **Traverse the Ulvenwald** (rare, `{G}`) finds a basic land normally, or any creature or land card with delirium.

### Madness

Madness returns from the Odyssey block. If you discard a card with madness, you can cast it for its madness cost instead of putting it in your graveyard. **Fiery Temper** is a three-mana Lightning Bolt variant that costs `{R}` if you discard it, and discard outlets like **Insolent Neonate** turn on the whole deck.

### Skulk and double-faced cards

Creatures with skulk can't be blocked by creatures with greater power, so small skulk creatures slip past big blockers. **Furtive Homunculus** is the plain version. Werewolves and other double-faced cards return: a werewolf transforms at the start of a turn if no spells were cast in the previous turn, and transforms back at the start of a turn after any player cast two or more spells in the previous turn. Our [double-faced cards guide](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) explains the rules in detail.

## Limited

Shadows over Innistrad leaned on creature types and graveyard themes more than strict gold signposts. The main draft lanes were:

- **Green-white and red-white: Humans.** A go-wide deck that wants to attack early and pump the team.
- **White-blue: Spirits and fliers.** Evasive Spirits with tricks like **Spectral Shepherd**.
- **Blue-black: Zombies and self-mill.** Fill your graveyard and reward it, with skulk creatures pecking in.
- **Black-red: Vampires and madness.** Discard outlets, madness spells and aggressive Vampires.
- **Red-green: Werewolves.** Cheap creatures that flip when the table stops casting spells.
- **Black-green: delirium.** Use self-mill and removal to hit four types in the graveyard for bonuses.
- **Blue-red and blue-green: spells and Clues.** Investigate and card flow, with value from instants and sorceries.

Removal at common included **Dead Weight**, **Puncturing Light** and **Sleep Paralysis**, and Clue makers like Thraben Inspector added card advantage to otherwise ordinary decks. Werewolves gave every game a decision about whether to cast a spell on your turn just to keep the other side from flipping. Our [Limited card evaluation guide](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains how to weigh cards like Clue makers.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Shadows over Innistrad arrived in a Standard full of Battle for Zendikar and Oath of the Gatewatch cards, and it mixed in fast. Before Pro Tour Shadows over Innistrad in Madrid, Bant Company and Mono-White Humans were the decks to beat. Thalia's Lieutenant powered the Humans decks, and Bant Company used Duskwatch Recruiter to find creatures. At the Pro Tour itself in April 2016, Steve Rubin won with Green-White Tokens after moving Archangel Avacyn into the main deck, beating Andrea Mengucci's Bant Company in the final.

From there, Archangel Avacyn became one of the format's defining cards and appeared in midrange decks of several colors through Kaladesh's first months. Declaration in Stone was a premium removal spell, Westvale Abbey gave tokens decks a game-ending land, and Sorin, Grim Nemesis anchored control. No Shadows over Innistrad cards were banned in Standard; the bans of that period hit cards from other sets, like Emrakul, the Promised End from Eldritch Moon. The set rotated out at the end of September 2017, when Ixalan arrived. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains how that works.

The set's lasting impact is bigger in older formats. In Modern, Thraben Inspector, Tireless Tracker, Thing in the Ice and Traverse the Ulvenwald all saw long-term play, Thalia's Lieutenant became a core card in Humans, and Prized Amalgam and Insolent Neonate fed graveyard decks like Dredge. In Pioneer, which begins with Return to Ravnica, Thalia's Lieutenant is a core card in Humans and Declaration in Stone is a playable white removal spell.

In Commander, Archangel Avacyn, Olivia, Mobilized for War, The Gitrog Monster, Tireless Tracker, Westvale Abbey and **Cryptolith Rite** are long-time favorites. If you're new to the format, our guide to [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains it. As of October 2026, Tireless Tracker and Thraben Inspector remain two of the most-played cards from the set.

## Cards from Shadows over Innistrad worth owning

- **Tireless Tracker**: a card-advantage engine for green decks in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Archangel Avacyn**: a flash finisher that protects your board.
- **Thraben Inspector**: a cheap, flexible creature in Modern and Pauper.
- **Thing in the Ice**: a spell-deck threat in Modern and Pioneer.
- **Thalia's Lieutenant**: a key piece of Humans decks.
- **Westvale Abbey**: a utility land for token and Commander decks.
- **The Gitrog Monster**: a popular commander for land-graveyard strategies.

Shadows over Innistrad rewards decks that turn small resources into value, whether that's Clues, a full graveyard or a discarded madness spell. If you're building with it today, Pioneer and Modern are where its creatures still compete, and you can test a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
