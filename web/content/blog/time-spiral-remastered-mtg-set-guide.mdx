Time Spiral Remastered is the Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in March 2021 that rebuilt the Time Spiral block of 2006 and 2007 as a single draft set. It collects the best of Time Spiral, Planar Chaos and Future Sight, including Tarmogoyf, Living End and Ancestral Vision, and adds one old-border "timeshifted" card from across Magic's history to every booster.

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | TSR |
| Release date | March 19, 2021 (paper and Magic Online; it never came to MTG Arena) |
| Plane | Dominaria |
| Set size | 289 main-set cards (121 commons, 100 uncommons, 53 rares, 15 mythics) plus a 121-card timeshifted bonus sheet |
| Product | Draft Boosters only, each with one timeshifted card; no Set or Collector Boosters |
| Headline mechanics | Suspend, split second, morph, flashback, madness, echo, shadow, storm, Slivers and other Time Spiral block returns |

## What the set was for

Time Spiral Remastered had two jobs. The first was to bring back one of the most loved draft formats in Magic's history. The original Time Spiral block was famously complex, full of callbacks to older sets, and many longtime players consider it the best draft environment ever made. A remastered version let a new generation try it with a single set rather than three.

Remastering also meant fixing the original's quirks. The original Time Spiral had a separate purple-rarity timeshifted sheet of old cards, Planar Chaos had color-shifted versions of classics, and Future Sight had futureshifted cards with a strange frame. Time Spiral Remastered folds the playable cards from all three sets into one 289-card set and replaces the original block's timeshifted reprints with a brand-new timeshifted sheet. The second job was reprints. The Time Spiral block is the start of what became the Modern card pool, and several of its cards (Tarmogoyf, Living End, Ancestral Vision, Damnation) had become scarce staples. Remastering the block put them back into circulation, with some upgraded from rare to mythic.

The cards also carry the block's story. Time Spiral is set on a ruined Dominaria torn by time rifts, where the planeswalker Teferi and his allies Jhoira, Radha and Venser try to seal the rifts before the plane collapses. Planar Chaos shows alternate versions of Dominaria bleeding through the rifts, and Future Sight shows glimpses of possible futures. You can read the full story in our [Time Spiral set guide](/blog/time-spiral-mtg-set-guide). The remastered set uses the same art and flavor, so cards like Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir and Venser, Shaper Savant still tell that story.

## The reprints that mattered

### The chase mythics

Fifteen cards were printed at mythic, including Tarmogoyf, Living End, Ancestral Vision, Damnation, Gemstone Caverns, Vesuva, Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir, Akroma's Memorial, Sliver Legion, Gauntlet of Power, Wheel of Fate, Restore Balance, Hypergenesis and Crovax, Ascendant Hero. Several of these were rares in their original printings, so the upgrade made them harder to open but kept the rare slot full of playables.

### The Constructed staples

Beyond the mythics, the set reprinted a long list of Modern and Legacy cards at rare and uncommon: Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth, Magus of the Moon, Pact of Negation, Summoner's Pact, Slaughter Pact, Lotus Bloom, Krosan Grip, Venser, Shaper Savant, Street Wraith and Rift Bolt among them. Combo players got Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens at common.

### The timeshifted bonus sheet

The bonus sheet was the set's surprise. Its 121 cards come from all over Magic's history and are printed in the pre-2003 card frame. They are not limited to the block: the sheet includes Path to Exile, Eternal Witness, Ponder, Solemn Simulacrum, Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker, Yawgmoth, Thran Physician, Restoration Angel, Dismember, Treasure Cruise, Mulldrifter, Abrupt Decay and dozens of Commander favorites. Each card keeps its normal legality, so Path to Exile is Modern-legal while the Ponder and Treasure Cruise printings are still banned there.

## Mechanics

Time Spiral Remastered keeps every mechanic from the original block. The most important ones:

- **Suspend:** pay a smaller cost to exile a card with time counters; when the last counter is removed during your upkeep, you cast it for free. Rift Bolt can be suspended for `{R}` to deal 3 damage next turn. Some cards, like Living End and Ancestral Vision, have no mana cost and can only be suspended.
- **Split second:** while the spell is on the stack, nobody can cast spells or activate non-mana abilities. Krosan Grip and Sudden Shock resolve before the opponent can respond.
- **Morph:** cast a card face down as a 2/2 for `{3}` and turn it face up later for its morph cost. Timebender flips for `{U}` and adds or removes time counters.
- **Vanishing and echo:** creatures that leave on a timer or demand an extra payment, such as Calciderm and Mogg War Marshal.
- **Flashback, madness, buyback, shadow, flanking and storm:** returning from earlier blocks on cards like Strangling Soot, Nightshade Assassin, Spell Burst, Looter il-Kor, Knight of the Holy Nimbus and Grapeshot.
- **Slivers:** creatures that share their abilities with every other Sliver, like Sinew Sliver, Two-Headed Sliver and Gemhide Sliver.

## Limited

Time Spiral Remastered is a slow, complex format that rewards experience. Many cards are designed to be suspended early and arrive later, and morph means you often can't tell what an opponent's 2/2 really is. Games tend to be decided by card advantage and timing rather than early aggression.

The main draft themes include:

- **Slivers:** spread across all five colors, so Sliver drafters often play three colors, with commons like Sinew Sliver and Two-Headed Sliver, and Gemhide Sliver helping the mana.
- **Suspend and time counters:** blue-red decks that suspend cheap threats and use Jhoira's Timebug, Timebender and Shivan Sand-Mage to speed up the clock.
- **Saprolings:** green Fungus creatures like Thallid Germinator and Sporesower Thallid that slowly build token armies.
- **Shadow and evasion:** blue and black shadow creatures like Looter il-Kor and Nether Traitor, plus white flanking knights, for evasive aggressive decks.
- **Madness and flashback:** black-red decks that discard for value and replay spells from the graveyard.

Removal is plentiful at common (Strangling Soot, Dead // Gone, Lightning Axe at uncommon), and the timeshifted slot means every pack has a card that can be anything from a Commander staple to a bomb. If you're coming from faster modern formats, read up on [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) and expect longer games.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

As a reprint set, Time Spiral Remastered created no new decks. It mattered by making existing staples easier to find for Modern, Legacy, Pauper and Commander. Tarmogoyf and Ancestral Vision were long-established Modern cards, and Living End remained a Modern deck built around cascade and cycling creatures; Street Wraith, reprinted here, is one of its cycling pieces.

A handful of cards in the set are banned in some formats as of October 2026: Dread Return, Simian Spirit Guide and Hypergenesis are on the Modern ban list, and Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens are banned in Pauper. Among the timeshifted cards, Ponder, Treasure Cruise, Mystic Sanctuary and Ancient Den are banned in Modern. For how bans work across formats, see our [ban list guide](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

Pauper players benefited as well. Rift Bolt, Mogg War Marshal, Momentary Blink, Looter il-Kor and Strangling Soot are all commons here and remain Pauper-legal as of October 2026, and the set gave many players their first chance to pick them up in a fresh printing.

The timeshifted sheet also made the set popular with Commander players, who could open Eternal Witness, Beast Within, Farseek, Vandalblast and other format staples in an old-school frame.

## Cards from Time Spiral Remastered worth owning

- **Tarmogoyf:** a historic Modern creature that still sees play in Legacy.
- **Living End:** the centerpiece of a long-running Modern combo deck.
- **Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth and Vesuva:** Commander land favorites.
- **Damnation:** a premium board wipe for black Commander decks.
- **Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir:** shuts down instant-speed interaction from opponents.
- **Path to Exile (timeshifted):** a removal spell played in every format it's legal in.
- **Krosan Grip and Pongify:** efficient answers for Modern and Commander.
- **Magus of the Moon:** a Blood Moon effect on a body.
