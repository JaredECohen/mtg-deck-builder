Fifth Dawn is the third and final set of the Mirrodin block in Magic: The Gathering, released in June 2004. It finished the story of the metal plane Mirrodin with the rise of its fifth sun, and it debuted scry, one of the most-used mechanics in the game's history, alongside sunburst. For a 165-card small set it's astonishingly deep: Eternal Witness, Serum Visions, Magma Jet, Engineered Explosives, Crucible of Worlds, Vedalken Shackles and Cranial Plating all came from here.

## The story

Mirrodin is an artificial world: a metal sphere built by the silver golem planeswalker Karn, originally named Argentum. When Karn left, he gave its stewardship to a golem warden and the Mirari, an artifact of great power. That warden became Memnarch, who renamed the world, filled it with beings abducted from other planes, and slowly went mad. A mysterious oil had also begun seeping into the plane, a detail that would matter a great deal years later.

The block follows Glissa Sunseeker, an elf from the Tangle forest whose family was killed by Memnarch's servants, along with her goblin friend Slobad and the golem Bosh. Over Mirrodin and Darksteel they uncover that Memnarch is behind the plane's troubles, and they assemble the legendary Kaldra equipment, the sword, shield and helm whose union summons a powerful avatar.

Fifth Dawn, told in the novel The Fifth Dawn by Cory Herndon, is the confrontation. The Kaldra avatar was supposed to save the world, but Memnarch seizes control of it and turns it on Glissa and her friends. It chases Glissa into the Tangle, wrecking everything in its path, until she reaches the Radix at the forest's heart. There, in rage and despair, her body calls a column of green mana up from Mirrodin's core. It destroys the avatar and hangs in the sky as a glowing green sphere: the fifth sun, joining the white, blue, black and red ones. The world now has light of all five colors, which is the story reason behind the set's five-color theme.

Glissa and Slobad also come face to face with Karn himself, Mirrodin's creator, and the block ends with Memnarch's grip on the world broken. The oil, however, was Phyrexian, and it's the seed of the story Wizards returned to in Scars of Mirrodin in 2010.

## Mechanics

### Scry

Scry debuted in Fifth Dawn. To scry 2, look at the top two cards of your library, then put any number of them on the bottom and the rest back on top in any order. In this set, scry was always scry 2 and attached to instants and sorceries. It was later templated as "scry N" and became an evergreen mechanic.

Serum Visions (draw a card, then scry 2, for one blue mana) and Magma Jet (2 damage, then scry 2) are the classics. Condescend, Stand Firm and Ferocious Charge put scry on counterspells and combat tricks.

### Sunburst

An artifact with sunburst enters with a counter for each color of mana spent to cast it: +1/+1 counters if it's a creature, charge counters otherwise. Skyreach Manta is a five-mana flier that's a 1/1 if you pay with one color and a 5/5 if you pay with all five. Pentad Prism stores colored mana, and Engineered Explosives uses its charge counters to choose which mana values it destroys.

### Five colors and the Bringers

The set encourages playing many colors. The Bringers, like Bringer of the Red Dawn, are huge creatures you can cast for one mana of each color instead of their normal cost. Fist of Suns lets you pay `{W}{U}{B}{R}{G}` instead of any spell's mana cost. The Beacons, like Beacon of Destruction and Beacon of Unrest, shuffle themselves back into your library after use.

### The Great Machine

Fifth Dawn also had a "machine" theme. Its four Stations (Blasting Station, Grinding Station, Salvaging Station and Summoning Station) untap each other in loops and were designed so that combinations of them can make infinite creatures, damage or mill. The art of all four forms a single mural.

## Limited

Once Fifth Dawn released, block drafts went Mirrodin, then Darksteel, then Fifth Dawn, following that era's oldest-set-first convention. Because Fifth Dawn pushed five-color play, the format rewarded mana fixing more than either earlier set. Commons like Pentad Prism, Sylvok Explorer and Wayfarer's Bauble let decks pick up a third color or splash, and sunburst cards like Skyreach Manta got much better with every extra color.

Removal and equipment carried over from the earlier sets. Fifth Dawn added Magma Jet, Beacon of Destruction at rare, and equipment such as Opaline Bracers, while scry helped every deck smooth its draws. Artifact synergies still mattered (affinity, artifact creatures and equipment were everywhere in the block), but the set shifted the format away from mono-artifact decks and toward colorful midrange. It wasn't the fastest Limited format; games often went long enough for sunburst and Bringers to come online.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Fifth Dawn arrived in a Standard dominated by Affinity, the Mirrodin artifact deck built on artifact lands, Arcbound Ravager, Disciple of the Vault and Frogmite. Cranial Plating made it even more explosive: any artifact creature could suddenly hit for a huge amount, and Plating's `{B}{B}` attach ability let it jump to an unblocked creature at instant speed.

- **Skullclamp** (from Darksteel) was banned in Standard in June 2004, the same month Fifth Dawn released.
- **Affinity** kept dominating through the 2004-05 season. In March 2005, Wizards banned eight cards in Standard to end it: Arcbound Ravager, Disciple of the Vault, Darksteel Citadel and the five artifact lands (Ancient Den, Seat of the Synod, Vault of Whispers, Great Furnace and Tree of Tales). Cranial Plating itself wasn't banned in Standard.
- **Fifth Dawn's own cards** fed many other decks. Eternal Witness became a staple of green ramp and midrange decks, Magma Jet went into red decks, and Vedalken Shackles anchored blue control.

The set rotated out of Standard with the rest of Mirrodin block in October 2006.

Its legacy today is enormous. As of October 2026:

- **Modern.** Cranial Plating is still a key piece of Affinity. Serum Visions and Magma Jet are long-time staples, Engineered Explosives is a flexible sideboard answer, and Vedalken Shackles shows up in mono-blue decks. Krark-Clan Ironworks has been banned in Modern since January 2019, after a hard-to-interact-with loop combo built on it. See our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).
- **Legacy and Vintage.** Crucible of Worlds pairs with Wasteland and Strip Mine to lock opponents out, and Engineered Explosives and Vedalken Shackles appear in blue and prison decks.
- **Pauper.** Cranial Plating was banned in Pauper in 2009, when Pauper debuted as an official Magic Online format. Trinket Mage, Night's Whisper, Serum Visions, Thought Courier and Wayfarer's Bauble all see play there; our [Pauper guide](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg) has more.
- **Commander.** Eternal Witness is one of the most-played green cards in the format, and Doubling Cube, Staff of Domination, Crucible of Worlds, Wayfarer's Bauble and Relentless Rats all have dedicated fans. Our [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains why recursion like Witness is so valued there.

None of Fifth Dawn's cards is on the Reserved List, which only covers sets through Urza's Destiny, so its staples have been reprinted.

## Cards from Fifth Dawn worth owning

- **Eternal Witness**: the classic green recursion creature for Commander.
- **Serum Visions**: a one-mana cantrip for Modern and Pauper.
- **Engineered Explosives**: a flexible answer to tokens and cheap permanents.
- **Crucible of Worlds**: land recursion for Legacy prison and Commander.
- **Cranial Plating**: the Modern Affinity finisher.
- **Magma Jet**: burn plus card selection.
- **Vedalken Shackles**: repeatable creature theft for blue decks.

## The takeaway

Fifth Dawn finished the Mirrodin story with a satisfying climax and quietly delivered one of the best-value small sets ever printed. Scry alone changed how cards are designed, and staples like Eternal Witness and Serum Visions still show up everywhere. To build with it in Modern, an artifact aggro deck around Cranial Plating is the most direct route.
