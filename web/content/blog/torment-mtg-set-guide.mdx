Torment is the second set of Magic: The Gathering's Odyssey block, released in February 2002. It is the game's "black set": black has far more cards than any other color, and the whole set leans into nightmares, madness and the criminal Cabal of the continent of Otaria. It introduced the madness mechanic and printed cards that are still busy decades later, including Grim Lavamancer, Cabal Coffers, Chainer's Edict, Deep Analysis and Ichorid.

## The story

The Odyssey block takes place on Dominaria about a hundred years after the Phyrexian Invasion, on the continent of Otaria. The block's story revolves around the Mirari, a mysterious silver sphere that grants its holder's deepest wishes, and warps them in the process.

Torment's story is told in Scott McGough's novel *Chainer's Torment*, which fills in the backstory of a character from Odyssey and then pushes the plot forward. Chainer is a young member of the Cabal, the crime syndicate that runs Cabal City and its famous pit fights. He was the one who found the Mirari in the first place and handed it to the Cabal's leader, known as the First, who made it the prize of a great pit-fighting tournament. Along the way Chainer became friends with Kamahl, the barbarian hero of the block.

As Torment unfolds, Chainer becomes a dementia summoner, a mage who pulls nightmare creatures out of minds, and the Mirari's influence drives him further into madness. He turns on the First, who survives and is driven into exile. Chainer's nightmares spill out over Cabal City. Meanwhile, the merfolk ambassador Laquatus schemes to take over the Mer Empire, but its rightful empress, Llawan, returns and thwarts him. The story's climax is a final pit fight between Chainer, now fused with his nightmares, and Kamahl. Chainer dies, but not before asking his old friend to keep the Mirari safe. The block concludes in Judgment.

That story explains the set's look: Chainer, Dementia Master turns dead creatures into Nightmares, and a whole family of "Nightmare Horror" creatures (Mesmeric Fiend, Faceless Butcher, Laquatus's Champion, Hypnox) take something away when they arrive and give it back when they leave.

## Mechanics

### Madness

Madness was Torment's new mechanic. When you discard a card with madness, you can exile it instead of putting it in your graveyard, and then cast it for its madness cost. That cost is usually much cheaper than its normal cost. Basking Rootwalla has a madness cost of zero, Arrogant Wurm (a 4/4 trampler for five) costs just three, Fiery Temper deals 3 damage for one red mana and Circular Logic becomes a one-mana counterspell.

Madness only works with discard outlets, so it pushed players toward cards like Wild Mongrel and Aquamoeba that let you discard at will. Mixed together, they made a deck that played cards at instant speed, out of nowhere, for very little mana.

### Threshold and flashback

Both came back from Odyssey. Threshold turns on once you have seven or more cards in your graveyard. Cabal Ritual adds three black mana normally and five with threshold, and the rare Possessed cycle (like Possessed Aven and Possessed Barbarian) get bigger, turn black and gain the ability to destroy creatures of their original color. Flashback lets you cast a card a second time from your graveyard: Chainer's Edict makes a player sacrifice a creature, and later you can pay seven mana to do it again. Deep Analysis draws two cards, then can be flashed back for two mana and 3 life.

### Nightmares and tainted lands

The "leave the battlefield" Nightmares were a black theme: Faceless Butcher exiles a creature until the Butcher leaves, and Mesmeric Fiend does the same with a card in the opponent's hand. The tainted lands (Tainted Field, Tainted Isle, Tainted Peak and Tainted Wood) tap for colorless or for black plus another color, but only if you control a Swamp.

## Limited

Torment was drafted after Odyssey, with one pack of Torment and two of Odyssey. The set's lopsided color split (about 40 black cards against roughly 21 each in white and green) changed the draft: black cards were everywhere in Torment packs, so pack two pushed many drafters into black whether they planned it or not, while white and green got thin.

What mattered at common and uncommon:

- **Black removal and value**: Chainer's Edict, Crippling Fatigue (which has flashback), Faceless Butcher and Waste Away gave black more removal than any other color.
- **Madness**: Aquamoeba and other discard outlets enabled madness cards like Basking Rootwalla and Fiery Temper, and blue-green or black-red madness decks could be very fast.
- **Threshold**: the graveyard filled naturally with flashback and discard, so threshold rewards like Krosan Restorer came online reasonably often.

The format wasn't built around ten scripted two-color archetypes like modern sets, and it is remembered mostly for how heavily black it was. If you are new to drafting, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains why the removal-rich color is so often the one to be in.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard and Block, 2002-2003

Torment's biggest Constructed contribution was **U/G Madness**. The deck paired blue and green discard outlets, like Wild Mongrel from Odyssey, with Torment's madness cards: Basking Rootwalla, Arrogant Wurm and Circular Logic. Add card draw like Deep Analysis and Odyssey's Careful Study, Wonder from Judgment to give the team flying, and Roar of the Wurm, and you had a deck that could dump its hand on the table for almost no mana. It was one of the top decks of Odyssey Block Constructed and also a major Standard archetype through 2002.

On the other side was **Mono-Black Control**. Chainer's Edict, Mutilate, Laquatus's Champion and the mana from Cabal Coffers gave black a dedicated control deck, with Nantuko Shade as an efficient threat. In Odyssey Block Constructed, U/G Madness and Mono-Black Control were the two decks to beat for a time. Torment left Standard in October 2003, when Mirrodin arrived.

### Legacy, Vintage and Pauper

Torment has a long tail in Eternal formats:

- **Grim Lavamancer** is a classic Legacy card for red decks, since fetchlands and cheap spells keep the graveyard stocked to fuel its 2-damage activations.
- **Ichorid** gave its name to a Legacy and Vintage Dredge deck that fills its graveyard and returns Ichorid each upkeep.
- **Cephalid Illusionist** was half of an old Legacy combo with Shuko or Nomads en-Kor: targeting it repeatedly mills your whole library.
- **Chainer's Edict**, **Deep Analysis**, **Mesmeric Fiend**, **Faceless Butcher** and **Circular Logic** are all legal in Pauper, and Chainer's Edict in particular has been a staple of black Pauper decks.

### Commander and Modern

In Commander, Cabal Coffers is a must-have for mono-black decks, and Chainer, Dementia Master is a fun reanimation commander. Torment itself predates Modern, so only cards reprinted later in Modern-legal sets, like Grim Lavamancer, Cabal Coffers, Deep Analysis, Faceless Butcher and Mutilate, can be played there. As of October 2026, no Torment card is banned in Legacy, Vintage or Commander. Our [formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) guide explains how each format's card pool is defined.

## Cards from Torment worth owning

- **Grim Lavamancer**: a one-drop that's still strong in Legacy and Modern red decks.
- **Cabal Coffers**: the best mono-black mana land in Commander.
- **Chainer's Edict**: a cheap removal spell for Pauper and Legacy.
- **Deep Analysis**: card advantage for blue Pauper and Legacy decks.
- **Ichorid**: the namesake of a Dredge deck in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Basking Rootwalla** and **Arrogant Wurm**: the core of any madness deck.

## The takeaway

Torment is a strange set, deliberately unbalanced toward black, but it gave the game madness, one of its most loved mechanics, and a group of cards that outlived their era. If you want to play its best ideas today, Legacy is the right home: a red deck with Grim Lavamancer or a madness deck with Basking Rootwalla both work there.
