A typical Commander deck wants around 8 to 10 targeted removal spells and 2 to 4 board wipes, with a preference for flexible, cheap, instant-speed answers. Three opponents means three times as many threats, so a deck that can't answer problem permanents will eventually lose to one of them. The skill is in choosing answers that hit many card types, and in timing your board wipes so they help you more than anyone else.

This guide covers how much interaction to run, the difference between spot and mass removal, when to cast a board wipe, and what each color does best. Deciding which opponent's permanent deserves your removal is a separate question, and the [Commander threat assessment guide](/blog/commander-threat-assessment) covers it.

## How much removal to run

Start here and adjust:

| Category | Rough count | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Targeted removal | 8-10 | Answers one specific problem permanent |
| Board wipes | 2-4 | Resets the board when you're behind |
| Counterspells (blue) | Some can replace removal | Stops threats before they land |

These numbers match the starting template in the [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck). A few adjustments:

- **Control decks** run more of both, and lean on board wipes as a core part of the plan.
- **Creature-heavy decks** like tokens or tribal run fewer board wipes, or only ones that spare their own board, since symmetrical wipes hurt them most.
- **Decks with a vulnerable commander** run more answers and protection, since losing the commander repeatedly slows the whole deck down.

Count cards that answer threats as a side effect. A creature that destroys an artifact when it enters is removal and a body. These double-duty cards free up slots for the rest of your plan.

## Spot removal vs mass removal

### Spot removal

Spot removal (also called targeted removal) deals with one permanent. It's precise: you answer exactly the card that's causing trouble and leave everything else alone. Most of your removal should be spot removal, because most problems are one card: a combo piece, a draw engine, a commander.

Not all spot removal is equal. The best answers in Commander tend to have three qualities.

**Flexibility.** Removal that hits any nonland permanent answers creatures, artifacts, enchantments and planeswalkers. **Beast Within** and **Generous Gift** destroy any permanent, at the cost of giving its controller a 3/3 token. That drawback matters much less in a 40-life multiplayer game than in one-on-one Magic.

**Efficiency.** Cheap removal lets you answer a threat and still develop your own board. **Swords to Plowshares** exiles a creature for one mana, and its life-gain drawback barely matters at 40 life.

**Instant speed.** In multiplayer, waiting is valuable. Instant-speed removal lets you see which threats the other players answer first and use yours only on what's left. It also lets you respond to a combo or an attack at the last moment.

### Exile vs destroy

Removal that exiles is generally stronger than removal that destroys. Exile gets around indestructible, and it stops graveyard recursion and death triggers. Commanders can return to the command zone when they would be exiled or go to the graveyard, so exiling a commander doesn't remove it from the game, but the commander tax still goes up each time it's cast from the command zone.

Some removal returns the permanent to its owner's hand or library instead. That's temporary, but it can be enough to stop a combo or reset an opponent's mana.

### Mass removal

A board wipe (or sweeper) removes many permanents at once. **Wrath of God** destroys all creatures. **Blasphemous Act** deals 13 damage to each creature and gets cheaper for each creature on the battlefield, so in Commander it often costs very little. **Toxic Deluge** gives all creatures -X/-X, where X is the amount of life you pay.

Board wipes are your way back from falling behind. One wipe can trade for several of an opponent's cards, which makes it one of the strongest sources of card advantage in the game. But they're symmetrical by default, so they hit your board too.

Some wipes are one-sided or flexible:

- **Cyclonic Rift** can be cast normally to return one nonland permanent you don't control to its owner's hand, or overloaded for a much higher cost to return each nonland permanent you don't control.
- **Vandalblast** works the same way for artifacts: one artifact you don't control normally, or all of them overloaded.
- **Austere Command** lets you pick two of four modes, such as destroying all creatures with mana value 3 or less and all enchantments.
- **Merciless Eviction** exiles every permanent of one type you choose: artifacts, creatures, enchantments or planeswalkers.

Flexible wipes let you pick what to hit, which means you can often keep your own key permanents while clearing the threats.

## When to cast a board wipe

Timing matters more for board wipes than for any other removal.

**When you're behind.** The classic use. If one or more opponents have built a board you can't handle, a wipe resets the game. Your opponents lose more than you do.

**After the table overcommits.** The longer you wait, the more permanents a wipe takes out. If you can survive another turn safely, let your opponents commit more cards first.

**Before your own big turn.** If your hand is full of creatures and the board is cluttered, wiping first and rebuilding next turn puts you ahead of everyone who just lost their board.

**Not when you're ahead.** If you have the best board, a symmetrical wipe throws your lead away. Hold it until you need it.

**Not just to stop one creature.** If one card is the problem, spot removal is usually better. Spending a wipe on one threat also costs the other players their boards, which tends to make enemies.

### Building around your own wipes

Decks that run several wipes can be built to survive them:

- **Planeswalkers and enchantments** survive creature wipes.
- **Indestructible** creatures survive destroy-based wipes.
- **Holding creatures in hand** lets you rebuild right after.
- **Card draw** refills your hand after the wipe. The [Commander card draw guide](/blog/commander-card-draw-guide) covers good ways to rebuild.

## Removal by color

Each color answers problems in its own way, and each has weaknesses. The [color pie guide](/blog/mtg-color-pie-guide) explains why.

### White

White has the best and most flexible removal.

- **Swords to Plowshares** and **Path to Exile** exile a creature for one mana.
- **Generous Gift** destroys any permanent.
- **Oblivion Ring** exiles any nonland permanent while it stays on the battlefield. If the Ring leaves, the card comes back.
- **Wrath of God** and **Day of Judgment** destroy all creatures.

White can also answer artifacts and enchantments well, and it has a deep selection of board wipes.

### Blue

Blue answers threats before they resolve, or bounces them.

- **Counterspell** counters any spell. Counterspells work as removal for things you can't otherwise answer, but they need open mana on opponents' turns.
- **Cyclonic Rift** is single-target bounce or a one-sided board reset.
- **Pongify** and **Rapid Hybridization** destroy a creature and give its controller a 3/3 token.
- **Evacuation** returns all creatures to their owners' hands.

Blue struggles to permanently remove things once they're on the battlefield.

### Black

Black kills creatures better than any other color.

- **Doom Blade** destroys a nonblack creature. **Go for the Throat** destroys a nonartifact creature.
- **Toxic Deluge** and **Damnation** wipe the board.

Black's weakness is artifacts and enchantments. Pair it with another color or use colorless answers to cover them.

### Red

Red answers creatures with damage and artifacts with destruction.

- **Chaos Warp** shuffles any permanent into its owner's library, then its owner reveals the top card of that library and puts it onto the battlefield if it's a permanent card. That small risk is the price of answering anything.
- **Vandalblast** destroys artifacts.
- **Blasphemous Act** wipes the board.

Red has trouble answering enchantments, and damage-based removal struggles against large creatures.

### Green

Green is strong against artifacts and enchantments, and weak against creatures.

- **Beast Within** destroys any permanent.
- **Krosan Grip** destroys an artifact or enchantment and has split second, so while it's on the stack, players can't cast spells or activate abilities other than mana abilities in response.
- **Nature's Claim** destroys an artifact or enchantment for one mana.
- **Reclamation Sage** destroys an artifact or enchantment when it enters.

For creatures, green uses fight effects and its large creatures. It has very few board wipes.

### Multicolor

Two-color removal is often the most flexible. **Anguished Unmaking** exiles any nonland permanent at instant speed. **Vindicate** destroys any permanent. **Assassin's Trophy** destroys any permanent an opponent controls.

## Don't forget the graveyard

Some threats live in the graveyard, like reanimation targets and cards with flashback. A little graveyard hate goes a long way. **Bojuka Bog** is a land that exiles a player's graveyard when it enters, so it costs you almost nothing to include.

## The takeaway

Run around 8 to 10 targeted answers and 2 to 4 board wipes, and favor cheap, flexible, instant-speed removal that hits many permanent types. Use spot removal on single problems and save board wipes for when you're behind or after the table overcommits. Play to your colors' strengths, cover their weaknesses, and build your deck to survive its own wipes.
