Tempest is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 1997 that sent the crew of the skyship Weatherlight to the stormy plane of Rath. It introduced Slivers, shadow and buyback, and its card pool includes some of the most important cards in Legacy and Commander today, among them Wasteland, Ancient Tomb, Lotus Petal and Reanimate.

## The story

Tempest is the second chapter of the **Weatherlight Saga**, the multi-year storyline that ran until Apocalypse. The Weatherlight's captain, **Sisay**, has been kidnapped and taken to **Rath**, an artificial plane with a sky full of storms and ground made of a shifting liquid metal called flowstone. Rath is ruled by the **Evincar Volrath** from his Stronghold, and it's secretly a staging ground for Phyrexia's planned invasion of Dominaria. The story was told mainly in short fiction later collected in the anthology *Rath and Storm*.

The ship's crew, led by **Gerrard Capashen**, plane-shifts to Rath to rescue her. They include the minotaur Tahngarth, the cat warrior Mirri, the healer Orim, the navigator Hanna, the young wizard Ertai, the cursed nobleman Crovax, the goblin Squee and the silver golem Karn. They're ambushed almost immediately by the Predator, a ship commanded by Volrath's brutal lieutenant **Greven il-Vec**. The Weatherlight crashes into the Skyshroud Forest, Gerrard falls overboard, and Greven captures Karn and Tahngarth.

Gerrard is rescued by the Skyshroud elves, whose leader **Eladamri** and the blind Oracle en-Vec believe he is a prophesied hero. The crew, with an uneasy ally, the defector **Starke**, sets out to storm the Stronghold. Along the way they fight Slivers in the Furnace of Rath, and Crovax faces the dark angel **Selenia**, a guardian he once knew. When he strikes her down, her death completes an ancient curse and he begins turning into a vampire.

The full rescue plays out over the rest of the block. In Stronghold and Exodus the crew frees Karn, Tahngarth and Sisay, Mirri and Selenia die, and Gerrard learns that Volrath is his adoptive brother Vuel. The Weatherlight escapes Rath, leaving Crovax behind, and the ship eventually lands on Mercadia, setting up the next block. That path leads straight to the Phyrexian invasion told in Invasion.

## Mechanics

### Shadow (new)

A creature with shadow can only block or be blocked by other creatures with shadow. In practice it's nearly unblockable, but it can't block your opponent's normal creatures either. Shadow appears on white Soltari, black Dauthi and blue Thalakos creatures. **Soltari Priest** (uncommon) is a two-mana 2/1 shadow creature with protection from red, and **Dauthi Slayer** (common) is a 2/2 that must attack each turn.

### Buyback (new)

Buyback is an optional extra cost. If you pay it, the spell returns to your hand as it resolves, so you can cast it again and again. **Capsize** (common) bounces a permanent for three mana, but with its `{3}` buyback it becomes a repeatable lock that control decks used to bounce something every turn.

### Slivers (new)

Slivers are a creature type where each Sliver gives its ability to every Sliver on the battlefield, yours and your opponent's. **Muscle Sliver** (common) gives all Slivers +1/+1, and **Winged Sliver** (common) gives them flying, so a board of Slivers gets stronger with each new one. Slivers became one of Magic's most beloved tribes, and they've returned many times since.

### Licids and flowstone

Licids are creatures that can pay a cost to become an Aura attached to another creature, such as **Stinging Licid** (uncommon). Flowstone is a red flavor theme for creatures that change their power and toughness. Tempest also had the first Spike, **Spike Drone**, a preview of the +1/+1 counter Spikes that arrived in Stronghold.

## Limited

Tempest arrived as Booster Draft was becoming the standard way to play Limited, and Tempest block, drafted in packs of Tempest, Stronghold and Exodus, is remembered fondly by players of the era. Sets were not designed around named two-color archetypes then, so the format was mainly about card quality and a few strong themes rather than signposted pairs.

What worked:

- **Shadow aggro.** White and black shadow creatures were hard to block, so decks that stacked Soltari and Dauthi creatures could win races quickly.
- **Removal.** Black and red had strong commons and uncommons like **Diabolic Edict**, **Kindle** and **Rolling Thunder**, which could kill several creatures at once.
- **Slivers.** Many Slivers were common, so a Sliver-heavy deck could happen naturally, though they also helped an opponent with Slivers of their own.

Games were usually decided by evasion and removal, and fundamentals like a sensible [mana curve](/blog/mana-curve-explained) mattered more than set-specific synergies.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Type II (1997-1999)

Tempest spent two years in Type II alongside Mirage, Visions, Fifth Edition, Weatherlight, the rest of its block and later the Urza block. Its cheap red creatures, like **Jackal Pup** and **Mogg Fanatic**, and **Cursed Scroll** fueled the red aggro decks of the time, such as Sligh and Deadguy Red. **Capsize** and counterspells powered blue control. Black aggro (Suicide Black) used Dauthi shadow creatures, and **Living Death** had its own graveyard deck. Slivers even had a Counter Sliver deck.

Tempest also played a part in Combo Winter, the Urza's Saga era when free spells and powerful engines broke Standard. In March 1999, Wizards banned a group of cards in Standard that included Tempest's **Earthcraft** and **Lotus Petal**, effective April 1, 1999. **Cursed Scroll** was banned in Tempest Block Constructed in July 1998. Tempest left Standard when Mercadian Masques arrived in late 1999. Our [formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) explains how Standard (Type II) relates to today's formats.

### Legacy and Vintage

Tempest's long-term impact is in eternal formats:

- **Wasteland** is one of Legacy's defining cards. It punishes greedy mana bases and keeps decks honest.
- **Ancient Tomb** is fast mana in Legacy and Vintage, especially for colorless and Sneak and Show decks.
- **Lotus Petal** has been restricted in Vintage since 1999 and is played in Legacy combo.
- **Reanimate** is a core piece of Legacy Reanimator, often paired with Entomb.
- **Aluren** has its own Legacy combo deck.
- **Earthcraft** is banned in Legacy.

Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains how these decks fit together.

### Commander and the Reserved List

In Commander, Tempest provides **Ancient Tomb**, **Scroll Rack**, **Living Death**, **Goblin Bombardment**, **Time Warp**, **Earthcraft** and **Aluren**, plus classic legends like **Eladamri, Lord of Leaves**. If you're new to the format, start with [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh). Several of these cards are game-changing combo pieces, so check the power level your table agrees on.

Tempest is also one of the sets most affected by the Reserved List, Wizards' list of older cards it has promised not to reprint. Earthcraft, Humility, Intuition, Aluren, Cursed Scroll, Corpse Dance, Recycle, Meditate and other rares are on it, so the only copies are from Tempest itself and a few other old printings. As of October 2026 the list still stands.

## Cards from Tempest worth owning

- **Wasteland**: a Legacy staple and a strong Commander utility land.
- **Ancient Tomb**: fast mana for Legacy, Vintage and Commander.
- **Reanimate**: the cheapest way to bring back a giant creature in Legacy.
- **Living Death**: a powerful graveyard reset for Commander and Legacy.
- **Scroll Rack**: a Commander favorite for manipulating your library.
- **Goblin Bombardment**: a top sacrifice outlet.
- **Humility**: a famous, rules-twisting prison card.

Tempest gave Magic Slivers, shadow and buyback, and its best cards define Legacy decks nearly three decades later. If you want to build with them, Legacy is the format where Tempest's power is most visible, and you can test a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
