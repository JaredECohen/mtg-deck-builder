Warhammer 40,000 Commander is the Magic: The Gathering Universes Beyond product released in October 2022 that turned Games Workshop's grim sci-fi war setting into four preconstructed Commander decks. Each deck represents one of the setting's great factions (the Imperium, Chaos, the Necrons and the Tyranids), and together they brought around 170 new cards, new mechanics like squad and ravenous, and some of the most popular commanders of the year.

## The story

Warhammer 40,000 is set in the 41st millennium, a future where humanity's Imperium spans a million worlds and is at war with almost everything else in the galaxy. The product does not adapt a single novel or campaign. Instead, each deck captures a faction, and the card names, art and flavor text are drawn from decades of the setting's lore.

**The Imperium of Man** is ruled in name by the Emperor, who sits motionless on the Golden Throne on Terra (the card The Golden Throne keeps you alive when you would lose the game). Its defenders appear across the white, blue and black deck: the Space Marines of the Ultramarines chapter under Marneus Calgar, the Inquisition through Inquisitor Greyfax and Inquisitor Eisenhorn, the Adepta Sororitas led by Celestine, the Living Saint, and the tech-priests of the Adeptus Mechanicus represented by Belisarius Cawl.

**Chaos** is the corrupting power of four dark gods. Abaddon the Despoiler, warmaster of the traitor Space Marines, leads the deck, with daemon princes and primarchs like Magnus the Red, Mortarion and Be'lakor, and champions of each god such as Khârn the Betrayer (Khorne) and Lucius the Eternal (Slaanesh). Demons like the Bloodthirster and Lord of Change fill out the ranks.

**The Necrons** are an ancient race whose souls were sealed inside living-metal bodies and who are now waking from millions of years of sleep in their tomb worlds. Szarekh, the Silent King, leads them, with Imotekh the Stormlord, Trazyn the Infinite and the C'tan star-god represented by Shard of the Nightbringer.

**The Tyranids** are an extragalactic swarm that consumes whole planets. The Swarmlord commands them, with creatures like Old One Eye and the Tervigon, while Genestealer Cult characters such as Magus Lucea Kane and Ghyrson Starn show their infiltration of human worlds.

There is no ending to resolve. Warhammer 40,000 is built on an eternal war, and the product leaves every faction locked in it.

## The decks

### Forces of the Imperium (white-blue-black)

The face commander is **Inquisitor Greyfax**, whose other creatures get +1/+0 and vigilance and who can tap a creature and investigate. The alternate commander is **Marneus Calgar**, a double strike general who draws a card whenever tokens enter under your control. The deck is a go-wide army of Astartes Warrior tokens, squad creatures and Vehicles like Thunderhawk Gunship.

### The Ruinous Powers (blue-black-red)

**Abaddon the Despoiler** gives cascade to spells you cast from your hand with mana value up to the life your opponents lost this turn, so the deck wants to hit first and then cast big spells. The alternate commander, **Be'lakor, the Dark Master**, draws cards for each Demon you control and turns every later Demon into a damage trigger. The deck is full of cascade and expensive demons.

### Necron Dynasties (mono-black)

**Szarekh, the Silent King** is a flier that mills three cards on each attack and returns an artifact creature or Vehicle to your hand. The alternate commander is **Imotekh the Stormlord**, who makes two Necron Warrior tokens whenever artifact cards leave your graveyard. It is an artifact graveyard deck with unearth creatures and recursion like Living Death.

### Tyranid Swarm (green-blue-red)

**The Swarmlord** draws a card whenever a creature you control with a counter on it dies and grows each time you recast it. The alternate commander, **Magus Lucea Kane**, adds counters each combat and copies your X spells and abilities. Ravenous creatures, which all have X costs, are the backbone of the deck.

## Mechanics

### Squad

Squad is an optional additional cost you can pay any number of times; for each payment, you create a token copy of the creature when it enters. Ultramarines Honour Guard has squad for `{2}` and gives your other creatures +1/+1, so each extra copy also boosts the rest of your board. It turns any creature into a scalable army in the late game.

### Ravenous

A ravenous creature has an X in its cost, enters with X +1/+1 counters, and draws you a card if X was 5 or more. Tervigon and Tyrant Guard are examples. Ravenous rewards ramp and combines with the Tyranid deck's counter synergies and with Magus Lucea Kane, who copies X spells.

### Returning mechanics and named abilities

Cascade returns heavily in the Chaos deck, unearth in the Necron deck (Triarch Praetorian), and miracle appears on a few cards. Most new cards also carry Warhammer-flavored ability names, such as "Mark of Chaos Ascendant" on Abaddon or "My Will Be Done" on Szarekh. These names have no rules meaning; they are flavor. ## How it plays

The four decks play very differently, and each is playable out of the box against other modern precons.

- **Forces of the Imperium** is the most straightforward: lots of creatures, tokens and anthems that win by attacking. Greyfax's tapping ability and Marneus Calgar's card draw keep it going.
- **The Ruinous Powers** is explosive but swingy. Once Abaddon connects, cascade turns every spell into two, but the deck needs to deal damage first.
- **Necron Dynasties** is the grindiest. Szarekh, unearth and reanimation spells let it replay the same artifact creatures over and over, and Their Name Is Death can wipe the board while leaving your Necrons standing.
- **Tyranid Swarm** ramps into huge X creatures. It is slow in the first few turns but can drop overwhelming boards later.

Upgrading follows the usual pattern: add better removal and ramp, cut the clunkiest cards, and lean harder into the deck's theme. ## The meta: how it played

The product had no effect on Standard, Pioneer or Modern, because its new cards are not legal there. Its impact was in Commander, where Szarekh, Marneus Calgar, Be'lakor and Magus Lucea Kane all became popular commanders and cards like Sceptre of Eternal Glory, Cryptothrall and Biophagus found homes in many unrelated decks.

None of the new Warhammer 40,000 cards are on the Commander ban list or the official Game Changers list as of October 2026, so all four decks sit comfortably in the middle [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) unless you add stronger cards. The product's success also helped establish Universes Beyond Commander decks as a regular product line, followed by later franchise crossovers.

## Cards from Warhammer 40,000 worth owning

- **Sceptre of Eternal Glory:** a strong mana rock for mono-color and two-color decks.
- **Szarekh, the Silent King:** one of the best artifact commanders, and great in the 99 of any artifact deck.
- **Marneus Calgar:** a card-draw engine for any token deck.
- **Be'lakor, the Dark Master:** the defining Demon commander.
- **Magus Lucea Kane:** pairs with any X-spell or counters deck.
- **Biophagus:** cheap ramp that also grows creatures.
- **Cryptothrall and Their Name Is Death:** protection and a one-sided sweeper for artifact armies.
- **Vexilus Praetor:** a flash answer that protects your commander.

Warhammer 40,000 Commander is one of the best-designed precon products of its era: each deck has a clear identity, the new commanders are strong without being oppressive, and the reprints were generous.
