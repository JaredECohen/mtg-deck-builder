Seventh Edition is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in April 2001, a white-bordered collection of 350 cards that served as Standard's baseline for more than two years. Every card in it got new artwork, it was the first core set to include foil cards, and it reprinted staples like Birds of Paradise, Counterspell, Llanowar Elves and Wrath of God that powered some of the most famous Standard decks of the era.

## The setting and flavor

Core sets don't have a storyline the way expansions do, and Seventh Edition doesn't take place on a specific plane. It did have a small story told through its art and flavor text: a war between four paladins. The Northern Paladin and Southern Paladin, both white, fight against the black Eastern Paladin and Western Paladin. Each of them destroys permanents of a particular color, so the Northern Paladin destroys black permanents and the Western Paladin destroys white creatures. All four have a missing eye, because in this flavor a paladin trades an eye for a magical gemstone when they are confirmed. Cards like Infernal Contract and Oppression show those gems.

Beyond the paladins, the set leaned on classic fantasy: Serra Angel, Shivan Dragon, Mahamoti Djinn, Lord of Atlantis, Goblin King and Elvish Champion all returned, the last three as tribal lords.

## What the set was for

### A refreshed baseline for Standard

Core sets of this era were the evergreen half of Standard. Seventh Edition replaced Sixth Edition, swapping out 177 cards and adding 177 others. Some of the cards it brought in from older expansions mattered right away, including Duress, Opposition, Static Orb, Ensnaring Bridge, Seismic Assault, Merfolk Looter, Engineered Plague, Pyroclasm and Yawgmoth's Edict. Others left: Armageddon, Mystical Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, Terror and Pestilence were all cut.

One notable swap was Glorious Anthem replacing Crusade. Crusade pumped every white creature, including your opponent's, while Glorious Anthem pumps only creatures you control, of any color. Glorious Anthem went on to appear in core sets through Tenth Edition.

### New art and foils

Seventh Edition's big selling point was that every card had new artwork, even long-time staples. It was also the first core set to include foil cards, which were printed with black borders rather than the set's usual white borders, and the first core set to replace a common in each booster with a basic land. It was sold in boosters, five monocolored theme decks (the first core-set theme decks) and a two-player starter set.

### Rules

Seventh Edition used the modern rules framework introduced with Sixth Edition in 1999, which brought in the stack and removed older timing quirks. It did not bring its own major rules overhaul. It was also the last core set printed in the original card frame; Eighth Edition in 2003 introduced the modern frame. ## Limited

Seventh Edition was a beginner-friendly set, and it was mostly played in casual drafts and sealed events rather than being a major focus of competitive Limited. Like most core sets of its time, it had simple creatures, a handful of strong commons and uncommons, and powerful but not overwhelming rares.

What mattered most in a Seventh Edition draft was the usual core-set formula: evasive creatures, removal and card quality. Dark Banishing, Lightning Blast, Volcanic Hammer, Pacifism and Shock were strong picks, and fliers like Serra Angel and Mahamoti Djinn could end games alone. The paladins were strong when the color they targeted was on the other side of the table. There's no reliable data on specific archetypes from that era, so the best advice is general: draft removal, play a good curve, and favor fliers. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Seventh Edition was in Standard alongside the Masques, Invasion and Odyssey blocks, and later Onslaught. Its cards were the glue in several decks that won major events:

- **Fires of Yavimaya** decks used Seventh Edition's Birds of Paradise and Llanowar Elves alongside Invasion's Fires of Yavimaya and Nemesis's Blastoderm. Jan Tomcani won the 2001 World Championship with a Fires build.
- **Psychatog** decks used Seventh Edition's Counterspell with Odyssey's Psychatog and Fact or Fiction. Carlos Romão won the 2002 World Championship with a blue-black Psychatog deck, playing four copies of Counterspell.
- **Squirrel Opposition** decks combined Opposition with Odyssey's Squirrel Nest to tap down the opponent's lands and creatures, and the archetype appeared at the 2002 World Championship.

Wrath of God and Duress were common in control and black decks throughout the period. Seventh Edition left Standard when Eighth Edition took its place in 2003. No Seventh Edition card was banned in Standard during its run.

### Eternal formats

Seventh Edition itself isn't legal in Modern, but many of its cards are legal there through later reprints, including Ensnaring Bridge, which became a key piece of Lantern Control, and Birds of Paradise and Wrath of God. As of October 2026, the set's most played cards in older formats are:

- **Counterspell** in Legacy, Vintage and Pauper blue decks.
- **Birds of Paradise** and **Llanowar Elves** in green creature decks across formats.
- **Duress** as a cheap discard spell and sideboard card.
- **Engineered Plague** as a sideboard card against tribal decks like Elves and Goblins.

In Commander, Coat of Arms is a classic tribal card, Wrath of God is still a common board wipe, and Birds of Paradise is a staple of green decks. For an overview of these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) or our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Seventh Edition worth owning

- **Birds of Paradise**: the best-known one-mana mana creature, playable everywhere.
- **Counterspell**: the standard two-mana answer to any spell.
- **Wrath of God**: the classic four-mana board wipe.
- **Ensnaring Bridge**: a prison piece for Modern and Legacy.
- **Llanowar Elves**: fast mana for green decks of every kind.
- **Coat of Arms**: an easy upgrade for tribal Commander decks.
- **City of Brass**: a five-color land for Commander.
