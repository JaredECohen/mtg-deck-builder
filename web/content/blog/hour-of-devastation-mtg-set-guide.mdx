Hour of Devastation is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in July 2017, the second half of the Egyptian-inspired Amonkhet block. It's the set where the promised God-Pharaoh finally returns and turns out to be Nicol Bolas, who breaks the plane's gods and crushes the Gatewatch. On the table it gave us The Scarab God, the Desert lands and Ramunap Red, the deck that defined its Standard season.

## The story

Amonkhet's people were raised to compete in the Trials of the Five Gods, a series of deadly tests meant to prove them worthy of the afterlife. The winners died, and their mummified bodies were stored away as the Worthy Dead. Everyone was waiting for the God-Pharaoh to return and open the Gate to the Afterlife. In the first Amonkhet set, the Gatewatch (Gideon, Jace, Liliana, Chandra and Nissa) arrived to hunt Nicol Bolas and learned the ugly truth: the trials were a factory for an undead army.

Hour of Devastation tells what happens when the God-Pharaoh comes home. The Hours arrive one after another. The Gate opens, and instead of paradise it releases the demon Razaketh, one of the four demons who hold Liliana's contract. Razaketh seizes control of her body using a hidden clause, but her friends distract him long enough for her to break free, and she raises the dead beasts of the river to tear him apart. Then the Worthy Dead rise as Eternals, armored undead soldiers that slaughter the city. Three long-buried gods also return: the Scarab God, the Locust God and the Scorpion God, Bolas's own servants.

When Bolas finally appears over Naktamun, the gods fall. The god Bontu reveals she helped Bolas conquer the plane long ago. He orders her to defeat her sister Hazoret, and once she does, he has Bontu killed anyway. The Gatewatch attacks Bolas directly and is taken apart one by one. He crushes Jace's mind, which triggers a hidden failsafe from Ugin that wipes Jace's memory and throws him to Ixalan. Bolas offers Liliana a deal and she leaves. Chandra and Nissa are beaten and flee, and Gideon is left with a choice between death and abandoning his oath. He flees too.

The set ends with Amonkhet in ruins. Hazoret, now called the God-Survivor, gathers the survivors, and the young dissenter Samut keeps fighting the Eternals and the returned Gods. Bolas now has the Eternal army he came for, and he later brings it to Ravnica as the Dreadhorde in War of the Spark.

## Mechanics

### Afflict

Afflict is a triggered ability: whenever a creature with afflict becomes blocked, the defending player loses life equal to its afflict number. It puts your opponent in a bind, since blocking costs life and not blocking costs life too. **Khenra Eternal** (common) is a two-mana 2/2 with afflict 1.

### Eternalize

Eternalize is a graveyard ability. You pay its cost and exile the creature card from your graveyard to make a token copy that is a 4/4 black Zombie with no mana cost. It's an upgrade on Amonkhet's embalm, which made a same-size copy. **Earthshaker Khenra** (rare) is a hasty two-drop that stops a small creature from blocking, then comes back as a 4/4 version that stops a 4-power creature from blocking.

### Deserts

Desert is a land type, and many cards care whether you control a Desert or have one in your graveyard. The uncommon "painland" Deserts, such as **Ramunap Ruins**, tap for colorless or for a color at 1 life, and can sacrifice a Desert for an effect. Ramunap Ruins deals 2 damage to each opponent, which is why it became a key card in red aggro.

### Returning: exert, cycling and aftermath

Exert lets a creature do something extra when it attacks, at the cost of not untapping next turn. Cycling lets you pay a cost and discard the card to draw another. Aftermath cards are split cards where you cast the top half from your hand and the bottom half later from your graveyard, like **Claim // Fame** (uncommon), which reanimates a cheap creature and later pumps and gives haste. -1/-1 counters return as well. ## Limited

Hour of Devastation was drafted with two packs of itself and one of Amonkhet. Amonkhet's gold cards had already set up the allied-color pairs, so Hour of Devastation's uncommon signposts covered the enemy pairs:

- **White-black: Zombies.** Unraveling Mummy rewards a board of Zombies and eternalized tokens.
- **Blue-red: instants and sorceries.** Bloodwater Entity recurs spells.
- **Black-green: -1/-1 counters.** Obelisk Spider punishes blocks and drains as counters are placed.
- **Red-white: exert.** Resolute Survivors rewards attacking and exerting.
- **Green-blue: ramp.** River Hoopoe turns spare mana into life and cards late.

The allied-color pairs leaned on Amonkhet's own themes, such as embalm, cycling and exert, with eternalize adding late-game value. The format was faster than Amonkhet alone. Afflict and exert pushed damage, cheap removal like Ambuscade and Doomfall was valuable, and Desert synergies rewarded picking up the common Deserts with cycling. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Hour of Devastation's Standard season belonged to red. Ramunap Ruins, Earthshaker Khenra and Abrade joined an already strong mono-red aggro deck alongside Kaladesh and Amonkhet staples like Bomat Courier and Hazoret the Fervent. The deck was so tied to its Desert that players called it **Ramunap Red**. At Pro Tour Hour of Devastation in Kyoto in July 2017, about a quarter of the field played it, and Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa won the event with the deck.

Other decks used the set too. **The Scarab God** was the premier midrange threat for most of its Standard life, reanimating opposing creatures as 4/4s and draining every upkeep, and it anchored blue-black and Grixis decks against the Temur Energy decks of the time. God-Pharaoh's Gift had its own Standard deck built around discard and self-mill. Red aggro kept winning after Ixalan arrived, and on January 15, 2018, Wizards banned Ramunap Ruins in Standard along with Rampaging Ferocidon, Attune with Aether and Rogue Refiner.

Outside Standard, the set has a long tail. As of October 2026, the Desert painlands still show up in Pioneer, and the Scarab God remains a Pioneer option. The Locust God is a common target for Indomitable Creativity decks there. In Modern, Hollow One became a graveyard staple with discard outlets, Striped Riverwinder is a cycler in Living End, Solemnity anchors enchantment-based lock decks, and Hour of Promise shows up in ramp decks. In Legacy, Ramunap Excavator is a key piece of Lands, and Bontu's Last Reckoning appears where players need a cheap sweeper. None of the set's cards are banned in Pioneer or Modern.

Commander players know the set for The Scarab God and The Locust God, both among the most popular commanders in their color pairs, plus Torment of Hailfire, Razaketh, Crested Sunmare and Neheb, the Eternal. ## Cards from Hour of Devastation worth owning

- **The Scarab God**: a Pioneer midrange threat and a top blue-black commander.
- **The Locust God**: a Creativity target in Pioneer and a strong blue-red commander.
- **Abrade**: flexible red removal that answers creatures or artifacts.
- **The Desert painlands**: playable dual-purpose lands in Pioneer red decks.
- **Hollow One**: a free-to-cast threat in Modern and Legacy graveyard decks.
- **Ramunap Excavator**: Legacy Lands staple and a Commander value engine.
- **Torment of Hailfire**: one of Commander's best mana sinks to close out a game.

Hour of Devastation is a set to revisit for its Gods and its red aggro tools. Its Standard years are long over, but in Pioneer a red deck with Deserts and Abrade, or a blue-black deck with The Scarab God, still uses its best cards.
