Secrets of Strixhaven is Magic: The Gathering's spring 2026 premier set, released in April 2026. It brings Magic back to Arcavios and its school of magic, Strixhaven, where the plane's ancient archaics are flickering out of existence and a first-year student is hiding a secret that sets up Reality Fracture. Its headline mechanic, prepare, gives creatures a spell they can cast, and a mythic cycle hides some of Magic's most famous spells inside new creatures.

## The story

The heroes are five friends who first met in Lorwyn Eclipsed: Abigale of Silverquill, Sanar of Prismari, Kirol of Lorehold, Tam of Quandrix, and Lluwen, a Lorwyn elf who has followed them home to study at Witherbloom. Ajani, who rescued them on Lorwyn, now advises Lorehold. The friends are scattered across campus and keep in touch through an enchanted message board.

The plot starts on a field trip to Titan's Grave, the bones of an ancient titan. Lluwen and Tam go along with the cold Witherbloom professor Dellian Fel and Jadzi, the plane's oracle. Lluwen sees things nobody believes, including a ring of huge archaics gathered around a spill of raw magic. Then he overhears Fel and Jadzi talking. Fel is secretly a planeswalker, archaics across Arcavios are acting strangely, and a professor has gone missing. Before anyone can act, a gigantic archaic passes the camp and Jadzi disappears.

The rescue pulls in the rest of the cast. Chandra shows up with members of the Shattered, former Oriq who broke their masks rather than attack the school. She is in pain and suspicious, and she tells Ajani that Jace survived Tarkir: Dragonstorm and is working with the archaics. Their minds have been linked for a while. Underground, the students face a Daemogoth, one of the demons that feed on negative emotion, and Fel admits he came to study the archaics' tie to nonlinear time in the hope of saving someone he loved. Jadzi reveals that archaics are oracles thrown back through history when they die. Magical snarls are destabilizing them, and if they vanish, Arcavios goes with them. Chandra decides the archaic has to die, and she and Ajani come to blows over it.

The twist comes at the climax. Reality freezes, and as Jadzi works a spell to calm the archaic, Tam pushes her into the snarl. Tam says she was made for this, then follows her in. The remaining friends finish Jadzi's spell with help from the frog-like Lumarets, stabilizing reality. In the epilogue Tam wakes in a strange copy of Strixhaven, where a white-clad Jace comforts her. That copy is Hexhaven, in the Echoverse that Reality Fracture explores.

## Mechanics

### Prepare

Prepare creatures have a second spell in their text box, in a frame that looks a lot like an Adventure. While the creature is *prepared*, you may cast a copy of that spell, which makes the creature unprepared. Some creatures enter prepared, and others become prepared when a condition is met, so the spell can come back.

**Studious First-Year** (common) is the simplest: a one-mana creature that enters prepared with Rampant Growth. The mythic Emeritus cycle is the showpiece. Each one carries a famous spell: **Emeritus of Woe** has Demonic Tutor, **Emeritus of Ideation** has Ancestral Recall, **Emeritus of Truce** has Swords to Plowshares, **Emeritus of Conflict** has Lightning Bolt and **Emeritus of Abundance** has Regrowth.

### Paradigm

Paradigm appears on a mythic cycle of big sorcery Lessons. After you resolve one, it goes to exile, and at the start of each of your first main phases from then on you may cast a free copy. **Germination Practicum**, which puts two +1/+1 counters on each of your creatures, becomes a repeating anthem.

### The college mechanics

Each two-color college has its own mechanic:

- **Repartee (Silverquill, white-black):** triggers when you cast an instant or sorcery that targets a creature, any creature. **Melancholic Poet** drains for 1 each time.
- **Infusion (Witherbloom, black-green):** a bonus if you gained life this turn. **Foolish Fate** destroys a creature and makes its controller lose 3 life if you're infused.
- **Increment (Quandrix, green-blue):** whenever you cast a spell and spent more mana than the creature's power or toughness, it gets a +1/+1 counter. **Textbook Tabulator** surveils 2 when it enters and grows over time.
- **Opus (Prismari, blue-red):** triggers on each instant or sorcery, with a bigger effect if you spent five or more mana. **Thunderdrum Soloist** pings for 1, or 3 off a big spell.
- **Flashback and graveyard-leaving (Lorehold, red-white):** cards that care when cards leave your graveyard, like **Garrison Excavator**, which makes a Spirit each time.

Converge returns too, counting the colors of mana spent, and it nudges the format toward three-plus colors. For a refresher on reading these frames, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

The main draft archetypes are the five colleges, each built around its mechanic.

- **Silverquill (white-black): targeted spells.** Cheap tricks and removal that target creatures, then payoffs like Melancholic Poet and the rare **Informed Inkwright**. **Silverquill Charm** and Harsh Annotation are premium.
- **Witherbloom (black-green): life gain.** Small lifegain triggers from **Bogwater Lumaret** and Pest tokens switch on infusion cards. **Witherbloom Charm** covers a lot of situations.
- **Quandrix (green-blue): counters and X spells.** Increment creatures like Textbook Tabulator plus scalable threats like **Pterafractyl**.
- **Prismari (blue-red): big spells.** Opus creatures such as **Muse Seeker** and Thunderdrum Soloist reward five-mana spells, with Vibrant Outburst as cheap removal.
- **Lorehold (red-white): graveyard recursion.** Flashback cards and graveyard payoffs like Garrison Excavator.

Converge, the two-color charms and the common tapped dual lands (Titan's Grave, Forum of Amity and the rest) make three-color decks realistic, and early reviews expected a slower, grindier format than usual. Cheap removal like **Wander Off** and Harsh Annotation stays valuable because many prepare creatures give you two cards' worth of value. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Secrets of Strixhaven landed in a fast Standard dominated by Izzet decks. At Pro Tour Secrets of Strixhaven in early May 2026, close to a third of the field played Izzet Prowess, yet the event was won by a Selesnya Landfall deck in a mirror-match final.

The set's biggest Standard contribution was quieter than its mythics. Wizards' June 29, 2026 banned and restricted announcement credited **Tablet of Discovery** with making Four-Color Control possible. By the last two weeks of May it was the second most-played deck at high-level events, behind Izzet Prowess. Wizards made no Standard changes then, noting the format had grown more diverse since the Pro Tour.

By late July, Frank Karsten's metagame breakdown showed the set's cards spread across the top decks:

- **Izzet Prowess** ran **Flow State** and **Colorstorm Stallion**.
- **Selesnya Offense** was built around **Practiced Offense**.
- **Jeskai Lessons** and **Four-Color Control** both leaned on Tablet of Discovery.
- **Temur Lessons** used **Mathemagics** as a finisher.

No Secrets of Strixhaven cards had been banned in Standard as of October 2026. The August 2026 Standard bans hit cards from other sets.

In Commander, the set shipped with five preconstructed decks. The Elder Dragon legends and the Emeritus creatures with iconic spells became natural deck-building targets. The Mystical Archive bonus sheet is collectible and playable in eternal formats, but most of its cards aren't Standard-legal, so check before you build. [What is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains the format if you're coming from Standard.

## Cards from Secrets of Strixhaven worth owning

- **Tablet of Discovery**: proven in multiple Standard control and Lessons decks.
- **Flow State**: a cheap card-selection spell that powered the format's most popular deck.
- **Emeritus of Woe**: Demonic Tutor on a body is a Commander favorite.
- **Emeritus of Truce** and **Emeritus of Conflict**: efficient creatures that come with premium removal.
- **Professor Dellian Fel**: a flexible planeswalker for black-green decks in any format.
- **The rare dual lands** (Dreamroot Cascade, Shattered Sanctum and the rest): core mana for Constructed.
- **Prismari, the Inspiration**: a storm-granting commander built for spell-heavy tables.
