Theros Beyond Death is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2020 that returned to Theros, a plane inspired by Greek myth, and sent players into its Underworld. Elspeth Tirel, killed by the sun god Heliod years earlier, fights her way out of the land of the dead. The set brought the escape mechanic, new Gods and some of the most-banned cards of its era, including Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath and Underworld Breach.

## The story

On Theros, the gods are real and powered by the belief of mortals. The original Theros block ended badly for Elspeth Tirel: she served Heliod as his champion, but when she grew too powerful for his comfort, he killed her with his own spear. Her friend Ajani carried her body away, but on Theros death isn't always the end. Elspeth's spirit went to the Underworld, the realm of the god Erebos, where most souls slowly lose their memories and identities.

Theros Beyond Death picks up there. Elspeth can't rest, partly because of Ashiok, a planeswalker who feeds on nightmares and has been spreading them across Theros, even into the Underworld. Each night Ashiok makes her relive her worst memories. During one of those nightmares, Elspeth pulls Heliod's spear out of the dream, and it becomes a real weapon: Shadowspear, a spear forged from her own trauma.

With Shadowspear in hand, Elspeth sets out to escape the Underworld, gathering other lost souls along the way. Her escape attempt angers Klothys, the god of destiny, who sees it as a violation of the natural order. Klothys sends Calix, a Nyxborn agent, to bring Elspeth back. Elspeth beats him again and again, but he learns from each fight.

The climax turns on how belief works on Theros. Elspeth declares that Shadowspear is the true Khrusor, Heliod's legendary spear, and that his own is a fake. As mortals come to believe her, Heliod's spear weakens until it shatters against hers. Defeated, Heliod is taken prisoner by Erebos in the Underworld. Grateful, Erebos lets Elspeth return to the world of the living. She says goodbye to her old friend Daxos and leaves Theros. Calix, whose planeswalker spark has ignited during the chase, follows her.

## Mechanics

### Escape

Escape is the set's signature mechanic: a card with escape can be cast from your graveyard by paying its escape cost, which includes exiling a number of other cards from your graveyard. Many escape creatures come back bigger. Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath is the best example: cast from your hand for three mana, it's sacrificed right away, but it still gains you 3 life, draws a card and lets you play a land. Later, you escape it from the graveyard as a 6/6 that repeats the trigger every time it attacks. Escape cards are fuel and threat at the same time, and they made self-mill a real strategy.

### Devotion and the Gods

Devotion to a color counts the colored mana symbols of that color among permanents you control. Each God is indestructible but isn't a creature until your devotion is high enough. Heliod, Sun-Crowned needs five white devotion to attack, but its ability to put a +1/+1 counter on a creature whenever you gain life works right away. The Demigods are uncommon legends whose power scales with devotion, like Anax, Hardened in the Forge.

### Constellation and enchantments

Constellation triggers whenever an enchantment enters under your control. Setessan Champion grows and draws a card each time. The set has many enchantment creatures and Sagas, so constellation decks had plenty of support. Sagas, first introduced in Dominaria, return with Greek-myth stories like Elspeth Conquers Death, which exiles an opposing permanent, taxes your opponent's spells, then returns a creature or planeswalker from your graveyard.

## Limited

Theros Beyond Death's draft format had ten two-color archetypes, each with a gold uncommon signpost:

- **White-blue tempo with Auras** (Staggering Insight).
- **Blue-black self-mill** (Devourer of Memory).
- **Black-red sacrifice** (Slaughter-Priest of Mogis).
- **Red-green big creatures** (Warden of the Chained).
- **Green-white Auras** (Siona, Captain of the Pyleas).
- **White-black reanimation** (Rise to Glory).
- **Blue-red spells on the opponent's turn** (Mischievous Chimera).
- **Black-green escape** (Acolyte of Affliction).
- **Red-white heroic go-wide** (Hero of the Nyxborn).
- **Green-blue constellation** (Eutropia the Twice-Favored).

Escape made the graveyard a resource in nearly every deck, so self-mill and graveyard size mattered more than usual. Commons with escape, like Satyr's Cunning, gave aggressive decks long-game value. Enchantment-based removal such as Mire's Grasp was efficient but vulnerable to enchantment removal, and Auras were riskier than usual against bounce. For general drafting skills, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Theros Beyond Death was one of the most powerful sets of its era, and its bans tell much of the story:

- **Uro, Titan of Nature's Wrath** became the centerpiece of green-blue ramp and control decks in Standard, and Wizards banned it there in September 2020. It was then banned in Pioneer and Modern in February 2021.
- **Underworld Breach** enabled combo decks almost immediately. It was banned in Legacy in March 2020 and in Pioneer in August 2020. Modern followed in March 2025.
- **Heliod, Sun-Crowned** combined with Walking Ballista to create an infinite combo: each time Ballista's ping gains you life (via the lifelink Heliod grants), Heliod adds a counter back to Ballista. Pioneer banned Walking Ballista, not Heliod, in August 2020, and Heliod Company stayed a Modern deck.

During its time in Standard, the set also fed Azorius Control, with Dream Trawler and Elspeth Conquers Death, and red aggro decks that used Anax and escape creatures. Omen of the Sea was a common cantrip in blue decks.

In eternal formats, the set left several permanent marks as of October 2026. Thassa's Oracle is the win condition for Legacy Doomsday decks and for one of competitive Commander's most-played combos, where a spell that exiles your library sets up an instant win. Dryad of the Ilysian Grove became a regular in Modern Amulet Titan. Shadowspear is a flexible sideboard card that beats hexproof and indestructible threats. Kroxa and Klothys have shown up in Pioneer and Modern midrange decks. In Commander, Nyxbloom Ancient, Setessan Champion, Heliod and Thassa's Oracle are the stand-outs. For how the ban list works across formats, see our guide to the [ban list](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained).

## Cards from Theros Beyond Death worth owning

- **Thassa's Oracle**: a two-mana win condition in Legacy and competitive Commander.
- **Dryad of the Ilysian Grove**: an extra land drop for Modern ramp and Commander lands decks.
- **Heliod, Sun-Crowned**: a lifegain engine and combo piece in Modern and Commander.
- **Shadowspear**: a one-mana answer to hexproof and indestructible for any deck.
- **Kroxa, Titan of Death's Hunger**: a sticky threat for black-red graveyard decks.
- **Nyxbloom Ancient**: one of Commander's biggest mana engines.
- **Elspeth Conquers Death**: removal plus reanimation in one card, playable in Pioneer and Commander.

Theros Beyond Death is a set to respect: many of its best cards are banned somewhere, and the rest are still strong. In Pioneer, Heliod, Klothys and Kroxa are the cards to build around.
