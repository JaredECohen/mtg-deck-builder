Outlaws of Thunder Junction is the April 2024 Magic: The Gathering expansion set on Thunder Junction, a Wild West plane of desert towns, train robberies and rival gangs. Its story is a heist: Oko assembles a crew of the multiverse's worst people to crack an ancient vault. Its mechanics reward you for committing crimes, plotting spells for later and riding Mounts into battle.

## The story

Thunder Junction was unknown to the rest of the multiverse until the Phyrexian invasion opened the Omenpaths, portals that let ordinary people travel between planes. Once those paths opened, the plane quickly filled with fortune-seekers, lawmen and criminals from everywhere. The big powers are the Sterling Company, which runs the railroads and guards cargo, and the Hellspurs, a gang led by the scorpion-dragon Akul, who rules the outlaw town of Tarnation.

At the center of Tarnation sits Maag Taranau, an ancient vault rumored to hold incredible treasure. It takes six keys to open. Oko, the shapeshifting planeswalker, decides to rob it. He recruits a who's-who of villains from across Magic's history, including Vraska, Rakdos, Gisa and Geralf, Tinybones, Satoru Umezawa, Kaervek, the pirates Breeches and Malcolm, a figure who appears to be Ashiok, and the gunslinger Annie Flash, a retired outlaw with a grudge against Akul. They steal one key in a train robbery and learn Akul holds the other five.

Kellan, a young hero and Oko's son, gets pulled in from the other side. An attempt to infiltrate Tarnation in disguise goes wrong, Kellan is forced to duel Akul, and Oko's crew escapes while leaving him behind. Kellan teams up with Ral Zarek, then joins the gang on his own terms.

The heist itself is full of double-crosses. The "Ashiok" on the crew was Jace in disguise the whole time. Once the vault opens, Jace takes the real prize, a small creature called Loot, and Vraska, who was in on his plan, turns on Oko. As the vault collapses, Kellan uses his fae magic to take control of Akul's mind to save Annie, and drives the dragon back into the crumbling vault. Jace and Vraska leave with Loot, Oko's gang breaks up, and Kellan stays behind to help Annie and explore the plane. Loot's role carries straight into the next stories, including Tarkir: Dragonstorm.

## Mechanics

### Crimes

Committing a crime means targeting an opponent, anything they control or a card in their graveyard. It's a mechanic you'll trigger naturally with removal, burn and graveyard hate. Cards with "whenever you commit a crime" reward it, like Magda, the Hoardmaster making a Treasure, or Duelist of the Mind letting you loot.

### Plot

Plot lets you pay a cost on your turn to exile a card from your hand face up. On a later turn, you can cast it as a sorcery without paying its mana cost. Slickshot Show-Off, a hasty flier that gets bigger with each noncreature spell, can be plotted for `{1}{R}` and cast for free later, freeing up mana for spells on the turn it attacks. Plot smooths out awkward turns and sets up big two-spell turns.

### Saddle and Mounts

Mounts are creatures with saddle N: tap other creatures you control with total power N or more and the Mount becomes saddled until end of turn, unlocking a bonus when it attacks. Seraphic Steed has first strike and lifelink and makes a 3/3 flying Angel when it attacks while saddled. Saddling is like crewing a Vehicle, except the Mount can still attack on its own.

### Spree

Spree spells have a base cost plus a menu of additional costs, and you pay for as many modes as you want. Three Steps Ahead costs `{U}` plus any combination of `{1}{U}` to counter a spell, `{3}` to copy one of your creatures or artifacts, and `{2}` to draw two and discard one. Explosive Derailment, a common, can deal 4 damage to a creature, destroy an artifact or both.

### Outlaws and Deserts

"Outlaw" isn't a keyword but a group of five creature types: Assassins, Mercenaries, Pirates, Rogues and Warlocks. Cards like Hellspur Posse Boss and Take the Fall care whether you control outlaws, while Shoot the Sheriff destroys any creature that isn't one. Deserts also return as a land type, with payoffs like Outcaster Greenblade.

## The Big Score and Breaking News

Outlaws of Thunder Junction came with two bonus sheets.

**The Big Score** is a 30-card, vault-themed mini-set of mythic-rarity cards, full of artifacts and treasure. Its cards appeared in roughly one in five Play Boosters, and they're Standard-legal. Highlights include Vaultborn Tyrant, a seven-mana Dinosaur that draws cards when big creatures enter and comes back as a token when it dies; Collector's Cage, a hideaway artifact for decks with creatures of different powers; Simulacrum Synthesizer for artifact decks; Pest Control, a sweeper for cheap permanents; and Loot, the Key to Everything, the creature the whole heist was about. The sheet also includes reprints like Grand Abolisher and Rest in Peace.

**Breaking News** is a 65-card sheet of reprints in a newspaper "wanted poster" style, one per Play Booster. They're mostly instants and sorceries, including Path to Exile, Thoughtseize, Mana Drain and Oko, Thief of Crowns. Breaking News cards are not Standard-legal; they're legal only in formats where the card was already legal.

## Limited

Outlaws of Thunder Junction had ten two-color archetypes:

- **Green-white, Mounts:** saddle payoffs like Seraphic Steed and Wylie Duke, Atiin Hero.
- **Black-green, recursion:** Honest Rutstein, one of the strongest signposts in the set.
- **White-black, sacrifice and tokens:** Baron Bertram Graywater and Ruthless Lawbringer.
- **Red-green, ferocious:** creatures with 4 or more power, with Jolene, Plundering Pugilist making Treasure.
- **Red-white, Mercenaries:** going wide with Ertha Jo, Frontier Mentor and Form a Posse.
- **Green-blue, plot ramp.**
- **Blue-black, crimes:** Lazav, Familiar Stranger grows with each crime.
- **Black-red, outlaws.**
- **White-blue, plot and flash.**
- **Blue-red, two-spell turns:** payoffs for your second spell each turn, like Malcolm, the Eyes.

Many players found the format slower and more interactive than Murders at Karlov Manor before it. Green-white, black-green and white-black were commonly rated the strongest pairs, while the blue-red and white-blue plot decks were harder to make work. Removal like Mystical Tether, Consuming Ashes and Explosive Derailment, plus value commons like Spinewoods Paladin, were the backbone of good decks. Plotting a spell on a slow turn and casting two things on the next was one of the most important skills to learn.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

At Pro Tour Thunder Junction in May 2024, Esper Midrange was the most-played deck, and it picked up several new cards: Concealed Courtyard, Duelist of the Mind, and Pest Control and Rest in Peace (both from The Big Score) in sideboards. Three Steps Ahead went into Azorius Control, Honest Rutstein powered a four-color Legends deck, and Slickshot Show-Off appeared in several red aggro decks. Wizards' recap noted that the eventual winner was a Domain Ramp deck that beat the popular Esper lists.

Slickshot Show-Off became the set's longest-lasting Standard card, a regular in red aggro and prowess-style decks over the following year. The set's lone ban came on **June 30, 2025**, when Wizards banned This Town Ain't Big Enough in Standard. The cheap two-permanent bounce spell had become a problem in decks that reused it with Stormchaser's Talent, and it was one of seven cards banned that day. As of October 2026, it is the only card from the set banned in Standard.

### Pioneer and Modern

Slickshot Show-Off was the most-played new card in Pioneer, ahead of Pillage the Bog and Magebane Lizard. In Modern, it showed up in large numbers across Izzet, Gruul, Temur and mono-red prowess decks. Modern also picked up Satoru, the Infiltrator for reanimator-style decks, Simulacrum Synthesizer and Fomori Vault for Affinity, Jace Reawakened for blue control and Wizards decks, Collector's Cage for white aggro, Pest Control and Roxanne, Starfall Savant as sideboard cards, and Nexus of Becoming in Mono-Green Tron. For when to bring in cards like Pest Control, see [Sideboarding 101](/blog/sideboarding-101).

### Commander

Commander players got a crowd of new legends, from Kellan, the Kid to Vraska, the Silencer and Gisa, the Hellraiser. Bristly Bill, Spine Sower is a popular landfall and counters commander, Terror of the Peaks got a reprint, and Breaking News made older staples like Path to Exile and Anguished Unmaking easier to find.

## Cards from Outlaws of Thunder Junction worth owning

- **Slickshot Show-Off:** a staple threat for red aggro in Standard, Pioneer and Modern.
- **Three Steps Ahead:** a flexible counterspell that scales into a card-advantage spell.
- **Vaultborn Tyrant:** a resilient ramp payoff and Commander favorite.
- **Collector's Cage:** a Constructed-playable hideaway card for creature decks.
- **Jace Reawakened:** a cheap planeswalker for blue decks in older formats.
- **Bristly Bill, Spine Sower:** a strong two-drop for landfall and counters in Commander.
- **The fast lands (Concealed Courtyard, Inspiring Vantage and others):** reprints that make two-color Constructed decks smoother.

Outlaws of Thunder Junction is still a strong source of Standard tools as of October 2026, especially for red aggro and blue control. Start with Slickshot Show-Off and Three Steps Ahead, and look to The Big Score for Vaultborn Tyrant.
