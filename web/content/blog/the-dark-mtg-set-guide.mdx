The Dark is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in August 1994, the fourth expansion ever made. It had no new keywords at all. Instead, it was the first set built around a mood: a grim, gothic look at the "dark underbelly" of all five colors, set in the age of persecution and decline after the Brothers' War. It's small and strange, but it gave the game Blood Moon, Maze of Ith, Ball Lightning and Tormod's Crypt.

## The setting and flavor

The Dark doesn't have a plot the way modern sets do. There was no novel, and the cards tell their story through art and flavor text. The setting is Terisiare, the continent where the Brothers' War between the artificers Urza and Mishra ended with the detonation of the Golgothian Sylex. That blast threw so much debris into the sky that it dimmed the sun. The climate began to cool, crops failed, and great cities fell into ruin. This era, which would eventually give way to the Ice Age, is called the Dark Age.

Frightened people blamed magic for the catastrophe. A religion, the **Church of Tal**, rose to power preaching against the arrogance of the artificers, and its zealots hunted down anyone who practiced magic. The cards are full of that persecution: **Inquisition** hurts a player for every white card in their hand, **Witch Hunter** and **Exorcist** stalk their enemies, and **Angry Mob** grows stronger against players with Swamps. Mages went into hiding, which is why the set has the legendary **City of Shadows**, a hidden refuge in the east, and **Safe Haven**, a land that hides your creatures away.

The set's flavor is deliberately unsettling. The art leans on gloom, ruins, swamps and monsters, from **Frankenstein's Monster** to **The Fallen**, and the card names reference local figures like **Uncle Istvan**, **Niall Silvain** and the knight **Tivadar of Thorn**, whose **Tivadar's Crusade** destroys every Goblin. The Dark also breaks the usual color rules on purpose, with blue land destruction (**Erosion**), white direct damage (**Fire and Brimstone**) and white creature theft (**Preacher**). Later Magic design would tighten up which colors can do what, but here the strangeness was the point: the colors, like the people, had lost their way.

## Mechanics

The Dark had no new mechanics, but it explored several themes that later sets would revisit.

### Goblin tribal

The Dark was one of the first sets to treat a creature type as a theme. It has six Goblins, Auras like **Goblin Caves** and **Goblin Shrine** that pump Goblins on a Mountain, the rare **Goblin Wizard**, which puts Goblins from your hand onto the battlefield, and Tivadar's Crusade to wipe them all out. It's an early ancestor of every tribal deck since.

### Graveyards and exile

Several cards use the graveyard as a resource or a target. **Tormod's Crypt** exiles a player's graveyard for zero mana, **Frankenstein's Monster** exiles creature cards from your graveyard to grow, and **Eater of the Dead** feeds on them. These ideas would become central to Magic much later.

### Poison and costly power

**Marsh Viper** gives two poison counters with each hit, one of the few poison cards before Scars of Mirrodin brought back infect. Many of the set's strongest cards ask for a lot of colored mana, such as **Ball Lightning** at `{R}{R}{R}` and **Season of the Witch** at `{B}{B}{B}`, or demand an upkeep payment, which made them hard to fit into multicolor decks.

### Multicolor firsts

The Dark has only a few multicolored cards, such as **Dark Heart of the Wood**, but they include the first multicolored common, the first multicolored noncreature card and the first enemy-color pair card. If you're unsure how older cards are worded, our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) covers the basics.

## Limited

The Dark was not drafted in any organized way. Booster Draft as a tournament format came years later, and The Dark was a small expansion sold in eight-card boosters (six commons and two uncommons) that players mixed into their collections and decks. If you open The Dark today, it's usually as part of a collection or a cube, not a draft.

That makes it a good illustration of how different early Magic was. Sets were made to add variety to the game rather than to support a self-contained Limited format, so there were no designed archetypes or set-wide synergies to draft around. Our [formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) explains how Limited and Constructed formats developed afterward.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Type II and early tournaments

When Wizards created Type II (now Standard) on January 10, 1995, the format consisted of the current core set and the two most recent expansions, which at that moment were The Dark and Fallen Empires. The Dark therefore was one of the very first sets in Standard. Its time there was short: Ice Age took its place later in 1995. **Maze of Ith** was restricted in both Type I and Type II at that time, and it stayed restricted in Type I (now Vintage) until April 1999. Ball Lightning was a natural fit for the aggressive red decks of the period.

### Modern, Legacy and Vintage

The Dark's biggest legacy comes from cards that were later reprinted:

- **Blood Moon** is one of Modern's most important hate cards. It punishes greedy multicolor mana bases and is played in red decks and sideboards in Modern and Legacy. It's Modern-legal through its later printings.
- **Tormod's Crypt** is a free graveyard-hate artifact, played in sideboards against Dredge, Reanimator and other graveyard decks in Modern, Legacy and Vintage.
- **Maze of Ith** has been a part of Legacy Lands decks and a strong defensive land in Commander.
- **Ball Lightning** remains a fun, high-damage burn creature for casual and budget red decks.

As of October 2026, none of The Dark's cards are banned or restricted in Legacy or Vintage. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains where these older cards fit.

### Commander and the Reserved List

In Commander, Maze of Ith, Fellwar Stone, Blood Moon and Tormod's Crypt all see play. Blood Moon can wreck a table full of three-color mana bases, so check with your group before you cast it. If you're new to the format, start with [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

Much of The Dark is on the Reserved List, Wizards' list of older cards it has promised never to reprint. City of Shadows, Preacher, Season of the Witch, Sorrow's Path, Goblin Wizard, Frankenstein's Monster and other rares are on it. Popular cards like Blood Moon, Maze of Ith and Tormod's Crypt are not on the list, which is why they've been reprinted.

## Cards from The Dark worth owning

- **Blood Moon**: a format-warping hate card in Modern and Legacy.
- **Tormod's Crypt**: free, efficient graveyard hate.
- **Maze of Ith**: a powerful defensive land for Commander.
- **Fellwar Stone**: a cheap mana rock for Commander.
- **Ball Lightning**: a classic all-in burn creature.

The Dark is Magic's first mood piece: no new keywords, just a dark, cohesive world in card form. Its best cards still matter in eternal formats, and you can test a Legacy deck built around them in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
