Alliances is a Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1996, the final set of the original Ice Age block. It is set on Dominaria as the long ice age finally begins to thaw, and old enemies band together against a common threat. Its legacy far outlasted its time: Alliances introduced pitch spells, including Force of Will, the most important counterspell in Legacy and Vintage, along with Elvish Spirit Guide, Gorilla Shaman and Helm of Obedience.

## The setting and flavor

Alliances doesn't have a novel-length plot told through its cards the way modern sets do, but it has a clear setting and plenty of named characters. It picks up on the continent of Terisiare after the Ice Age set. For centuries Dominaria had been frozen, a long aftermath of the Brothers' War between Urza and Mishra. In the Ice Age era, the elf goddess Freyalise cast a great "World Spell" to end the cold. Alliances is the thaw: the ice is melting, coastlines are flooding, plagues are spreading and the old balance of power is falling apart.

The set's title describes its core story. The human nations of Kjeldor and Balduvia had long been enemies, and the floods made things worse as Kjeldorans pushed into Balduvian territory. But the necromancer Lim-Dûl, whose undead armies were rising in the chaos, threatened them both, and the two peoples were forced into an uneasy alliance. That partnership would eventually lead to the founding of New Argive. Lim-Dûl's influence shows up all over the set's black cards, like Lim-Dûl's High Guard and Lim-Dûl's Vault.

Elsewhere, the Soldevi, a scholarly people, were digging up artifacts from the Brothers' War. Some of what they unearthed was Phyrexian, which is why cards like Phyrexian Portal, Phyrexian War Beast and Phyrexian Devourer appear in the set, and why the Soldevi's story ends badly. The green cards follow the elves and the gorillas of the forests, including the elf druid Kaysa. And then there is Phelddagrif, a winged purple hippo that became one of Magic's best-loved jokes. The block's story was expanded in the novel *The Shattered Alliance*.

## Mechanics

### Pitch spells

Alliances introduced the pitch spell: a spell with an alternative cost that lets you exile a card of the same color from your hand instead of paying mana. The five uncommon pitch spells are:

- **Force of Will** (blue): pay 1 life and exile a blue card to counter target spell.
- **Contagion** (black): pay 1 life and exile a black card to put two -2/-1 counters on creatures.
- **Pyrokinesis** (red): exile a red card to deal 4 damage divided among creatures.
- **Bounty of the Hunt** (green): exile a green card to give out three temporary +1/+1 counters.
- **Scars of the Veteran** (white): exile a white card to prevent 7 damage.

The cost is real: you spend two cards to do one thing. But being able to act with all your lands tapped, even on turn one, is enormously powerful. That's why Force of Will became the defining card of Vintage and Legacy.

### Cumulative upkeep

Returning from Ice Age, cumulative upkeep puts an age counter on a permanent at the start of each of your upkeeps, and you have to pay the upkeep cost once for each counter or sacrifice it. Dystopia makes each player sacrifice a green or white permanent every upkeep, but costs you more life each turn. Sustaining Spirit keeps your life total from dropping below 1 for as long as you can keep paying.

### Sacrifice-a-basic lands

A rare cycle of lands asks you to sacrifice a basic land of the matching type when you play them, and in return gives you something extra. Lake of the Dead taps for black, or can sacrifice a Swamp for four black mana. Kjeldoran Outpost makes 1/1 Soldier tokens. Heart of Yavimaya pumps a creature. Thawing Glaciers, though it works differently, fetches a basic land and returns to your hand each turn.

### Delayed card draw and banding

Several cards replace themselves on your next upkeep instead of immediately, like Arcane Denial, which counters a spell and gives both players cards next turn. Banding, the old combat ability that let creatures attack and block as a group, appears on a few white cards like Kjeldoran Escort. It has since been retired.

## Limited

Alliances came out before the modern booster draft was the standard way to play Limited, and it wasn't designed around draft archetypes. When people did play it in Sealed or a draft, it was a slow, odd format: lots of creatures with cumulative upkeep, few cheap removal spells, and color balance that was far from modern standards. The pitch spells were most valuable there, since a free removal spell or a free combat trick changes a game.

If you open some Alliances packs today, treat it like any classic set: value removal and evasive creatures, avoid most cumulative upkeep cards unless they win the game quickly, and expect long games. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Type II, 1996-1997

Alliances arrived in a Type II format dominated by Necropotence from Ice Age. Summer 1996 is often called "Black Summer" because so many players ran **Necro** decks, black discard and removal piles that used Necropotence to draw huge numbers of cards. Alliances gave those decks two important additions: **Contagion**, a free removal spell, and **Dystopia**, which punished the green and white decks that tried to beat Necro. After Mirage arrived that fall, **Lake of the Dead** gave black more mana for big Drain Life turns.

White weenie decks were a common opponent, and **Kjeldoran Outpost** gave white a land that made a steady stream of Soldiers. Force of Will was a fine card in Type II, but its true reputation came later, in formats with more powerful spells to stop.

Alliances left Type II with the rest of the Ice Age block when Tempest became legal at the end of October 1997.

### Legacy and Vintage

As of October 2026, no Alliances card is banned or restricted in Legacy or Vintage, and its best cards are fixtures there:

- **Force of Will** is the most important blue card in both formats, the free answer that keeps fast combo decks honest.
- **Elvish Spirit Guide** gives combo decks free mana from hand, for turn-one kills.
- **Helm of Obedience** combines with Rest in Peace for a two-card win: with Rest in Peace out, Helm never puts a creature into the graveyard, so it exiles the opponent's entire library.
- **Gorilla Shaman** is a cheap way for Vintage decks to destroy Moxen and other cheap artifacts.

### Reserved List, Commander and Pauper

Alliances has 41 cards on the Reserved List, Wizards' policy of never reprinting certain cards, including Lake of the Dead, Thawing Glaciers, Kjeldoran Outpost, Helm of Obedience, Dystopia and Phyrexian Devourer. Force of Will isn't one of them, which is why it has been reprinted many times.

In Commander, Arcane Denial is a popular cheap counterspell and Phelddagrif is a fun "group hug" commander. Gorilla Shaman and Arcane Denial are also legal in Pauper. Alliances is not legal in Modern. Our [formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) guide explains which formats allow which sets.

## Cards from Alliances worth owning

- **Force of Will**: the most important card in Legacy and Vintage.
- **Elvish Spirit Guide**: free mana for combo decks.
- **Helm of Obedience**: half of a classic Legacy combo.
- **Arcane Denial**: a cheap counterspell for Commander.
- **Gorilla Shaman**: a Vintage-legal answer to Moxen.
- **Thawing Glaciers**: a slow, steady mana engine for Commander control decks.

## The takeaway

Alliances is a strange, dated set with one of the most important cards ever printed. If you play Legacy or Vintage, you play against Force of Will constantly, and it's worth knowing where it came from. Building a blue Legacy deck around it is the most direct way to put Alliances to work today.
