The Portal sets are three Magic: The Gathering beginner products released between 1997 and 1999: Portal, Portal Second Age and Portal Three Kingdoms. They used simplified cards with no instants, artifacts or enchantments to teach new players, and while they were not tournament-legal at first, all three have been legal in Legacy and Vintage since 2005. A handful of their cards, like Natural Order, Imperial Seal and Goblin Matron, are still played in eternal formats and Commander.

None of the three is Standard-legal, and a Portal card is Modern-legal only if it was also printed in a Modern-legal set (Goblin Matron, for example, was reprinted in Modern Horizons).

## What the Portal sets were for

By the mid-1990s, Magic's rules had grown complicated. Interrupts, instants, the stack, enchantments and artifacts were a lot for a brand-new player, and Wizards wanted a product that could be sold in toy and mass-market stores to people who had never played. Portal was the answer: a self-contained introductory game that used the same basic structure as Magic but stripped out most of the complexity.

Portal cards had no instants, artifacts or enchantments, and even creature types were missing at first; the type line simply read "Summon Creature" (later errata gave them normal types). Spells that worked during combat were printed as sorceries with a restriction, such as "cast this spell only during the declare attackers step." Each set was meant to be played on its own or with the other Portal sets.

Because the cards were designed for beginners, Wizards kept them out of sanctioned tournaments. That changed on October 20, 2005, when Portal, Portal Second Age and Portal Three Kingdoms, along with the Starter sets, became legal in Legacy and Vintage. Today the cards are also legal in Commander. If you're learning the game, our [beginner's guide to playing Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers the full rules that Portal simplified.

## Portal (1997)

The original Portal had no story. Its cards were generic fantasy creatures and spells, many of them simplified reprints or functional reprints of classic cards. Because it was designed to work without instants, cards like Cruel Tutor (a sorcery that puts any card on top of your library for 2 life) and Sylvan Tutor (a creature tutor to the top of your library) are slower versions of better-known tutors.

The set still contains real power. Natural Order, a rare sorcery first printed in Visions that sacrifices a green creature to put any green creature from your library onto the battlefield, is the reason most Legacy players know the set. Armageddon, Wrath of God, Earthquake, Hurricane, Prosperity and Wood Elves also appear. Some cards were printed in two versions: the two-player starter set contained a few cards with extra reminder text that differ from their booster versions.

## Portal Second Age (1998)

Portal Second Age gave the line its first story. It is set on Caliman, an island on Dominaria, and each color is a people with its own tribe:

- **Alaborn (white):** a human kingdom of knights and soldiers, with gunpowder-like weapons, such as Alaborn Grenadier and Alaborn Veteran.
- **Talas (blue):** coastal merchants and pirates, such as Talas Warrior and Talas Researcher.
- **Dakmor (black):** the swamp realm of the nightstalkers, led by the swamp queen Tojira, with cards like Dakmor Sorceress and Nightstalker Engine.
- **Goblins (red):** mountain Goblins with Goblin General and Goblin Matron.
- **Norwood (green):** forest elves, such as Norwood Priestess.

The story is told only through the cards. Its central thread is the Dakmor nightstalkers raiding Alaborn, and it is open-ended so players could play out the conflict by building faction decks. The set's tribal structure made it a better casual game than the original Portal, and it introduced several cards that are still played, including Goblin Matron, Temporal Manipulation, Ancient Craving, Relentless Assault, Wildfire and Sleight of Hand.

## Portal Three Kingdoms (1999)

Portal Three Kingdoms is the most unusual of the three. It adapts *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*, the classic 14th-century Chinese historical novel about the wars that followed the fall of the Han dynasty. It was designed for Asian markets and released mainly in Asia, with a small English printing sold mostly in Australia and New Zealand, which is why English copies are so scarce.

The set is organized around the three rival kingdoms and the figures who fought for them:

- **Shu (white):** Liu Bei, Lord of Shu and his sworn brothers Guan Yu, Sainted Warrior and Zhang Fei, Fierce Warrior. Liu Bei gets +2/+2 while you control either brother, a nod to the famous Oath of the Peach Garden. The strategist Kongming, "Sleeping Dragon" also fights for Shu.
- **Wu (blue):** Sun Quan, Lord of Wu, who gives all your creatures horsemanship, and the general Lu Xun, Scholar General.
- **Wei (black):** Cao Cao, Lord of Wei, who makes an opponent discard two cards, and Sima Yi, Wei Field Marshal.
- **Red** collects warlords outside the three kingdoms, such as Lu Bu, Master-at-Arms and Diaochan, Artful Beauty.

Many spells are named after episodes from the novel: Borrowing 100,000 Arrows draws a card for each tapped creature an opponent controls, Empty City Ruse makes an opponent skip their next turn's combat phases, and Capture of Jingzhou takes an extra turn.

### Horsemanship

The set replaced flying with **horsemanship**: a creature with horsemanship can't be blocked except by creatures with horsemanship. It plays exactly like flying with a historical theme, since there are no dragons or angels in the novel. Shu Cavalry and Wei Night Raiders are typical examples, and Rolling Earthquake (damage to every creature without horsemanship and each player) mirrors Earthquake. Horsemanship has never been printed again.

## How they played

As beginner products, the Portal sets were designed to be played straight out of the box rather than drafted. Games were simple creature battles: no tricks during combat, few complicated interactions, and large, easy-to-read creatures. That makes them a good introduction for new players even today, and some teachers still use Portal-style decks for exactly that reason. Our guide to [reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) explains the modern frame for comparison.

Draft and sealed were possible, but not the point. Portal Second Age's tribes gave sealed games a little more structure than the original Portal, and Portal Three Kingdoms' horsemanship battles play much like any flying-heavy format.

## The meta: how they played in Constructed

None of the Portal sets were ever Standard-legal, and their cards became legal in Legacy and Vintage only in October 2005. Since then, a few have mattered:

- **Natural Order** has been a Legacy staple for years in green decks that use it to find a single huge creature, such as Craterhoof Behemoth in Elves.
- **Goblin Matron** is a classic part of Legacy Goblins.
- **Imperial Seal** is a one-mana sorcery-speed Vampiric Tutor. It is restricted in Vintage and banned in Legacy as of October 2026.
- **Imperial Recruiter** was later reprinted in Modern Horizons 2, which made it Modern-legal.

In Commander, the tutors and extra-turn spells are the big draws. As of October 2026, both **Natural Order** and **Imperial Seal** are on the official Game Changers list, which affects a deck's [Commander bracket](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). Several commons, such as Three Visits, Wood Elves and Nature's Lore, are also Pauper-legal.

Because Portal Three Kingdoms had a small English print run, its cards are among the scarcest English cards from the 1990s, and many players use reprints where they exist (Imperial Seal, Imperial Recruiter, Three Visits and Ambition's Cost have all been reprinted in later sets).

## Cards from the Portal sets worth owning

- **Natural Order:** a Legacy and Commander staple for green creature decks.
- **Imperial Seal:** a cheap tutor for Vintage and high-power Commander.
- **Goblin Matron:** a Goblin tutor on a body for Legacy and Commander Goblins.
- **Imperial Recruiter:** a flexible creature tutor that fits many Commander decks.
- **Three Visits and Nature's Lore:** Forest-fetching ramp for green Commander and Pauper decks.
- **Temporal Manipulation and Capture of Jingzhou:** extra-turn spells for blue Commander decks.
- **Cruel Tutor and Sylvan Tutor:** budget tutors for Commander.

The Portal sets are a fascinating detour in Magic's history: simplified for beginners, but with a few cards that still show up at Legacy tournaments and Commander tables.
