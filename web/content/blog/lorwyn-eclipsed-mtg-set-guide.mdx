Lorwyn Eclipsed is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2026 that returns to Lorwyn, the plane of Kithkin, Elves, Merfolk, Faeries, Goblins and Elementals, for the first time since 2008. This time the sunny side and the dark side, Shadowmoor, share the same set: day and night now trade places wherever two great beasts walk. It's a creature-type set built on new blight and vivid mechanics, a cycle of returning Elemental Incarnations, and five reprinted shocklands.

## The story

Lorwyn and Shadowmoor have always been two faces of one plane: an endless summer day and an endless autumn night, swapping every few centuries. That cycle was the work of Oona, the faerie queen, and when it broke, the plane gained two living guardians. Eirdu, the sun-headed Carrier of Dawn, brings day wherever it walks. Isilu, the moon-headed Carrier of Twilight, brings night. The borders between day and night now shift with them, and the plane's peoples change as the light does. That's why many legends in the set are double-faced cards with a Lorwyn side and a Shadowmoor side.

Visitors arrive by accident. Four Strixhaven students (Abigale, Kirol, Sanar and Tam) chase a faerie through an Omenpath during a field trip and land in the middle of the plane's crisis. Ajani follows to bring them home. The Kithkin hero Brigid guides them away from Isilu, who has woken early and dragged night over Goldmeadow.

The conflict has two threads. High Perfect Morcant, leader of the Lys Alana Elves, wants eternal day back and plans to kill Isilu with a poison called dawnglove, forcing the captured Kirol to help gather it. Meanwhile Maralen, the faerie ruler who was once an avatar of Oona, shows signs that Oona is returning. Her old ally Rhys, sworn to kill her if that ever happened, poisons her. The shapeshifting faerie Oko turns out to have stirred up much of this, after mistaking Maralen for Oona.

The resolution is a reconciliation rather than a battle won. Oko owns up to his mistake and races to find an antidote. Kirol and Sanar, with the Elf Lluwen, steal the poison back from Morcant, and Oko uses it to revive Maralen. Abigale brings Eirdu to the battlefield, and the two beasts greet each other and play-fight like old friends, restoring the balance between day and night. Rhys, whose life was bound to his pact, fades away. Ajani takes the students home, with Lluwen joining them, and an epilogue at Strixhaven sets up the next set, Secrets of Strixhaven.

## Mechanics

### Blight

Blight is the set's signature new mechanic. To blight N, you put N -1/-1 counters on a creature you control, all on the same creature. It usually appears as an optional cost that upgrades a spell. Requiting Hex is a one-mana instant that destroys a creature with mana value 2 or less, and gains you 2 life if you blight 1 as you cast it. Cinder Strike deals 2 damage, or 4 if you blight 1. Some cards flip it around and make *opponents* blight, which works like edict-style removal: High Perfect Morcant makes each opponent blight 1 whenever it or another Elf enters.

In practice, blight rewards having a spare creature you don't mind shrinking, and it pairs with the set's -1/-1 counter theme in black-white and red-white.

### Vivid

Vivid is an ability word that counts the colors among permanents you control. Bloom Tender taps for one mana of each color among your permanents, and Shinestriker draws a card for each color when it enters. Hybrid cards count as both of their colors, which makes vivid easy to grow in a multicolor deck.

### Behold

Behold is a cost that asks you to show a creature of a certain type: either one you control or a card of that type revealed from your hand. It replaces the old "champion" mechanic from the original Lorwyn. Celebrating your tribe is the point, so cards with behold reward decks with plenty of one creature type.

### Evoke and the Elemental Incarnations

Evoke returns on a mythic cycle of five Elemental Incarnations: Deceit, Emptiness, Vibrance, Wistfulness and Catharsis. Evoke lets you cast a creature for a cheaper cost and sacrifice it when it enters, keeping its enter-the-battlefield trigger. The twist is that each Incarnation has double-hybrid costs and two triggers, and which one fires depends on the mana you spent. Deceit, for example, costs `{4}{U/B}{U/B}` or evokes for `{U/B}{U/B}`; paying `{U}{U}` bounces a nonland permanent, while paying `{B}{B}` makes the opponent discard a card you choose.

### Changeling, kindred, convoke and the rest

Changeling (the card has every creature type) is back, including the first hybrid changelings, and kindred (the renamed "tribal" type) puts creature types on spells like Brigid's Command. Convoke lets you tap creatures to help pay for spells, and persist and wither appear on a handful of cards. Finally, the set's day-and-night legends, such as Ashling, Rekindled and Sygg, Wanderwine Wisdom, let you pay one mana at the start of your first main phase to flip to the other face.

## Limited

Lorwyn Eclipsed is a creature-type draft format with all ten color pairs supported. The tribal pairs are the easiest to read:

- **White-blue Merfolk:** a tempo deck where tapping your Merfolk matters, with Deepchannel Duelist as the signpost.
- **Blue-black Faeries:** flash and instant-speed play, led by Voracious Tome-Skimmer.
- **Black-red Goblins:** midrange with Boggart synergies.
- **Blue-red Elementals:** midrange built around Twinflame Travelers, which doubles your other Elementals' triggers.
- **Black-green Elves:** Elf synergies and recursion, signposted by Morcant's Loyalist.
- **Green-white Kithkin:** go-wide aggro with Thoughtweft Lieutenant.

The other four pairs are built around the mechanics: white-black and red-white play blight and -1/-1 counters (one grindier, one aggressive), while red-green and green-blue chase vivid, the latter as a ramp deck.

The set has plenty of removal at common. Blight Rot, Liminal Hold and Cinder Strike are the kind of cards you take early, while blue's Unexpected Assistance and Silvergill Peddler keep the cards flowing. Hybrid mana and changelings make it easier than usual to stay flexible during the draft, since many cards fit two colors or several tribes. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Lorwyn Eclipsed landed in a Standard format dominated by Badgermole Cub ramp decks from Avatar: The Last Airbender. At Pro Tour Lorwyn Eclipsed (January 30 to February 1, 2026, in Richmond, Virginia), Cub decks made up nearly half the field but won only about 40% of their matches against everything else, according to Wizards' coverage. The breakout deck was Izzet Spellementals, which used cheap Elementals to discount Sunderflock and reset the opponent's board, and posted one of the best win rates of the event. Christoffer Larsen won the Pro Tour with Dimir Excruciator. Wizards named Spell Snare, Sear, Deceit, Wistfulness, Formidable Speaker and Requiting Hex as the set's most important new Standard tools.

Months later, the set is everywhere. As of October 2026, MTGGoldfish's Standard data shows Lorwyn Eclipsed cards in most top decks:

- **Izzet Spellementals** still plays Sunderflock and Spell Snare, and is one of the format's most played decks.
- **Dimir Midrange and Dimir Reanimator** use Requiting Hex and Spell Snare, and the reanimator lists play four Deceit.
- **5-color Elementals** is a full Lorwyn Eclipsed deck, built on Ashling, Rekindled, Flamebraider and four of the five Incarnations.
- **Landfall decks** add Sapling Nursery, and the five reprinted shocklands (Blood Crypt, Hallowed Fountain, Overgrown Tomb, Steam Vents and Temple Garden) show up across the format.

No Lorwyn Eclipsed card has been banned in any format as of October 2026.

The set also made a real splash in Modern. In Wizards' first look at the post-release Modern field, Formidable Speaker was the most-played new card, appearing in close to one in ten decks, including Eldrazi Ramp and Living End. Wistfulness and Deceit gave Living End new cascade targets, Moonshadow found a home in Grixis Shadow, Aurora Awakener in Domain Zoo, and Sygg, Wanderwine Wisdom in Merfolk. For how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Lorwyn Eclipsed worth owning

- **The shocklands**: Blood Crypt, Hallowed Fountain, Overgrown Tomb, Steam Vents and Temple Garden are mana-base staples in every format that allows them.
- **Formidable Speaker**: a creature tutor that made an impact in both Standard and Modern.
- **Deceit and Wistfulness**: flexible Incarnations that work as free spells in Modern and full creatures in Standard.
- **Spell Snare**: a precise one-mana counter that slots into blue decks in several formats.
- **Bitterbloom Bearer**: a steady stream of fliers for Faeries and Commander tables.
- **Bloom Tender and Springleaf Drum**: mana pieces Commander players reach for in multicolor decks.
- **Mutable Explorer**: a creature that brings a Mutavault with it, great in any creature-type deck.

Lorwyn Eclipsed rewards picking a tribe and committing, both in draft and in Constructed. For Standard, the Elemental shell around Sunderflock and the Incarnations is the most distinctive deck the set created, and its uncommons (Requiting Hex, Spell Snare, Sear) are the cheapest upgrades to almost any deck.
