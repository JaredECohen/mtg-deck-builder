Morningtide is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2008 as the second and final set of the Lorwyn block. It stays on the sunny, fairy-tale plane of Lorwyn but shifts the tribal focus from races like Elves and Merfolk to classes like Rogues, Warriors and Wizards. Small as it is, Morningtide printed some of the most important cards of its era: Bitterblossom, Mutavault, Vendilion Clique, Reveillark and Scapeshift.

## The story

Morningtide continues the Lorwyn novels by Cory J. Herndon and Scott McGough. Lorwyn is a plane of endless daylight where Elves, Kithkin, Merfolk, Faeries, Giants, Treefolk, Goblins and Flamekin live in a pastoral world with no real night. Under that bright surface, though, something is wrong, and the faerie queen Oona sits at the center of it.

The main thread follows Rhys, a Gilt-Leaf Elf hunter whose broken horns and a catastrophic use of poison magic in a battle made him an outcast among the beauty-obsessed Elves. He travels with a group of unlikely companions, including the Giants Brion and Kiel and an Elf woman who calls herself Maralen of the Mornsong. Maralen is not what she seems. The faeries of the Vendilion clique can't read her dreams, and even Oona treats her strangely.

The flamekin Ashling is the other lead. She had spent her life seeking a meeting with a greater elemental, but the yew sage Colfenor forced that meeting years early, and in Morningtide the elemental haunts her. Ashling's mind starts to break, and she sets off to hunt the creature that marked her, with the scholar Rosheen Meanderer and others drawn into her path.

The set ends with the story mid-stride, which is why it feels incomplete on its own. The full picture comes together across the rest of the cycle: Maralen is a self-aware avatar of Oona, and Oona's plan involves the Great Aurora, an event that flips sunny Lorwyn into its nightmarish mirror, Shadowmoor. Ashling's ritual at the moment of the Aurora goes wrong when she seizes the elemental's power for herself, and part of that power flows into Maralen. That setup pays off in Shadowmoor and Eventide, where Maralen eventually turns on Oona.

Several story characters appear as cards in Morningtide, including Maralen of the Mornsong, Rhys the Exiled and Vendilion Clique.

## Mechanics

### Class tribal

Lorwyn was about eight races. Morningtide is about five classes: Soldiers, Wizards, Warriors, Shamans and Rogues. Every class got a "Banneret" at common that reduces the cost of spells of its race and class, like Stonybrook Banneret for Merfolk and Wizards, and many cards hand out +1/+1 counters to creatures of a class as they enter. Kindred (then called tribal) cards return too, including spells like Notorious Throng that count as Rogues.

### Prowl

Prowl is an alternative cost for Rogue cards. If you dealt combat damage to a player this turn with a creature that shares a type with the prowl card, you can cast it for its cheaper prowl cost, and some cards get a bonus when you do. Latchkey Faerie is a four-mana 3/1 flier that costs three with prowl and draws a card if you paid that way. Earwig Squad exiles three cards from your opponent's library when cast for prowl. Prowl rewards evasive early drops, so Rogue decks played lots of cheap fliers and creatures with fear.

### Kinship

Kinship is an ability word for Shamans. At the start of your upkeep, you look at the top card of your library, and if it shares a creature type with the kinship creature you can reveal it for a bonus. Wolf-Skull Shaman makes a 2/2 Wolf, Winnower Patrol grows itself, and Leaf-Crowned Elder lets you play the revealed card for free. Changelings, which have every creature type, always count.

### Reinforce

Reinforce is an instant-speed ability you use from your hand: pay the cost and discard the card to put +1/+1 counters on a creature. Burrenton Bombardier is a 2/2 flier you can cast normally, or discard to give a creature two counters. It gives otherwise dead cards a combat-trick mode, and it works with the set's "creatures with counters" payoffs.

### Returning mechanics

Clash, evoke and changeling all carried over from Lorwyn. Reveillark and Spitebellows use evoke, which lets you cast a creature cheaply for just its leave-the-battlefield effect. Taurean Mauler and Chameleon Colossus are changelings. Our [keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers the evergreen keywords these cards lean on.

## Limited

Morningtide was drafted with Lorwyn in a Lorwyn-Lorwyn-Morningtide pack order, which was the format for Pro Tour Kuala Lumpur in early 2008. Players who had spent months on triple Lorwyn found it played quite differently. Morningtide's pack arriving last shifted the color balance, and white went from one of the weaker colors in triple Lorwyn to a strong one.

The biggest strategic question was prowl. Blue and black Rogue decks could assemble evasive early creatures, connect, and then cast Latchkey Faerie or Stinkdrinker Bandit at a discount. Because Morningtide was only one of three packs, class decks rarely came together fully, and the more reliable plan was to draft solid creatures in two colors and treat class synergies as a bonus. Changelings were valuable because they count for every type, and Weight of Conscience rewarded you for having two creatures that shared a type.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Morningtide entered a Standard that held Coldsnap, Time Spiral block, Tenth Edition and Lorwyn, and it stayed through Shadowmoor block, Shards of Alara and Magic 2010. In that time it shaped several of the format's best decks.

- **UB Faeries** was the defining deck of 2008 Standard. Bitterblossom gave it a steady stream of evasive Rogues that turned on prowl, Mutavault counted as a Faerie, and Vendilion Clique provided flash disruption. The deck stayed near the top of Standard until the block rotated.
- **Reveillark** decks, including the WR version known as "Boat Brew," used Reveillark to return value creatures and combo pieces when it left the battlefield.
- **Elves** decks in Standard used Heritage Druid alongside Lorwyn's Elves.

No Morningtide card was banned in Standard. The set rotated out when Zendikar released in October 2009.

The set's long tail is in Modern and Legacy. Bitterblossom was on Modern's original ban list in 2011 and was unbanned in February 2014. Scapeshift gave its name to a Modern combo deck that fetches Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle plus enough Mountains to win on the spot. Vendilion Clique was a fixture of blue tempo and control in Modern and Legacy for years, Mutavault plays in mono-colored aggro in Modern and Pioneer (where it's legal through its Magic 2014 reprint), and Heritage Druid powers Elves combo in Modern and Legacy. Negate is still reprinted and sideboarded constantly.

In Commander, Idyllic Tutor is a staple for enchantment decks, Door of Destinies and Shared Animosity are popular in tribal decks, and Maralen of the Mornsong anchors tutor-based builds. As of October 2026, no Morningtide card is banned in Modern, Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Morningtide worth owning

- **Bitterblossom**: a token engine that still sees play in Modern and Legacy.
- **Mutavault**: a creature land for almost any mono-colored aggro deck.
- **Vendilion Clique**: flash disruption and a fast clock in one card.
- **Scapeshift**: the namesake of a long-running Modern combo deck.
- **Heritage Druid**: the mana engine of Elves in Modern and Legacy.
- **Idyllic Tutor**: the only card that tutors any enchantment straight to hand.
- **Negate**: a cheap, flexible counter that every blue player uses eventually.

## The takeaway

Morningtide is a 150-card set with the impact of a full expansion, mostly thanks to Bitterblossom, Mutavault and Vendilion Clique. Its story is a middle chapter, so read it alongside Lorwyn and Shadowmoor. If you want to build with it today, a Modern Faeries-style tempo deck is the most faithful way to play its best cards.
