Modern Masters 2015 is the Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in May 2015, the second in the line of draftable sets made to put Modern staples back into circulation. Its 249 cards came from Eighth Edition through New Phyrexia, with Tarmogoyf, Karn Liberated, Mox Opal, Noble Hierarch and the three Eldrazi titans at the top, and a pair of Eldrazi lands that would end up at the center of one of Modern's most lopsided seasons.

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | MM2 (officially "Modern Masters 2015 Edition") |
| Release date | May 22, 2015 (Magic Online: May 29, 2015) |
| Setting | Multiplanar reprint set, with a Zendikar and Scars of Mirrodin lean |
| Set size | 249 cards: 101 common, 80 uncommon, 53 rare, 15 mythic rare, all reprints |
| Product | 15-card draft boosters, each with a foil card in place of a basic land; boxes held 24 packs, exactly one eight-player draft |
| Headline mechanics | Eldrazi and annihilator, metalcraft and affinity, proliferate, bloodthirst, graft, convoke, soulshift |
| Formats | Not a Standard set. Every card keeps its normal legality; all were Modern-legal on release |

## What the set was for

Modern was barely four years old in 2015, and its staples were scattered across sets that had sold out years earlier. Modern Masters (2013) had proven that a draftable reprint set could ease that, and Modern Masters 2015 followed with a deeper card pool. The rule was simple: every card had to be legal in Modern and first printed between Eighth Edition and New Phyrexia.

Lead designer Erik Lauer and lead developer Tom LaPille picked themes that were already present in the constructed staples, then built a draft environment around them. With Zendikar and Scars of Mirrodin blocks newly eligible, the set leaned on Eldrazi from Rise of the Eldrazi and artifacts from Scars. The tagline was "Draft Like a Master."

There's no story in a Masters set, but there was one short piece of fiction: "The Dragon's Errand," a Kamigawa tale featuring Kiki-Jiki and Meloku that ran with the set's release.

The packaging made a small piece of history too: English boosters were made of recyclable paperboard instead of foil wrap, an experiment that raised some worries about resealing at the time.

## The reprints that mattered

### Modern staples

- **Tarmogoyf**: the two-mana green beater that defined Modern midrange.
- **Noble Hierarch**: an exalted mana creature for green aggro.
- **Karn Liberated**: the Tron deck's top end.
- **Mox Opal**: free mana for Affinity, a legendary artifact with metalcraft.
- **Cryptic Command and Vendilion Clique**: blue control staples.
- **Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker and Splinter Twin**: the pieces of Modern's best-known two-card combo.
- **Dark Confidant**: the card-advantage two-drop for black aggro.
- **Spellskite, Leyline of Sanctity and Surgical Extraction**: sideboard regulars.
- **Lightning Bolt**, moved up to uncommon, and **Dismember**, one of the defining Phyrexian-mana removal spells.

### The Eldrazi package

- **Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Kozilek, Butcher of Truth and Ulamog, the Infinite Gyre** at mythic.
- **Eldrazi Temple** (moved down to uncommon) and **Eye of Ugin** (moved down to rare), the two lands that accelerate Eldrazi.
- **All Is Dust** and **Artisan of Kozilek** for big-mana decks.

### Commander and casual favorites

- **Primeval Titan, Elesh Norn, Grand Cenobite and Iona, Shield of Emeria** at mythic.
- **Wildfire, Comet Storm and Mystic Snake** at rare.
- All ten Ravnica bounce lands at uncommon, plus **Expedition Map, Everflowing Chalice, Wayfarer's Bauble and Evolving Wilds**.

### Rarity changes

Several cards moved rarity to make the draft work or to fit the mythic rare slot, which didn't exist before Shards of Alara:

- **Up:** Bitterblossom went from rare to mythic. Lightning Bolt, Expedition Map, Cranial Plating and all ten bounce lands went from common to uncommon.
- **Down:** All Is Dust, Eye of Ugin and Indomitable Archangel dropped from mythic to rare, and Eldrazi Temple went from rare to uncommon.
- **Way down:** Scion of the Wild went from rare to common.

Lower rarities meant more copies, which is why the Eldrazi lands were so easy to find a few months later.

### Modern Masters Weekend

As with the first Modern Masters, the release was tied to a weekend of Grand Prix events played with the set's Limited, held in Las Vegas, Utrecht and Chiba. The Las Vegas event drew well over 7,000 players, a record for a single Magic tournament at the time, and showed how much demand there was for a high-powered draft format.

## Limited

Modern Masters 2015 was built for draft from the ground up, and its archetypes were spelled out during coverage of the Modern Masters Weekend Grand Prix:

- **White-blue: metalcraft and artifacts.** Ethercaste Knight and Glassdust Hulk with cheap artifacts.
- **Blue-black: proliferate.** Spread the Sickness and Tezzeret's Gambit, alongside -1/-1 counter cards.
- **Black-red: bloodthirst aggro.** Hit early so creatures enter bigger; Ashenmoor Gouger is the kind of beater it wants.
- **Red-green: domain ramp.** Rampant Growth and the bounce lands into big spells.
- **Green-white: convoke tokens.** Selesnya Guildmage and Sigil Blessing on a wide board.
- **White-black: spirits and soulshift.** Restless Apparition and recursion.
- **Blue-red: Elementals.** Shrewd Hatchling and other hybrid creatures.
- **Black-green: sacrifice.** Drooling Groodion and Necrogenesis.
- **Red-white: double strike and Equipment.** Boros Swiftblade and Hearthfire Hobgoblin.
- **Green-blue: graft.** Plaxcaster Frogling moving counters around.

The real twist was colorless Eldrazi ramp. Kozilek's Predator and Nest Invader make Eldrazi Spawn tokens, and Eldrazi Temple and Artisan of Kozilek let any green deck dream big. Drafters who took the Spawn makers early often ended games with annihilator.

At common and uncommon, the best removal included Lightning Bolt, Dismember, Burst Lightning, Electrolyze, Vapor Snag and Spread the Sickness, and Mulldrifter was a premium pick in any blue deck. The format rewarded a good [mana curve](/blog/mana-curve-explained), since the bloodthirst and double strike decks punished slow starts.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Modern Masters 2015 added no new cards, but its reprints shaped Modern in two ways.

First, supply. Tarmogoyf, Noble Hierarch, Karn and Mox Opal were all expensive bottlenecks before the set, and a fresh print run opened up Jund, Zoo, Tron and Affinity to more players during 2015.

Second, the Eldrazi lands. In January 2016 Oath of the Gatewatch added cheap, powerful colorless Eldrazi such as Thought-Knot Seer. Paired with Eldrazi Temple and Eye of Ugin, Eldrazi decks could cast three- and four-drops on turn two. Eldrazi took six of the Top 8 slots at Pro Tour Oath of the Gatewatch, and the period became known as "Eldrazi Winter." Wizards banned **Eye of Ugin** in Modern on April 4, 2016, keeping Eldrazi Temple legal. As of October 2026, Eye of Ugin is still banned in Modern.

Two other MM2 cards have ban histories:

- **Splinter Twin** was banned in Modern on January 18, 2016, and unbanned in the December 16, 2024 announcement.
- **Mox Opal** was banned in Modern in January 2020 and was also unbanned on December 16, 2024.

In Commander, Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Primeval Titan and Iona, Shield of Emeria are banned as of October 2026. In Vintage, Lodestone Golem is restricted. For how these lists work, see our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) and the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

## Cards from Modern Masters 2015 worth owning

- **Tarmogoyf**: a Modern and Legacy fixture since it was first printed in 2007.
- **Noble Hierarch**: still the best green one-drop for aggressive decks.
- **Cryptic Command**: the classic blue control finisher's best friend.
- **Eldrazi Temple**: the core of every Eldrazi deck in Modern and Legacy.
- **Splinter Twin**: legal again in Modern since December 2024, with Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker as its classic partner.
- **Mox Opal**: back in Modern artifact decks.
- **Primeval Titan**: banned in Commander but still a Modern ramp payoff.

## The takeaway

Modern Masters 2015 is best remembered for what it reprinted and for the Eldrazi lands that came back to bite Modern a year later. If you collect older Modern staples, its Tarmogoyf, Hierarch, Cryptic and Twin printings are as good as any.
