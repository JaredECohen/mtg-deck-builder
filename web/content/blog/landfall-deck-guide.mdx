In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), a landfall deck turns every land you play into an extra effect. Landfall abilities trigger whenever a land enters the battlefield under your control, so a deck built around them plays extra lands each turn, uses fetch lands to get two triggers from one land drop, and runs payoffs that grow creatures, make tokens or draw cards. The result is a deck where your mana base is also your engine.

This guide covers how landfall works, the three pieces every landfall deck needs and how the archetype changes between Commander and 60-card formats. If you want the broader picture of decks that play lots of lands, the [ramp deck guide](/blog/mtg-ramp-deck-guide) covers the closely related strategy.

## How landfall works

Landfall is an **ability word**. It has no rules meaning of its own. It just labels abilities that start with "Whenever a land you control enters." Some older cards phrase it as "enters the battlefield under your control," but the meaning is the same.

A few rules details matter for building:

- **Any land entering counts.** Playing a land from your hand, putting one onto the battlefield with a spell, or returning one from your graveyard all trigger landfall.
- **It's a triggered ability.** Each land that enters triggers each landfall ability you control once. Two lands entering at the same time trigger each ability twice.
- **It works on any turn.** Your normal land drop only happens on your turn, but effects that put lands onto the battlefield at instant speed trigger landfall on opponents' turns too.
- **It counts lands under your control.** An opponent's land entering doesn't trigger your landfall abilities, unless the card says otherwise.

Landfall first appeared in the original Zendikar block and has returned in later sets, so there's a deep pool of cards to choose from.

## The three pieces of a landfall deck

Every landfall deck is built from three groups of cards:

1. **Payoffs:** cards with landfall abilities, or that reward you for lands entering.
2. **Extra land drops and land ramp:** ways to get more lands onto the battlefield each turn.
3. **Multipliers:** lands and effects that turn one land drop into more than one trigger.

The balance between them changes with the format, but a deck missing any of the three tends to feel flat.

## Landfall payoffs

Payoffs fall into a few categories. Mixing them gives you both pressure and staying power.

### Creatures that grow

Cheap creatures that get bigger when a land enters are the core of aggressive landfall decks.

- **Steppe Lynx** is a one-mana 0/1 that gets +2/+2 until end of turn for each landfall trigger.
- **Plated Geopede** is a two-mana 1/1 with first strike that also gets +2/+2 until end of turn on landfall.

These creatures are small when you aren't playing lands, so they want a steady stream of triggers to keep up.

### Token makers

Token payoffs build a board over time and are the backbone of most Commander landfall decks.

- **Rampaging Baloths** makes a 4/4 Beast every time a land enters.
- **Avenger of Zendikar** makes a 0/1 Plant for each land you control when it enters, then puts a +1/+1 counter on each Plant you control with every landfall trigger.
- **Scute Swarm** makes a 1/1 Insect on each landfall, and once you control six or more lands, it makes a copy of itself instead.

If your deck leans this way, the [tokens deck guide](/blog/mtg-tokens-deck-guide) has more on going wide.

### Card draw and mana

- **Lotus Cobra** adds one mana of any color whenever a land enters, which can let you play a land and immediately cast more spells.
- **Tatyova, Benthic Druid** draws a card and gains you 1 life on each landfall trigger.

Card draw payoffs are what keep a landfall deck from running out of gas, since an engine that plays several lands a turn also runs through its hand quickly.

### Enchantments and other permanents

Not every payoff needs to be a creature. The Retreat enchantment cycle includes cards like Retreat to Kazandu, which puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature or gains you 2 life on each landfall trigger, and Retreat to Coralhelm, which taps or untaps a creature or scries 1. Noncreature payoffs survive creature removal and board wipes.

## Extra land drops

Normally you can play one land per turn. Landfall decks break that limit.

| Card | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Exploration | You may play an additional land on each of your turns. |
| Azusa, Lost but Seeking | You may play two additional lands on each of your turns. |
| Oracle of Mul Daya | You may play an additional land each turn, play with the top card of your library revealed, and play lands from the top of your library. |
| Explore | You may play an additional land this turn. Draw a card. |
| Burgeoning | Whenever an opponent plays a land, you may put a land card from your hand onto the battlefield. |

Extra land drops are only useful if you have lands to play, so they pair well with card draw and with effects that let you play lands from places other than your hand.

### Ramp spells

Spells that put lands onto the battlefield trigger landfall too. Rampant Growth puts one basic land onto the battlefield. Cultivate and Kodama's Reach put one onto the battlefield and one into your hand, which also feeds your next land drop. Harrow sacrifices a land and puts two basic lands onto the battlefield untapped, which is two landfall triggers at instant speed.

## Multipliers: fetch lands and friends

Multipliers get more than one trigger from a single land drop.

### Fetch lands

A fetch land is a land that sacrifices itself to search your library for another land and put it onto the battlefield. Playing the fetch land triggers landfall once, and the land it finds triggers it again: two triggers from one land drop.

- **Evolving Wilds** and **Terramorphic Expanse** find a basic land that enters tapped.
- **Fabled Passage** finds a basic land tapped, then untaps it if you control four or more lands.
- **Prismatic Vista** costs 1 life and finds a basic land untapped.
- The two-color fetch lands, like **Wooded Foothills**, cost 1 life and find a land with either of two basic land types, untapped.

You can also hold a fetch land and crack it later, such as on an opponent's turn, to trigger landfall at a useful time.

### Bounce lands

Bounce lands, like Simic Growth Chamber, enter tapped and return a land you control to its owner's hand. That land can be played again next turn for another trigger. With extra land drops, you can replay it the same turn.

### Playing lands from the graveyard

Cards like Crucible of Worlds let you play lands from your graveyard. Combined with fetch lands, every land drop can be a fetch land that triggers landfall twice. Life from the Loam returns up to three land cards from your graveyard to your hand, refilling your land drops, and Splendid Reclamation returns all land cards from your graveyard to the battlefield at once for a burst of triggers.

## Building landfall in Commander

Commander is the natural home of landfall. The format is slow enough for engines to come online, singleton deck building rewards a wide pool of payoffs, and 40 life gives you time to set up.

### Choosing a commander

Several commanders are built around landfall:

- **Omnath, Locus of Rage** makes a 5/5 Elemental on each landfall trigger and deals damage when Elementals die.
- **Omnath, Locus of Creation** draws a card when it enters, then gives escalating rewards the first, second and third time its landfall ability resolves each turn, ending with damage to each opponent.
- **Tatyova, Benthic Druid** turns every land into a card and a point of life.
- **Aesi, Tyrant of Gyre Strait** gives you an extra land drop each turn and draws a card on landfall.

[How to build your first Commander deck](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) covers the general deck structure. For landfall specifically:

### A sample Commander structure

| Group | Rough count |
| --- | --- |
| Lands | 38-42 |
| Ramp and extra land drops | 10-14 |
| Landfall payoffs | 12-18 |
| Card draw | 8-10 |
| Removal and interaction | 8-10 |
| Board wipes and protection | 3-5 |

The land count is higher than in most Commander decks, because lands are your fuel. Fetch lands, bounce lands and lands that sacrifice themselves are all worth more here than in a typical deck.

### Commander tips

- **Draw cards.** A landfall engine that runs out of lands in hand stops. Tatyova-style effects and card draw spells keep it going.
- **Protect the engine.** Landfall payoffs are often the first thing opponents remove. Having more payoffs than you need, and a few ways to protect key ones, matters.
- **Watch the threat level.** A board that grows every turn draws attention in multiplayer. Expect to be targeted once your engine is obviously running.

## Building landfall in 60-card formats

In 60-card formats, landfall usually takes one of two shapes.

### Aggressive landfall

The aggressive version uses cheap creatures that grow on landfall, like Steppe Lynx and Plated Geopede, with fetch lands to double the triggers. The deck plays a land every turn, attacks with creatures that are much bigger on its own turn than on the opponent's, and finishes with burn or pump. It runs a lower land count than you might expect, because it doesn't want to flood, but it relies on fetch lands to get two triggers per turn.

### Value landfall

The value version plays a higher land count, ramp spells and extra land drops, and uses payoffs like Lotus Cobra and token makers to build a board and outgrow the opponent. It plays more like a ramp deck, with landfall as the reason its lands do more than produce mana.

Either way, playing four copies of your key payoffs makes the deck much more consistent than a singleton list. Check each card's legality in your chosen format before you build.

## Common landfall mistakes

- **Playing your land too early.** If a landfall payoff is in your hand, cast it first when you can, then play the land. Order matters.
- **Cracking fetch lands without thinking.** Holding a fetch land until you have a payoff on the battlefield, or until combat, can be worth more than cracking it right away.
- **Too few lands.** Landfall decks are tempted to cut lands for payoffs. Without lands in hand, the payoffs do nothing.
- **All payoffs, no engine.** A deck with twenty payoffs and no extra land drops or multipliers only gets one trigger a turn.

The [deck archetypes overview](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains where landfall fits among other strategies, and why it often sits between ramp and aggro.

## The takeaway

A landfall deck combines three things: payoffs that reward lands entering, extra land drops and ramp to get more lands out, and multipliers like fetch lands that double the triggers. In Commander, lean on token makers and card draw with a high land count. In 60-card formats, pick an aggressive or value shape and play multiple copies of your best payoffs. Play payoffs before lands, and never cut so many lands that the engine runs dry.
