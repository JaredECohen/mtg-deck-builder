Magic: The Gathering has always needed a front door, and between 1996 and 2017 Wizards built several small sets just for brand-new players: the Introductory Two-Player Set, Starter 1999, Starter 2000 and the Welcome Decks of 2016 and 2017. Most of their cards are simple reprints, but a few are surprisingly important, starting with Grim Tutor, which debuted in Starter 1999 and spent two decades as one of Magic's rarest tutors.

## What the sets were for

Magic's early rules were hard to pick up from a booster pack. Every few years Wizards tried a new way in. Portal (1997) had its own simplified rules. Starter 1999 kept the real rules but trimmed the cards: almost no artifacts or enchantments, very few instants, and no creatures with tap abilities, so a new player could learn turns, combat and spells without stack puzzles. Later products like the Welcome Decks did the same job with modern templating, handing out free decks so a new player could try the game at a store for no cost.

None of these sets has a story. They're set loosely on Dominaria (the early ones) or nowhere in particular (the Welcome Decks), and their art and flavor text come mostly from the original printings.

This guide is a roundup, so each product gets its own short section, followed by the key cards and what still matters today. For the basics these sets were teaching, see [how to play Magic](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) and [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Introductory Two-Player Set (1996)

This boxed set was the European and Asian version of the American Rivals Quick Start Set. It contained two prebuilt decks of 60 cards for two players, called Kazz's deck and Zakk's deck, plus player guides that walked you through a demo game. The cards look like Fourth Edition cards with a 1996 copyright line, and a gift-box version added a Fourth Edition rulebook, two booster packs and glass beads for counters.

The cards are all classic early commons and uncommons: Fireball, Terror, Dark Ritual, Unsummon, Power Sink, Pyrotechnics, Jayemdae Tome, Grizzly Bears and Hill Giant. Phantom Monster was the only addition over the US version. Nothing here was new, so the set matters mostly to collectors.

## Starter 1999

Starter (now called Starter 1999 to avoid confusion) was the bigger project. Lead designer Bill Rose and a team that included Mark Rosewater built a full 173-card set with 15-card boosters, five 40-card theme decks and a two-player starter box with playmats and a guide. Rules text was printed in bold and separated from flavor text by a thick line, and all terms matched the main game, a first for a beginner product.

It had 26 new cards. Most are simple, like the vanilla 1/1 one-drops Eager Cadet, Muck Rats and Willow Elf, or the 6/6 for seven mana Vizzerdrix. A few stand out:

- **Grim Tutor**: three mana, search your library for any card, lose 3 life. It was a rare in a low-print beginner set, so it became one of the hardest-to-find cards in Magic until Core Set 2021 reprinted it in 2020.
- **Thunder Dragon**: a seven-mana 5/5 flier that deals 3 damage to each creature without flying when it enters.
- **Tidings**: a five-mana sorcery that draws four cards.
- **Pride of Lions** and the reprinted **Lone Wolf** and **Thorn Elemental**: creatures that can deal their combat damage as though they weren't blocked. Wizards thought trample was confusing for beginners, so it tried this instead.

The reprints were generous for a beginner set: Counterspell, Time Warp, Armageddon, Nature's Lore, Wood Elves, Sleight of Hand, Man-o'-War, Gravedigger, Relentless Assault, Summer Bloom and Ancient Craving among them. Starter 1999 cards became legal in Legacy and Vintage on October 20, 2005.

## Starter 2000

Starter 2000 was a single product: a game box with two 22-card decks, two 15-card packs of more advanced cards (artifacts, enchantments and instants), two playmats, a rulebook, play guides and a CD-ROM of mini-games. The box also included Rhox, a green creature that can deal combat damage as though unblocked and regenerate, as a premium card with exclusive art.

All of its cards were reprints from Sixth Edition, Portal and Starter, including Llanowar Elves, Counterspell, Shock, Terror, Giant Growth and Prodigal Sorcerer. It's a footnote today, mostly for collectors chasing every printing.

## Welcome Deck 2016 and Welcome Deck 2017

The Welcome Decks were Wizards' free introduction for the 2010s: five mono-color 30-card decks that stores could hand to anyone who wanted to try Magic. Each year's decks came with a small set of reprints, numbered separately, that also appeared in Deck Builder's Toolkits and Booster Battle Packs.

- **Welcome Deck 2016** has 16 cards, from Serra Angel and Shivan Dragon to Sengir Vampire, Nightmare, Aegis Angel, Sphinx of Magosi and Soul of the Harvest. It accompanied Shadows over Innistrad.
- **Welcome Deck 2017** has 30 cards, adding Garruk's Horde, Victory's Herald, Air Elemental, Stalking Tiger and Rabid Bite, and accompanied Amonkhet.

Unlike the older starter sets, these cards were Standard-legal: Welcome Deck 2016 for as long as Shadows over Innistrad was in Standard, and Welcome Deck 2017 for as long as Amonkhet was. That also makes them legal in Pioneer today. Wizards later wound down the free Welcome Deck program in favor of other new-player products.

## The meta: how these cards played in Constructed

None of these sets was designed with tournaments in mind, but a few cards had real effects.

- **Grim Tutor** became a fixture in high-powered Commander decks once players could find copies, and the Core Set 2021 reprint made it legal in Modern and Pioneer as well. As of October 2026 it's not on the official Game Changers list.
- **Counterspell** in Starter 1999 and Starter 2000 is one of many printings of a card that still defines blue in Legacy, Pauper and Commander.
- **Summer Bloom**, a Visions card reprinted in Starter 1999, was banned in Modern on January 18, 2016, as part of the same announcement that banned Splinter Twin. It fueled the Amulet Bloom deck. As of October 2026 it's still banned in Modern.
- **Dark Ritual** remains a Legacy and Pauper combo card, and **Armageddon**, the classic land wipe, is still legal in Legacy and Commander for anyone brave enough.
- **The Welcome Deck cards** had no real Standard impact, but they kept 1990s icons like Serra Angel and Shivan Dragon in the Standard card pool for new players.

The bigger impact was on players: Starter 1999's tighter templating and bold rules text were early steps toward the plainer, beginner-friendly templating that later core sets used.

## Cards from these sets worth owning

- **Grim Tutor**: the reason most players look up Starter 1999 at all.
- **Counterspell**: a classic you'll use in almost any blue deck.
- **Nature's Lore and Wood Elves**: cheap, strong Forest ramp for Commander.
- **Time Warp**: a simple extra-turn spell that many blue Commander decks still play.
- **Dark Ritual**: fast mana for Legacy and casual combo.
- **Garruk's Horde and Soul of the Harvest**: solid green Commander value creatures.

## The takeaway

Magic's beginner sets are mostly a history of how Wizards taught the game, but a couple of them, Starter 1999 above all, quietly carry important cards. If you're digging through an old collection, check for Grim Tutor and the Starter 1999 rares first.
