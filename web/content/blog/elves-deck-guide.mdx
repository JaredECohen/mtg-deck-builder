In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), an Elves deck is a green tribal deck that floods the battlefield with cheap Elf creatures, uses the mana many of them produce to cast even more, and then wins with a single huge turn: pumping the whole team, drawing a pile of cards or casting a finisher that scales with how many creatures you have. When it works, it can go from a handful of 1/1s to a lethal attack faster than almost any other creature deck.

This guide covers the pieces that make Elves work, how to sequence an explosive turn, where the deck is weak, and how Elf tribal changes in Commander. If you're new to tribal decks in general, read the [tribal decks guide](/blog/mtg-tribal-decks-guide) first. It explains lords, payoffs and creature counts for any tribe, so this guide focuses on what's specific to Elves.

## Why Elves are different from other tribes

Most tribes are built around combat. Goblins attack, Merfolk evade, Zombies come back. Elves have plenty of combat cards, but the tribe's real identity is mana. A large share of Elves tap for green mana, and several tap for more mana the more Elves you control.

That makes an Elves deck a hybrid of tribal aggro and [ramp](/blog/mtg-ramp-deck-guide). It plays creatures that are also mana sources, so each Elf you cast makes the next one easier to cast. The deck's best draws snowball: a first-turn mana Elf leads to two plays on turn two, which leads to a board and a mana surplus on turn three that most decks can't match.

## The building blocks

### Mana Elves

These are the foundation. The classic examples cost one green mana and tap for one green mana:

- **Llanowar Elves:** the original one-mana Elf. A 1/1 that taps to add `{G}`.
- **Elvish Mystic:** functionally the same card under a different name, which matters in a 60-card deck because you can play four of each.
- **Fyndhorn Elves:** another copy of the same effect from an older set.

Then come the Elves that scale:

- **Priest of Titania:** taps for one green mana for each Elf on the battlefield, counting every Elf, including your opponent's.
- **Elvish Archdruid:** taps for one green mana for each Elf you control, and also gives your other Elves +1/+1.
- **Heritage Druid:** lets you tap three untapped Elves you control to add three green mana. Because its cost taps other Elves rather than using the tap symbol, those Elves can be ones that just entered the battlefield and still have summoning sickness.

Heritage Druid is a good example of why reading exact wording matters. Most mana Elves can't tap the turn they arrive, but Heritage Druid turns freshly cast Elves into mana right away.

### Lords

Lords give other creatures of their type a bonus. Elf lords turn a board of small creatures into a real threat:

- **Elvish Champion:** other Elves get +1/+1 and forestwalk, which makes them unblockable while the defending player controls a Forest.
- **Elvish Archdruid:** already mentioned as a mana source, it's also a lord.
- **Imperious Perfect:** other Elves you control get +1/+1, and it can pay `{G}` and tap to create a 1/1 green Elf Warrior token, so it keeps growing the board on its own.

Lords stack. Two of them on the battlefield give each other Elf +2/+2, which quickly turns 1/1s into dangerous attackers.

### Card draw

Elves spend cards quickly, so the deck needs a way to refill:

- **Elvish Visionary:** a two-mana Elf that draws a card when it enters the battlefield. It replaces itself, which helps keep your hand stocked.
- **Glimpse of Nature:** a one-mana sorcery that makes you draw a card whenever you cast a creature spell for the rest of the turn. With cheap Elves and mana Elves, it can draw a large chunk of your deck in one turn.

### Payoffs and finishers

These are the cards that turn a big board into a win:

- **Ezuri, Renegade Leader:** a legendary Elf with an expensive activated ability that gives your Elves +3/+3 and trample until end of turn. With a wide board, one activation is usually lethal. It can also regenerate other Elves for `{G}`.
- **Craterhoof Behemoth:** an eight-mana creature with haste that, when it enters, gives your creatures +1/+1 and trample for each creature you control until end of turn. In a deck that produces huge amounts of mana and creatures, it's often a one-card win.
- **Gaea's Cradle:** a legendary land that taps for one green mana for each creature you control. It's an enormous mana source in decks that support it.

## How an explosive turn works

The signature Elves turn usually follows the same shape:

1. **Develop mana early.** Play a one-mana Elf on turn one, then add more mana Elves and cheap Elves over the next turn or two.
2. **Grow the board.** Cast lords and scaling mana Elves. Each new Elf adds to both your mana and your Elf count.
3. **Chain spells.** With scaling mana sources online, each Elf you cast makes more mana available, letting you cast several creatures in one turn. Card draw like Glimpse of Nature or Elvish Visionary keeps your hand from running dry.
4. **Cash in.** Spend the surplus on a finisher: activate Ezuri, cast Craterhoof Behemoth, or just attack with a team boosted by several lords.

The key skill is **sequencing**. Cast the cards that produce mana or draw cards first, and save the payoff for last, when it does the most. Elvish Archdruid and Priest of Titania can't tap for mana the turn they enter unless something gives them haste, so play your scaling mana creatures a turn before you plan to go off. Count your mana and your Elves before you start a chain, so you know whether the turn ends in a win or just a bigger board.

## Weaknesses

Elves is powerful but fragile in specific ways. Knowing them helps you build around them and play around them.

### Board wipes

The deck's biggest weakness is mass removal. A whole board of small creatures is exactly what cards like Pyroclasm, which deals 2 damage to each creature, are designed to punish. Wrath of God or similar sweepers wipe out everything at once, including your mana. Since your creatures are your mana sources, a sweeper can leave you without enough mana to rebuild.

**How to play around it:** against decks you know have sweepers, don't overextend. Keep a few Elves in hand so you can rebuild after a wipe, and try to set up a lethal turn before your opponent has the mana for a sweeper.

### Early removal on key Elves

Cheap removal on your one-mana Elf slows your whole game plan. Removal on a lord or scaling mana Elf can turn an explosive turn into a fizzle. Elves tend to win by having more threats than the opponent has answers, so plenty of redundancy matters.

### Running out of cards

Elves can empty its hand fast. If your big turn doesn't win, you may be left with a board of small creatures and nothing in hand. That's why card draw is so important: it gives you fuel for a second wave.

### Blockers and life gain

Since most Elves are small, decks with large blockers can stall your attacks until you assemble a lord or finisher. Life gain also makes it harder to win with a single alpha strike.

## Elves in Commander

Elves are one of the most popular tribes in Commander, where their mana and wide boards shine in multiplayer games. A few things change in the 100-card singleton format:

- **One of each card.** You can't run four Llanowar Elves, so you need many different Elves to reach a good density. The tribe's huge card pool makes this easy.
- **Bigger payoffs.** With more time in a typical Commander game, expensive finishers become much more realistic. Cards that care about the number of creatures you control scale well in multiplayer.
- **A reliable engine.** Your commander is always available, so choose one that is a payoff on its own.

Popular Elf commanders include **Ezuri, Renegade Leader**, the mono-green team pump; **Lathril, Blade of the Elves**, a black-green commander that creates Elf tokens when it deals combat damage and can tap a large group of Elves to drain each opponent; and **Marwyn, the Nurturer**, a mono-green Elf that grows as other Elves enter and taps for mana equal to its power.

Elf decks in Commander still need the basics every Commander deck needs: removal, card draw and protection against board wipes. The [tokens deck guide](/blog/mtg-tokens-deck-guide) covers how go-wide strategies protect themselves and close out games, which applies directly to Elves.

## Building your Elves deck

For a 60-card Elves deck, a simple framework looks like this:

- **A low land count.** Mana Elves stand in for some lands, so Elf decks can often run fewer lands than other creature decks.
- **A high creature count.** Most of the deck should be Elves.
- **A few lords and payoffs.** Enough to turn your board into a threat.
- **Some card draw.** To refuel after you've emptied your hand.
- **A little interaction.** A few removal spells or protection cards so you're not helpless against an opposing threat.

The [60-card deck building guide](/blog/how-to-build-a-60-card-deck) walks through land counts, curves and four-ofs step by step.

## The takeaway

Elves win by turning cheap creatures into mana, mana into more creatures, and a wide board into one overwhelming turn. Build with redundant mana Elves, a few lords, card draw and a finisher, sequence your mana and draw before your payoffs, and respect board wipes by keeping a few Elves back. In Commander, pick a commander that's an engine on its own and fill the deck with as many different Elves as you can.
