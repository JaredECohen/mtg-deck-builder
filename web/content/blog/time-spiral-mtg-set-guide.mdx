Time Spiral is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2006 that returned to a shattered Dominaria and built an entire set out of nostalgia. It brought back a dozen old mechanics, added suspend and split second, and slipped one purple-rarity "timeshifted" reprint of a classic card into every booster. Its cards, from Living End to Ancestral Vision to Krosan Grip, are still played in older formats today.

## The story

Time Spiral picks up roughly three centuries after the Phyrexian Invasion that ended the Weatherlight Saga. Dominaria won that war, but the plane never recovered. Salt rain falls on dead forests, swamps are full of rotting Phyrexian remains, and mana itself is draining away. Worse, the plane is riddled with time rifts, tears in reality created by the great disasters of Dominaria's past, like the Sylex blast that ended the Brothers' War and the destruction of the academy island Tolaria. People, places and creatures from other eras flicker in and out of existence, which is the in-world explanation for the timeshifted cards.

The main source is Scott McGough's novel *Time Spiral*. Its hero is Teferi, the planeswalker who saved part of his homeland, Zhalfir, and the island continent of Shiv during the Invasion by phasing them out of time. Now Shiv is about to phase back in, and the land has changed so much in three hundred years that its return could tear the plane apart. Teferi returns with his old friend Jhoira, an artificer from the Weatherlight crew, to find a solution.

Along the way they recruit two young people with unusual planeswalker sparks: Radha, a half-elf warrior who wants to become a true Keldon and can draw power from the rift over Skyshroud, and Venser, a stubborn artificer from Urborg who has built a teleportation device. When the group gets pulled into the Urborg rift, they witness Dominaria's past disasters, and they are spat out on a beach where a disembodied voice tricks Venser. It uses the sparks of Venser and Radha to regain a body and reveals itself as Nicol Bolas, the elder dragon planeswalker, who had been reduced to a bodiless voice. Teferi cannot beat Bolas, but he persuades the dragon that the rifts threaten the whole Multiverse, and Bolas leaves.

In the climax, Radha defeats the Gathan warlord menacing Skyshroud and becomes the new warlord of Keld. Teferi pours his own life force into the rift over Shiv. It closes and Shiv returns safely, but the effort burns out his spark, and Teferi becomes a mortal wizard. That is the version of him printed on the card Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir. Planar Chaos and Future Sight continue the struggle against the remaining rifts.

## Mechanics

### Suspend

Suspend sends a spell into the future. Instead of casting a suspended card normally, you pay its suspend cost and exile it with a number of time counters. Each of your upkeeps you remove one counter, and when the last one is gone you cast it for free. Rift Bolt costs `{2}{R}` to cast, or you can suspend it for `{R}` and it deals 3 damage on your next turn. Some suspend cards have no mana cost at all, so the only way to cast them is through suspend. Ancestral Vision draws three cards four turns after you suspend it for `{U}`, and Living End, the famous mass-reanimation spell, is suspended for three turns.

### Split second

While a spell with split second is on the stack, no one can cast spells or activate abilities other than mana abilities. In practice, it can't be countered or responded to in the usual way. Krosan Grip destroys an artifact or enchantment before its controller can sacrifice or use it, and Sudden Shock deals 2 damage before the target can be saved.

### Flash

Flash, the ability to cast a nonland spell any time you could cast an instant, existed long before Time Spiral, but this set made it a keyword. Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir has flash and gives it to all your creature cards. Flash is now an evergreen keyword.

### Returning mechanics

Nearly every old mechanic got a cameo: buyback on Spell Burst, echo on Firemaw Kavu, flanking on Blazing Blade Askari, flashback on Momentary Blink and Strangling Soot, madness on Dark Withering, morph on Brine Elemental, shadow on Looter il-Kor, and storm on Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens. Slivers returned too, with dozens of new ones like Gemhide Sliver and Fury Sliver. Our [MTG keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) defines each of these.

### Timeshifted cards

Each booster held one timeshifted card, a straight reprint of a pre-Mirrodin card in the old card frame with a purple expansion symbol. The 121-card subset ranged from Akroma, Angel of Wrath, Psionic Blast and Call of the Herd to Dragonstorm and Tormod's Crypt. They counted as part of Time Spiral for format purposes.

## Limited

Time Spiral was drafted as triple Time Spiral and later with Planar Chaos and Future Sight packs. It had a reputation as one of the most complex Limited formats ever. A typical game had face-down morphs, suspended creatures counting down, flanking and shadow attackers, Slivers sharing abilities between both players, and timeshifted cards you might not recognize.

Rather than clean two-color archetypes, the format rewarded careful card evaluation:

- **Suspend creatures** like Errant Ephemeron and Durkwood Baloth gave you a big body for a small investment early, at the cost of waiting.
- **Morph** made combat a guessing game, as it would again in later sets.
- **Slivers** could be strong, but they helped any opponent with Slivers as well.
- **Shadow and flanking creatures** were hard to block, which made aggressive white and black decks dangerous.
- **Thallids and Saprolings** in green and black gave a slower, grinding plan.

Removal at common, like Strangling Soot, Dark Withering, Assassinate and Temporal Isolation, was valuable, and card advantage from flashback mattered in long games. If you are learning to draft, our guide to [reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) helps with the keyword-heavy text.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Time Spiral joined a Standard with the Ravnica block, Coldsnap and Ninth Edition. At the 2006 World Championship in Paris, Makihito Mihara won with a Dragonstorm combo deck that used Lotus Bloom and rituals to cast the timeshifted Dragonstorm and fetch Bogardan Hellkites. Other notable decks of the period included Solar Flare, blue-white Tron control, and "Pickles", a lock deck that copied Brine Elemental with Vesuvan Shapeshifter so the opponent skipped untap step after untap step. Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir and Mystical Teachings were central to blue control. The Time Spiral block left Standard in October 2008.

### Eternal formats

Time Spiral had a long life after rotation, and as of October 2026 its key cards remain legal in Modern and Legacy, with the exceptions below.

- **Living End** is the namesake of a Modern combo deck that cascades into it with three-mana cascade spells, then wipes the board and returns a graveyard full of cycling creatures.
- **Ancestral Vision** was banned when Modern began and unbanned in April 2016. It shows up in blue control and tempo decks.
- **Dread Return** is banned in Modern but legal in Legacy, where it appears in graveyard combo decks.
- **Empty the Warrens and Grapeshot** are storm finishers in Legacy and Modern, and both are banned in Pauper.
- **Krosan Grip** and **Tormod's Crypt** are long-time sideboard cards, and **Smallpox** has appeared in Legacy and Modern prison decks.
- **Gemstone Caverns** and **Lotus Bloom** appear in Legacy combo decks.

In Commander, Scion of the Ur-Dragon is a classic Dragon commander, Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir and Academy Ruins appear in blue decks, and Time Spiral's Slivers support Sliver decks. For more on these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained), our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) and our guide to [Pauper](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg).

One Time Spiral card is banned in Modern: **Hypergenesis**, a suspend sorcery with no mana cost that cascade spells can cast for free, letting a deck dump its hand of huge creatures onto the battlefield on turn three.

## Cards from Time Spiral worth owning

- **Living End**: the centerpiece of a long-running Modern combo deck.
- **Ancestral Vision**: one-mana card draw for patient blue decks in Modern.
- **Krosan Grip**: an answer to artifacts and enchantments that can't be responded to.
- **Teferi, Mage of Zhalfir**: a flash enabler that frustrates opponents in Commander.
- **Scion of the Ur-Dragon**: a five-color Dragon commander.
- **Academy Ruins**: artifact recursion for Commander and Modern.
- **Momentary Blink**: cheap flicker with flashback, popular in Pauper.

Time Spiral is a love letter to Magic's history, and its suspend cards gave combo players some of their best tools.
