Khans of Tarkir is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2014 that introduced Tarkir, a plane of warring clans whose dragons died out long ago. It was built around three-color "wedge" clans, brought back morph, and reprinted the allied fetch lands. It also printed Treasure Cruise, Dig Through Time, Siege Rhino and Monastery Swiftspear, cards that shaped several formats for years.

## The story

Tarkir is the home plane of Sarkhan Vol, a planeswalker obsessed with dragons. In Khans of Tarkir's present day, there are no dragons left. They were wiped out centuries ago, and the five clans that rule the plane now honor the memory of a different dragon trait each. The clans fight constantly for land and supremacy:

- **The Abzan Houses** (white, black, green) value endurance and family. Their khan is Anafenza.
- **The Jeskai Way** (blue, red, white) values cunning and discipline, led by the monk Narset.
- **The Sultai Brood** (black, green, blue) values ruthlessness and luxury, led by the naga Sidisi.
- **The Mardu Horde** (red, white, black) values speed and conquest, led by the orc Zurgo.
- **The Temur Frontier** (green, blue, red) values savagery and survival, led by Surrak.

Sarkhan returns to Tarkir with his mind badly damaged. For years he has heard the voice of Ugin, the spirit dragon, who was killed by Nicol Bolas over a thousand years earlier in a battle on Tarkir. Sarkhan wanders into Jeskai lands, where Narset finds him. She has had visions of other worlds herself, and she helps calm his mind. He tells her about planeswalkers, about Bolas, and about Ugin's death. Narset believes Ugin was the soul of Tarkir, and that his death threw the plane out of balance.

Narset agrees to lead Sarkhan to Ugin's tomb. There they're attacked by Zurgo Helmsmasher, who wants revenge on Sarkhan for something that happened years before. Narset holds Zurgo off and urges Sarkhan into Ugin's Nexus, but she's distracted at a crucial moment and Zurgo kills her. Grieving and furious, Sarkhan steps through the Nexus and vanishes in a flash of light.

That ending sets up the rest of the block. In Fate Reforged, Sarkhan finds himself more than a thousand years in the past, at the moment Bolas defeats Ugin, and his choices there change Tarkir's history. Dragons of Tarkir then shows a new present where the dragons survived and the clans became dragonlords' armies.

## Mechanics

Each clan has its own keyword, and morph ties them together.

### Raid (Mardu)

Raid abilities check whether you attacked with a creature this turn. Mardu Hordechief, a common, makes a 1/1 Warrior when it enters if you've attacked, so Mardu decks want to attack first and cast creatures after combat.

### Prowess (Jeskai)

Prowess gives a creature +1/+1 until end of turn whenever you cast a noncreature spell. Monastery Swiftspear, a one-mana haste creature with prowess, became one of the most-played red creatures in Magic's history. Prowess was later made an evergreen keyword, so it appears in nearly every set.

### Delve (Sultai)

Delve lets you exile cards from your graveyard to pay for generic mana in a spell's cost, one card per mana. Treasure Cruise is an eight-mana "draw three cards," but with a full graveyard it can cost a single blue mana. That cost reduction turned out to be extremely strong in older formats where graveyards fill quickly.

### Outlast (Abzan)

Outlast is a sorcery-speed activated ability: pay the cost and tap the creature to put a +1/+1 counter on it. Many Abzan cards reward creatures with counters. Herald of Anafenza makes a 1/1 Warrior every time you use its outlast ability.

### Ferocious (Temur)

Ferocious abilities turn on if you control a creature with power 4 or greater. Savage Punch is a common fight spell that gives your creature +2/+2 first if ferocious is active.

### Morph

Morph lets you cast a card face down as a 2/2 for three mana and turn it face up later by paying its morph cost. Rattleclaw Mystic is a mana creature that, when turned face up, also adds three mana. Morph made every face-down creature a guessing game, which defined the set's Limited format.

## Limited

Khans of Tarkir is remembered as one of the best draft formats Magic has made. The five clans were the main archetypes, and most decks played three colors, supported by tri-lands at uncommon, gain lands at common and banners. That meant a lot of decisions: you drafted lands early, and you balanced your colors more carefully than in a two-color set.

- **Abzan** decks were sturdy and grew their creatures with outlast and counters.
- **Jeskai** decks used prowess, flyers like Jeskai Windscout and cheap tricks.
- **Sultai** decks played delve creatures like Hooting Mandrills and Sultai Scavenger and filled their graveyards.
- **Mardu** decks attacked early and used raid to punish slow opponents.
- **Temur** decks played big creatures and leaned on ferocious.

Morph was the format's defining tension. With so many face-down 2/2s, combat became a bluffing game, and knowing which morphs could flip for three or five mana was a real skill. Removal like Debilitating Injury, Throttle and Arc Lightning was valuable, and blocking a morph without knowing what it was could be a big risk. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

In its first months, Khans of Tarkir Standard was a three-color format, made possible by fetch lands, tri-lands and gain lands. At Pro Tour Khans of Tarkir in October 2014, Ari Lax won with Abzan Midrange, beating Shaun McLaren's Jeskai deck in the final. Siege Rhino became the face of Abzan decks, which stayed at the top of Standard for much of the set's time in the format. Jeskai tempo and burn decks with Mantis Rider and Monastery Swiftspear were the main rivals, and Mardu decks used Wingmate Roc and Butcher of the Horde.

The Tarkir block stayed in Standard until April 2016 because of a one-time transition between rotation schedules, so Khans of Tarkir had a long run. No Khans card was banned in Standard.

### Eternal formats and bans

Khans of Tarkir caused much more trouble in older formats:

- **Treasure Cruise** took over Modern and Legacy blue tempo decks almost immediately. In January 2015, Wizards banned it in Modern and Legacy, restricted it in Vintage, and banned Dig Through Time in Modern. Pauper banned Treasure Cruise in March 2015.
- **Dig Through Time** was later banned in Legacy and restricted in Vintage in September 2015.
- **The allied fetch lands** were banned in Pioneer when the format launched in October 2019.
- **Monastery Swiftspear** was banned in Pauper in December 2023.

Treasure Cruise and Dig Through Time are still legal in Pioneer, where Treasure Cruise appears in blue-red spells decks. As of October 2026, Monastery Swiftspear remains a staple of red aggressive decks in Modern and Pioneer. Hardened Scales gave its name to a Modern artifact-and-counters deck. The fetch lands are among the most-played lands in Modern, Legacy and Commander.

In Commander, the set's khans and three-color cards make it a popular source of commanders and staples. Narset, Enlightened Master, Sidisi, Brood Tyrant and Anafenza, the Foremost all lead decks, and Hardened Scales, Utter End and Crackling Doom appear widely. For an overview of these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Khans of Tarkir worth owning

- **The allied fetch lands**: Polluted Delta, Flooded Strand, Bloodstained Mire, Windswept Heath and Wooded Foothills are staples of Modern, Legacy and Commander mana bases.
- **Monastery Swiftspear**: the classic one-mana prowess creature for Modern and Pioneer red decks.
- **Treasure Cruise**: card draw for Pioneer spell decks.
- **Hardened Scales**: an engine for +1/+1 counter decks in Modern and Commander.
- **Siege Rhino**: a fan favorite that still shows up in Abzan decks.
- **Utter End**: instant-speed exile for any nonland permanent in Commander.
- **Narset, Enlightened Master**: a free-spells commander for Jeskai players.

Khans of Tarkir is a landmark set: it proved three-color sets could work, gave Limited one of its best formats and printed cards strong enough to get banned years later. In Pioneer, Swiftspear and Treasure Cruise are the cards to build around.
