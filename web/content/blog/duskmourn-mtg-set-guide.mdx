Duskmourn: House of Horror is the Magic: The Gathering horror-movie set, released in September 2024. It takes place on Duskmourn, a plane that has been entirely swallowed by a living, endless House ruled by the demon Valgavoth. The headline is genre play: split-card Rooms you unlock door by door, face-down monsters from manifest dread, and a cycle of Overlords that became some of the most played cards in Standard.

Wizards has announced that the release of Nauctis: The Sunken Realm in early 2027 triggers the next Standard rotation, and Duskmourn is on the list of sets leaving. After that it remains legal in Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander.

## The story

Duskmourn used to be an ordinary plane. Long ago, its people summoned a demon and bound him beneath the basement of a house. The demon, Valgavoth, merged with the building and kept growing until the House was the plane. The survivors who remain live in hidden pockets of the House, scavenging and hiding from its monsters, while a cult devoted to Valgavoth kidnaps people to feed him. Part of the House's history runs through Marina Vendrell, one of its original owners, who befriended the demon and helped him grow stronger.

The Omenpaths opened by the Phyrexian Invasion let the House reach out to other planes and lure in fresh victims. One of them is Nashi, a young nezumi (ratfolk) from Kamigawa who followed a door into the House while searching for something connected to his late mother, Tamiyo. A rescue party forms on Ravnica to bring him back: Kaito Shizuki, the Kamigawan ninja who still has his planeswalker spark; the Wanderer, who feels responsible for Tamiyo's death; Tyvar Kell, a boastful elf warrior; Niko Aris, who lost their spark but still fights with magical shards; and Zimone, a Strixhaven student sent by Niv-Mizzet to study the House's anomalies.

The House splits the group almost immediately. Kaito falls into the basement and runs into Jace Beleren, who is chasing Vraska and later abandons him. Tyvar and Zimone find a library full of the House's history and meet Marina. Niko and the Wanderer are guided by Winter, a cautious-seeming survivor. They rescue Nashi, but Winter has been leading them toward the cult all along, and the group is captured and brought before Valgavoth, who appears as an enormous moth.

The finale is an escape rather than a victory. Tyvar and Zimone break in to free their friends, Kaito returns, and together they hurt Valgavoth enough to flee through a door opened by the detective Alquist Proft. Nashi leaves his mother's scroll behind, letting her go. Valgavoth survives, weakened, and Winter's betrayal doesn't earn him freedom: the House keeps him, which is why he turns up racing for his life in Aetherdrift.

## Mechanics

### Rooms

Rooms are enchantments printed as two halves, each a door with its own cost and ability. You cast one door, and the other stays locked. Later, as a sorcery, you can pay the locked door's cost to unlock it. Glassworks // Shattered Yard is a typical common: unlocking Glassworks for `{2}{R}` deals 4 damage to an opponent's creature, and unlocking Shattered Yard later pings each opponent every end step. Rooms give you a cheap effect now and a payoff for later.

### Manifest dread

To manifest dread, you look at the top two cards of your library, put one onto the battlefield face down as a 2/2 creature, and put the other into your graveyard. If the face-down card is a creature, you can turn it face up at any time by paying its mana cost. It's card selection, a body, and graveyard filling at once. Abhorrent Oculus manifests dread at the beginning of every opponent's upkeep, which is why it was so strong.

### Eerie

Eerie abilities trigger whenever an enchantment enters under your control and whenever you fully unlock a Room. Gremlin Tamer, the white-blue signpost, makes a 1/1 Gremlin each time. Eerie rewards decks dense with enchantments, which in this set includes Rooms and the many enchantment creatures.

### Survival

Survival triggers at the beginning of your second main phase if the creature is tapped. In practice, that means it attacked (or was tapped another way). Shrewd Storyteller puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature whenever its survival triggers, so it rewards you for attacking every turn.

### Impending

Impending is an alternate, cheaper casting cost for the Overlords. Cast for impending, an Overlord enters with time counters and isn't a creature until the counters run out (you remove one at each of your end steps), but its enter-the-battlefield ability still happens. Overlord of the Mistmoors, for instance, costs `{5}{W}{W}` normally but only `{2}{W}{W}` with impending, and either way it makes two 2/1 flying Insects on entry and again every time it attacks.

### Delirium and Glimmers

Delirium returns from Shadows over Innistrad: it turns on when your graveyard holds four or more card types. Fear of Missing Out gets an extra combat with delirium. Glimmer is a creature type the set uses for enchantment creatures, and the rare "Enduring" cycle is built from them: when an Enduring creature dies, it returns as a plain enchantment that keeps its other abilities.

## Limited

Duskmourn's ten color pairs lean on its mechanics more than its tribes:

| Pair | Theme | Example signposts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Eerie enchantments | Gremlin Tamer, Inquisitive Glimmer |
| Blue-Black | Eerie control | Skullsnap Nuisance, Fear of Infinity |
| Black-Red | Sacrifice | Sawblade Skinripper, Disturbing Mirth |
| Red-Green | Delirium aggro | Wildfire Wickerfolk, Beastie Beatdown |
| Green-White | Survival | Shrewd Storyteller |
| White-Black | Reanimation and drain | Rite of the Moth, Shroudstomper |
| Blue-Red | Rooms | Intruding Soulrager, Smoky Lounge // Misty Salon |
| Black-Green | Graveyard delirium | Broodspinner, Drag to the Roots |
| Red-White | Gremlin go-wide aggro | Midnight Mayhem |
| Green-Blue | Manifest dread | Growing Dread, Oblivious Bookworm |

Early consensus put white on top, with fast white-blue and red-white decks punishing slow starts. The format overall wasn't blisteringly fast, though, and the strongest uncommons were spread around enough that drafters often blended archetypes around their best cards. Removal was distributed carefully: black and white had unconditional kill spells like Murder, while red's Scorching Dragonfire and the uncommon Pyroclasm handled small creatures. Rooms at common, such as Glassworks // Shattered Yard, gave every deck a two-for-one over a long game.

The "13 or less life" dual lands at common are a nice detail: they enter tapped early but untapped late, which makes splashing easy. For a refresher on judging cards in a new format, see our guide to [evaluating Limited cards](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Duskmourn was one of the most influential Standard sets of its era. The Overlords were the backbone of Domain Overlords, the deck Matt Nass used to win Pro Tour Aetherdrift in February 2025, running four Overlord of the Mistmoors and four Overlord of the Hauntwoods. Abhorrent Oculus anchored tempo decks such as Jeskai Oculus, which also used Fear of Missing Out as a discard enabler. Mono-Red Aggro played Screaming Nemesis alongside Bloomburrow's Mice.

That last card drew a ban. On November 10, 2025, Wizards banned Screaming Nemesis in Standard. The announcement said the card removed most of the counterplay against Mono-Red, since it beats both life gain and big blockers, and Wizards didn't want aggro to dominate once Izzet Cauldron was gone. Screaming Nemesis remains legal in Pioneer and Modern.

Duskmourn reached older formats too. Abhorrent Oculus showed up in Modern Dimir decks and Pioneer Izzet Phoenix, Overlord of the Balemurk spread from Pioneer graveyard decks into Modern, Overlord of the Hauntwoods slotted into Pioneer's Enigmatic Incarnation decks, and Leyline of Resonance fueled fast Pioneer decks that copy pump spells. The set's Leylines (including Leyline of the Void, a classic graveyard hoser) give it long-term sideboard value. In Commander, Valgavoth, Terror Eater, the Enduring cycle and the Overlords are popular, and four Duskmourn Commander precons released alongside the set.

## Cards from Duskmourn worth owning

- **Overlord of the Mistmoors**: a powerful value creature in Standard, Pioneer and Commander token decks.
- **Overlord of the Hauntwoods**: a land-making ramp piece for multicolor decks.
- **Abhorrent Oculus**: a cheap, hard-to-answer threat for graveyard-fueled blue decks in nonrotating formats.
- **Enduring Curiosity**: a card-draw engine that is very hard to remove permanently.
- **Leyline of the Void**: a reprint of a long-standing graveyard-hate sideboard card.
- **Valgavoth, Terror Eater**: a splashy Commander finisher for black decks.

## The takeaway

Duskmourn mixes flavorful mechanics with real Constructed power, and its Overlords and Enduring cards will outlast its time in Standard. If you play Standard before the early-2027 rotation, its cards are still worth building around; after rotation, they move to Pioneer and Commander.
