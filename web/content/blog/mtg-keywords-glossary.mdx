A keyword in Magic: The Gathering is a single word or short phrase that stands in for a longer rule, like flying or trample. Instead of printing a full paragraph on every card, Magic uses keywords so you can learn a rule once and recognize it everywhere. This glossary defines the keywords you'll see most often, in plain English, sorted alphabetically within each group.

Keywords come in a few kinds. **Keyword abilities** are things a card has, like flying. **Keyword actions** are verbs a card tells you to do, like scry or mill. And some **mechanics** show up across many sets, like flashback or kicker. If you're still learning where these appear on a card, the [guide to reading a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card) shows the text box layout.

Most cards with a keyword also print a short reminder in italics explaining it. That reminder text is a summary. The full, official definitions live in the Comprehensive Rules, which is the place to check when an interaction gets tricky.

## Core keyword abilities

Most of the keywords in this group are evergreen, meaning they can appear in almost any set, and all of them show up constantly. These are the ones worth learning first. Many of them affect combat, which the [combat guide](/blog/mtg-combat-explained) walks through step by step.

### Deathtouch

Any amount of damage a source with deathtouch deals to a creature is enough to destroy that creature. A 1/1 with deathtouch can trade with a much bigger creature in combat. Deathtouch also works with noncombat damage, such as a creature ability that deals 1 damage to a target creature.

### Defender

A creature with defender can't attack. It can still block normally. Defenders are usually cheap creatures with high toughness, built to hold the ground.

### Double strike

A creature with double strike deals combat damage twice: once in a first-strike damage step and again in the regular damage step. If it isn't blocked, it hits the player for its power twice, which is why pump effects and double strike combine so well.

### Enchant

Enchant is the keyword on every Aura. It says what the Aura can be attached to, such as "Enchant creature" or "Enchant land." When you cast an Aura, you target something of that type. If the Aura ends up attached to something it can't legally enchant, or the enchanted object leaves the battlefield, the Aura goes to its owner's graveyard.

### Equip

Equip is an activated ability on Equipment artifacts. You pay the equip cost to attach the Equipment to a creature you control. You can only activate equip at sorcery speed, meaning during your own main phase when the stack is empty. If the creature leaves the battlefield, the Equipment stays on the battlefield, unattached, and you can equip it again.

### First strike

A creature with first strike deals its combat damage before creatures without first strike. If it deals enough damage to kill its opponent first, it takes no damage back from that creature. This makes first strike very strong on both attack and defense.

### Flash

You can cast a card with flash any time you could cast an instant, including during your opponent's turn or in combat. Flash on a creature lets you ambush an attacker as a surprise blocker, or hold up mana and decide at the end of the opponent's turn. The [instant speed guide](/blog/instant-speed-explained) explains why that flexibility matters.

### Flying

A creature with flying can't be blocked except by creatures with flying or reach. A creature with flying can block creatures with or without flying.

### Haste

A creature with haste can attack and use abilities with the tap or untap symbol in their cost as soon as it comes under your control. Normally a creature has to be under your control since the start of your turn first, which players call summoning sickness.

### Hexproof

A permanent with hexproof can't be the target of spells or abilities your opponents control. You can still target it with your own cards. Effects that don't target, like a board wipe that destroys all creatures, still affect it. Some cards have "hexproof from" a quality, such as a color, which only stops targeting from things with that quality.

### Indestructible

A permanent with indestructible can't be destroyed. Lethal damage and effects that say "destroy" don't put it in the graveyard. It can still leave the battlefield in other ways: it can be exiled, sacrificed or returned to a hand, and a creature with indestructible still dies if its toughness drops to 0 or less.

### Lifelink

Damage dealt by a source with lifelink also causes its controller to gain that much life. A 3/3 lifelink creature that deals 3 damage gains you 3 life. Lifelink works with combat and noncombat damage alike.

### Menace

A creature with menace can't be blocked except by two or more creatures. A single blocker isn't enough, so the defending player has to commit two creatures or take the damage.

### Protection

Protection always comes with a quality, such as "protection from red" or "protection from creatures." A permanent with protection from something can't be:

- **Damaged** by sources with that quality (the damage is prevented),
- **Enchanted or equipped** by things with that quality,
- **Blocked** by creatures with that quality,
- **Targeted** by spells or abilities with that quality.

Players often remember this with the acronym DEBT. Protection doesn't stop effects that don't target or deal damage, like "destroy all creatures."

### Reach

A creature with reach can block creatures with flying. Reach doesn't let a creature fly or evade blockers. It's purely defensive, and it often shows up on green spiders.

### Trample

When a creature with trample is blocked, it only has to assign lethal damage to its blockers. Any damage beyond that can be assigned to the player, planeswalker or battle it's attacking. A 6/6 trampler blocked by a 2/2 can assign 2 damage to the blocker and 4 to the player. Deathtouch combines with trample nicely, because 1 damage counts as lethal for a creature with deathtouch.

### Vigilance

Attacking doesn't cause a creature with vigilance to tap. It can attack and still be untapped to block on your opponent's turn, and it can still use tap abilities after combat.

### Ward

Ward is a tax on targeting. Whenever a permanent with ward becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, that spell or ability is countered unless its controller pays the ward cost. Ward is usually a mana amount, such as "Ward 2," but some cards ask for a different payment, like life. Ward is a triggered ability, so the opponent decides whether to pay when it resolves.

## Other common keyword abilities

These aren't in every set, but you'll see them often enough to know.

### Prowess

Whenever you cast a noncreature spell, a creature with prowess gets +1/+1 until end of turn. It rewards decks full of instants and sorceries.

### Shroud

A permanent with shroud can't be the target of any spells or abilities, including your own. It's an older, stricter cousin of hexproof.

## Keyword actions

Keyword actions are verbs that a card tells you to perform.

### Fight

When two creatures fight, each deals damage equal to its power to the other. Fight is not combat, so it doesn't involve attacking or blocking, and abilities that only care about combat damage don't apply. Green uses fight as a form of removal.

### Investigate

To investigate, create a Clue token. A Clue is an artifact with an ability that lets you pay two generic mana and sacrifice it to draw a card.

### Mill

To mill a number of cards, put that many cards from the top of a library into its owner's graveyard. Some decks mill their own library to fill the graveyard, and others mill the opponent to try to empty theirs. The [graveyard and exile guide](/blog/mtg-graveyard-and-exile-zones) covers why the graveyard matters.

### Sacrifice

To sacrifice a permanent, you move it from the battlefield to its owner's graveyard. You can only sacrifice permanents you control. Sacrifice isn't destruction, so indestructible doesn't stop it.

### Scry

To scry a number, look at that many cards from the top of your library. Put any number of them on the bottom in any order, and the rest back on top in any order. Scry doesn't draw cards. It improves the quality of your next draws.

### Surveil

Surveil works like scry, but instead of putting unwanted cards on the bottom, you put them into your graveyard. Look at the top cards, put any number into your graveyard, and the rest back on top in any order. It's better than scry for decks that use the graveyard.

## Frequently seen mechanics

### Convoke

A spell with convoke lets you tap your untapped creatures to help pay for it. Each creature you tap pays for one generic mana or one mana of that creature's color.

### Cycling

Cycling is an ability you use while the card is in your hand. Pay the cycling cost and discard the card to draw a card. It lets you swap a card that isn't useful right now for a fresh one.

### Flashback

A card with flashback can be cast from your graveyard by paying its flashback cost instead of its normal mana cost. If you cast it that way, it's exiled afterward instead of going back to the graveyard, so you get exactly one extra use.

### Kicker

Kicker is an optional additional cost you can pay when you cast a spell. If you pay it, the spell is "kicked" and does something extra, as described on the card. If you don't, it works normally without the bonus.

## How to learn keywords faster

You don't need to memorize every keyword before you play. Learn the evergreen ones above, read reminder text when you see something new, and look up the rest as they come up. After a few games, the common ones become automatic. Keywords that trigger on events, like prowess, follow the same rules as other triggered abilities, which the [triggered abilities guide](/blog/mtg-triggered-abilities-explained) explains.

## The takeaway

Most keywords fall into a few patterns: ways to evade blockers (flying, menace, trample), ways to win combat (first strike, deathtouch, double strike), ways to protect a permanent (hexproof, indestructible, protection, ward), and actions that manipulate your library (scry, surveil, mill). Learn those groups and new cards get much easier to read. When an interaction is unclear, the Comprehensive Rules have the final word.
