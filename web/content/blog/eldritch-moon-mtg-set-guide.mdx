Eldritch Moon is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in July 2016 that finished the Shadows over Innistrad story. Emrakul, the last free Eldrazi titan, arrives on Innistrad and twists its people into horrors, while the Gatewatch makes a last stand. The set introduced emerge, escalate and meld, and its namesake card, Emrakul, the Promised End, became one of the few cards ever banned in Standard.

## The story

Shadows over Innistrad, the set before Eldritch Moon, was a mystery: why were the angels mad, and why was the plane falling apart? The answer was Nahiri, a planeswalker who had been imprisoned for a thousand years by the vampire Sorin Markov. Seeking revenge, she drew Emrakul through the Blind Eternities toward Innistrad. Avacyn, the archangel who protected humanity, went mad and turned on the people she once guarded, and Sorin, who had created her, destroyed her. Nahiri then trapped Sorin in stone.

Eldritch Moon begins with that protection gone. Emrakul physically arrives and her influence spreads across Innistrad, turning plants, animals, humans and monsters into Eldrazi horrors with tentacles, extra eyes and strange colors. Some victims fuse together into new creatures, which is where the set's meld mechanic comes from. The archangels Bruna and Gisela merge into a single monstrous angel, Brisela, Voice of Nightmares, and the human city of Thraben is largely destroyed.

Jace Beleren returns to call in the rest of the Gatewatch, Gideon, Chandra and Nissa, who had just defeated two Eldrazi titans on Zendikar. They're joined by Tamiyo, a moonfolk planeswalker and scholar, and by Liliana Vess, who brings armies of undead that Emrakul's influence can't twist. The heroes can't kill Emrakul, so they decide to imprison her. In the final battle, Jace makes contact with something like Emrakul's mind and is told she is incomplete and that Innistrad should welcome her. The Gatewatch completes a ritual that seals her inside Innistrad's moon. Emrakul seems to allow it, which leaves an unsettling question for later stories.

Afterward, Liliana takes the Gatewatch's oath and joins the group. Emrakul stays sealed in the moon, which becomes important again years later when Innistrad: Midnight Hunt's endless night begins.

## Mechanics

### Emerge

Emerge is an alternative cost on big Eldrazi creatures: you sacrifice a creature and pay the emerge cost, reduced by the sacrificed creature's mana value. Many emerge creatures also have a "when you cast" trigger, which happens even if the creature is countered. Elder Deep-Fiend is an eight-mana flash creature whose emerge cost is `{5}{U}{U}`. Sacrifice a three-mana creature and it costs four, and its cast trigger taps up to four permanents, often an opponent's whole team or their lands during their upkeep.

### Escalate

Escalate cards are modal spells where you can choose more than one mode by paying the escalate cost once for each extra mode. Collective Brutality costs two mana, and its escalate cost is discarding a card. It can make the opponent discard an instant or sorcery, give a creature -2/-2, and drain 2 life, all in one spell if you discard two cards.

### Meld

Meld cards come in pairs. If you control both halves and meet the condition, you exile them and they return as a single giant card made from both backs. Gisela, the Broken Blade and Bruna, the Fading Light meld into Brisela, Voice of Nightmares, a huge flier that stops opponents from casting spells with mana value 3 or less. At common, Graf Rats and Midnight Scavengers meld into Chittering Host.

### Delirium, madness and transform

Delirium and madness return from Shadows over Innistrad. Delirium turns on when your graveyard has four or more card types, which the set rewards with cards like Grim Flayer, a two-mana trampler that becomes a 4/4. Madness lets you cast a card for its madness cost when you discard it. Double-faced cards return too, with many creatures transforming into their Eldrazi-corrupted forms. Our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) covers how they work.

## Limited

Eldritch Moon was drafted with two packs of Eldritch Moon and one of Shadows over Innistrad, so its archetypes overlapped with the earlier set. A few themes stood out:

- **Emerge** rewarded creatures that left value behind when sacrificed, like Primal Druid, which fetches a basic land when it dies. Wretched Gryff, a common emerge flier that draws a card, was a mainstay of blue decks.
- **Spirits and flyers** in white and blue, helped by flash creatures like Drogskol Shieldmate.
- **Delirium** in black and green, filling the graveyard with several card types.
- **Madness and discard** in black and red.
- **Humans and go-wide aggression** in white and red, where the gold uncommon Ride Down cleared blockers.
- **Meld pairs** at common (Graf Rats and Midnight Scavengers) gave black decks a strong late-game payoff.

Cheap removal was plentiful, with Galvanic Bombardment and Borrowed Malevolence at common and Murder at uncommon. Escalate cards gave flexible two-for-ones. For drafting fundamentals, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Eldritch Moon had a big effect on Standard from its first weeks. Spell Queller and Selfless Spirit joined Collected Company decks to form Bant Spirits and Bant Company, which were top decks in the summer of 2016. Elder Deep-Fiend gave rise to emerge decks that sacrificed creatures for value and tapped down opponents at instant speed.

After Kaladesh arrived in the fall of 2016, the set's cards stayed central:

- **Emrakul, the Promised End** became the top end of green-based delirium decks, which used Grim Flayer, Ishkanah, Grafwidow and Liliana, the Last Hope to fill their graveyards with card types. Taking control of your opponent's turn was often game-winning.
- **Thalia, Heretic Cathar and Selfless Spirit** fit into aggressive white decks such as Mardu Vehicles.
- **Liliana, the Last Hope** dominated against decks of small creatures.

In January 2017, Wizards banned Emrakul, the Promised End in Standard, alongside Smuggler's Copter and Reflector Mage. Emrakul is the only Eldritch Moon card ever banned in a major format.

As of October 2026, the set is still well represented in older formats. Collective Brutality is a staple of black decks in Modern, where it handles small creatures, discard and burn, and Liliana, the Last Hope has seen extensive Modern play in black midrange. Spell Queller, Selfless Spirit and Mausoleum Wanderer are core pieces of Spirits decks in Modern and Pioneer. Thalia, Heretic Cathar appears in white hatebear decks in Modern and Legacy, and Bedlam Reveler was a regular in Modern prowess. In Commander, Gisa and Geralf lead Zombie decks, Splendid Reclamation and Geier Reach Sanitarium fit lands and wheel strategies, and Selfless Spirit protects creature boards. For Commander basics, see [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

## Cards from Eldritch Moon worth owning

- **Collective Brutality**: flexible two-mana interaction in Modern and Pioneer.
- **Liliana, the Last Hope**: a three-mana planeswalker that grinds out small-creature decks.
- **Spell Queller**: a flash threat and counterspell in one, the heart of Spirits.
- **Selfless Spirit**: protects your whole board from removal and sweepers.
- **Thalia, Heretic Cathar**: slows opponents down in every format where it's legal.
- **Gisa and Geralf**: a Zombie-recursion commander.
- **Splendid Reclamation**: a lands-deck finisher in Commander.

Eldritch Moon is small, but its cards punch above their weight. In Pioneer, the Spirits package and Liliana are the cards to start with. If you're new to the format, read [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained), then build a list in the [Deck Lab builder](/).
