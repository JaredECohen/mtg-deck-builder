Conspiracy is a Magic: The Gathering supplemental set released in June 2014, and it was the first booster set designed to be drafted for multiplayer games. It takes place in Paliano, the High City, on the plane of Fiora, where a ghostly king, a spymaster and a planeswalker thief all scheme for power. Its headline idea is that the draft itself is part of the game: some cards change how you pick, and new Conspiracy cards start the game in your command zone instead of your deck.

## The story

Paliano is a sprawling, wealthy city state where everyone has a plot. Its ruler is **Brago, King Eternal**, a monarch who would not let death end his reign. He still rules as a spirit, served by the Custodi, the city's clergy, and nearly every faction in Paliano would like to see his reign finally end.

The most dangerous of them is Marchesa, known as the Black Rose: an aristocrat who runs a network of spies and assassins and wants the crown for herself. Below the city, the goblin Grenzo runs the dungeons and quietly decides who disappears. Muzzio, the city's architect, builds cogwork constructs and holds the secrets of Paliano's machinery. Selvala, an elf explorer, has returned from the wild lands beyond the city walls with news the court doesn't want to hear.

Wandering through all of it is Dack Fayden, a planeswalker and master thief who calls Fiora home. He was already known from Magic's comics, and Conspiracy gave him his first planeswalker card. The set's short web stories cover a betrayal at court, Marchesa's rise and the cogwork that holds the city together, but they end with the plots still moving. The payoff came two years later in Conspiracy: Take the Crown, where Marchesa finally seizes the throne.

## Mechanics

Everything in Conspiracy is built for a pod of three to five players. Eight people draft together, then split into smaller free-for-all games.

### Will of the council

Will of the council cards hold a vote. Starting with you, each player votes for one of the options, and the option with the most votes happens. You almost never control the result in a four-player game, so these cards reward reading the table. **Council's Judgment** (rare, `{1}{W}{W}`) has each player vote for a nonland permanent you don't control and exiles whatever gets the most votes. Since it never targets, it handles hexproof and protection threats. **Brago's Representative** (common) gives you an extra vote, and **Grudge Keeper** (common) drains anyone who voted against you.

### Parley

Parley makes every player reveal the top card of their library, gives you a bonus for each nonland card revealed, and then lets every player draw a card. It's a group-hug effect with a selfish edge. **Selvala, Explorer Returned** (rare) is the famous one: tap her to make green mana and gain life for each nonland card revealed, then everyone draws. **Selvala's Charge** makes a 3/3 Elephant per nonland card.

### Dethrone

Dethrone puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature whenever it attacks the player with the most life, or tied for most. It pushes the whole table to attack whoever is ahead, which keeps multiplayer games from running away. **Marchesa, the Black Rose** (mythic) gives all your creatures dethrone and brings back any creature with a +1/+1 counter that dies. **Scourge of the Throne** (mythic) has dethrone and grants an extra combat when it attacks the leader.

### Conspiracies and hidden agenda

Conspiracy is a new card type. You don't cast these cards; you put them in your command zone before the game starts, and they change the rules for you. **Backup Plan** lets you draw two opening hands and keep one, and **Power Play** makes you the starting player. Hidden agenda Conspiracies start face down with a secretly named card. **Double Stroke**, for example, copies every instant or sorcery you cast with the named card, and you flip it face up when you want the reveal.

### Draft-matters cards

Twelve cards change the draft itself. **Cogwork Librarian** (common) lets you later swap it into a pack to take two cards at once. **Whispergear Sneak** lets you peek at an unopened pack. **Lore Seeker** adds a whole extra booster to the draft. **Paliano, the High City** lets you and the players beside you each name a color for the land to make. Once the game starts, most of these are just modest artifact creatures, since their real effect happened at the draft table.

## Limited

Conspiracy draft plays differently from a normal set. You draft in an eight-player pod as usual, then play in groups of three to five, and that changes what's good. Cheap aggressive creatures are weaker because no single opponent takes all your damage. Cards that make you look unthreatening, or that punish the leader, get better.

The main themes were:

- **Dethrone aggression** in red, blue and black, led by Marchesa's cards and cheap creatures like **Grenzo's Cutthroat** and **Enraged Revolutionary**.
- **Voting** in white and black, where extra votes from **Brago's Representative** let you steer will of the council cards.
- **Parley group draw** in green and white, which turns everyone's extra cards into tokens or mana for you.
- **+1/+1 counter recyclers**: a rare cycle such as **Custodi Soulbinders** and **Realm Seekers** enters with counters based on the game state and spends them on effects.
- **Constructs and artifacts**, since the draft-matters cards are all artifact creatures and Muzzio rewards them.

Political removal and card advantage were the most valuable cards in the format. The draft-matters cards were rarely the best card in a pack for your deck, but they were a lot of fun. Picking a Cogwork Librarian early and using it later to take two strong cards from one pack is the classic Conspiracy story. The format was slow and swingy by design, more like a game night than a tournament, and that's why it later became a model for multiplayer [cube drafts](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Conspiracy was never legal in Standard or Modern, so its impact was limited to eternal formats and Commander.

In Legacy, **Council's Judgment** became a regular answer in white decks. It could deal with threats like **True-Name Nemesis** that most removal can't touch, and it stayed in sideboards and main decks until newer, cheaper options like Prismatic Ending pushed it aside. **Dack Fayden** saw play in Vintage, where stealing an opponent's artifact can decide a game.

No card from Conspiracy has been banned in Legacy or Vintage. The thirteen Conspiracy cards are banned everywhere by rule, since they only make sense in a draft.

Commander is where the set lasted. **Brago, King Eternal** and **Marchesa, the Black Rose** became some of the most popular commanders in their color identities, and **Selvala, Explorer Returned** became a known combo piece. The voting idea returned in Conspiracy: Take the Crown in 2016. None of these cards are Commander Game Changers as of October 2026, so they fit comfortably in most mid-power tables. ## Cards from Conspiracy worth owning

- **Council's Judgment**: removal that gets around hexproof and protection in Commander and Legacy.
- **Brago, King Eternal**: a commander that turns every enters-the-battlefield effect into a repeatable engine.
- **Marchesa, the Black Rose**: a sticky Grixis commander that punishes the leader.
- **Selvala, Explorer Returned**: big green mana for creature-heavy decks.
- **Dack Fayden**: still a strong artifact answer for Vintage and Commander tables.
- **Deathreap Ritual**: simple, steady card draw for sacrifice decks.

## Takeaway

Conspiracy is worth knowing for two reasons: it proved that multiplayer draft could be its own product, and it gave Commander some of its best political cards. If you have a few of its legends, build around the table instead of a single opponent. Brago and Marchesa both reward patience and punishing the leader more than racing.
