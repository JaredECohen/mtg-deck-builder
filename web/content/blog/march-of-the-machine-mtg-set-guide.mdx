March of the Machine is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2023 that ends the Phyrexian saga: Elesh Norn's armies pour out of New Phyrexia and invade dozens of planes at once, and heroes from across the Multiverse fight back. It introduced battles, a brand-new card type, along with incubate and backup, and it was followed three weeks later by March of the Machine: The Aftermath, a 50-card epilogue set.

## The story

March of the Machine is the finale of a four-set Phyrexian arc that began with Dominaria United. After seizing sole control of New Phyrexia, Elesh Norn built an invasion engine: Realmbreaker, a Phyrexian version of a World Tree grown with material stolen from Kaldheim. Its branches can punch through to other planes, and Jin-Gitaxias had found a way to compleat planeswalkers, turning heroes into Phyrexian agents.

The story opens with a desperate gamble. Jace leads a strike team of planeswalkers, including Elspeth, Kaya, Nissa, Nahiri, Vraska, Kaito and Tyvar, to New Phyrexia with a copy of the Sylex, hoping to detonate it at Realmbreaker's core. A planar shield scatters them on arrival, and the mission falls apart. Lukka, Nissa, Nahiri and Vraska are compleated, Jace is infected, and Elspeth is thrown out of the Blind Eternities when the Sylex goes off in her hands.

With the strike team broken, the invasion begins. Realmbreaker's branches tear into Dominaria, Ravnica, Zendikar, Kaldheim, Kamigawa, Ixalan, Innistrad, Theros, Eldraine and many more planes. Most of the "Invasion of..." battle cards depict one of these fights, so the set reads like a tour of Magic's worlds under attack. Locals band together in unusual pairings, which is why the set is full of two-character legends like Thalia and The Gitrog Monster and Zurgo and Ojutai.

The turn comes from an unexpected direction. Wrenn, a dryad planeswalker, bonds with Realmbreaker itself and uses its branches to find Teferi, who has been stranded on Zhalfir, the long-lost kingdom he phased out of reality centuries ago. Zhalfir's armies counter-invade New Phyrexia. Elspeth returns as an archangel, and in the final battle Elesh Norn, Jin-Gitaxias and Vorinclex are all killed. Wrenn then swaps the positions of Zhalfir and New Phyrexia in the Blind Eternities, cutting Phyrexia off. Without that connection, the glistening oil goes inert everywhere and the invaders collapse.

### The Aftermath

March of the Machine: The Aftermath is the epilogue. The war is over, but the Multiverse is changed: most planeswalkers have lost their sparks, and permanent "omenpaths" left by Realmbreaker's branches now let ordinary people travel between planes. The set reflects this with characters like Nissa, Narset, Nahiri and Ob Nixilis printed as legendary creatures instead of planeswalkers. Ajani and Nissa are cured of their compleation, at the cost of Melira's life and Karn giving up a spark he carried. The Aftermath was sold in small Epilogue Boosters and couldn't be drafted, so it plays a minor role in this guide's Limited section.

## Mechanics

### Battles and Sieges

Battles are a card type that sits on the battlefield like a permanent you attack. Every battle in this set is a Siege: when it enters, you choose an opponent to protect it, then you and your creatures attack it to strip off defense counters. When the last counter is removed, you exile the battle and cast its back face for free. Most battles also do something immediately when they enter. Invasion of Zendikar fetches two basic lands as soon as it lands, then flips into a land creature that taps for any color. In practice, many battles are spells with a delayed bonus attached.

### Incubate

Incubate N creates an Incubator token: an artifact with N +1/+1 counters and "`{2}`: Transform this token." Once you pay, it becomes a Phyrexian artifact creature with those counters. It's flexible because the token is hard to remove while it's just an artifact, and you choose when to spend the mana. Sunfall, a five-mana sorcery that exiles all creatures, uses incubate to leave you with a creature as big as the board you just wiped.

### Backup

Backup N appears on creatures. When one enters, you put N +1/+1 counters on a target creature, and if you choose another creature, it also gains the backup creature's abilities until end of turn. Doomskar Warrior can lend trample and its card-finding combat trigger to something already on the battlefield the turn it arrives. Backup rewards attacking, because you get to use the ability right away on a creature without summoning sickness.

### Convoke and the transforming legends

Convoke returns, letting your creatures tap to help pay for a spell. Knight-Errant of Eos looks deeper into your library the more creatures convoked it. The set also has transforming legends with a Phyrexian mana activation, such as Etali, Primal Conqueror and Rona, Herald of Invasion, and the five praetors (Elesh Norn, Sheoldred, Jin-Gitaxias, Urabrask and Vorinclex) each flip into a Saga. For how these cards work in general, see our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained).

## Limited

March of the Machine's draft format had ten two-color archetypes, each marked by a gold uncommon:

- **White-blue Knights** (Marshal of Zhalfir).
- **Blue-black spells and recursion** (Halo Forager).
- **Black-red sacrifice** (Stormclaw Rager).
- **Red-green battle attackers** (Rampaging Geoderm).
- **Green-white +1/+1 counters** (Botanical Brawler).
- **White-black incubate** (Sculpted Perfection).
- **Blue-red convoke and tokens** (Joyful Stormsculptor).
- **Black-green incubate value** (Elvish Vatkeeper).
- **Red-white backup** (Mirror-Shield Hoplite).
- **Green-blue transformed permanents** (Mutagen Connoisseur).

Battles changed how combat worked. Every attack meant choosing between pressuring your opponent's life total and flipping a battle for its back face, and defending a battle you were protecting cost you blockers. Decks with cheap creatures and backup were good at doing both. Efficient removal like Deadly Derision, Volcanic Spite and Mirrodin Avenged was in high demand, and the common thriving-style gain lands made two- and three-color decks easy to build. For the general skills that carry from set to set, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

March of the Machine arrived in a Standard dominated by black-red midrange. At Pro Tour March of the Machine in May 2023, Nathan Steuer won with Rakdos Midrange, and Chandra, Hope's Beacon was one of the new cards in his list. A few weeks later, on May 29, 2023, Wizards banned three non-MOM cards in Standard (Fable of the Mirror-Breaker, Invoke Despair and Reckoner Bankbuster), which opened the format up.

The set fed several decks during its Standard run:

- **Domain Ramp** used Invasion of Zendikar to reach Atraxa, Grand Unifier early, with Etali, Primal Conqueror as another top end.
- **Esper Legends** picked up Rona, Herald of Invasion, and the archetype won the 2023 World Championship in the hands of Jean-Emmanuel Depraz.
- **Mono-White and Boros Humans** were built around Coppercoat Vanguard from The Aftermath.
- **Boros Convoke** used Knight-Errant of Eos as a card-advantage engine.
- **Control decks** relied on Sunfall as their sweeper for the rest of the set's time in Standard.
- **Lithomantic Barrage** became a common sideboard card against white and blue decks.

Standard moved to a three-year rotation in May 2023, so the set stayed legal much longer than earlier sets, until the rotation in summer 2025. No March of the Machine or Aftermath card was banned in Standard. Our guide to [Standard rotation](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains how that schedule works.

Outside Standard, the set's footprint is smaller but real. Sunfall shows up in Pioneer control, and Faerie Mastermind is a strong card in eternal blue decks. The biggest lasting impact is in Commander, where the praetor double-faced cards, Omnath, Locus of All, Tribute to the World Tree, Ozolith, the Shattered Spire and the reprinted Monastery Mentor all became popular. The Aftermath added Commander favorites too, notably Training Grounds, Karn, Legacy Reforged and Ob Nixilis, Captive Kingpin.

## Cards from March of the Machine worth owning

- **Sunfall**: an exile sweeper that leaves you a creature, still useful in Pioneer control.
- **Faerie Mastermind**: a cheap flash threat that punishes extra card draw in eternal formats and Commander.
- **Tribute to the World Tree**: a steady card-advantage engine for green creature decks in Commander.
- **Jin-Gitaxias**: a spell-heavy Commander engine that flips into a huge card-draw Saga.
- **Thalia and The Gitrog Monster**: a strong three-color commander that slows opponents and draws cards.
- **Training Grounds** (The Aftermath): makes activated abilities much cheaper, a combo piece in Commander.
- **Ozolith, the Shattered Spire**: adds an extra counter every time, for any +1/+1 counters deck.

March of the Machine is the payoff for years of Phyrexian storytelling, and its cards are a good mix of Constructed tools and Commander engines. If you're building with them today, Pioneer and Commander are where they matter.
