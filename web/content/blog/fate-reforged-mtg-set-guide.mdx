Fate Reforged is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in January 2015, the middle set of the Khans of Tarkir block. Sarkhan Vol travels more than a thousand years into Tarkir's past and changes history by saving the dragon planeswalker Ugin, and the set's cards show a Tarkir where the clans are still fighting off living dragon broods. It's a small set, but it printed Ugin, the Spirit Dragon, Monastery Mentor and Tasigur, the Golden Fang, cards that shaped formats for years.

## The story

In Khans of Tarkir, Tarkir is a plane without dragons. They died out long ago, and five warlike clans now rule, each shaped by the memory of the dragon it once revered. Sarkhan Vol, a dragon-obsessed planeswalker born there, is haunted by the feeling that his world went wrong. At the end of that set, guided by the echo of the dead dragon Ugin, he finds Ugin's resting place and is pulled 1,280 years into the past.

The Tarkir he arrives in is very different. Dragons are everywhere, born from great storms called dragon tempests, and the clans exist in an earlier form, constantly fighting to survive the five dragon broods. Sarkhan meets some of the earlier clan leaders, including Yasova Dragonclaw, khan of the Temur. The set's legends reflect this moment: the five dragonlords (Atarka, Dromoka, Kolaghan, Ojutai and Silumgar) appear as rares, and the khans of the past, such as Alesha, Who Smiles at Death, Tasigur, the Golden Fang and Shu Yun, the Silent Tempest, stand against them.

The turning point is the fight that Sarkhan's own history records: Nicol Bolas arrives on Tarkir and battles his twin, Ugin. Bolas wins and leaves Ugin dying. In the original timeline, Ugin's death is what ends the dragon tempests and dooms Tarkir's dragons. This time, Sarkhan is there. He uses a hedron shard to wrap Ugin in a protective hedron cocoon, keeping him alive in stasis.

That one act rewrites history. With Ugin alive, the tempests keep coming, the dragons survive, and the clans lose. One by one, the khans fall to or submit to the dragonlords, and the clans are remade as the dragons' servants. When Sarkhan returns to the present, Tarkir is ruled by the five dragonlords, and the clans are gone. That's the world of the next set, Dragons of Tarkir, where Ugin eventually wakes up and becomes a key ally against Bolas in later stories.

## Mechanics

### Manifest

Manifest puts the top card of your library onto the battlefield face down as a 2/2 creature. If the face-down card is a creature card, you can turn it face up at any time by paying its mana cost. If it's not a creature, it stays a 2/2. It's a cousin of Khans of Tarkir's morph, and the two mechanics play well together. **Whisperwood Elemental** (mythic) manifests a card at the end of each of your turns and can protect your team from a sweeper.

### Bolster

Bolster N, the Abzan mechanic, puts N +1/+1 counters on whichever creature you control has the least toughness. You don't choose the target freely, which keeps small creatures growing. **Dragonscale General** (rare) bolsters at your end step based on how many of your creatures are tapped.

### Dash

Dash, the Mardu mechanic, lets you cast a creature for its dash cost instead. It gains haste and returns to your hand at the next end step, so you can attack every turn while dodging sorcery-speed removal. **Goblin Heelcutter** (common) can be dashed for `{2}{R}` and stops a creature from blocking whenever it attacks.

### Returning clan mechanics

The other three clans kept their Khans of Tarkir mechanics. Prowess (Jeskai) gives a creature +1/+1 until end of turn whenever you cast a noncreature spell, as on **Monastery Mentor**. Delve (Sultai) lets you exile cards from your graveyard to pay generic mana, as on **Tasigur, the Golden Fang** and **Gurmag Angler**. Ferocious (Temur) rewards you for controlling a creature with power 4 or greater, as on **Wild Slash**, a one-mana shock that stops damage prevention if you're ferocious.

### Choice and the Sieges

The set's theme is choosing between paths, matching its crossroads-of-history story. The rare Siege cycle asks you to choose "Khans" or "Dragons" as each enters. **Outpost Siege** is either a steady card-advantage engine or a source of damage whenever your creatures leave the battlefield. Many commons and uncommons also offer a mode choice, such as a +1/+1 counter or an enter-the-battlefield effect.

## Limited

Fate Reforged was designed to be drafted with Khans of Tarkir, with one pack of Fate Reforged followed by two of Khans. That shaped everything. The clans in this set are less defined because it's the past, so there are few gold cards: a cycle of enemy-colored common spells and a cycle of rare dragonlords, with no three-color cards at all. Clan cards instead use hybrid mana or check for another color, which let them work in two-color decks as well as full wedges.

Fixing was a priority. The basic land slot in each pack held one of the ten common gain lands (taplands that gain 1 life) or, rarely, a Khans of Tarkir fetchland. Manifest interacted with Khans' morph, so face-down creatures were common, and the mind games of guessing what was under a face-down 2/2 carried over.

The clan identities held up in the combined format: Abzan with bolster and counters, Jeskai with prowess and spells, Sultai with delve and the graveyard, Mardu with dash and Warriors, and Temur with ferocious and big creatures. Strong commons included removal like **Bathe in Dragonfire**, **Douse in Gloom** and **Sandblast**, along with Goblin Heelcutter, which turned many red decks into racing machines. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2015 to 2016

Fate Reforged joined a Standard full of Khans of Tarkir and Theros block cards. **Warden of the First Tree** gave Abzan Aggro a one-drop that scaled into the late game, and Abzan decks built on Siege Rhino also picked up **Valorous Stance**, a flexible instant that protects a creature or kills a big one. Jeskai decks used **Monastery Mentor** and **Soulfire Grand Master** alongside burn and cheap spells. Red aggro picked up Goblin Heelcutter and Wild Slash. **Outpost Siege** was a popular source of card advantage, and **Ugin, the Spirit Dragon** gave ramp and control decks a board-clearing finisher. Once Dragons of Tarkir arrived, **Crux of Fate** became a one-sided sweeper in Dragon-based control decks.

No Fate Reforged card was banned in Standard. Under the short-lived split rotation of the time, Khans of Tarkir and Fate Reforged were treated as one block, and they rotated out together in April 2016 when Shadows over Innistrad arrived.

### Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander

Fate Reforged's Constructed legacy is much bigger than its size. As of October 2026:

- **Ugin, the Spirit Dragon** has been a finisher in Modern Tron decks for years, and it's Pioneer-legal too, where it has shown up in big-mana ramp decks.
- **Tasigur, the Golden Fang** and **Gurmag Angler** made delve threats a fixture of Modern and Legacy blue-black decks during their peak years.
- **Monastery Mentor** saw Legacy play and became a Vintage finisher strong enough that it was restricted there in 2017; it remains restricted.
- **Temur Battle Rage** has been a combo-aggro finisher in Modern and a strong card in Pauper.
- **Temporal Trespass** found a home in Pioneer Arclight Phoenix decks, where delve can make an extra turn cost only a few mana.

No Fate Reforged card is banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy. In Commander, Temur Sabertooth, Reality Shift, Whisperwood Elemental, Alesha, Who Smiles at Death and Ugin are all popular, and Alesha leads her own aggressive recursion decks. ## Cards from Fate Reforged worth owning

- **Ugin, the Spirit Dragon**: a colorless finisher in Modern, Pioneer and Commander.
- **Monastery Mentor**: a powerful spells-matter threat in older formats.
- **Tasigur, the Golden Fang**: a cheap delve threat with a value engine attached.
- **Gurmag Angler**: a long-running delve staple from Modern to Pauper.
- **Temur Sabertooth**: a Commander combo and protection piece.
- **Reality Shift**: clean blue removal for Commander.
- **Whisperwood Elemental**: card advantage and sweeper insurance in green decks.
- **Temur Battle Rage**: a finisher for combo-aggro decks.

Fate Reforged is a time-travel story whose cards outlived their own timeline. If you're building with it today, Ugin and the delve cards are the core, with Monastery Mentor and Temur Battle Rage as the specialists.
