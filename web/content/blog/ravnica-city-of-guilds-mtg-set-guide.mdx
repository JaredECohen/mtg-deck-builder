Ravnica: City of Guilds is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2005 that introduced Ravnica, a plane covered by a single endless city and run by ten guilds. This first set of the Ravnica block features four of them, Boros, Dimir, Golgari and Selesnya, and it debuted hybrid mana, the shock lands, the bounce lands and the Signets. It's one of the most influential sets ever printed, and Wizards has gone back to Ravnica again and again since.

## The story

Ravnica is a world that is one enormous city, layer upon layer of streets, cathedrals and sewers. For ten thousand years, its peace has rested on the **Guildpact**, a magical treaty that gives each of ten guilds a role in running the city: the Boros Legion keeps order, the Selesnya Conclave gathers the faithful, the Golgari Swarm recycles the dead and the rot, and so on. The story opens as the city approaches the pact's ten-thousandth anniversary.

The tie-in novel, Ravnica by Cory J. Herndon (2005), is a police procedural more than a quest. Its hero is **Agrus Kos**, a veteran officer of the Wojek, the Boros-run city watch, who appears on the card **Agrus Kos, Wojek Veteran**. Kos investigates a string of crimes that leads to the Dimir, a guild most citizens believe is only a myth. In truth the Dimir are real, they're spies and information brokers, and their ancient vampire guildmaster **Szadek, Lord of Secrets** is plotting against the Guildpact itself.

Along the way Kos crosses paths with the Golgari, where the elf **Savra, Queen of the Golgari** and her brother Jarad are vying for influence, and with the Selesnya Conclave. The climax comes when Szadek announces the end of the Guildpact, and Kos arrests him for crimes against the treaty. The pact survives for now, but Szadek's move cracks the system open. The next two sets, Guildpact and Dissension, introduce the other six guilds and follow the treaty to its collapse.

That broken Guildpact is the backdrop for every later Ravnica set. When players went back in 2012, the city had rebuilt itself without the treaty; our [Return to Ravnica set guide](/blog/return-to-ravnica-mtg-set-guide) picks up from there.

## Mechanics

Each guild has its own keyword, and every multicolored card belongs to a guild.

### Radiance (Boros)

A spell with radiance affects its target and each other creature (or permanent) that shares a color with it. **Wojek Siren** gives +1/+1 to a target creature and everything that shares a color with it, which is often your whole team in a two-color aggro deck. It's a double-edged keyword: it can just as easily help your opponent if they share colors.

### Transmute (Dimir)

Transmute lets you pay a cost and discard the card to search your library for another card with the same mana value, at sorcery speed. **Muddle the Mixture** is a two-mana counterspell that can instead find any two-mana card, which made it a famous tutor for combo pieces. **Dimir House Guard** finds four-drops. Transmute turned Dimir decks into toolboxes.

### Dredge (Golgari)

If a card with dredge is in your graveyard and you'd draw a card, you may instead mill that many cards and return the dredge card to your hand. **Stinkweed Imp** has dredge 5 and **Golgari Grave-Troll** has dredge 6, which can fill a graveyard at alarming speed. Dredge looks modest on paper, but it turned out to be one of the most powerful graveyard engines ever printed, and whole decks are built to never draw normally.

### Convoke (Selesnya)

Convoke lets your creatures help pay for a spell: each creature you tap pays for one generic mana or one mana of its color. **Chord of Calling** uses convoke to put a creature from your library onto the battlefield at instant speed, and **Conclave Equenaut** is a six-mana flier that a wide board makes cheap.

### Hybrid mana, shock lands, bounce lands and Signets

Ravnica introduced hybrid mana. A `{G/W}` symbol can be paid with green or white, so **Selesnya Guildmage** fits either a green deck or a white one. The set also introduced four of the ten shock lands, such as **Watery Grave**, dual lands with basic land types that enter untapped if you pay 2 life. The common "bounce lands," like **Boros Garrison**, enter tapped and return a land to your hand but tap for two mana. The Signets, like **Dimir Signet**, turn one mana into two colored mana. If you're still learning to read hybrid costs, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Ravnica was drafted as RAV-RAV-RAV at first, and it's remembered as one of the great draft formats, especially once Guildpact and Dissension joined it. The format had four guilds instead of ten color pairs:

- **Boros (red-white)**: an aggressive deck of cheap creatures, radiance tricks and burn such as **Galvanic Arc**.
- **Dimir (blue-black)**: a slower, controlling deck with evasive creatures like **Dimir Infiltrator**, transmute and card draw such as **Compulsive Research**.
- **Golgari (black-green)**: a grindy deck that used dredge and the graveyard, with creatures like **Shambling Shell** that came back again and again.
- **Selesnya (green-white)**: a go-wide deck of tokens and convoke, using **Selesnya Evangel** and **Votary of the Conclave**.

The common bounce lands and Signets made three-color decks unusually easy, so it was normal to play a guild and splash a third color. Strong commons included **Faith's Fetters** and **Last Gasp** for removal. For more on drafting multicolor formats, read [how to draft and read signals](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Ravnica hit a Standard full of Kamigawa and Ninth Edition cards, and it quickly became the core of the format. The best-known Ravnica-era Standard deck is **Ghazi-Glare**, a green-white deck combining Glare of Subdual with token makers like Vitu-Ghazi, the City-Tree; Katsuhiro Mori won the 2005 World Championship with it. Boros aggro decks used Lightning Helix, and Dimir and Golgari pieces showed up in control and midrange decks once Guildpact and Dissension filled out the other guilds. No Ravnica card was banned in Standard, and the set rotated out with the rest of its block when Lorwyn arrived in fall 2007.

The set's long life has been in older formats:

- **Modern**: Ravnica cards are everywhere. The shock lands are core mana, Lightning Helix is a Boros and Jeskai staple, Remand had a long run in tempo decks, Chord of Calling powers creature toolbox lists and Dark Confidant has seen play in black midrange. Golgari Grave-Troll is banned in Modern as of October 2026.
- **Legacy and Vintage**: Dredge decks are built on Ravnica's dredge creatures, with Stinkweed Imp and Golgari Grave-Troll as classic pieces (Golgari Grave-Troll is restricted in Vintage). Life from the Loam is the engine of Legacy Lands decks, and Dark Confidant was a long-time staple of Vintage and Legacy.
- **Commander**: Doubling Season, the Signets, the bounce lands, Farseek, Putrefy and the shock lands are all heavily played. See [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) if you're new.
- **Pauper**: the bounce lands, Stinkweed Imp and the transmute commons are Pauper-legal.

## Cards from Ravnica: City of Guilds worth owning

- **The shock lands** (Overgrown Tomb, Sacred Foundry, Temple Garden, Watery Grave): top-tier dual lands in Modern and Commander.
- **Dark Confidant**: a classic card-advantage creature.
- **Lightning Helix**: Modern's premier red-white burn spell.
- **Life from the Loam**: the heart of Legacy Lands.
- **Doubling Season**: a Commander powerhouse for tokens and counters.
- **Chord of Calling**: a creature toolbox tutor.
- **The Signets and bounce lands**: inexpensive Commander ramp and fixing.

## The takeaway

Ravnica: City of Guilds introduced a structure Magic still uses, and its mana fixing alone is reason to know the set. If you want to build with it today, the easiest start is a Modern Boros deck with Lightning Helix and Sacred Foundry, or a Commander deck that leans on Signets and Doubling Season.
