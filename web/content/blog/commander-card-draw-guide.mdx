Most Magic: The Gathering Commander decks want roughly ten sources of card draw, with a lean toward repeatable engines over one-time spells. Commander games are long, you face three opponents, and you start with the same seven cards as everyone else, so the player who keeps drawing extra cards usually has the answers and threats when it counts. What kind of draw you run depends heavily on your colors, since each color draws cards in its own way.

This guide covers how much draw to run, the difference between engines and burst draw, and what each color does best. If you want the theory behind why drawing more cards matters, the [card advantage guide](/blog/card-advantage-explained) explains it.

## How much card draw to run

Treat these as starting points, not rules:

| Deck type | Rough draw count | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical Commander deck | About 10 | Keeps your hand stocked through a long game |
| Spell-heavy or combo deck | 12 or more | Needs to dig for specific pieces |
| Aggressive, low-curve deck | 8 or so | Empties its hand fast and refills in bursts |
| Commander that draws cards itself | Fewer | The commander is already an engine |

A repeatable engine that draws a card every turn is worth more than a single spell that draws two. When you count your draw, give engines extra weight. A deck with six good engines and four burst spells is in better shape than a deck with ten one-shot spells.

Also count the cards that draw as a side effect. A creature that draws a card when it enters is draw and a body at once. A removal spell that replaces itself counts too. The [first Commander deck guide](/blog/how-to-build-your-first-commander-deck) uses about ten draw slots in its starting template, and that's a sensible baseline before you adjust.

### Signs you need more draw

- You often have an empty hand by the middle of the game.
- You have a strong board but nothing to rebuild with after a board wipe.
- You regularly can't find your win conditions.
- You lose long games to players who seem to always have an answer.

If those sound familiar, swap a few marginal synergy cards for draw before cutting anything else.

## Engines vs burst draw

Card draw comes in two broad shapes.

### Engines

An engine is a permanent that draws cards repeatedly. **Phyrexian Arena** is the classic example: at the beginning of your upkeep, you draw a card and lose 1 life. Over five turns that's five extra cards for one card spent. **Rhystic Study** is another: whenever an opponent casts a spell, you may draw a card unless that player pays one extra generic mana. In a four-player game that triggers constantly, and opponents either slow themselves down paying the tax or hand you cards.

Engines are strongest when cast early, because every turn they stay on the battlefield is another card. Their weakness is that they're permanents, so they can be removed, and an engine that dies before it draws anything is a card lost.

### Burst draw

Burst draw is a spell that draws several cards at once. **Harmonize** draws three cards for four mana. **Night's Whisper** draws two for two mana at the cost of 2 life. These are reliable: once they resolve, you have the cards, and no one can destroy the effect later.

Burst draw is best for refilling your hand after a board wipe or after you've dumped your hand onto the battlefield. It's weaker early, when you'd rather spend your mana building a board, and late if you need a steady stream rather than one big refill.

### Mixing the two

Most decks want both. Engines give you a long-term edge, and burst spells recover from setbacks. A good rule is to have enough cheap engines that you can play one in the first few turns most games, plus a few burst spells for the moments when your board has been reset.

## Conditional and triggered draw

Some of the best draw in Commander is tied to something your deck already does. These cards draw a card each time a condition happens:

- **Creature-entering draw**, for decks that cast lots of creatures.
- **Creature-dying draw**, for sacrifice and aristocrats decks.
- **Spell-casting draw**, for decks that cast lots of instants and sorceries.
- **Attack or combat-damage draw**, for aggressive decks.

These scale with your plan, which makes them strong in focused decks and weak in unfocused ones. A card that draws whenever a creature enters under your control is excellent in a tokens deck and nearly blank in a spells deck. Pick the triggers your deck hits most often.

### Equipment-style engines

Some colorless engines draw cards when your creatures die or deal damage. **Skullclamp** is the best-known example: equipment that makes a creature smaller and draws you cards when the equipped creature dies. With small creatures or tokens, it can turn each creature into extra cards. Because it's colorless, it fits any deck that makes lots of small bodies, and it shows how strong draw tied to your deck's natural actions can be.

## Card draw by color

Each color draws cards in its own way, which is why a mono-red deck and a mono-blue deck solve this problem differently. The [color pie guide](/blog/mtg-color-pie-guide) covers each color's strengths in more detail.

### Blue

Blue has the most and the most efficient draw. It draws outright, filters through the top of the library, and can draw at instant speed.

- **Rhystic Study**: draw off opponents' spells unless they pay.
- **Brainstorm**: draw three cards, then put two cards from your hand back on top of your library.
- **Fact or Fiction**: reveal the top five cards, an opponent splits them into two piles, and you take one pile.
- **Mulldrifter**: a creature that draws two cards when it enters, with an option to cast it more cheaply just for the draw.

In blue, the question is less "how do I draw" and more "which draw fits my mana and plan."

### Black

Black draws cards by paying life. The cost is real, but Commander's 40 starting life makes it easier to afford.

- **Phyrexian Arena**: a card and 1 life every upkeep.
- **Night's Whisper** and **Sign in Blood**: two cards for 2 life.
- **Read the Bones**: scry 2, then draw two cards, and lose 2 life.
- **Midnight Reaper**: draws a card whenever a nontoken creature you control dies, at the cost of 1 life each time.

Watch your life total. Several life-payment engines at once can drain you faster than your opponents do, especially against aggressive decks.

### Green

Green draws most often off creatures and big bodies, and has some straightforward draw spells too.

- **Harmonize**: draw three cards.
- **Beast Whisperer**: draws a card whenever you cast a creature spell.
- **Guardian Project**: draws when creatures enter under your control, with a restriction on duplicate names.
- **Elvish Visionary** and similar creatures that draw a card when they enter.

Green's draw rewards casting a lot of creatures, which matches its strength at ramp.

### White

White's draw tends to be conditional: it rewards casting small creatures or taxing opponents.

- **Mentor of the Meek**: when another creature with power 2 or less enters under your control, you may pay one mana to draw a card.
- **Esper Sentinel**: a cheap artifact creature that draws when opponents cast noncreature spells unless they pay a tax.

White decks often lean on colorless artifacts and Equipment to fill the gap.

### Red

Red's draw is about speed and volume over long-term advantage.

- **Impulse draw**: exile the top cards of your library and play them for a limited time. **Light Up the Stage** exiles the top two cards and lets you play them until the end of your next turn.
- **Looting**: draw, then discard. **Faithless Looting** draws two and discards two, with flashback so you can use it again.
- **Wheels**: **Wheel of Fortune** makes each player discard their hand and draw seven cards.

Impulse draw rewards using the cards right away, so it pairs well with a lower curve and plenty of mana. Wheels refill everyone, so be sure your deck benefits more than your opponents do.

### Colorless

Every deck can play colorless draw, though it's usually less efficient than colored options.

- **Skullclamp**: covered above.
- **Mind's Eye**: whenever an opponent draws a card, you may pay one mana to draw a card.
- **Jayemdae Tome**: a slow but steady engine that turns mana into cards.

These are most useful in colors with weak draw, like white or red.

## Common card draw mistakes

1. **Too little draw.** The most common Commander building mistake. Count your sources.
2. **All burst, no engines.** One big refill late doesn't keep up with players drawing an extra card every turn.
3. **Draw that doesn't match the deck.** A creature-entering engine in a spells deck rarely triggers.
4. **Ignoring life costs.** Black draw is excellent, but several life-payment cards together add up.
5. **Casting an engine into open removal when a burst spell would do.** If an opponent is clearly holding an answer, a resolved draw spell can't be undone.

## The takeaway

Start with about ten draw sources and give repeatable engines extra weight. Play engines early, keep a few burst spells for rebuilding after a board wipe, and pick conditional draw that triggers off what your deck already does. Then lean into your colors: blue draws freely, black pays life, green rewards creatures, white rewards small creatures and taxes, and red trades long-term advantage for speed.
