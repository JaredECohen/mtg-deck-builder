Fallen Empires is a Magic: The Gathering expansion released in November 1994, the fifth expansion in the game's history. It tells the story of five civilizations on the Dominarian continent of Sarpadia collapsing as the Ice Age closes in, and it was the first set built around creature types as tribes. It's also famous for being massively overprinted, and for a few cards that still matter decades later, above all Hymn to Tourach and High Tide.

## The setting and flavor

Fallen Empires doesn't have a novel-length plot. Its story is told almost entirely through card names, art and flavor text, which could be pieced together like a history book. The setting is Sarpadia, an isolated continent in Dominaria's southern hemisphere, in the centuries after the Brothers' War. That war ended when Urza set off a cataclysmic artifact, and the world has been cooling ever since. On Sarpadia, crops fail, resources run short, and old alliances break down.

Each color had its own civilization, and each one fell:

- **White: Icatia.** A human kingdom defended by the knights of the Order of Leitbur, undermined from within by the Farrelites, a cult of zealots. Icatia held out longest but finally fell to a combined invasion.
- **Blue: Vodalia.** An undersea empire of merfolk, overrun by the Homarids, a crustacean race moving north as the oceans grew colder.
- **Black: the Order of the Ebon Hand.** Clerics who created Thrulls as servants and sacrifices. The Thrulls rebelled and destroyed their creators.
- **Red: the Dwarves of the Crimson Peaks.** Toppled by hordes of orcs and goblins, the first empire to fall.
- **Green: the elves of Havenwood.** They bred the fungus-creatures called Thallids as a food source, and the Thallids ended up overwhelming them.

By the end, the Thrulls are left in charge of most of Sarpadia. The colors also come with a planeswalker cameo: later stories say the planeswalker Tevesh Szat helped the orcs and goblins bring down Icatia.

The Thallids had the longest afterlife. Their 1/1 Saproling tokens, introduced here, became a recurring part of green on Dominaria, and Thallids and Saprolings have returned in later sets set on the plane.

## Mechanics

Fallen Empires has no keywords of its own. Its design ideas, though, were new for 1994.

### Creature types as tribes

Fallen Empires was the first set to tie its creatures together by type and reward you for playing several of the same kind. Thrull Champion gives Thrulls +1/+1 and steals other Thrulls, Icatian Lieutenant pumps Soldiers, Dwarven Lieutenant pumps Dwarves, and Fungal Bloom adds spore counters to Fungus creatures. Today we'd call this tribal design, and almost every modern set does a version of it.

### Tokens and counters everywhere

Fallen Empires is famous for its counters: spore counters on Thallids, tide counters on Homarids, credit counters on Icatian Moneychanger, javelin counters on Icatian Javelineers and more. By MTG Wiki's count there were 14 kinds of counters and five kinds of tokens, so many that Wizards printed a sheet of cardboard counters for players in its magazine, The Duelist. The confusion this caused is one reason later sets settled mostly on +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters for creatures.

Thallid is a good example of the style. It's a 1/1 for `{G}` that gets a spore counter every upkeep, and when it has three you can remove them to make a 1/1 Saproling. The tokens came from many directions: Icatian Town makes four Citizens, Homarid Spawning Bed makes Camarids and Goblin Warrens trades two Goblins for three.

### Storage lands and sacrifice lands

Two land cycles stuck around. The **storage lands** (Bottomless Vault, Dwarven Hold, Hollow Trees, Icatian Store and Sand Silos) can stay tapped to collect storage counters, then cash them in for a burst of mana. The **sacrifice lands** (Ruins of Trokair, Svyelunite Temple, Ebon Stronghold, Dwarven Ruins and Havenwood Battleground) enter tapped and can be sacrificed for two mana of their color at once.

## Limited

Fallen Empires was sold in eight-card packs with six commons and two uncommons, and it came out before booster draft was the standard way to play Limited. There's no reliable record of draft archetypes for it, and it would be misleading to invent them. If you play it today, as some players do for nostalgia, it behaves like other early sets: low-powered, slow, and full of awkward counters to track. The tribal cards give the clearest direction, so drafting one civilization's creature type is the natural plan. If you're new to Limited, our [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains how aggro, midrange and control decks differ.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Type II (January 1995 to the end of 1996)

When Type II (the original name of Standard) was created on January 10, 1995, Fallen Empires was one of only three legal sets, alongside Revised Edition and The Dark. It stayed legal through the arrival of Fourth Edition, Ice Age, Chronicles, Homelands, Alliances and Mirage, and left when new construction rules took effect at the start of 1997.

Its signature contribution was **Hymn to Tourach**, which was part of black's best Type II decks of the era, including the Necropotence decks that defined "Necro Summer" in 1996. Order of the Ebon Hand and Order of Leitbur showed up in aggressive black and white decks, helped by the fact that each has protection from the other's color. Our [formats guide](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) explains how Type II became today's Standard.

### Legacy, Vintage, Pauper and Commander (as of October 2026)

Fallen Empires is far too old for Modern, but its best cards are still around:

- **Hymn to Tourach** remains a classic Legacy and Vintage card in black decks, and is banned in Pauper.
- **High Tide** powers the Legacy combo deck named after it and is banned in Pauper.
- **Goblin Grenade** and **Icatian Javelineers** are Pauper-legal commons that have seen play in aggressive decks.
- **Commander** players find homes for Thallid and other Saproling makers in green Saproling decks, and the storage lands in decks that want big turns.

Many Fallen Empires rares are on the Reserved List, but because of the set's huge print run they've always been widely available. Our [Vintage format guide](/blog/mtg-vintage-format-guide) explains the format where Hymn to Tourach still sees play.

### The overprint

Fallen Empires is a lesson in Magic's business history. After the frenzy of 1994, Wizards printed far more Fallen Empires than players wanted; the company later announced a print run of roughly 312 to 340 million cards. Shipments stopped in early 1995, but boxes sat on store shelves for years. The overprint changed how Wizards decided print quantities afterward.

## Cards from Fallen Empires worth owning

- **Hymn to Tourach**: one of the most iconic black discard spells, still played in Legacy and Vintage.
- **High Tide**: the namesake engine of a classic Legacy combo deck.
- **Goblin Grenade**: a Pauper-legal burn spell for Goblin decks.
- **Thallid**: the original Saproling maker, for green Commander decks.
- **Bottomless Vault** and the other storage lands: fun mana tricks for Commander.

Fallen Empires was a commercial misstep that turned out to be a design milestone. It invented tribal sets, gave Dominaria the Thrulls and the Saprolings, and left Legacy two all-time classics.
