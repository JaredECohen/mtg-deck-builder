Magic: The Gathering has four big joke sets, Unglued, Unhinged, Unstable and Unfinity, but the "Un" spirit also lives in a handful of smaller products most players never see. This guide covers four of them: Unsanctioned, a 2020 box of ready-to-play half-decks; the Unknown Event cards, playtest-style oddities handed out at MagicCon since 2023; the Happy Holidays cards Wizards has given out every December since 2006; and the Heroes of the Realm cards made as internal gifts. None of them are tournament-legal, and that's the point.

## What the products were for

Un-cards break rules on purpose. They reference the real world, the cards' own art, the people at the table and Magic's own history, which is why Wizards marks them so they can't sneak into tournaments. For years the marker was a silver border. Starting with Unfinity in 2022, Wizards switched to an acorn-shaped security stamp, and it began printing some fully tournament-legal cards in black border inside Un products. The four products in this guide all sit on the "not legal" side of that line.

## Unsanctioned

Unsanctioned released at the end of February 2020, timed for Leap Day. Rather than booster packs, it was a single box with five 30-card mono-color half-decks. You and a friend each pick two colors, shuffle the two half-decks together into a 60-card deck, and play. It's the easiest Un-product to just open and play, with no drafting or deckbuilding required.

Most of its 96 cards are reprints from Unglued, Unhinged and Unstable, chosen for how well they play together, including classics like Infernal Spawn of Evil, Look at Me, I'm R&D and Richard Garfield, Ph.D. The 16 new cards were spread across the colors, with three per deck plus one shared card:

- **Surgeon General Commander** is a green legendary Wombat Bat Chameleon that taps for any color and draws when you augment, enchant or mutate a creature.
- **Stet, Draconic Proofreader** is a red flying Dragon that deals 4 damage to anything whose name starts with the same letter as a card you exile from your graveyard, and can delete letters from names.
- **Pippa, Duchess of Dice** turns six-sided dice into Die creature tokens with power and toughness equal to the roll.
- **Acornelia, Fashionable Filcher** counts squirrels in your cards' art, rewarding you with acorn counters.
- **B.O.B. (Bevy of Beebles)** is a blue planeswalker whose loyalty is the number of Beebles you control.
- **Boomstacker** is a red Goblin that literally stacks dice on itself and dies when the stack falls over.
- **Underdome** is a land whose colored mana only pays for "Un-costs."

The half-deck design meant every pairing played differently, and the box's tokens and dice covered the cards that need them. If you want to bring Un-cards into a casual Commander game, ask your group first; our [Rule Zero guide](/blog/commander-rule-zero-guide) covers that conversation.

## The Unknown Event and its playtest cards

The Unknown Event is a recurring MagicCon event that began at MagicCon: Philadelphia in February 2023. Players open sealed product and also get a stack of new "playtest" cards: cards in a mock-up, printed-on-a-test-sheet style, many designed by Gavin Verhey, that do things no normal card would. Scryfall groups them under the code UNK, and new waves arrived at MagicCons through 2026.

They're a tour of design ideas that are too silly, too rules-breaking or too unfinished for a real set. Long-Term Phyresis Study gives you "poison tolerance" so it takes an extra poison counter to lose. The Inspector Inspector makes your other creatures enter as copies of Thraben Inspector. Fear of Unsleeved Decks gets bigger if you aren't playing with sleeves. Seventh Stage of Magic Design simply doubles everything you do. Many are written for the event's Commander sealed play, with legends like The Beleaguered Boxer that only work in a deck built from a limited number of booster packs.

Playtest cards first appeared in 2019's Mystery Booster and later in Mystery Booster 2, but the Unknown Event cards are their own set. They aren't legal anywhere, though they're fair game in a casual game if everyone agrees. For etiquette around playing with non-standard cards, see our [proxies and playtest etiquette guide](/blog/mtg-proxies-and-playtest-etiquette).

## Happy Holidays

Since 2006, Wizards has printed a small number of holiday-themed joke cards each year and given them out at its holiday parties and to business partners and friends. They've never been sold. Early ones are silver-bordered, and since the early 2020s they've moved to black borders with the acorn stamp.

The cards are puns on winter holidays: Fruitcake Elemental is indestructible but deals 7 damage to you at the end of your turn; Gifts Given is a riff on Gifts Ungiven that searches an opponent's library for presents to give yourself; Naughty // Nice is a split card where one half steals a card from another player's library and the other gifts a card from yours; Stocking Tiger enters with a sealed booster pack under it that you unwrap when it deals combat damage. Recent years brought Chaos Wrap, Seasonal Sequels, Eggnogger's 'Stache and Cheer, a holiday spin on the Elemental Incarnation cycle.

## Heroes of the Realm

Heroes of the Realm cards are another internal-only series, released most years between 2017 and 2025 under Scryfall codes PHTR and PH17 through PH23. They're one-off mythic legends, planeswalkers and other oddities, many of them nodding to Wizards' own products and teams. Examples include Dungeon Master, a white-blue planeswalker that rolls a d20; The Legend of Arena, a Saga that can be your commander; Keeper of the Secret Lair, which lets you cast Secret Lair spells with flash; and Mr. Monopoly, On the Go, a Monopoly-themed planeswalker. They were never sold, so most players will only ever see them in a card database.

## How they played

None of these products has a Constructed meta, because none of their cards are legal in any sanctioned format, Commander included. As of October 2026, the official Commander rules allow only tournament-legal cards, so silver-bordered and acorn-stamped cards need your playgroup's agreement. They live in casual games, Un-themed cubes and collections. If you're curious how a cube can fold joke cards in alongside real ones, see our [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## Cards from these products worth owning

- **Unsanctioned (the whole box)**: the only product here built to play right out of the box, and still the easiest way to show friends what Un-cards are.
- **Surgeon General Commander**: a favorite casual commander for Un-games.
- **Stet, Draconic Proofreader**: one of the funniest Dragons ever printed.
- **Acornelia, Fashionable Filcher**: a natural fit for a casual Squirrel deck.
- **Happy Holidays cards**: scarce collectibles with a fun yearly tradition behind them.

These are corners of Magic built for laughs, not tournaments. If you love the Un-sets' sense of humor but want something your Commander table will accept without a Rule Zero talk, build around real, black-bordered cards that share the theme, like a Squirrel deck.
