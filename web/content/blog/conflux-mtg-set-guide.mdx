Conflux is the second set of Magic: The Gathering's Shards of Alara block, released in February 2009. It's the moment the five shards of the plane Alara began crashing back together, so the set mixes shard colors, adds five-color cards and brings back domain. It also printed two of the most-played cards in Modern history, Path to Exile and Noble Hierarch, plus Nicol Bolas's first planeswalker card.

## The story

Alara was once a single plane. Long ago it split into five "shards," each one holding only three allied colors of mana and each missing the other two. Shards of Alara, the first set in the block, showed those five worlds in isolation: Bant, a sunlit kingdom of knights and angels; Esper, a realm where everything is grafted with the magical metal etherium; Grixis, a dying land of necromancers and the undead; Jund, a volcanic wilderness ruled by dragons; and Naya, a jungle of giant beasts and the elves who worship them.

Conflux is where those worlds start to collide. The shards drift back together in an event the story also calls the Conflux, and each realm suddenly meets cultures it never knew existed, often at the point of a sword. The cards show it: commons like Esper Cormorants and Rhox Bodyguard are two-color cards, and creatures throughout the set get stronger "if you control a blue permanent" or "if you control a green permanent," a sign that the shards' missing colors are coming back.

The force behind it is Nicol Bolas, the ancient elder dragon planeswalker. Bolas lost much of his old power in the multiversal event known as the Mending, and he saw Alara's reunion as a vast surge of mana he could harvest to get it back. His agents across the shards sped things up by destroying the obelisks that kept each shard stable. Conflux marked his first appearance as a planeswalker card, Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker, and it introduced Malfegor, a demon-dragon he had pressed into service.

The main point-of-view character is Ajani Goldmane, a leonin from Naya whose brother Jazal was murdered at the end of the first set. Ajani's search for the killer leads him across the merging shards and eventually to Bolas. The full arc is told in Doug Beyer's novel *Alara Unbroken*: the reunification creates a swirling vortex of mana called the Maelstrom, wakes the five-color hydra Progenitus, and spawns a war between the former shards. In the block's last set, Alara Reborn, Ajani manages to stop Bolas before the dragon can take in the Maelstrom's power, and Bolas leaves the plane. Planeswalkers Sarkhan Vol, a dragon-worshipping shaman who ends up as Bolas's servant, and Elspeth Tirel, who had been protecting Bant, also play parts.

## Mechanics

### Domain

Domain counts the basic land types among lands you control, from zero to five. Cards with domain get better for every type you have: Matca Rioters has power and toughness equal to that count, Aven Trailblazer is a flier whose toughness equals it, and Exploding Borders fetches a basic land and then deals damage to a player equal to it. Domain first appeared in Invasion; Conflux turned it into an ability word, meaning the label explains the theme but carries no rules meaning of its own. It rewards running several colors, which suited a set about five worlds merging.

### Basic landcycling

A common cycle of instants and sorceries pairs a pricey effect with basic landcycling for two mana. Fiery Fall, for example, deals 5 damage to a creature for six mana, or you can pay `{1}{R}` and discard it to search your library for a basic land. These cards are never dead: early they fix your mana, late they're spells. That made them perfect for the set's multicolor decks.

### Five-color cards

Conflux was the first set with a real five-color theme. Fusion Elemental is a plain 8/8 at uncommon, Child of Alara destroys every nonland permanent when it dies, Maelstrom Archangel lets you cast a free spell whenever it connects, and the mythic sorcery named Conflux tutors one card of each color. Progenitus costs two of each colored mana and has protection from everything. A few cards, like Paragon of the Amesha, carry a five-color activated ability on a one-color body.

### Returning shard mechanics

The first set's mechanics returned. Exalted (Bant) gives a creature +1/+1 when it attacks alone, and every instance stacks; Noble Hierarch is the famous example. Devour (Jund) lets a creature sacrifice others as it enters for extra +1/+1 counters, as on Voracious Dragon. Unearth (Grixis) brings a creature back from the graveyard for one hasty attack, as on Hellspark Elemental. Naya's "power 5 or greater" theme also continued with cards like Paleoloth.

## Limited

Conflux was drafted with two packs of Shards of Alara followed by one Conflux pack, since block drafts of that era opened the oldest set first. Because the first set was built around three-color shards, Limited in this block was mostly about drafting one of the five shard combinations (Bant, Esper, Grixis, Jund or Naya) and fixing your mana, rather than the ten two-color pairs a modern set would use.

Conflux nudged that format in two directions:

- **Bigger, greedier mana.** Domain cards, basic landcyclers, Rupture Spire, Armillary Sphere and Exotic Orchard made four- and five-color "domain" decks possible. Players could take the best card in each pack and patch the mana together.
- **Cross-shard pairs.** The two-color Outlanders (2/2s with protection from an enemy color) and the allied-color commons gave decks that weren't strictly a shard a little more to work with.

At common and uncommon, removal like Fiery Fall, Wretched Banquet, Dark Temper and Drag Down, plus the efficient fliers and exalted creatures, were the cards you wanted early. The format was generally on the slower side: three-color decks with taplands and fixers rarely curved out fast, so card quality and two-for-ones mattered more than tempo. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (February 2009 to October 2010)

Conflux joined a Standard that already had Lorwyn and Shadowmoor blocks, Tenth Edition and Shards of Alara. Faeries, Five-Color Control, Kithkin and RW Reveillark decks were all around, and Conflux added pieces to most of them. Path to Exile became the go-to white removal spell for Kithkin, Reveillark and control decks. Volcanic Fallout gave red a clean answer to Faeries' small fliers. Martial Coup became a control finisher.

When Lorwyn and Shadowmoor rotated in October 2009, the Alara block became the core of Standard alongside Magic 2010 and Zendikar. Jund, built around Alara Reborn's Bloodbraid Elf, dominated that year. Conflux supplied cards to the decks that fought it: Noble Hierarch and Knight of the Reliquary in Bant and Naya decks, Path to Exile and Martial Coup in white decks, and Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker in Grixis control. No Conflux card was ever banned in Standard. The set left Standard when Scars of Mirrodin arrived in October 2010. For how this rotation works, see our [rotation explainer](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### Modern, Legacy, Vintage and Commander (as of October 2026)

Conflux punches far above its size in older formats:

- **Path to Exile** has been a Modern staple since the format began and still sees play in Legacy and Commander.
- **Noble Hierarch** was a mainstay of Modern's green creature decks for years and appears in Legacy as well.
- **Knight of the Reliquary** is a long-running Legacy card in land-heavy green-white decks.
- **Progenitus** has been a target for Legacy decks that cheat big creatures into play.
- **Commander** players love Child of Alara, Progenitus and Nicol Bolas, Planeswalker as five- and three-color centerpieces, plus utility cards like Exotic Orchard, Reliquary Tower and Path to Exile.

No Conflux card is banned in Modern, Legacy, Vintage or Commander, and the set has no Reserved List cards. Our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) explains where these cards fit today.

## Cards from Conflux worth owning

- **Path to Exile**: one of the best one-mana removal spells ever printed, legal in every eternal format.
- **Noble Hierarch**: fixing, acceleration and exalted on a one-drop.
- **Knight of the Reliquary**: a land-fetching threat for Legacy and Commander lands decks.
- **Progenitus**: a famous cheat target and a five-color Commander option.
- **Exotic Orchard** and **Reliquary Tower**: easy additions to many Commander decks.
- **Martial Coup**: a flexible white board wipe for Commander.

Conflux is small, but its best commons and uncommons aged remarkably well, and it remains the set where Nicol Bolas first stepped onto the battlefield as a planeswalker.
