Chronicles is a Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in July 1995, and it was the first set made entirely of reprints. It brought back cards from four early expansions, Arabian Nights, Antiquities, Legends and The Dark, in white borders for English-speaking players. A month later, Europe got its own versions: Renaissance in German and French, and Rinascimento in Italian. Together they're the reason the Reserved List exists.

## What the sets were for

### Chronicles

In 1994 and 1995 Magic was growing faster than Wizards could print it. The early expansions had small print runs, so players who joined later couldn't find cards like City of Brass, Erhnam Djinn or the Elder Dragons from Legends. Chronicles was originally pitched as a "best of" collection to fix that. It reprinted a selection of cards from the four early expansions with white borders and new copyright dates, in packs that sold alongside Fourth Edition.

It worked as a reprint, but it upset collectors and store owners who owned the originals, since the scarce cards they'd invested in were suddenly common. The response from players and store owners was strong enough that in 1996 Wizards announced the Reserved List, a promise not to reprint certain older cards in tournament-legal form. Ironically, cards that Chronicles had already reprinted were mostly left off that list, which is why cards like City of Brass, Blood Moon and Nicol Bolas could be printed again later while cards from the same era such as the original dual lands cannot. For more on how old card text compares to modern wording, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

### Renaissance

Renaissance solved a different problem. Wizards had a rule at the time that a card had to appear in a black-bordered printing in a language before it could appear in a white-bordered core set in that language. Fourth Edition was coming to German and French, and it included cards from Arabian Nights, Antiquities, Legends and The Dark that had never been printed in those languages. Renaissance gave those cards a black-bordered German and French printing first. That's why its list overlaps heavily with Chronicles but isn't identical, and why it includes cards like Strip Mine, Sylvan Library and Land Tax.

### Rinascimento

Rinascimento was the Italian equivalent. Because Legends and The Dark had already been printed in Italian, it didn't need their cards, so it's a shorter list drawn mainly from Arabian Nights and Antiquities, plus a handful of other older cards.

## The story

None of these sets has a plot. Their cards come from four very different early worlds: the Arabian Nights setting drawn from the One Thousand and One Nights stories, Antiquities' Brothers' War between Urza and Mishra on Dominaria, the wizards, heroes and Elder Dragons of Legends, and the gloomy Dark Ages of The Dark. Chronicles' most famous legends are the five Elder Dragons: Nicol Bolas, Arcades Sabboth, Vaevictis Asmadi, Palladia-Mors and Chromium. Nicol Bolas went on to become Magic's longest-running villain.

## Limited

These weren't designed as draft sets. They were sold for collectors and Constructed players to fill out their decks, and their packs mixed cards from four different design eras with no shared mechanics. Some groups did draft or play sealed with them as a novelty, but they have no archetypes in the modern sense. If you want to recreate the feeling of drafting old cards, building a cube from early Magic is the usual route; see our [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

In 1995 Chronicles mattered because it made strong cards from small early print runs available to everyone, at a time when those cards were legal in the main tournament format. Cards like City of Brass, Erhnam Djinn and the Urza lands went from hard-to-find to easy to pick up.

Today the reprints follow each card's normal legality. As of October 2026:

- **Blood Moon** and **City of Brass** are legal in Modern thanks to later reprints, and Blood Moon is a regular sideboard card there.
- **The Urza lands** power Tron decks in Modern and Legacy and are popular in Commander.
- **Strip Mine** is banned in Legacy and restricted in Vintage, while still legal in Commander.
- **Sylvan Library** is legal in Legacy and a Commander staple in green, and **Mishra's Factory** is legal in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Ante cards** like Jeweled Bird, Bronze Tablet, Rebirth and Tempest Efreet are banned in every sanctioned format, as all ante cards are.
- **Pradesh Gypsies**, reprinted in Renaissance, was removed from all formats in 2020 when Wizards banned a group of cards with offensive names or depictions.

For a broader look at which formats allow which cards, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) or our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Chronicles and Renaissance worth owning

- **City of Brass**: a five-color land for Commander and Legacy.
- **Blood Moon**: a powerful sideboard card in Modern and Legacy.
- **Urza's Tower, Urza's Mine and Urza's Power Plant**: the Tron engine.
- **Ashnod's Altar**: one of Commander's key sacrifice outlets.
- **Sylvan Library**: card selection that Legacy and Commander players both value.
- **Strip Mine**: a Commander utility land, banned in Legacy.
- **Fellwar Stone**: an efficient mana rock in multiplayer.

Chronicles and Renaissance are mostly history today, but they're also a way to own original 1995 printings of cards that still see play.
