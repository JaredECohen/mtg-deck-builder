Modern Horizons 3 is a Magic: The Gathering supplemental set released in June 2024 that skipped Standard and went straight into Modern. Like the two Horizons sets before it, it mixed brand-new cards designed with Modern in mind with dozens of older cards reprinted into the format for the first time, and it had the biggest effect on competitive eternal formats of any 2024 product. Its headline themes were the return of energy and a new wave of colorless Eldrazi.

## What the set was for

Modern Horizons sets exist to add cards to Modern without passing through Standard first. That lets Wizards print effects that would be too strong or too niche for a rotating format, and it lets them inject old favorites into Modern for the first time. MH3 did both: alongside the new cards it reprinted the five enemy-colored fetch lands, Flusterstorm, Toxic Deluge, Buried Alive, Phyrexian Tower and a full cycle of color Medallions.

The set has flavor rather than a single plot. Its cards visit planes from across Magic's history, and its biggest storyline beat is the Eldrazi: Ulamog, Kozilek and Emrakul all return in new forms, with a supporting cast of devoid creatures that make Eldrazi Spawn. Several planeswalkers appear as their younger selves, printed as legendary creatures that transform into planeswalkers once you meet a condition, which tells a small "before the spark and after" story on each card.

The set was also built as a Limited environment in its own right, with ten two-color draft archetypes and a lot of mana fixing. It's a popular choice for cube designers, too; if that's your thing, see our [cube draft guide](/blog/mtg-cube-draft-guide).

## Mechanics

MH3 uses more than forty keywords and named mechanics, most of them callbacks. These are the ones that defined the set.

### Energy

Energy, first seen in Kaladesh, is a resource you collect as counters on yourself (shown as `{E}`). It stays until you spend it, so cards can bank energy now and cash it in later. Galvanic Discharge is the clearest example: for `{R}` you get three energy and then spend any amount of it as damage to a creature or planeswalker, so it can deal three damage on its own or more if you have energy saved up. Guide of Souls, Static Prison, Amped Raptor and Wrath of the Skies all run on the same counters. In practice, energy turned cheap creatures into engines, and it became the backbone of the format's best aggressive deck.

### Devoid, Eldrazi Spawn and emerge

Devoid cards have colored mana symbols in their cost but no color, which matters for cards that care about colorless spells. Many of the set's Eldrazi make 0/1 Eldrazi Spawn tokens that you can sacrifice for `{C}`, so they ramp you toward huge threats. Writhing Chrysalis is a common that makes two Spawn when you cast it and grows when you sacrifice other Eldrazi. Emerge lets you cast a big creature by sacrificing one of yours and paying less, and MH3 added a variant, "emerge from artifact," on cards such as Crabomination.

### Transforming planeswalker legends

A cycle of mythic legendary creatures starts as a one- or two-mana creature and transforms into a planeswalker when you meet a condition. Ajani, Nacatl Pariah makes a 2/1 Cat when it enters and flips when another Cat you control dies. Tamiyo, Inquisitive Student flips when you draw your third card in a turn. Grist, Voracious Larva, Sorin of House Markov and Ral, Monsoon Mage complete the cycle. They're cheap enough to play early and threatening enough to matter late.

### Modal double-faced spell lands

An uncommon cycle pairs a spell on the front with a land on the back. Fell the Profane is a four-mana kill spell that can instead be played as a Swamp that enters untapped if you pay 3 life. Sink into Stupor and Witch Enchanter work the same way. These cut the cost of running interaction because a card that isn't needed as a spell becomes a land drop.

### Flares and Titans

The Flare cycle of rares lets you sacrifice a nontoken creature of the matching color instead of paying mana: Flare of Denial is a free counterspell, Flare of Cultivation free ramp. The Titans are three-mana legends that sacrifice themselves on entry unless they escaped from the graveyard. Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury is the famous one: cast it for three damage and three life, then later escape it as a recurring 6/6 that repeats the effect every time it attacks.

### Returning keywords

Kindred (the new name for tribal), umbra armor (the new name for totem armor), storm, evoke, madness, eternalize, bestow, flashback, escalate and many more all show up. For quick definitions, see our [MTG keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary).

## Limited

MH3 drafted as a powerful, mid-to-fast format with plenty of fixing from the common Landscape lands, which sacrifice to fetch one of three basic types. Reviewers agreed every two-color pair had a real plan:

- **White-blue:** energy and fliers, with commons like Solstice Zealot and Petrifying Meddler.
- **Blue-black:** "draw your third card each turn" payoffs, such as Serum Visionary and Unfathomable Truths.
- **Black-red:** artifacts and sacrifice, with Refurbished Familiar and Cranial Ram.
- **Red-green:** Eldrazi Spawn ramp, the deck built around Writhing Chrysalis and Eldrazi Repurposer.
- **Green-white:** aggressive counters and go-wide creatures.
- **White-black:** modified creatures and sacrifice, with Chthonian Nightmare and Accursed Marauder.
- **Blue-red:** energy spells, led by Izzet Generatorium and Electrozoa.
- **Black-green:** modified creatures, with Cursed Wombat, Expanding Ooze and Nyxborn Hydra.
- **Red-white:** the fastest deck, using energy and haste to end games early.
- **Green-blue:** colorless Eldrazi ramp that often splashed for the best red-green common.

Red-white aggro and red-green Eldrazi were widely rated the top decks. At common, Writhing Chrysalis, Galvanic Discharge and Fanged Flames were the premium picks, while uncommons like Static Prison, Spawn-Gang Commander and Voltstorm Angel pulled drafters into colors. Removal was plentiful but sweepers were rare, which rewarded creature decks. If you're new to the format type, our [draft vs sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide) explains the basics.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Modern

MH3 changed Modern more than any set since Modern Horizons 2. Three new decks came straight out of it in its first months:

- **Nadu combo.** Nadu, Winged Wisdom turned cheap targeting effects into a stream of free cards and lands. It was banned in Modern on August 26, 2024, in the same announcement that banned Grief.
- **Eldrazi ramp.** Ugin's Labyrinth, alongside Eldrazi Temple from older sets, and new threats like Emrakul, the World Anew, Ulamog, the Defiler and Devourer of Destiny gave Modern a fast colorless ramp deck that's stayed a fixture.
- **Boros Energy.** Ajani, Nacatl Pariah, Guide of Souls, Ocelot Pride, Galvanic Discharge, Static Prison and Phlage, Titan of Fire's Fury formed the most played deck in Modern for long stretches of 2024 and 2025. Wizards banned Amped Raptor on December 16, 2024 to slow it down. That didn't break the deck's hold, and on May 18, 2026 Wizards banned Phlage, saying nearly every aggressive or midrange deck had either adopted it or disappeared.

Other MH3 cards spread into existing decks: Psychic Frog in blue-black tempo, Nethergoyf in graveyard decks, the Flares and the spell lands as flexible interaction, and Ruby Medallion in red storm lists. For the bigger picture of the format, see our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

### Legacy and Vintage

Legacy felt the set almost as hard. On December 16, 2024, Wizards banned Psychic Frog and Vexing Bauble in Legacy (Vexing Bauble had already been restricted in Vintage in August 2024). On March 31, 2025, Sowing Mycospawn was banned in Legacy for letting colorless Eldrazi decks fetch utility lands and exile opposing ones. As of October 2026, Nadu is banned in Legacy too, while Ocelot Pride and Kozilek's Command remain legal there.

### Commander

Nadu, Winged Wisdom was banned in Commander on September 23, 2024. Beyond that, MH3 became a deep Commander supply set: the enemy fetch lands, Toxic Deluge, Phyrexian Tower, the Medallions and Six are all among the most played cards from the set on EDHREC. For how Commander groups rate decks by power, see our guide to [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained).

Pauper felt the set too: **Basking Broodscale**, a two-mana common that makes an Eldrazi Spawn token whenever +1/+1 counters are put on it, became the engine of a Pauper combo deck and is banned in Pauper as of October 2026.

## Cards from Modern Horizons 3 worth owning

- **The enemy fetch lands** (Flooded Strand, Polluted Delta, Bloodstained Mire, Wooded Foothills, Windswept Heath): mana base staples in every eternal format.
- **Ocelot Pride**: a one-drop that is still legal in both Modern and Legacy as of October 2026.
- **Ugin's Labyrinth**: the engine of Modern Eldrazi.
- **Emrakul, the World Anew**: a finisher in Modern Eldrazi and a feared Commander bomb.
- **Psychic Frog**: banned in Legacy but still legal in Modern as of October 2026.
- **Six**: one of the best lands-matter commanders and support creatures.
- **Toxic Deluge**: a premier Commander board wipe.
- **Fell the Profane** and the other spell lands: quiet upgrades for any deck that plays these colors.

Modern Horizons 3 is the rare supplemental set whose cards still shape tournaments years later. If you play Modern, the energy package and the Eldrazi package are worth understanding even if you don't play them, since you'll face both.
