Modern Horizons 2 is the Magic: The Gathering supplemental set released in June 2021 that skipped Standard and put its new cards straight into Modern. It is probably the most influential non-Standard set ever printed: Ragavan, Urza's Saga, Murktide Regent, Solitude and the rest of the evoke Elementals rewrote Modern and Legacy within weeks, and several of its cards were later banned.

## What the set was for

Modern Horizons sets exist to solve a specific problem. Modern is a non-rotating format, and for years the only way new cards entered it was through Standard sets, which have to be balanced for a much smaller card pool. Wizards' answer, starting with the first Modern Horizons in 2019, was a set designed for Modern players: new cards that would never have to survive a Standard season, plus older cards that had never been legal in Modern, such as Counterspell, Vindicate and Imperial Recruiter.

Modern Horizons 2 leaned into nostalgia even harder than the first set. Its flavor is a tour of Magic history rather than a plot. Squirrels return from the Odyssey era, Dauthi shadow creatures from Tempest, Kaldra Compleat reworks the legendary Kaldra equipment from the original Mirrodin block, and Urza's own lands and constructs via Urza's Saga. The five evoke Elementals are a nod to Lorwyn's Incarnations and Asmoranomardicadaistinaculdacar (yes, that is the full card name) shows the set did not take itself too seriously. There is no single villain or ending; each card is a small callback.

The reprints mattered as much as the new cards. All five enemy-colored fetch lands (Arid Mesa, Marsh Flats, Misty Rainforest, Scalding Tarn and Verdant Catacombs) appear at rare, and Counterspell finally became legal in Modern at uncommon. For the wider picture of which formats these cards play in, see our [guide to Magic's formats](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Mechanics

### Evoke

Evoke is an alternative cost that lets you cast a creature cheaply, but the creature is sacrificed as soon as it enters, so you get only its enters-the-battlefield effect. The original Lorwyn version of evoke was a mana cost. MH2's five mythic Elemental Incarnations have an evoke cost of exiling a card of the same color from your hand, which means they cost zero mana. Solitude has flash and lifelink and exiles another creature when it enters; evoking it is a free removal spell. Grief makes an opponent discard their best nonland card, Fury deals 4 damage divided among creatures and planeswalkers, Subtlety puts a creature or planeswalker spell on the top or bottom of its owner's library, and Endurance shuffles a graveyard to the bottom of its owner's library. Paying a card for a free spell is card disadvantage, but in fast formats tempo matters more, and these became some of the most played cards in Modern and Legacy.

### Returning mechanics

Flashback (Strike It Rich, Kaleidoscorch), madness (Hell Mongrel, Kitchen Imp), suspend (Profane Tutor, Glimpse of Tomorrow), cycling (Fractured Sanity, Flame Blitz), modular (Arcbound Javelineer, Arcbound Mouser), affinity (Sojourner's Companion, Junk Winder) and storm (Chatterstorm, Galvanic Relay) all came back. Delirium returned on Dragon's Rage Channeler and Unholy Heat, and converge on Prismatic Ending and Kaleidoscorch. Our [MTG keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) defines each one.

### Squirrels, Food, Treasure and Clues

Green and black shared a Squirrel token theme, led by Chatterfang, Squirrel General, which adds a Squirrel to every batch of tokens you make. Food, Treasure and Clue tokens show up across the set, from Tireless Provisioner to Fae Offering, and fed the blue-green and black-green draft decks.

## Limited

Modern Horizons 2 is a powerful and complicated draft format, built around ten two-color archetypes:

- **White-blue, artifacts:** affinity creatures and artifact payoffs such as Filigree Attendant, whose power equals the number of artifacts you control.
- **White-black, reanimator:** recursion like Late to Dinner at common and Young Necromancer at uncommon to bring back your best creatures.
- **Blue-black, graveyard control:** slower decks that use surveil, self-discard and value spells like Said // Done.
- **Blue-red, delirium:** looting and spells that turn on bigger effects, with Prophetic Titan as a payoff.
- **Black-red, madness:** discard outlets like The Underworld Cookbook feeding madness cards such as Hell Mongrel, Kitchen Imp and Skophos Reaver.
- **Red-green, storm:** cheap spells and Galvanic Relay; often more of a support pair for five-color converge decks with Kaleidoscorch and Glinting Creeper.
- **Black-green, Squirrels:** aggressive sacrifice decks with Nested Shambler and Ravenous Squirrel.
- **Red-white, artifact aggro:** modular creatures and equipment beating down.
- **Green-white, +1/+1 counters:** reinforce cards like Bannerhide Krushok and counter payoffs.
- **Blue-green, tokens:** Clues, Food and Treasure, with Fae Offering and Junk Winder as engines.

Removal at common was unusually good. Bone Shards kills any creature or planeswalker for one mana plus a sacrifice or discard, and Unholy Heat and Mine Collapse handle most threats. Common indestructible artifact dual lands like Silverbluff Bridge made five-color converge decks a real option for patient drafters. The format punished slow starts from the Squirrel and artifact aggro decks, but the graveyard and token decks could take over long games. If you are new to drafting, our guide on [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains how to weigh removal against synergy.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Modern

Modern Horizons 2 hit Modern harder than any set before it. Within months, the most common decks were built on its cards:

- **Izzet Murktide** combined Ragavan, Dragon's Rage Channeler, Murktide Regent, Unholy Heat and Counterspell into a tempo deck. Wizards' own metagame coverage in 2022 described it as at or near the top of Modern for a year after release.
- **Hammer Time** used Urza's Saga and Esper Sentinel alongside equipment to threaten very fast kills while still grinding.
- **Four-color Omnath and Elemental decks** used Solitude, Fury and Endurance, often with Ephemerate, to gain card advantage from evoke triggers.
- **Living End** got Shardless Agent, a Modern-legal cascade creature.

Two cards were eventually banned in Modern. Fury was banned on December 4, 2023, and Grief was banned on August 26, 2024, after decks evoking it and returning it to the battlefield made turn-one discard too common. Ragavan, Solitude, Urza's Saga and Murktide Regent remain legal in Modern as of October 2026. For how Modern's ban list is managed, see our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

### Legacy and Vintage

Legacy felt the set too. Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer was banned there on January 25, 2022, aimed at blue-red Delver decks, and Grief was banned in Legacy in the same August 26, 2024 announcement that removed it from Modern. Murktide Regent, Dragon's Rage Channeler, Endurance, Solitude and Dauthi Voidwalker all became Legacy staples. In Vintage, Urza's Saga was restricted on August 26, 2024.

### Pauper and Commander

Several commons were banned in Pauper: Chatterstorm, Galvanic Relay and Sojourner's Companion are all on the Pauper ban list as of October 2026. Unholy Heat, Counterspell and Lose Focus are still Pauper-legal.

In Commander, Chatterfang, Esper Sentinel, Yavimaya, Cabal Coffers, Grist and Archon of Cruelty are widely played. Braids, Cabal Minion, reprinted here, is on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026, which matters for [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). The set also reprinted Upheaval, which has been banned in Commander since long before MH2 and stays banned.

## Cards from Modern Horizons 2 worth owning

- **Ragavan, Nimble Pilferer:** still one of the best one-drops in Modern.
- **Urza's Saga:** the backbone of Modern artifact decks and a strong Commander land.
- **Solitude:** free, instant-speed removal for any white deck in Modern or Legacy.
- **The five enemy fetch lands:** played in every format that allows them.
- **Murktide Regent and Dragon's Rage Channeler:** the core of blue-red tempo in Modern and Legacy.
- **Counterspell and Prismatic Ending:** cheap interaction that never goes out of style.
- **Esper Sentinel:** a one-drop that draws cards in Modern artifact decks and multiplayer games.
- **Chatterfang, Squirrel General:** a top token commander and support card.
- **Yavimaya, Cradle of Growth and Cabal Coffers:** Commander mana staples.

If you play Modern, Modern Horizons 2 is close to unavoidable: a large share of the format's best cards come from this one set, and understanding Ragavan, Urza's Saga and the evoke Elementals explains much of how the format plays.
