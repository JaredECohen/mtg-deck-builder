Commander 2013 is a set of five preconstructed Magic: The Gathering Commander decks released in November 2013. Each 100-card deck is built around one of the three-color shards from the plane of Alara and comes with three legendary creatures that can lead it. The product introduced the tempting offer mechanic and Magic's first cards that work from the command zone, and it gave eternal formats two lasting staples: **True-Name Nemesis** and **Toxic Deluge**.

## What the set was for

Commander 2013 was the second set of Commander decks after the original 2011 product, and it set the pattern for the decade that followed: ready-to-play decks for newer players, with enough new cards to tempt the people who already played the format.

Its design goals show up on almost every new card. The three-color shards give each deck a clear identity. Tempting offer cards are built for four-player tables, and the curses push opponents to attack someone other than you. Most importantly, Wizards started designing for the command zone. **Oloro, Ageless Ascetic** gains you life every turn even when you never cast it, and **Derevi, Empyrial Tactician** can be put onto the battlefield straight from the command zone. That idea later got a name, eminence, in Commander 2017.

The product had no storyline of its own. A short web story set on Fiora accompanied the release, but the decks themselves draw on characters and flavor from across the Multiverse, especially Alara.

## The decks

Each deck has a face commander, a second new legend in the same colors and a reprinted legend from Alara. All three come as oversized foils, so you can pick which one leads.

### Evasive Maneuvers (green-white-blue, Bant)

**Derevi, Empyrial Tactician** (mythic) is a 2/3 flier that taps or untaps a permanent when it enters and whenever one of your creatures deals combat damage to a player. It can also be put onto the battlefield from the command zone for `{1}{G}{W}{U}`, which is cheaper than casting it after a few deaths. The deck is about evasive creatures and tap-untap tricks. The alternate commanders are **Roon of the Hidden Realm**, which flickers creatures for value, and the reprinted **Rubinia Soulsinger**.

### Eternal Bargain (white-blue-black, Esper)

**Oloro, Ageless Ascetic** (mythic) gains you 2 life every upkeep, even while it sits in the command zone, and turns each life gain into a card and a drain if you pay `{1}`. The deck is a slow lifegain control deck with plenty of board wipes. **Sydri, Galvanic Genius** animates artifacts, and **Sharuum the Hegemon** returns an artifact from your graveyard when it enters.

### Mind Seize (blue-black-red, Grixis)

**Jeleva, Nephalia's Scourge** (mythic) exiles cards from the top of every library when it enters, then lets you cast an instant or sorcery from among them for free each time it attacks. The deck is a spell-theft and group-punishment deck. Its alternate commander, **Nekusar, the Mindrazer**, makes everyone draw an extra card and deals 1 damage to an opponent for each card they draw, and it became more popular than the face commander. **Thraximundar** is the reprint.

### Power Hungry (black-red-green, Jund)

**Prossh, Skyraider of Kher** (mythic) makes a 0/1 Kobold token for each mana spent to cast it, then sacrifices creatures to pump itself. Recasting it from the command zone makes more Kobolds each time. It's a sacrifice and tokens deck. **Shattergang Brothers** forces opponents to sacrifice whatever you sacrifice, and **Sek'Kuar, Deathkeeper** makes hasty tokens when your creatures die.

### Nature of the Beast (red-green-white, Naya)

**Marath, Will of the Wild** (mythic) enters with +1/+1 counters equal to the mana spent on it and spends them to grow other creatures, deal damage or make an Elemental token. The deck is about +1/+1 counters, tokens and big creatures. **Gahiji, Honored One** gives every creature that attacks an opponent +2/+0, and **Mayael the Anima** digs for large creatures.

### Tempting offer

Each tempting offer card is a sorcery with a generous effect that every opponent may also take. For each opponent who accepts, you get the effect again. **Tempt with Discovery** fetches a land, lets each opponent fetch one, and gives you another land for each opponent who does. **Tempt with Vengeance** makes X hasty 1/1 Elementals on the same terms. The cycle covers all five colors and makes players choose between helping themselves and feeding you.

### Notable reprints

The decks are also a good source of format staples: **Sol Ring**, **Command Tower**, **Swiftfoot Boots**, **Cultivate**, **Avenger of Zendikar**, **Baleful Strix**, **Propaganda**, **Goblin Bombardment**, **Sanguine Bond** and **Mirror Entity**. Each deck also has its shard's tri-land, panorama and cycling land, and the ten Return to Ravnica guildgates are spread across the five decks.

## How it plays

Out of the box, Commander 2013 decks are slow. They have lots of tapped lands and taplands, a few clunky reprints and big, swingy top ends. They work best against each other, which was the point: five friends can each open a deck and play a fair four- or five-player game. In today's [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) the unmodified decks sit at the low end.

Each deck has a clear path to upgrade. Eternal Bargain gets much stronger with more card draw and cheaper interaction around Oloro. Mind Seize turns into a scary "wheel" deck if you build around Nekusar with cards that make everyone draw. Power Hungry becomes a combo-capable aristocrats deck with more sacrifice outlets and death triggers. Nature of the Beast wants more counter doublers and mana creatures. Evasive Maneuvers is the most different: Derevi decks often use cheap fliers and tap effects to lock opponents' lands.

The tempting offer cards and curses also make the decks political. Offering the table extra lands from **Tempt with Discovery** or extra cards in a curse deck is a real decision for each player, and new players learn quickly that the person sharing resources is often the person winning. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Commander products aren't legal in Standard or Modern, but Commander 2013 had a real effect on Legacy. **True-Name Nemesis** has protection from one chosen player, so in a two-player game the opponent can't block it, target it or damage it with their own sources. It was quickly adopted by blue tempo and Stoneblade-style decks and was a common Legacy creature for years. **Toxic Deluge** became the format's go-to sweeper for black decks and still shows up in Legacy and Vintage.

No card from Commander 2013 is banned in Legacy, Vintage or Commander as of October 2026, and none of its new cards are on the Commander Game Changers list.

In Commander, the set's influence is wider than its card pool. **Nekusar, the Mindrazer**, **Derevi, Empyrial Tactician** and **Roon of the Hidden Realm** became some of the most played commanders in their color identities. **Darksteel Mutation** is a go-to removal spell because it neutralizes a commander without sending it back to the command zone. **Bane of Progress** and **Toxic Deluge** remain in thousands of decks, and command-zone abilities became a standard tool for later designs.

## Cards from Commander 2013 worth owning

- **Toxic Deluge**: one of the best sweepers in Commander and Legacy.
- **True-Name Nemesis**: a hard-to-answer Legacy threat and a good blocker in multiplayer.
- **Darksteel Mutation**: cheap, permanent answer to opposing commanders.
- **Bane of Progress**: a green sweeper for artifacts and enchantments.
- **Ophiomancer**: a free sacrifice fodder engine for aristocrats decks.
- **Primal Vigor**: a token and counter doubler, best when you build around it.

## Takeaway

Commander 2013 is worth picking up if you want a classic, slow precon to upgrade, or if you need its two eternal staples. Pick the commander you like, cut the weakest taplands and clunky reprints for cheaper ramp and interaction, and the deck gets much better fast. New to the format?
