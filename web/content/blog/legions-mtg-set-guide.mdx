Legions is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2003 as the second set of the Onslaught block, set on the continent of Otaria on Dominaria. Its famous hook is that every one of its 145 cards is a creature: there are no instants, sorceries, enchantments, artifacts or lands. It continues the war between two legendary creatures, the angel Akroma and the death-touched Phage, and it pushed Onslaught's creature-type theme even further with Slivers, Goblins, Elves and Zombies.

## The story

The Onslaught block takes place on Otaria about a century after the Phyrexian Invasion, and its story is told in a trilogy of novels by J. Robert King: Onslaught, Legions and Scourge. The block's driving force is the **Mirari**, a mysterious artifact that warps everything around it, and the people it ruins.

The first novel set up two central figures. **Phage** was once Jeska, the sister of the barbarian hero Kamahl, until the Cabal, Otaria's crime syndicate and death cult, remade her into a living weapon whose touch rots flesh. **Akroma** is an angel created by Ixidor, an illusionist who discovered he could make his imaginings real after Phage killed his beloved Nivea. Ixidor built Akroma to destroy Phage, and Legions is largely the story of that hunt.

The two meet as near-mirror opposites, and the cards show it. **Akroma, Angel of Wrath** is a white 6/6 with a whole paragraph of keywords, and **Phage the Untouchable** is a black 4/4 whose combat damage makes a player lose the game outright. Around them, the Mirari's influence is warping Otaria's creatures, which is why the set is full of "Mutant" and mutated beasts, and Kamahl is trying to undo the damage he helped cause.

Legions doesn't resolve the fight. In Scourge, the third set, Akroma and Phage are fused into a single being, **Karona, False God**, a goddess born from the Mirari's power, and the block's story ends by dealing with the consequences of her creation. Ixidor himself dies before that happens. Legions is the middle chapter: the war between angel and avatar, told almost entirely through creature cards.

## Mechanics

### An all-creature set

Legions' design experiment is that every card is a creature. Removal, card draw and tricks were all printed as creature abilities instead of spells, which is why so many cards here do something when they're turned face up, cycled or enter the battlefield. In Limited, it means your spells come from the other sets in the block.

### Morph (returning)

Morph lets you cast a card face down as a 2/2 creature for three generic mana, then turn it face up at any time by paying its morph cost. Many morph creatures have a "when turned face up" effect. **Skinthinner** destroys a nonblack creature when it flips, **Echo Tracer** bounces a creature and **Willbender** changes the target of a spell or ability. Morph creates a bluffing game: every face-down 2/2 might be anything.

### Amplify

As a creature with amplify enters, you may reveal cards that share a creature type with it from your hand. It gets that many +1/+1 counters (times the amplify number). **Embalmed Brawler** is a three-mana Zombie that grows for each Zombie you reveal. It rewards decks built tightly around one creature type.

### Provoke

When a creature with provoke attacks, you may have target creature the defending player controls untap and block it if able. It's a way to force bad blocks or pull a key blocker out of the way. **Deftblade Elite** is a one-mana Soldier with provoke that can also prevent its own combat damage, and **Krosan Vorine** combines provoke with only being blockable by one creature.

### Cycling and the Gempalm cycle

Cycling returns from Onslaught. The Gempalm creatures, like **Gempalm Incinerator**, can be cycled for a card and a bonus that scales with how many of their creature type are on the battlefield. Gempalm Incinerator deals damage equal to the number of Goblins in play to a creature.

### Slivers

Slivers return to Dominaria in force. Every Sliver shares its ability with every other Sliver on the battlefield, yours or your opponent's. **Synapse Sliver** lets each Sliver draw a card when it connects, **Crypt Sliver** gives them all regeneration and **Brood Sliver** makes more Slivers. For a refresher on reading keyword-heavy creatures like these, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Legions was drafted together with Onslaught, one pack of Legions alongside two of Onslaught. Because Legions has only creatures, its pack mostly supplied bodies, morphs and creature-type support, while Onslaught supplied removal spells and tricks. The block format was built around creature types, so drafters tried to collect a tribe: Goblins in red, Elves in green, Zombies in black, Clerics and Soldiers in white, Wizards in blue, plus Beasts and Birds.

Morph made combat a guessing game, and cheap "when turned face up" creatures like Skinthinner and Echo Tracer were among the most valuable commons, since they're removal or tempo hidden in a 2/2. The format is generally remembered as slower and grindier than many modern formats, because face-down creatures traded and cycling kept hands full. If you'd like the modern framework for reading a Limited format, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Legions entered a Standard dominated by Onslaught and the Odyssey block, and its creatures fed the era's tribal decks. The most important was **Goblins**. Onslaught's Goblin Piledriver and Scourge's Goblin Warchief and Siege-Gang Commander anchored the deck, and Legions added Gempalm Incinerator as removal that grew with the tribe. Wolfgang Eder's "Goblin Bidding" deck from the 2003 World Championship, which used Patriarch's Bidding to return a whole army, played Gempalm Incinerator. Elves decks also gained Timberwatch Elf and Wirewood Channeler. Legions and the rest of the Onslaught block left Standard in fall 2004 when Champions of Kamigawa arrived. No Legions card was ever banned in Standard.

Its lasting impact:

- **Legacy**: Gempalm Incinerator has been a long-time piece of Legacy Goblins, and Wirewood Channeler and Timberwatch Elf of Elves. Akroma, Angel of Wrath was one of the classic targets for Reanimator decks in Legacy's early years, before larger threats replaced her. Withered Wretch is a creature-based answer to graveyard decks. See our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) for how the format works.
- **Commander**: Seedborn Muse is one of the format's best-known "untap" cards, Slivers are a perennial Commander tribe, and Windborn Muse, Caller of the Claw and Imperial Hellkite all have fans. Phage is a famous joke: as a commander it can't work, because casting it from the command zone isn't casting it from your hand, so you'd lose the game. Read [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) if you're new.
- **Pauper**: Legions commons such as Timberwatch Elf, Skinthinner and Crypt Sliver are Pauper-legal.

Legions isn't legal in Modern because Modern starts with Eighth Edition, released in July 2003.

## Cards from Legions worth owning

- **Seedborn Muse**: a Commander classic for untapping everything on every turn.
- **Akroma, Angel of Wrath**: the most iconic angel of its era and still a fun Reanimator or Commander target.
- **Gempalm Incinerator**: a Legacy Goblins staple.
- **Wirewood Channeler** and **Timberwatch Elf**: Elves engine pieces.
- **Synapse Sliver** and **Brood Sliver**: strong cards for any Sliver Commander deck.
- **Willbender**: a surprising morph trick for any blue deck.

## The takeaway

Legions is a design curiosity, an all-creature set, but its best cards are still very real. If you're building today, it's a natural fit for a tribal deck: Goblins or Elves in Legacy, or Slivers in Commander.
