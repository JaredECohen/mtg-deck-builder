Onslaught is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2002, the first and only large set of the Onslaught block. Set on the continent of Otaria on Dominaria, it built an entire set around creature types, introduced morph, and brought cycling back with new twists. It also printed the original allied-color fetchlands, Exalted Angel and Goblin Piledriver, which is why it still comes up in Legacy and Commander more than two decades later.

## The story

Onslaught picks up roughly a century after the Phyrexian invasion of Invasion block, in a Dominaria still recovering. It continues the Odyssey cycle's characters, and the main source is J. Robert King's novel *Onslaught*.

Kamahl, once a barbarian pit fighter, has become a druid of the Krosan Forest. He brought the Mirari, a powerful and corrupting artifact, with him, and its energy starts to warp the forest. Krosa's creatures mutate and grow to enormous size, and the forest itself begins to spread. That's the flavor behind the set's huge green Beasts and morph creatures that seem to change shape.

Kamahl's sister Jeska suffers the worst fate. Wounded and then "healed" by the Cabal, a criminal-religious organization, she is remade as Phage, a pit fighter whose touch kills. Meanwhile an illusionist named Ixidor discovers that his imagination can become real, a power called reality sculpting. Ixidor and his partner Nivea had made their living betting on themselves in the Cabal's pit fights until Phage killed Nivea. Exiled to the desert, Ixidor uses his new power to build a dream-shaped realm and to create the angel Akroma, who leads his army against the Cabal to avenge Nivea.

The conflict runs through the rest of the block. Akroma and Phage meet in Legions, and at the climax of the block, the simultaneous deaths of Phage, Akroma and the sorceress Zagorka fuse them into Karona, False God, a being Kamahl must then confront in Scourge. Onslaught itself is the opening act: the forest is growing, the Cabal is ascendant, and Ixidor's creation is just stepping into the world.

## Mechanics

### Tribal

Onslaught is the first set built from the ground up around creature types. The main tribes were Beasts, Birds, Clerics, Elves, Goblins, Soldiers, Wizards and Zombies, and many cards care about creatures that share a type. A few examples:

- **Lords** like Voice of the Woods and Catapult Master tap several creatures of their type for a big effect.
- **Mistform creatures** like Mistform Dreamer can become any creature type for `{1}`, letting them slot into whichever tribe you need.
- **Payoffs** like Goblin Piledriver (gets +2/+0 for each other attacking Goblin), Aven Brigadier (pumps Birds and Soldiers) and Patriarch's Bidding (returns every creature of a chosen type from the graveyard) rewarded committing to a tribe.

Tribes also bled into off-colors, so there were black Goblins and green Soldiers, which made tribal decks more flexible.

### Morph

Morph lets you cast a creature face down as a nameless 2/2 for `{3}`. Later, at any time you could cast an instant, you can pay its morph cost to flip it face up. The mechanic was inspired by the Rules Team's fix for old Alpha cards like Illusionary Mask, which had never defined what a face-down card was.

Morph turned every three-mana 2/2 into a guessing game. Exalted Angel could be played as a morph on turn three and flipped for `{2}{W}{W}` on turn four into a 4/5 flier that gains you life whenever it deals damage, which is a huge swing. Blistering Firecat flips for `{R}{R}` into a 7/1 trampler with haste. Others, like Daru Healer or Skirk Commando, have smaller payoffs, and part of the skill was bluffing that your morph was something scary.

### Cycling

Cycling, which first appeared in Urza's Saga, returned. You pay a card's cycling cost and discard it to draw a card. Onslaught pushed it further with cards that trigger when cycled (Krosan Tusker fetches a basic land when cycled), a cycle of common lands that enter tapped but can be cycled, and permanents that reward any player cycling. Astral Slide flickers a creature whenever a player cycles, and Lightning Rift deals 2 damage for `{1}`. Akroma's Vengeance and Starstorm were board wipes that could cycle when you didn't need them.

### Gustcloak

Gustcloak creatures like Gustcloak Runner can untap and leave combat when they become blocked. It's a non-keyworded ability that made white Soldier and Bird attackers hard to block profitably.

## Limited

Onslaught is remembered as one of the great draft formats, and triple-Onslaught was drafted at the Pro Tour level. Morph defined it. Since nearly every deck could play a 2/2 on turn three, the game became about tempo and information: which face-down creature to block, when to flip, and whether your opponent's untapped mana meant a trick or a bluff. Strong morph commons included Daru Lancer, Skirk Commando, Haunted Cadaver and Gravel Slinger.

The tribal theme raised a classic question: draft by color or by tribe? Most good drafters still drafted colors first and let tribal bonuses come naturally, since a deck forced into one creature type often ended up with weaker cards. Removal was precious: Pinpoint Avalanche and Solar Blast in red, Cruel Revival in black, Pacifism in white, and Choking Tethers as a blue tempo play. Sparksmith, a Goblin that pings for the number of Goblins on the battlefield, was one of the most-discussed commons in the set.

Cycling smoothed draws, and the cycling lands meant you could run a few extra land slots without flooding. For a general framework on evaluating commons in formats like this, see our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Onslaught entered Standard in November 2002 alongside Seventh Edition and the Odyssey block, and from October 2003 it shared the format with Eighth Edition and Mirrodin block until it rotated in October 2004.

- **Astral Slide** decks, often red-white, cycled cards to flicker their own creatures for value and their opponent's attackers out of combat, while Lightning Rift turned every cycle into damage.
- **Goblin Bidding** combined Goblins like Goblin Piledriver and Goblin Sharpshooter with Patriarch's Bidding to bring an entire army back at once.
- **Exalted Angel** was one of the best threats in the format, and a turn-three morph that flipped on turn four was hard for many decks to beat.
- **Beasts** decks used Ravenous Baloth, and red decks used Rorix Bladewing as a hasty finisher.

No Onslaught card was banned in Standard. The big ban of its era, Skullclamp in June 2004, came from Darksteel.

The set's lasting influence is in eternal formats. The five fetchlands (Polluted Delta, Flooded Strand, Bloodstained Mire, Windswept Heath and Wooded Foothills) became staples of Legacy and Vintage and, after later reprints, of Modern. They're banned in Pioneer. Legacy Goblins used Goblin Piledriver and Skirk Prospector for years, and Pauper Elves borrowed Onslaught Elves like Wellwisher and Birchlore Rangers. Pauper also uses the cycling lands and Krosan Tusker. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) has more on how fetchlands fit eternal mana bases.

In Commander, Trade Secrets has been banned since April 2013 because it can go infinite between two cooperating players. Patriarch's Bidding, Goblin Sharpshooter, Akroma's Vengeance and Kamahl, Fist of Krosa are all popular. Onslaught has no Reserved List cards, so many of its key cards have been reprinted.

## Cards from Onslaught worth owning

- **The fetchlands**: Polluted Delta and its four allies are part of almost every Legacy and Modern mana base.
- **Goblin Piledriver**: the classic payoff for Goblin decks in Legacy and casual play.
- **Exalted Angel**: a historical powerhouse and a fun Commander card.
- **Patriarch's Bidding**: a tribal Commander deck's best mass reanimation spell.
- **Astral Slide**: the core of a beloved cycling archetype.
- **Skirk Prospector**: cheap mana for Goblin decks.

## The takeaway

Onslaught gave Magic two ideas it keeps coming back to, morph and creature types that matter, and it printed the fetchlands that still define eternal mana bases. To play its best cards today, Legacy is the natural home: a Goblins deck with Goblin Piledriver and Skirk Prospector, or any blue deck built on fetchlands.
