Streets of New Capenna is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in April 2022, set in an Art Deco city built by angels and run by five demon-led crime families. It's a gangster story with three-color factions, and it gave each family its own mechanic. The set left Standard in 2024, but its tri-lands, Ledger Shredder and a shelf of Commander favorites keep it in play.

## The story

Long ago, Phyrexians invaded Capenna. The plane's archangels couldn't win alone, so they made a deal with their old enemies, the archdemons, and together they built a fortress city that became New Capenna. After the war, the demons betrayed the angels. They trapped them and slowly turned their bodies into **Halo**, a glowing substance that's part drug, part magic and part currency. The archdemons then handed the city to five families, each led by a demon-blooded boss:

- **The Brokers** (green-white-blue) are lawyers and contract-makers who protect their clients.
- **The Obscura** (white-blue-black) deal in secrets, information and Halo trafficking.
- **The Maestros** (blue-black-red) are vampire art collectors and assassins, led by Lord Xander.
- **The Riveteers** (black-red-green) are builders and brawlers, led by the dragon-demon Ziatora.
- **The Cabaretti** (red-green-white) run the nightclubs and the parties, led by Jetmir.

Elspeth Tirel comes to the city looking for answers. She has learned that Capenna is her birth plane and that it somehow beat back a Phyrexian invasion, and she wants to know how. She takes odd jobs, gets recruited by the Maestros, and is sent to infiltrate the Cabaretti, who seem to have an endless supply of Halo while the other families run short.

Their secret is **Giada**, a teenage girl who can turn even sewage into pure Halo. She's an angel, possibly the last free one in the city. Meanwhile, a figure known only as the Adversary has been quietly recruiting members from all five families. The Adversary is the demon planeswalker Ob Nixilis. On the night of the Crescendo, the Cabaretti's new year party, his agents strike at every family's leadership at once. Ob Nixilis kills Lord Xander himself, and Jetmir is badly wounded.

Elspeth escapes the party with Giada, helped by the planeswalker Vivien Reid. Ob Nixilis catches them at a cathedral in Park Heights and nearly kills Elspeth. Giada gives herself up to save her. She dissolves into Halo, restores Elspeth, and leaves her the sword **Luxior**. Elspeth wounds Ob Nixilis badly and he flees the plane. Giada's sacrifice also starts waking the city's other trapped angels. Beneath the streets, the Phyrexian praetor Urabrask has been pursuing plans of his own, a thread that leads into the Phyrexian war of the following sets.

## Mechanics

Each family has its own mechanic, and the set leans on three-color cards for all five.

### Shield counters (Brokers)

A shield counter protects a permanent once. If the permanent would be destroyed or dealt damage, you remove the counter instead. **Boon of Safety** (common, `{W}`) puts one on a creature at instant speed, and **Disciplined Duelist** enters with one already on it. Shields make removal awkward for your opponent, since they have to spend two spells on one creature.

### Connive (Obscura)

When a creature connives, you draw a card, then discard a card. If you discard a nonland card, the creature gets a +1/+1 counter. **Raffine's Informant** connives when it enters. **Ledger Shredder** connives whenever any player casts their second spell in a turn, which is what made it a Constructed star.

### Casualty (Maestros)

Casualty N is an optional extra cost: as you cast the spell, you may sacrifice a creature with power N or greater, and if you do, you copy the spell. **Make Disappear** (common) is a two-mana counterspell that taxes `{2}`, and with casualty 1 you get a second copy. **Ob Nixilis, the Adversary** has casualty X, so sacrificing a big creature hands you a second, non-legendary Ob Nixilis.

### Blitz (Riveteers)

Blitz is an alternative cost. The creature gets haste and "when this dies, draw a card," and you sacrifice it at the next end step. It turns creatures into one-shot attacks that replace themselves. **Tenacious Underdog** can even be cast with blitz from your graveyard, over and over.

### Alliance (Cabaretti)

Alliance is an ability word for triggers that fire whenever another creature enters under your control. **Gala Greeters** grows, makes a Treasure or gains life each time, and token makers turn alliance on several times a turn.

### Hideaway (returning)

Hideaway N looks at the top N cards, exiles one face down, and lets you play it for free once a condition is met. The set's rare hideaway enchantments, like **Rabble Rousing** and **Fight Rigging**, all use hideaway 5. To brush up on how these frames read, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

The set's headline archetypes are the five families, each a three-color deck:

- **Brokers (green-white-blue): shield counters.** Protected creatures and value from counters.
- **Obscura (white-blue-black): connive.** Card filtering, fliers and slow control.
- **Maestros (blue-black-red): casualty.** Sacrifice small bodies to copy spells, plus graveyard value.
- **Riveteers (black-red-green): blitz.** Hasty attacks and sacrifice synergies.
- **Cabaretti (red-green-white): alliance.** Go wide with Citizens and tokens, then trigger alliance repeatedly.

Under them sit five two-color allied pairs: white-blue counters, blue-black "five mana values in your graveyard," black-red sacrifice, red-green Treasure and green-white Citizens. Each family's common fetch land (such as **Obscura Storefront**) and the dual lands help with mana.

In practice, the format didn't play the way the families promised. Two-color decks were more reliable than three-color piles, and white was the strongest color by a wide margin, in large part because of **Inspiring Overseer**. Wizards responded with an unusual experiment on MTG Arena, rebalancing 43 cards for a later "Planeshifted" draft run to support multicolor decks and weaker archetypes. If you're drafting it in paper or a cube, take the strong white commons, removal like **Strangle**, **Murder** and **Kill Shot**, and fixing early, and don't stretch into a third color without the lands to support it. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

In its first months, the set slotted into a Standard full of Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty and Innistrad cards. **Raffine, Scheming Seer** anchored Esper Midrange, a deck that paired efficient threats with good answers like **Void Rend**. Ob Nixilis, the Adversary was the most-hyped card of preview season but saw little play by the set's Pro Tour, a reminder that a planeswalker's ceiling doesn't always match its floor. Jetmir's Garden helped Naya decks with their mana, and the hideaway enchantments and Arcane Bombardment found occasional homes.

The set's quietest cards lasted the longest in Standard. The tri-lands stayed relevant for over two years and were a key part of Domain Ramp, which lost them when the set rotated out. Wizards' 2023 move to a three-year rotation gave Streets of New Capenna an extra year, so it left Standard with Innistrad: Midnight Hunt, Crimson Vow and Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty when Bloomburrow arrived in summer 2024. No cards from the set were banned in Standard.

Outside Standard, **Ledger Shredder** became the set's breakout card. It's a natural fit for spell-heavy decks that cast two spells a turn, and it found homes in Izzet Phoenix in Pioneer and Izzet Murktide in Modern. **Unlicensed Hearse** has been a graveyard-hate option in sideboards, and the tri-lands still show up in Pioneer multicolor decks. As of October 2026, none of the set's cards are on the Pioneer, Modern or Legacy ban lists.

Commander is where New Capenna has the widest reach. Jetmir, Nexus of Revels, Ziatora, the Incinerator and Lord Xander, the Collector lead their own decks. Bootleggers' Stash, Arcane Bombardment and Riveteers Ascendancy show up as engines, and each family's three-color identity fits [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) naturally.

## Cards from Streets of New Capenna worth owning

- **Ledger Shredder**: a two-mana threat that keeps a home in Pioneer and Modern spell decks.
- **The tri-land cycle** (Jetmir's Garden, Raffine's Tower, Spara's Headquarters, Xander's Lounge, Ziatora's Proving Ground): three-color lands that work with land-type searching and cycle when you don't need them.
- **Unlicensed Hearse**: colorless graveyard hate that becomes a threat.
- **Jetmir, Nexus of Revels**: a go-wide commander that turns tokens into a lethal attack.
- **Bootleggers' Stash**: turns every land into a Treasure maker in Commander.
- **Arcane Bombardment**: a spell-copy engine for instants-and-sorceries Commander decks.
- **Ziatora, the Incinerator**: a Jund commander for sacrifice and Treasure themes.

Streets of New Capenna is a set about choosing a family, but its lasting cards are the ones that work in any deck: flexible lands, a cheap flier for spell decks and graveyard hate.
