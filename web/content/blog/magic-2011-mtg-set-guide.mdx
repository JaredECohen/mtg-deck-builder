Magic 2011, usually shortened to M11, is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2010. It was the second of the "new-style" core sets that mixed fresh designs with classic reprints, and it gave the game the five mythic Titans, a cycle of Leylines and a one-mana cantrip called Preordain. For a set meant to teach new players, it left a remarkably long footprint on Modern, Legacy and Commander.

## What the set was for

Core sets in 2010 had two jobs. They were the product a brand-new player was most likely to buy, so they needed clean, readable cards. They also had to hold their own in Standard next to the Zendikar and Scars of Mirrodin blocks. Magic 2011 leaned harder into new cards than older core sets did: nearly half of its cards were new designs rather than reprints.

There is no story arc in M11. The flavor hook was a three-part webcomic, *The Raven's Eye*, about Liliana Vess, and the set put its five planeswalkers front and center. Ajani Goldmane, Jace Beleren, Liliana Vess, Chandra Nalaar and Garruk Wildspeaker all returned as mythic rares, and each got a common and an uncommon "signature spell" named after them, such as Chandra's Outrage and Ajani's Pridemate. Since planeswalker cards themselves are rare to open, the signature spells were a way to make sure every player met those characters in their packs.

Black kept building its Vampire identity from Zendikar. M11 had more Vampires than Zombies, a first for a core set, with Captivating Vampire as the lord and cards like Child of Night and Bloodthrone Vampire at common.

### The rules change

Magic 2011 shipped with one rules update that still matters. Deathtouch used to be a special case that ignored the normal damage assignment rules. After M11, deathtouch simply means any nonzero amount of damage from that source counts as lethal damage. In practice, a deathtouch creature blocked by several creatures can assign 1 damage to each of them and destroy them all, and a deathtouch trampler only needs to assign 1 damage to each blocker before the rest tramples over. Our [keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers deathtouch and trample in more detail.

## Mechanics

### Scry

Magic 2011 was the first core set to use a non-evergreen keyword. Scry N means you look at the top N cards of your library, then put any of them on the bottom and the rest back on top in any order. It's card selection rather than card advantage, and it smooths draws in a way that shows up across a whole game. Preordain (scry 2, then draw), Augury Owl (a 1/1 flier that scries 3 on entry), Foresee and Crystal Ball all used it. Scry did not return in Magic 2012, but it later became an evergreen keyword.

### The Titans

The set's signature cycle is five mythic 6/6 Giants, each costing `{4}` plus two colored mana. Each one has a strong ability that triggers both when it enters and whenever it attacks, so it generates value right away and keeps doing it.

- **Sun Titan** has vigilance and returns a permanent card with mana value 3 or less from your graveyard to the battlefield.
- **Frost Titan** taps down a permanent so it skips its next untap, and taxes spells that target it.
- **Grave Titan** has deathtouch and makes two 2/2 Zombies each time.
- **Inferno Titan** can pump its power with red mana and deals 3 damage divided among up to three targets.
- **Primeval Titan** has trample and fetches any two lands from your library onto the battlefield.

### Leylines

The Leylines are rare enchantments that cost `{2}` plus two colored mana, but if one is in your opening hand you can start the game with it on the battlefield for free. Leyline of Sanctity gives you hexproof, Leyline of the Void exiles any card that would go to an opponent's graveyard, Leyline of Anticipation lets you cast spells at instant speed, Leyline of Punishment stops lifegain and damage prevention, and Leyline of Vitality pumps toughness and gains life. They are sideboard cards by design: you bring them in when the effect matters on turn zero. Our [sideboarding guide](/blog/sideboarding-101) explains how to plan swaps like that.

### Hosers and checklands

M11 reprinted the white, blue and black "enemy color hosers" from Magic 2010 and added new red and green ones. Combust deals 5 uncounterable, unpreventable damage to a white or blue creature, and Autumn's Veil protects green spells and creatures from blue and black. Plummet, a two-mana answer to any flier, was clearly aimed at Baneslayer Angel. The set also carried the five allied-color checklands, such as Glacial Fortress, which enter untapped if you control a matching basic land type.

## Limited

Magic 2011 drafted like a traditional core set. Raw card quality and picking an open color pair mattered more than intricate synergies, and most two-color combinations could work. Removal was the backbone: Lightning Bolt, Fireball and Chandra's Outrage in red, Doom Blade and Corrupt in black, Pacifism in white and Ice Cage in blue. Blue had strong tempo tools like Aether Adept and Unsummon, and Sleep at uncommon could end a game in one swing.

Green was deep in solid bodies. A typical green curve ran Llanowar Elves into Sacred Wolf into Cudgel Troll, with Acidic Slime as a strong late play, and green's splashable creatures paired well with black's removal-heavy, Swamp-heavy decks. White had cheap fliers and Squadron Hawk, and the Vampire commons gave black a real aggressive plan.

The format was a good one for learning fundamentals. If you want a framework for ranking removal against creatures and tricks, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) walks through it.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Magic 2011 entered Standard in July 2010 alongside Shards of Alara block, Magic 2010 and the Zendikar block. In its first months the format had Jund, planeswalker control decks and a ramp deck built around Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle, which picked up Primeval Titan as a way to fetch Valakut and Mountains at once. When Scars of Mirrodin arrived in October 2010 and Shards rotated, M11 became one of only five sets in the format, and its cards carried more weight.

- **Valakut ramp** used Primeval Titan and green ramp spells like Cultivate to assemble the lands quickly.
- **UB and UW control** finished games with Grave Titan, Frost Titan and Sun Titan, and played Preordain and Mana Leak.
- **Caw-Blade**, the dominant deck of early 2011, used Squadron Hawk as a cheap flier that carried Equipment and refilled a hand with more Hawks. Two of its cards from other sets, Jace, the Mind Sculptor and Stoneforge Mystic, were banned in Standard in June 2011. No Magic 2011 card was banned in Standard.
- **Fauna Shaman** decks used it as a repeatable tutor for situational creatures.

M11 rotated out of Standard when Innistrad released at the end of September 2011.

Its long-term legacy is in older formats. Preordain was on Modern's initial ban list in 2011 and stayed there until it was unbanned in August 2023, and it has been a Legacy and Pauper staple throughout. Primeval Titan became the payoff of Modern's Amulet Titan deck, which has existed in some form for most of the format's life. Leyline of Sanctity is a common sideboard card against discard and burn, Steel Overseer has appeared in Modern artifact decks and Vintage artifact aggro, and Viscera Seer and Pyretic Ritual show up in combo decks. Our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) covers where these fit.

In Commander, Primeval Titan has been banned since September 2012 because it made ramp decks too consistent. The other Titans, Serra Ascendant, Ajani's Pridemate and Cultivate are all familiar Commander cards. As of October 2026, Primeval Titan's Commander ban is the only ban on a Magic 2011 card in the major formats.

## Cards from Magic 2011 worth owning

- **Preordain**: the cleanest one-mana cantrip in blue, legal in Modern, Legacy and Pauper.
- **Primeval Titan**: the engine of Modern's Amulet Titan and other land-based decks.
- **Leyline of Sanctity**: a free answer to discard and burn when it starts on the battlefield.
- **Grave Titan**: a two-for-one on entry that Commander players still love.
- **Steel Overseer**: an artifact aggro payoff for Modern and Vintage.
- **Viscera Seer**: a free sacrifice outlet for combo and aristocrats decks.
- **Squadron Hawk**: a Pauper staple that gives white decks card advantage.

## The takeaway

Magic 2011 looks like a gentle core set, but the Titans, the Leylines and Preordain give it more lasting Constructed impact than most expansions. If you're building with it today, Modern is the natural home: a ramp deck around Primeval Titan, or a blue tempo deck with Preordain and Leyline of Sanctity in the sideboard.
