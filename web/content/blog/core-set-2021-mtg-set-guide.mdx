Core Set 2021 is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2020, and the last of the yearly core sets before Wizards paused the line. It is built around Teferi, the time-bending planeswalker from Dominaria, and introduced Basri Ket as a new planeswalker. The headline was a mix of beginner-friendly reprints and some very strong new cards: Teferi, Master of Time, Elder Gargaroth, Terror of the Peaks and Ugin, the Spirit Dragon all came out of the same packs.

## What the set was for

Core sets have a specific job: they are the products Wizards points new players toward, so they lean on simple cards, iconic creatures and reprints of staples. Core Set 2021 did that, but it also carried a lot of new cards meant for Constructed players. It has no web story and no Story Spotlight cards, so the "plot" lives in the card frames and flavor.

### Teferi and friends

Teferi was the face of the set. His main card, Teferi, Master of Time, can use his loyalty abilities at instant speed on any player's turn, so he can act on your turn and again on your opponent's. A showcase treatment gave each mono-colored planeswalker three themed "signature spells" in matching frames, a nod to the Signature Spellbook products. Teferi's set included Teferi's Ageless Insight and Teferi's Tutelage, plus cards for characters from his past such as Barrin, Tolarian Archmage.

Basri Ket, a white planeswalker from Amonkhet, debuted here. Garruk, Liliana and Chandra rounded out the mono-colored walkers, and Ugin, the Spirit Dragon was reprinted as the colorless mythic.

### The flavor themes

Two light themes ran through the set. The first was Dogs vs. Cats: the set added the Dog creature type, folded old Hounds into it, and printed cards like Pack Leader and Feline Sovereign that pump one tribe and fight the other. Rin and Seri, Inseparable is the legendary pair that rewards you for playing both. The second was Shrines, the enchantment subtype from the original Kamigawa block. Each Shrine grows stronger with the number of Shrines you control, and the five-color Sanctum of All ties them together.

Core Set 2021 also tied directly into Jumpstart, a product released two weeks later that shared 120 cards with the set.

## Mechanics

Core sets avoid complicated new keywords, but Core Set 2021 made a few rules changes and brought back a couple of old favorites.

### Mill becomes a keyword action

Before this set, cards spelled out "put the top cards of your library into your graveyard." Starting with Core Set 2021, the shorthand "mill" became an official keyword action. Teferi's Tutelage makes an opponent mill two cards every time you draw, which turned it into the core of a casual mill deck.

### Phasing

Phasing returned to a Standard-legal set for the first time since the 1990s. A permanent that phases out is treated as though it doesn't exist until its controller's next untap step, and then it phases back in. Teferi, Master of Time uses this as removal that doesn't stick: his minus ability phases out a creature you don't control, which is often enough to swing a race.

### Prowess

Prowess gives a creature +1/+1 until end of turn whenever you cast a noncreature spell. Heartfire Immolator is the set's best example: a two-mana prowess creature that can sacrifice itself to deal damage equal to its power to a creature or planeswalker.

### Shrines

The Shrines aren't a keyword, but they play like one. Sanctum of Stone Fangs, for example, drains each opponent for the number of Shrines you control at the start of your first main phase each turn. One Shrine is modest; three or four together start winning games.

## Limited

The set's draft format was built on ten two-color archetypes. As with most core sets, the themes were straightforward.

| Pair | Theme |
| --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Flyers |
| Blue-Black | Reanimation and graveyard value |
| Black-Red | Sacrifice |
| Red-Green | Big creatures (power 4 or greater) |
| Green-White | +1/+1 counters |
| White-Black | Lifegain |
| Blue-Red | Prowess and spells |
| Black-Green | Creatures dying |
| Red-White | Going wide, with Dogs |
| Green-Blue | Drawing extra cards |

The format rewarded the basics. Cheap removal at common, like Grasp of Darkness, Scorching Dragonfire and Frost Breath for tempo, was worth taking early. White-Blue fliers got Watcher of the Spheres and Lofty Denial, a counterspell that is much stronger if you control a flier. Gold uncommons like Indulging Patrician (White-Black lifegain) and Conclave Mentor (Green-White counters) were reliable signals that a lane was open. Bombs were splashy, though: Elder Gargaroth and Terror of the Peaks could win a game by themselves, and Limited players learned to keep a removal spell for them.

Because the archetypes are simple, Core Set 2021 is a good format to learn on. If you're new to drafting, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) covers how to rank removal, bombs and filler.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Core Set 2021's Standard season ran from July 2020 to September 2021, a period dominated by bans of cards from other sets: Growth Spiral, Wilderness Reclamation and Teferi, Time Raveler in August 2020, then Omnath, Locus of Creation, Lucky Clover and Escape to the Wilds in October 2020. No Core Set 2021 card was banned in Standard.

The set's cards fed several decks. Elder Gargaroth became one of the most feared threats in Standard, since a 6/6 that gains value every time it attacks or blocks is hard for aggro decks to answer; it showed up in green ramp and midrange lists. Thieves' Guild Enforcer joined the Zendikar Rising Rogues to form Dimir Rogues, a deck that milled opponents while beating down with small flash creatures. Heartfire Immolator was a staple of red aggro, Kitesail Freebooter and Eliminate supported black decks, and Teferi, Master of Time saw play in blue control. Ugin, the Spirit Dragon gave ramp decks a colorless finisher option, and Fabled Passage smoothed out mana across the format.

In Pioneer, the set's lasting cards are the efficient ones: Fabled Passage in many mana bases, Kitesail Freebooter and Eliminate in black decks, and Heartfire Immolator in red. In Modern and Legacy, the set's main contributions are reprints rather than new cards, such as Scavenging Ooze and Containment Priest.

Commander is where Core Set 2021 has had the biggest long-term footprint. Terror of the Peaks became a go-to finisher for creature-heavy decks, Fiery Emancipation is a favorite in damage-tripling red decks, and Grim Tutor gave black players another efficient tutor. As of October 2026, none of the set's cards are banned in Pioneer, Modern, Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Core Set 2021 worth owning

- **Fabled Passage**: a simple, flexible land that fits into nearly any mana base.
- **Terror of the Peaks**: a Commander finisher that turns every creature into a burn spell.
- **Elder Gargaroth**: a huge, hard-to-handle threat for green Commander and casual decks.
- **Eliminate**: efficient, flexible black removal for Pioneer.
- **Grim Tutor** and **Fiery Emancipation**: two Commander staples.
- **Heroic Intervention** and **Cultivate**: Commander reprints that appear in a huge share of green decks.

## The takeaway

Core Set 2021 looks like a beginner set on the surface, and it is a good place to learn the game, but it also delivered real Constructed cards. If you're building a Pioneer deck with it, think in terms of support pieces: Eliminate and Kitesail Freebooter for black, Heartfire Immolator for red, and Fabled Passage for your mana.
