Journey into Nyx is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in May 2014, the third and final set of the original Theros block. It's set on Theros, a plane inspired by Greek myth, and it finishes the story of the satyr who made himself a god and the champion sent to stop him. The headline is an "enchantments matter" set with two new mechanics, constellation and strive, five new Gods, and a few cards, like Eidolon of the Great Revel and Mana Confluence, that are still played over a decade later.

Because the Theros block was Standard-legal in 2013 and 2014, Journey into Nyx is one of the oldest sets in Pioneer, which begins with Return to Ravnica.

## The story

### A plane in chaos

Theros is a world where gods are real, and their power depends on mortal belief. Their home is Nyx, the starry night sky, and their enchantment-creatures and champions walk among mortals. At the end of the previous set, Born of the Gods, the satyr planeswalker Xenagos pulled off something unheard of: he ascended to godhood, becoming a god of revels and madness.

Xenagos's ascension threw the pantheon into turmoil. Journey into Nyx's tagline, "Rival the Gods," captures what follows: the gods, unsettled and suspicious, grow harsh toward mortals, and mortals in turn push back against their gods. Strange new constellations appear in the night sky, and monsters roam the land. Five lesser-known gods also step forward in this set: Athreos, who ferries the dead; Keranos, god of storms; Pharika, god of affliction and medicine; Kruphix, the enigmatic god of horizons; and Iroas, god of victory.

### Elspeth's journey

The hero of the block is Elspeth Tirel, a planeswalker who came to Theros seeking peace and became the champion of Heliod, the sun god. With the help of her friend Ajani Goldmane, Elspeth travels into Nyx itself to confront Xenagos. Armed with Godsend, the divine weapon Heliod gave his champion, she kills the new god and ends the threat he poses to the plane.

Her victory is also her undoing. Heliod, frightened that a mortal could slay a god, turns Godsend on his own champion and kills her. It's one of the darkest endings in Magic's story at the time. Elspeth's fate wasn't the end of her, though: her story continues when the plane returns in Theros Beyond Death.

## Mechanics

### Constellation

Constellation is an ability word on enchantments: it triggers whenever that enchantment or another enchantment enters under your control. Eidolon of Blossoms, a four-mana 2/2 enchantment creature, draws you a card each time. Doomwake Giant gives the opponent's creatures -1/-1 until end of turn on each trigger. Constellation turns every enchantment you cast into a small bonus, and it rewards the bestow creatures and enchantment removal already in the block.

### Strive

Strive is an ability word on spells that can target any number of creatures, with an extra cost for each target beyond the first. Ajani's Presence costs `{W}` to make one creature indestructible and +1/+1, plus `{2}{W}` for each extra target. Strive makes combat tricks scale: cheap when you need one target, a one-sided blowout when you can afford several.

### Returning mechanics

Journey into Nyx brought back the block's earlier mechanics:

- **Heroic** triggers whenever you cast a spell that targets the creature. Dawnbringer Charioteers, a flying, lifelink 2/4, grows with each heroic trigger.
- **Bestow** lets you cast an enchantment creature as an Aura for its bestow cost; when the creature it enchants leaves, it becomes a creature again. Gnarled Scarhide can be a 2/1 for `{B}` or give +2/+1 for `{3}{B}`.
- **Inspired** triggers when the creature untaps. King Macar, the Gold-Cursed exiles a creature and makes a Gold token each time.
- **Monstrosity** is an activated ability that puts counters on a creature once. Hydra Broodmaster also creates Hydra tokens when it becomes monstrous.
- **Devotion** counts the colored mana symbols among your permanents and powers the Gods, who aren't creatures until your devotion to their colors is at least seven.

### The minor Gods

Each two-color God is indestructible and has an ability that works even when it isn't a creature. Kruphix, God of Horizons gives you no maximum hand size and turns unspent mana into colorless mana instead of losing it. Athreos, God of Passage returns your dying creatures to your hand unless an opponent pays 3 life.

## Limited

Journey into Nyx was drafted with one pack each of Journey into Nyx, Born of the Gods and Theros, opening the newest set first. That meant the block's archetypes carried over, with Journey adding fuel:

- **Heroic** decks, especially white-based ones, used strive tricks like Ajani's Presence and Aerial Formation to target several creatures at once.
- **Enchantment and constellation** decks, mostly green, black and white, built around enchantment creatures and payoffs like Harvestguard Alseids and Strength from the Fallen.
- **Devotion and big monsters** in green, where monstrosity creatures like Ravenous Leucrocota gave every deck a mana sink.
- **Bestow** creatures across colors, which protect you from removal because a bestowed creature stays behind when the enchanted creature dies.

Removal at common was limited, so cards like Magma Spray, Pin to the Earth, Oppressive Rays, Feast of Dreams and Starfall (which punishes enchantment creatures) went early. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### In Standard (May 2014 to October 2015)

Journey into Nyx joined a Standard ruled by devotion decks like Mono-Black and Mono-Blue Devotion and Jund Monsters. Its first contribution was mana: Mana Confluence made three-color decks much easier to build alongside the Theros temples and Ravnica shock lands, and players were quick to call it the most important card in the set. Banishing Light became a staple answer for white decks throughout the rest of the block's time in Standard.

After Khans of Tarkir arrived in September 2014, Eidolon of Blossoms and Doomwake Giant formed the core of black-green and Abzan constellation decks, often alongside enchantments like Courser of Kruphix from Born of the Gods. Heroic decks in white and blue also benefited from the set's tricks. No Journey into Nyx card was ever banned in Standard. The Theros block rotated out when Battle for Zendikar released on October 2, 2015. For background, our [rotation explainer](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) covers how that works.

### In Modern and Legacy

Eidolon of the Great Revel is the set's most enduring Constructed card. It punishes the cheap spells that both Modern and Legacy are built on, and it has been a fixture of red Burn decks in both formats for years. Mana Confluence also appears as a five-color land in eternal formats.

### In Pioneer

Journey into Nyx is part of Pioneer's earliest legal block. As of October 2026, none of its cards are banned there. Mana Confluence remains one of the most flexible lands in the format, Eidolon of the Great Revel is available to red aggro decks, and Banishing Light is a clean catch-all answer for white enchantment decks.

### In Commander

Kruphix, God of Horizons and Athreos, God of Passage are long-running commanders, Eidolon of Blossoms is a key enchantress engine, Dictate of Erebos is an aristocrats favorite, and Mana Confluence fixes mana in any multicolor deck.

## Cards from Journey into Nyx worth owning

- **Eidolon of the Great Revel**: a Burn staple in Modern and Legacy.
- **Mana Confluence**: an any-color land for Pioneer, Modern and Commander.
- **Eidolon of Blossoms**: card draw for every enchantment deck.
- **Dictate of Erebos**: a flash punisher for sacrifice decks in Commander.
- **Banishing Light**: a flexible answer to any nonland permanent.
- **Kruphix, God of Horizons**: a popular green-blue commander for big-mana decks.

## The takeaway

Journey into Nyx closed the Theros block with one of Magic's most memorable story endings and gave Constructed players two cards, Eidolon of the Great Revel and Mana Confluence, that have outlived nearly everything else in the block. In Pioneer, its constellation engine still offers a fun enchantment-based midrange deck.
