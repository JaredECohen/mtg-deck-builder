Modern is a non-rotating Constructed Magic: The Gathering format whose card pool starts with Eighth Edition and includes every set released after it, plus sets printed directly into Modern such as the Modern Horizons sets. Decks are at least 60 cards with up to a 15-card sideboard and a maximum of four copies of any card except basic lands. Cards don't rotate out, so a Modern deck can stay playable for years, subject to the format's ban list.

The [MTG formats overview](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) compares Modern with every other format. This guide goes deeper: how Modern games actually play, what to expect from the card pool, how to choose and learn your first deck, and how to get in without overspending.

## The rules in brief

| Rule | Modern |
| --- | --- |
| Card pool | Eighth Edition forward, plus sets printed directly into Modern |
| Deck size | 60 cards minimum |
| Sideboard | Up to 15 cards |
| Copies per card | Up to four, except basic lands and cards that say otherwise |
| Rotation | None |
| Ban list | Yes, maintained by Wizards of the Coast |

A few notes:

- **Legality is about the card, not the printing.** If a card has ever been printed in a Modern-legal set, every printing of it is legal, including older ones from before Eighth Edition. Lightning Bolt, for example, is legal in Modern no matter which version you own.
- **The ban list changes.** Wizards periodically bans or unbans cards to keep the format healthy. Always check the official banned and restricted list before you buy cards or build a deck.
- **Some cards break the four-copy rule.** A few cards say a deck can contain any number of them. Those are exceptions written on the card itself.

## What Modern feels like

Modern is a fast, high-powered format with a large and varied card pool. Some things you'll notice right away:

### Games can end quickly

Efficient threats and spells mean the fastest decks can threaten to win in the first few turns. That changes how you evaluate cards. A clunky five-mana spell that's great in a slower format may be too slow here, and cheap interaction is essential.

### Cheap, efficient interaction

The removal and disruption in Modern are some of the most efficient Magic has. Lightning Bolt deals 3 damage for one mana. Path to Exile and Fatal Push each answer a creature for one mana (Fatal Push only hits cheaper creatures). Thoughtseize takes the best nonland card from an opponent's hand for one mana and 2 life. When answers are this cheap, players can develop their own board and interact on the same turn, which makes games interactive even when they're fast.

### Life is a resource

Many Modern mana bases use lands that cost life. Fetch lands like Arid Mesa pay 1 life to search for a land, and shock lands like Steam Vents can enter untapped if you pay 2 life. Add Thoughtseize and other life-costing spells, and it's common to hurt yourself for 5 or more life over a game. Against aggressive decks, that self-damage matters. Learning when to take a land tapped instead of paying life is a real Modern skill.

### A wide range of archetypes

Modern supports aggro, control, midrange, combo and tempo decks, plus graveyard strategies, artifact strategies and plenty of hybrids. You'll face many different decks across a tournament. That variety is one of the format's main attractions, but it also means you can't prepare for everything. The [deck archetypes guide](/blog/mtg-deck-archetypes-explained) explains the broad strategies you'll meet.

### Sideboards matter a lot

Because the format is so diverse, your 15-card sideboard is how you adjust to the specific deck across from you. Graveyard hate, artifact removal, extra counters or discard, and cards against fast aggro all show up in Modern sideboards. If you're new to sideboarding, read [sideboarding 101](/blog/sideboarding-101) before your first event.

## How to choose your first Modern deck

Picking a deck is the biggest decision you'll make, and it's worth taking time over.

**Pick a play style you enjoy.** You'll play the deck a lot. If you love attacking, start with an aggressive deck. If you like answering everything and winning late, try control. Enjoying the deck keeps you practicing, and practice is how you get good.

**Prefer a deck you can play for a long time.** Modern rewards experience with a single deck. Players who stick with one deck for months learn its matchups, its sideboard plans and its tricky interactions. That knowledge is worth more than switching to whatever seems strongest this month.

**Consider the complexity.** Some decks are straightforward to pilot and are good for learning the format. Others involve long, complicated turns and many decisions. A simpler deck lets you focus on learning what opponents are doing.

**Look at the mana base.** Mono-color and two-color decks generally need fewer expensive lands than three-color decks. That affects both cost and consistency.

## How to learn the format

- **Learn the common threats.** Know the key cards from the most-played archetypes so you recognize them from the first land or spell. Recognizing an opponent's deck on turn one or two changes how you play the whole game.
- **Read every unfamiliar card.** Modern has a huge pool. Asking to read an opponent's card is always fine.
- **Goldfish your deck.** Play games alone to learn your deck's speed and sequencing before playing against people.
- **Play online and in person.** Magic Online is the long-established digital home of Modern. Locally, many game stores run Modern nights and tournaments, which are great for meeting players and getting feedback.
- **Track your games.** Write down what you lost to and why. Patterns show up quickly and tell you what to practice or sideboard.

## Getting into Modern on a budget

Modern has a reputation for being expensive, and competitive decks often do cost more than decks in rotating formats. But the format doesn't require you to buy a top deck all at once.

### Start with a cheaper archetype

Some strategies are naturally cheaper than others. Mono-color decks running basic lands avoid the cost of fetch lands and shock lands. Aggressive red decks built around burn spells and cheap creatures are a long-standing budget entry point, since many of their key cards are commons and uncommons. The [budget deck guide](/blog/budget-mtg-decks-under-50) covers general principles for building cheaply.

### Buy the core first

If you're working toward a more expensive deck, buy the cards that define the strategy first and fill in the rest with cheaper substitutes. Upgrade one piece at a time.

### Prioritize cards that cross over

Some cards appear in many different Modern decks, and certain lands are used across many color combinations. Buying those first means your collection stays useful if you change decks later. Because Modern doesn't rotate, these cards also tend to stay relevant for a long time.

### Test before you buy

Before committing money, try decks on Magic Online or with proxies for casual playtesting among friends who are comfortable with it. Playing a deck a few times shows you whether you enjoy it before you buy a single card.

### Buy singles

Buying individual cards is almost always cheaper than opening packs to build a specific deck.

## Common mistakes new Modern players make

1. **Bringing a slow deck to a fast format.** Cards that cost five or more need to be game-winning to justify their place.
2. **Ignoring the sideboard.** A sideboard with random good cards isn't a plan. Build it around the matchups you expect.
3. **Paying too much life.** Fetching and shocking every turn adds up against aggressive decks.
4. **Switching decks constantly.** You learn more by playing one deck many times than by trying ten decks once.
5. **Not checking the ban list.** Always confirm a card is legal before buying it for a Modern deck.

## The takeaway

Modern is a fast, deep, non-rotating format where cheap interaction and a well-planned sideboard matter as much as raw power. Choose a deck you'll enjoy playing for a long time, learn it thoroughly, check the official ban list before you buy, and build toward your target deck one piece at a time.
