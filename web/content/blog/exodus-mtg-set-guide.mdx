Exodus is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1998 as the final set of the Tempest block, the chapter of the Weatherlight Saga set on the artificial plane of Rath. It tells the story of the Weatherlight crew's escape and gave the game some of its most powerful engines, including Survival of the Fittest, Recurring Nightmare, Oath of Druids and City of Traitors, several of which are on the Reserved List.

## The story

Exodus closes the Rath Cycle, the part of the Weatherlight Saga in which the crew of the flying ship *Weatherlight* travels to Rath to rescue their captain. The storyline was told in the novel *Rath and Storm* and on the cards themselves.

The hero is Gerrard Capashen, the reluctant heir to the Legacy, a collection of artifacts that the Weatherlight was built to gather. Rath is ruled by the evincar Volrath, a shapeshifter in service to Phyrexia. Volrath was once Vuel, Gerrard's foster brother, and he blames Gerrard for the humiliation that drove him from their family. He kidnapped Captain Sisay to lure the Weatherlight to Rath. Over Tempest and Stronghold, the crew crash-landed in the Skyshroud Forest, allied with the elf leader Eladamri, and fought their way into Volrath's Stronghold.

When Exodus begins, the crew has rescued Sisay and recovered the Legacy artifacts, and they now have to get out. While Eladamri leads the elves of Skyshroud and the Kor, Vec and Dal tribes in a frontal assault on the Stronghold to draw its defenders away, the Weatherlight slips out the back. Volrath and his brutal lieutenant Greven il-Vec pursue them.

The escape is costly. The nobleman Crovax, who came to Rath looking for his lost angel Selenia, has become a vampire after killing her, and his bloodlust turns on the crew. Mirri, the cat warrior who was one of Gerrard's oldest friends, dies fighting him. Meanwhile the young mage Ertai holds open a portal off the plane, with help from the ghostly Soltari. The Weatherlight escapes through it, but with Volrath's skyship *Predator* close behind, it can't stop, and Ertai is left behind. He is captured by Greven, and the crew's choice to leave him eventually turns him into one of their enemies. Volrath, as it turns out, has stowed away aboard the Weatherlight in disguise, which sets up the next block, Mercadian Masques.

## Mechanics

Exodus introduced no new keywords. Instead it expanded the block's existing themes and added two cycles built around one idea: helping the player who is behind.

### Oaths

The Oaths are enchantments that trigger at the beginning of each player's upkeep. If that player has less of some resource than an opponent, they get a bonus. Oath of Druids is the famous one: if a player controls fewer creatures than an opponent, they reveal cards from the top of their library until they hit a creature and put it onto the battlefield. Built correctly, a deck with almost no creatures can use it to cheat out an enormous one on turn two or three. Others in the cycle include Oath of Ghouls and Oath of Lieges.

### Keepers

The Keepers are two-mana creatures with abilities you can activate only if you're behind. Keeper of the Light gains you 3 life if an opponent has more life than you, and Keeper of the Mind draws you a card if an opponent has at least two more cards in hand.

### Buyback and shadow

Both block mechanics returned. Buyback lets you pay an extra cost to return a spell to your hand as it resolves. Exodus experimented with non-mana buyback costs: Forbid's buyback is discarding two cards, and Slaughter's is paying 4 life. Shadow creatures can block or be blocked only by other shadow creatures, so they are mostly unblockable. Dauthi Jackal and Soltari Champion are Exodus examples.

### Spikes and licids

Spikes are green creatures that enter with +1/+1 counters and can move them to other creatures, like Spike Weaver, which can also remove a counter to prevent all combat damage for a turn. Licids are creatures that can turn themselves into Auras; Dominating Licid becomes a Control Magic. To learn how to read keyword-heavy older cards, see our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Exodus was drafted as part of the Tempest block, alongside packs of Tempest and Stronghold. Draft in 1998 was much less studied than it is today, and Exodus was a small set feeding into a larger block format, so there aren't reliable descriptions of color-pair archetypes from that period.

What we can say from the cards is that shadow creatures like Dauthi Jackal and Dauthi Cutthroat were very strong, since few decks could block them, and that Exodus offered useful removal and card advantage at common and uncommon, including Merfolk Looter, Sonic Burst, Death's Duet and Fade Away. Spikes and buyback spells rewarded long games. If you want a modern way to think about drafting, our guide to [evaluating cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) applies to any set.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Exodus arrived in a Standard format built around Fifth Edition and the Tempest block, and its impact was immediate:

- **Summer of Hate.** Hatred gave Suicide Black, an aggressive black deck that spent life freely, a way to swing for lethal with one unblocked creature, often a shadow creature. The summer of 1998 became known as the "Summer of Hate".
- **Survival and Recurring Nightmare.** Survival of the Fittest and Recurring Nightmare became one of the era's best-known engines: Survival put creatures into the graveyard, and Nightmare brought them back. Wizards banned Recurring Nightmare in Standard in 1999.
- **Bans.** As Urza's Saga added more combo cards, Standard went through the period called Combo Winter. In March 1999, Wizards banned Recurring Nightmare in Standard along with Dream Halls, Earthcraft, Fluctuator, Lotus Petal and Time Spiral. Mind Over Matter followed in June 1999.

The Tempest block rotated out of Standard in late 1999 when Mercadian Masques arrived.

### Eternal formats

Exodus was released before Eighth Edition, so it isn't legal in Modern. As of October 2026, Scryfall's legality data shows:

- **Survival of the Fittest** has been banned in Legacy since December 2010 and was banned in Extended in 2001. It is legal in Vintage and Commander.
- **Oath of Druids** has been banned in Legacy since the format's first official ban list in 2004, but Oath decks remain a Vintage archetype, usually paired with Forbidden Orchard.
- **Recurring Nightmare** is legal in Legacy and Vintage but has been banned in Commander since 2008.
- **City of Traitors** is a fast-mana land for Legacy and Vintage artifact decks.
- **Sphere of Resistance** is a classic piece of Vintage Workshop prison decks.
- **Price of Progress** punishes Legacy decks with nonbasic lands, and is banned in Pauper.
- **Mind Over Matter** was banned in many formats in 1999, then unbanned in Vintage in 2005 and Legacy in 2007.

In Commander, Survival of the Fittest, Coat of Arms and Cataclysm see play, while Recurring Nightmare and Limited Resources are banned. Because nine Exodus cards are on the Reserved List, they will never be reprinted in a normal set. For more on how these formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) and our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Exodus worth owning

- **Survival of the Fittest**: a creature tutor engine for Commander and Vintage.
- **City of Traitors**: fast mana for Legacy and Vintage.
- **Recurring Nightmare**: a reanimation engine for Legacy and cube.
- **Price of Progress**: a red finisher against multicolor Legacy decks.
- **Coat of Arms**: a big payoff for tribal Commander decks.
- **Sphere of Resistance**: a Vintage prison staple.
- **Merfolk Looter**: the original looter, a Pauper and cube favorite.

Exodus is a small set that left a large footprint, with engines strong enough to dominate a World Championship and get banned across formats. In Legacy today, its best fit is a red deck that punishes greedy mana bases with Price of Progress.
