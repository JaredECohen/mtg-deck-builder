Bloomburrow is the Magic: The Gathering storybook set of animalfolk adventurers, released in August 2024. It's set on a brand-new plane with no humans at all, where mice, frogs, bats, otters and six other animal peoples share a sprawling land called Valley. The headline is ten animal "tribes," one per color pair, plus a pile of new mechanics and a few cards strong enough to get banned years later.

Wizards has announced that the release of Nauctis: The Sunken Realm in early 2027 triggers the next Standard rotation, and Bloomburrow is one of the sets leaving. Its cards stay legal in Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander.

## The story

Bloomburrow's animalfolk are the same size as their real-world counterparts, which means a mouse village sits in the roots of a tree and a bird is a terrifying predator. The great threats of Valley are the Calamity Beasts: huge elemental creatures that arrive like natural disasters. Maha, a vast night owl, wrecks the frog village of Pondside at the start of the story, and other Beasts flood rivers and shake the land.

The hero is Mabel, a mousefolk from Goodhill who takes up Cragflame, an heirloom sword tied to a legendary hero who once drove the Calamity Beasts away. Helga, a frogfolk whose visions of the Beasts nobody believes, stumbles into Goodhill, and Mabel decides to do something about it. She gathers a party: Zoraline the bat, Finneas the rabbit archer, Hugs the badger, and Gev the lizard, along with Helga herself. Partway through they meet Ral Zarek, the Ravnican planeswalker, who arrived on Bloomburrow as an otter while following Jace Beleren's trail.

The party follows the trouble to its source. A mercenary named Cruelclaw stole one of Maha's eggs, and the trail leads to King Glarb of Fountainport, who hired him. Glarb had been dreaming of Valley's destruction and wanted a Calamity Beast of his own to control. In the confrontation at Fountainport, the heroes get hold of the egg, Glarb escapes after calling in a storm hawk, and Maha arrives, drives off the hawk, and takes her egg back. With the egg returned, her rampage ends.

Mabel's party goes home to a celebration, but Helga has one more vision warning that a "mage in blue" will bring about an ending. Ral is sure she means Jace and leaves the plane to find him, a thread that runs through Duskmourn and Aetherdrift and into the Tarkir: Dragonstorm story.

## Mechanics

### Offspring

Offspring is an optional extra cost on a creature. Pay it, and when the creature enters, you also create a 1/1 token copy of it. Manifold Mouse, a two-mana Mouse that grants double strike or trample to a Mouse each combat, can pay `{2}` more to bring a 1/1 copy that does the same thing. Offspring makes creatures with strong abilities scale up late in the game.

### Forage

To forage, you either exile three cards from your graveyard or sacrifice a Food. It's a cost found mostly on black-green Squirrel cards. Feed the Cycle, a two-mana removal spell, needs you to forage or pay an extra `{B}`, so Food and graveyard decks get a cheap kill spell.

### Gift

A card with gift lets you promise your opponent a small present, such as a card, a Food or a 1/1 Fish, in exchange for a bigger effect. Into the Flood Maw bounces a creature for one blue mana, but if you give the opponent a tapped Fish, it can return a nonland permanent instead. Gift asks a real question each time: is the upgrade worth the help you hand over?

### Valiant

Valiant triggers the first time each turn a creature becomes the target of a spell or ability you control. It's the Mice mechanic. Heartfire Hero gets a +1/+1 counter each time, and when it dies, it deals damage equal to its power to each opponent. Valiant turns cheap pump spells and equipment into lasting growth.

### Expend

Expend triggers when you spend your fourth (or, on one card, eighth) total mana on spells in a turn. Raccoons use it: Wandertale Mentor gets a +1/+1 counter whenever you expend 4. It rewards casting two spells in a turn or one big one.

### Seasons, Classes and threshold

The mythic "Season" cycle lets you choose modes with a budget of five paw-print points, picking the same mode more than once if you like. Season of Loss can wipe creatures, draw cards or drain life in whatever mix you want. Classes return from Adventures in the Forgotten Realms as enchantments you level up, like Stormchaser's Talent. Threshold returns for the Rats, turning on once seven or more cards are in your graveyard.

## Limited

Bloomburrow's ten animal archetypes are about as clean as draft design gets. Each pair has a creature type and a "Mentor" signpost uncommon:

| Pair | Animal | Theme | Signpost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Birds | Fliers and counters | Plumecreed Mentor |
| Blue-Black | Rats | Threshold | Tidecaller Mentor |
| Black-Red | Lizards | Opponents losing life | Fireglass Mentor |
| Red-Green | Raccoons | Expend | Wandertale Mentor |
| Green-White | Rabbits | Go wide | Burrowguard Mentor |
| White-Black | Bats | Gaining and losing life | Starseer Mentor |
| Blue-Red | Otters | Noncreature spells | Stormcatch Mentor |
| Black-Green | Squirrels | Forage and Food | Vinereap Mentor |
| Red-White | Mice | Valiant | Seedglaive Mentor |
| Green-Blue | Frogs | Flicker and bounce | Lilysplash Mentor |

The format was fast and creature-focused. Aggressive decks, especially those with cheap Mice, Rabbits and Lizards, punished clunky draws, and two-drops were at a premium. Many creatures carry two animal types (Lifecreed Duo is a Bat Bird, Bakersbane Duo a Squirrel Raccoon), which lets them fit more than one deck. Removal was good at common, with Banishing Light, Agate Assault and Sonar Strike all worth early picks. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Bloomburrow's Mice reshaped Standard. Heartfire Hero, Manifold Mouse and Emberheart Challenger formed the core of Mono-Red and Gruul Mice aggro, which was one of Standard's top decks in its first year, often with Duskmourn's Screaming Nemesis and Sunspine Lynx alongside. The same shell crossed into Pioneer, where Mono-Red Aggro eventually posted the format's highest win rate, especially on MTG Arena.

That success led to bans. On June 30, 2025, Wizards banned Heartfire Hero in Standard (along with its common partner Monstrous Rage from Wilds of Eldraine) as part of a seven-card update. On November 10, 2025, Heartfire Hero was banned in Pioneer too, with Wizards citing Mono-Red's high win rate and the early pressure the card forced. Then on August 10, 2026, Wizards banned Stormchaser's Talent in Standard, calling it the keystone of Izzet Prowess: a one-mana card that gave the deck both fast starts and late-game strength. As of October 2026, those are the only Bloomburrow cards banned in any format.

Bloomburrow's Commander footprint is large. Three Tree City is a go-to land for typal decks, Innkeeper's Talent doubles counters, the Seasons are flexible bombs, and legends like Ygra, Eater of All, Vren and Mabel lead popular decks. Four Bloomburrow Commander precons released with the set, and the main set also reprinted Fabled Passage.

## Cards from Bloomburrow worth owning

- **Three Tree City**: a strong mana land for any Commander typal deck.
- **Innkeeper's Talent**: a counter-doubling engine for +1/+1 counter decks.
- **Manifold Mouse** and **Emberheart Challenger**: aggressive red creatures that remain playable in Pioneer and Standard.
- **Fabled Passage**: a simple, reliable land for multicolor decks across formats.
- **Season of Loss**: a flexible board wipe and finisher for black Commander decks.
- **Maha, Its Feathers Night**: a flashy Commander threat that makes opposing creatures fragile.

## The takeaway

Bloomburrow is charming on the surface and dangerous underneath: its Mice and Otters warped Standard enough to draw bans, and its Commander cards will stay popular long after the early-2027 rotation. If you're building in Standard before then, a Mice deck without Heartfire Hero is still a real option, and mostly built from commons, uncommons and a few rares.
