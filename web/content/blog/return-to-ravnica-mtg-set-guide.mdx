Return to Ravnica is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2012 that brought players back to Ravnica, the plane-wide city run by ten magical guilds. The first set of the block features five of those guilds, each with its own mechanic, and its story begins Jace Beleren's search for a hidden maze that will decide the city's future. It's also the oldest set in Pioneer, which uses Return to Ravnica as its starting point.

## The story

Ravnica is a world that is one enormous city, and for ten thousand years its ten guilds were bound by the Guildpact, a magical treaty that gave each guild a role and kept them from destroying each other. In the original Ravnica block, that pact was broken. Return to Ravnica picks up years later. The guilds have rebuilt themselves without the old treaty, and they now coexist in an uneasy balance of politics, rivalry and threats.

This set focuses on five guilds. The **Azorius Senate** (white-blue) writes and enforces the law under the sphinx Isperia. The **Izzet League** (blue-red) runs the city's infrastructure and its wildest experiments for its founder, the dragon Niv-Mizzet. The **Cult of Rakdos** (black-red) is a cult of performers and thrill-seekers devoted to the demon Rakdos. The **Selesnya Conclave** (green-white) is a nature-loving community guided by the dryad Trostani. And the **Golgari Swarm** (black-green) recycles the city's dead and decay from the undercity, now led by Jarad and watched closely by the gorgon assassin Vraska.

The central mystery belongs to the Izzet. Niv-Mizzet has discovered strange patterns in the city's ley lines and believes they trace a hidden path, the **Implicit Maze**, left behind by the Guildpact's creator. He sets his researchers, including the storm mage Ral Zarek, to map it. Jace, who has made Ravnica his home, notices the patterns too and starts investigating, which makes him a target for guilds that want the secret for themselves. Meanwhile, the Rakdos grow restless, Vraska hunts the Golgari's enemies, and the Azorius try to keep order in a city sliding toward conflict.

Return to Ravnica only starts the story. The other five guilds arrive in Gatecrash, and the Maze is finally run in Dragon's Maze, where each guild sends a champion through it. Jace solves the puzzle and becomes the **Living Guildpact**, the living embodiment of the treaty, which is why later cards like Jace, the Living Guildpact carry that title.

## Mechanics

Each guild has one mechanic, and the set's two-color cards are built around them.

### Detain (Azorius)

When a card detains a permanent, it can't attack or block and its activated abilities can't be used until your next turn. **Lyev Skyknight** (uncommon) is a 3/1 flier that detains something as it enters, buying a turn of tempo.

### Overload (Izzet)

Overload is an alternate, more expensive cost that changes a spell's "target" to "each." **Mizzium Mortars** (rare) deals 4 damage to a creature you don't control for two mana, or to every creature you don't control if you overload it for six. **Cyclonic Rift** works the same way with bounce.

### Unleash (Rakdos)

A creature with unleash can enter with a +1/+1 counter, but then it can't block as long as it has one. **Rakdos Cackler** is a one-drop that becomes a 2/2 attacker if you unleash it.

### Populate (Selesnya)

To populate, you create a copy of a creature token you already control. **Growing Ranks** populates every upkeep, and cards like **Armada Wurm** make big tokens worth copying.

### Scavenge (Golgari)

Scavenge lets you exile a creature card from your graveyard at sorcery speed to put +1/+1 counters equal to its power on a creature. **Deadbridge Goliath** is a 5/5 for four that can later give five counters.

### Hybrid mana and the shock lands

Hybrid mana symbols like `{B/R}` can be paid with either color, which let some guild cards fit mono-colored decks. The set also reprinted five of the ten shock lands, such as **Blood Crypt** and **Hallowed Fountain**, dual lands that enter untapped if you pay 2 life. For more on reading two-color costs, see our guide on [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Return to Ravnica is a guild set, so drafting it meant picking one of five guilds and leaning into its mechanic:

- **Azorius (white-blue): detain and fliers.** A tempo deck that keeps blockers out of the way while fliers attack.
- **Izzet (blue-red): spells and overload.** Instants and sorceries backed by creatures like **Goblin Electromancer** and **Guttersnipe**.
- **Rakdos (black-red): unleash aggro.** Fast, aggressive creatures that don't need to block.
- **Selesnya (green-white): populate and tokens.** Go wide, then copy your best tokens.
- **Golgari (black-green): scavenge and attrition.** Trade creatures, then move their power onto survivors.

Two-color decks were the norm, but the Guildgates made three-color decks possible, and the format's removal, like **Annihilating Fire**, **Stab Wound** and **Arrest**, was strong at common and uncommon. Pack Rat became one of the most famous Limited bombs ever printed, because a single copy can take over a game by turning every extra card into another Rat. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Return to Ravnica shaped Standard for two years. Sphinx's Revelation and Supreme Verdict made Azorius and Esper control one of the format's pillars, and Revelation was the default way control decks won long games through 2013 and into the Theros era. On the other side, Jund and Golgari midrange decks used Abrupt Decay, Dreadbore and Vraska, Selesnya decks used Loxodon Smiter and populate, and Desecration Demon later became a key threat in Mono-Black Devotion. No Return to Ravnica cards were banned in Standard, and the set rotated out in September 2014, when Khans of Tarkir arrived. Our [rotation guide](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained) explains how that works.

Its biggest legacy is in other formats. When Wizards created Pioneer in 2019, it chose Return to Ravnica as the format's first set, so every card from here onward is Pioneer-legal. The shock lands are central to Pioneer mana bases, Supreme Verdict is a staple of Azorius control there, and Dreadbore and Sphinx's Revelation have both seen Pioneer play. Our [Pioneer guide](/blog/mtg-pioneer-format-guide) explains the format.

In Modern and Legacy, Abrupt Decay became a long-time staple of black-green decks, and Rest in Peace is a common sideboard card against graveyard strategies. Deathrite Shaman was strong enough to be banned in both formats: in Modern on February 3, 2014, and in Legacy on July 2, 2018, where Wizards pointed to its dominance in Grixis Delver and other decks.

In Commander, Cyclonic Rift is one of the most powerful blue cards in the format, because an overloaded Rift at the end of an opponent's turn bounces everything they control. Chromatic Lantern, Rest in Peace, Vandalblast and the shock lands are common picks, and Trostani and Niv-Mizzet, Dracogenius are popular guild commanders. Swingy cards like Cyclonic Rift are worth weighing against your table's [Commander bracket](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). As of October 2026, the shock lands and Cyclonic Rift remain the set's most-played cards across formats.

## Cards from Return to Ravnica worth owning

- **The shock lands**: Blood Crypt, Hallowed Fountain, Overgrown Tomb, Steam Vents and Temple Garden are staples in Pioneer, Modern and Commander.
- **Cyclonic Rift**: one of the strongest blue cards in Commander.
- **Abrupt Decay**: efficient removal for black-green decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Supreme Verdict**: the board wipe Azorius control decks have played for over a decade.
- **Rest in Peace**: a powerful graveyard-hate card in every format.
- **Chromatic Lantern**: mana fixing for multicolor Commander decks.
- **Sphinx's Revelation**: a classic control finisher that still sees Pioneer play.

Return to Ravnica is a guild set that rewards picking a side and leaning into its mechanic, and many of its cards are still played more than a decade later.
