Gatecrash is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2013, the second set of the Return to Ravnica block. It covers five of Ravnica's ten guilds, the Boros Legion, House Dimir, the Gruul Clans, the Orzhov Syndicate and the Simic Combine, each with its own two-color mechanic. It's also the set that reprinted five shock lands, which is a big reason it matters to Pioneer players today.

## The story

Ravnica is a plane covered by a single city, run by ten guilds that each control one part of civic life. For thousands of years, a magical treaty called the Guildpact held them in balance. That treaty broke down years before this block, and in Return to Ravnica and Gatecrash, the guilds are testing how far they can push one another without open war.

The block's central mystery is the Implicit Maze, a hidden path across the city that the guilds' founder left behind. Jace Beleren, a mind mage planeswalker who lives on Ravnica, has been studying it. The second part of the block's story, told in the e-book Gatecrash: The Secretist, Part Two, has Jace trying to step away from the mystery as guild tensions grow, until his friend Emmara Tandris is kidnapped by the Cult of Rakdos. Rescuing her pulls him back in, and along the way he begins to understand what the maze is. By the end, it's a race to see who can solve it first, with Ral Zarek, the Izzet planeswalker working for Niv-Mizzet, also on the trail.

Each of Gatecrash's guilds has its own story on the cards:

- **The Boros Legion** (red-white) is Ravnica's army and police, led by the angel Aurelia. The planeswalker Gideon Jura serves in its ranks.
- **House Dimir** (blue-black), the guild of spies, has returned to public view, while its true master, the shapeshifter Lazav, stays hidden behind other faces.
- **The Gruul Clans** (red-green) are wild outcasts, and the young planeswalker Domri Rade is pushing to overthrow their leader, the cyclops Borborygmos.
- **The Orzhov Syndicate** (white-black) is a guild of bankers and priests run by Obzedat, a council of ancient ghosts.
- **The Simic Combine** (green-blue) has been reborn under Prime Speaker Zegana as a guild that improves nature through biological experiments.

The story concludes in Dragon's Maze, where the guilds' champions run the maze and Jace ends up as the Living Guildpact, a living embodiment of the old treaty.

## Mechanics

Gatecrash gives each guild its own keyword.

### Battalion (Boros)

Battalion triggers whenever the creature and at least two other creatures attack. Boros Elite, a one-mana 1/1, becomes a 3/3 when it attacks in a battalion. The mechanic pushes Boros decks to flood the board with cheap creatures and attack as a group.

### Bloodrush (Gruul)

Bloodrush lets you discard a creature card from your hand to give an attacking creature a bonus, like an instant-speed pump spell. Ghor-Clan Rampager is a 4/4 trampler on its own, or you can pay `{R}{G}` and discard it to give an attacker +4/+4 and trample. It turns every Gruul creature in hand into a combat trick.

### Cipher (Dimir)

Cipher spells can be encoded on a creature you control after they resolve. Each time that creature deals combat damage to a player, you cast a free copy. Hands of Binding taps down a blocker, and once encoded on an evasive creature, it does that every turn.

### Evolve (Simic)

A creature with evolve gets a +1/+1 counter whenever a bigger creature (more power or more toughness) enters under your control. Experiment One, a one-mana 1/1, can grow into a 4/4 over the course of a game if you play creatures in increasing size.

### Extort (Orzhov)

Extort triggers whenever you cast a spell: you may pay `{W/B}` to drain each opponent for 1 life. Crypt Ghast and Syndic of Tithes both have it. Extort turns every spell into a little extra drain and makes games longer in your favor.

### Gates and shock lands

The block's Guildgates enter tapped and tap for two colors, and some cards care about controlling a Gate. Gatecrash also reprinted five shock lands: Godless Shrine, Watery Grave, Breeding Pool, Stomping Ground and Sacred Foundry. Each can enter untapped if you pay 2 life.

## Limited

Gatecrash was drafted with three packs of the same set, and the five guilds were the archetypes. Each guild's gold commons and uncommons pointed you toward it:

- **Boros** was the format's aggressive deck, with cheap battalion creatures like Daring Skyjek and Wojek Halberdiers, and Skyknight Legionnaire as a strong flier.
- **Gruul** played big creatures and used bloodrush cards like Zhur-Taa Swine as tricks.
- **Orzhov** was the controlling, extort-based deck, with removal like Executioner's Swing and lifelinking flyers.
- **Simic** grew its creatures with evolve, using cheap evolve creatures like Cloudfin Raptor and Shambleshark.
- **Dimir** played evasive creatures and cipher spells, plus a mill subtheme.

The format was widely regarded as fast, because battalion and bloodrush both rewarded early attacks. Cheap removal and good blockers were at a premium, and Gatecrash rewarded drafters who settled into one guild early. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Gatecrash entered a Standard format that already included the Innistrad block and Return to Ravnica, and the shock lands made two- and three-color decks easy to build. In 2013, Burning-Tree Emissary and Ghor-Clan Rampager powered red-green and Naya aggro decks, Domri Rade added card advantage to red-green creature decks, and Boros Reckoner was a strong three-drop in red and white midrange. Obzedat, Ghost Council gave white-black midrange a hard-to-answer threat. After Theros joined Standard in the fall of 2013, Boros Charm was a key card in red-white burn decks.

No Gatecrash card was banned in Standard. Its biggest Constructed impact came later.

As of October 2026, Gatecrash is still relevant in eternal formats:

- **The shock lands** are in nearly every two-color Pioneer and Modern deck. Gatecrash was their first Pioneer-legal printing, since that format starts with Return to Ravnica.
- **Boros Charm and Skullcrack** have long been parts of Modern Burn.
- **Balustrade Spy and Undercity Informer** fueled graveyard combo decks that won by milling their own library. Pioneer banned both in February 2021, and Legacy banned Undercity Informer in May 2026.
- **Thespian's Stage** combines with Dark Depths in Legacy Lands decks to make a 20/20 Marit Lage token.

In Commander, Sylvan Primordial was banned in February 2014, and it's still banned there as of October 2026. Many other Gatecrash cards are Commander favorites: Crypt Ghast in mono-black ramp, Master Biomancer and Prime Speaker Zegana in Simic decks, Aurelia, the Warleader for extra combats, and Lazav, Dimir Mastermind as a commander. In Pauper, Prophetic Prism was banned in January 2022 and unbanned in March 2025. For more on how formats differ, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Gatecrash worth owning

- **The shock lands**: Godless Shrine, Watery Grave, Breeding Pool, Stomping Ground and Sacred Foundry are staples of Pioneer, Modern and Commander.
- **Boros Charm**: a flexible burn spell for Modern and Pioneer red-white decks.
- **Skullcrack**: a burn spell and sideboard answer to lifegain.
- **Crypt Ghast**: a mana doubler for mono-black Commander decks.
- **Master Biomancer**: a +1/+1 counter engine for Commander.
- **Aurelia, the Warleader**: an extra-combat commander for Boros players.
- **Thespian's Stage**: a utility land for Legacy and Commander lands decks.

Gatecrash is more than a decade old, but its shock lands keep it relevant in every format.
