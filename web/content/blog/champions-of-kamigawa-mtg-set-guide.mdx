Champions of Kamigawa is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2004 that introduced Kamigawa, a plane inspired by Japanese myth where mortals and the spirits called kami share the world. The set is set during the Kami War, a conflict between the spirit world and the mortal one, and it is packed with legends, samurai, spirits and some of Commander's most-played cards, from Sakura-Tribe Elder and Kodama's Reach to Sensei's Divining Top.

## The story

Kamigawa is a world where every place and thing has a spirit. The physical world, the utsushiyo, sits beside the spirit realm, the kakuriyo, home to the kami, and the two lived in balance until one man broke it. **Lord Konda**, the daimyo who ruled from the fortress of Eiganjo, carried out a forbidden ritual with the help of moonfolk and wizards and pulled a young kami out of the spirit world. That kami was the child of **O-Kagachi**, the great serpent spirit that embodies all the kami, and Konda locked it inside a stone disc in his tower. The stolen spirit came to be called **That Which Was Taken**. In return, Konda gained long life and the power to see the future.

The kami answered with war. Spirits began attacking the mortal world: villages were destroyed, monasteries burned, and the samurai, monks, ninja and moonfolk found themselves fighting creatures they had worshipped. Most mortals had no idea why. That is the **Kami War**, and it had been raging for about twenty years when Champions of Kamigawa begins.

The set's story is told in the novel *Outlaw: Champions of Kamigawa* by Scott McGough. Its two leads are **Toshiro Umezawa**, a ronin and outlaw who uses black magic and wits more than honor, and **Princess Michiko**, Konda's daughter, who was born on the night of the theft and is bound up with its secret. Around them are the samurai of Konda's house, the nezumi rat-folk, the snake-folk orochi, the kitsune foxes, the akki goblins and the soratami moonfolk, each pulled into the war on different sides.

Champions sets up the conflict, and the block resolves it. In later sets, Toshiro steals That Which Was Taken from Eiganjo and frees the spirit trapped inside, which becomes **Kyodai**. O-Kagachi itself enters the mortal world, Michiko and the freed kami stand against it, Konda pays for his crime, and Michiko and Kyodai together take up the role of gatekeeper between the two worlds. Toshiro's famous weapon, Umezawa's Jitte, arrives in Betrayers of Kamigawa. See our guides to [Betrayers of Kamigawa](/blog/betrayers-of-kamigawa-mtg-set-guide) and [Saviors of Kamigawa](/blog/saviors-of-kamigawa-mtg-set-guide) for the rest of the block.

## Mechanics

### Bushido

When a creature with bushido N blocks or becomes blocked, it gets +N/+N until end of turn. It's the samurai mechanic, and it made those creatures dangerous to block or attack into. **Kitsune Blademaster** is a 2/2 first striker with bushido 1, and the flip card **Bushi Tenderfoot** becomes **Kenzo the Hardhearted**, a double striker with bushido 2.

### Arcane and splice onto Arcane

Arcane is a spell subtype on many instants and sorceries in the block. Splice onto Arcane lets you reveal a card from your hand while casting an Arcane spell and pay its splice cost to add the card's effect to that spell, while keeping the spliced card in your hand. **Glacial Ray** deals 2 damage, and you can splice it onto every Arcane spell you cast for `{1}{R}` to add 2 more damage each time. **Through the Breach** puts a creature from your hand onto the battlefield with haste for one turn, and it can be spliced the same way. Splice made card advantage out of the same cards again and again, but it needed a deck full of Arcane spells.

### Soulshift

Soulshift N appears on Spirits: when the creature dies, you may return a Spirit card with mana value N or less from your graveyard to your hand. **Thousand-legged Kami** has soulshift 7, so its death can return nearly any Spirit. Soulshift made Spirit decks resilient, since trading creatures often meant getting one back.

### Flip cards

Flip cards are printed with a second creature upside down at the bottom. When a condition is met, you turn the card around and it becomes the other creature, usually a legend. **Nezumi Graverobber** exiles cards from an opponent's graveyard and flips into **Nighteyes the Desecrator**, who reanimates creatures. **Budoka Gardener** puts lands onto the battlefield and flips into **Dokai, Weaver of Life** once you control ten lands.

### Legends everywhere

Champions printed many legendary creatures, artifacts, enchantments and lands, including the five legendary lands such as **Boseiju, Who Shelters All** and **Minamo, School at Water's Edge**, and the Shrines, legendary enchantments like **Honden of Seeing Winds** that grow stronger with each Shrine you control. The legend rule was updated with Champions so that if two legendary permanents with the same name are on the battlefield, both go to the graveyard, which affected how players handled mirror matches.

The dragon cycle deserves special mention. Each of the five "Star" dragons, **Kokusho**, **Keiga**, **Yosei**, **Ryusei, the Falling Star** and **Jugan, the Rising Star**, is a six-mana 5/5 flier with a big effect when it dies. Kokusho was the strongest in Constructed, and its drain made it a favorite way to close games.

## Limited

Champions was drafted as triple Champions at first, then with Betrayers and Saviors added as the block grew. It was a big set with a lot to learn: splice, soulshift, flip cards and bushido all rewarded drafters who knew what was in the packs.

The core divide was between samurai and spirits. White and red had bushido creatures that attacked and blocked well, with Kitsune Blademaster and Nezumi Ronin as efficient examples. Blue, black and green had many Spirits and Arcane spells, and cards like Kami of the Waning Moon and Kami of Fire's Roar triggered whenever you cast a Spirit or Arcane spell. Removal was the most important thing to pick. **Rend Flesh** killed any non-Spirit creature, **Glacial Ray** could be spliced for repeated damage, **Pull Under** gave -5/-5, and **Befoul** destroyed a land or nonblack creature. Black's removal depth made it a popular color.

Bombs like Meloku the Clouded Mirror and the dragons could take over a game, and the legendary theme meant players often opened powerful rares they could only play one copy of at a time. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Standard (2004-2006).** Champions landed in a Standard dominated by Mirrodin block's Affinity deck, and many of its cards were overshadowed at first. After Affinity's key cards were banned in early 2005 and Betrayers of Kamigawa arrived with Umezawa's Jitte, Kamigawa cards became central. Kokusho and Yosei appeared in control and midrange decks, Sensei's Divining Top showed up in control lists, and Cranial Extraction was a common answer to combo decks. At the 2005 World Championship, Frank Karsten finished second with a "Greater Gifts" deck built on Gifts Ungiven, Yosei and Kokusho. In 2006, Heartbeat of Spring powered a Standard combo deck that doubled its mana and used it to cast huge spells. None of Champions' cards were banned in Standard.

**Modern.** Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker was a combo staple for years, pairing with creatures like Pestermite or Restoration Angel to make infinite hasty copies. Through the Breach cheats in big creatures, Gifts Ungiven had a period in Modern control, and Lava Spike and Desperate Ritual feed Burn and storm. Sakura-Tribe Elder appears in ramp decks. Sensei's Divining Top and Glimpse of Nature are banned in Modern.

**Legacy.** Sensei's Divining Top was a fixture of Legacy control, especially the Miracles deck, until it was banned in Legacy in 2017. Glimpse of Nature is a key draw engine for the Elves combo deck.

**Vintage.** Gifts Ungiven became a Vintage staple, and Sensei's Divining Top is still legal there.

**Commander.** This is where Champions shines today. Kodama's Reach and Sakura-Tribe Elder are among the most played ramp cards in the format, and Sensei's Divining Top, Ghostly Prison, Kiki-Jiki, Night of Souls' Betrayal and the dragon cycle are popular. Many of the set's legends, like Azusa, Lost but Seeking, have become commanders. For how the format works, see [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

**Pauper.** Sakura-Tribe Elder, Kodama's Reach, Lava Spike and Desperate Ritual have all been played in Pauper.

## Cards from Champions of Kamigawa worth owning

- **Sensei's Divining Top**: a Commander and Vintage staple.
- **Kodama's Reach** and **Sakura-Tribe Elder**: two of Commander's most reliable ramp spells.
- **Kiki-Jiki, Mirror Breaker**: a combo piece in Modern and Commander.
- **Through the Breach**: a way to cheat huge creatures in Modern and Legacy.
- **Ghostly Prison**: a Commander deterrent for go-wide decks.
- **Kokusho, the Evening Star**: the best of the dragon cycle.
- **Gifts Ungiven**: a Vintage and casual control favorite.

## The takeaway

Champions of Kamigawa was a polarizing set when it came out, but it aged into one of the most important sources of Commander staples and Modern combo pieces. Its legends, its spirits and its splice engines still have a following. If you want to build with its cards today, a Kiki-Jiki combo shell is the most direct Modern route.
