Odyssey is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2001 that began a new era of the game's story on Otaria, a continent of Dominaria a century after the Phyrexian invasion. It was built around the graveyard as a resource, introducing both flashback and threshold, and it gave Constructed players Psychatog, Entomb, Nimble Mongoose and a pile of cards that still matter in Legacy and Pauper.

## The story

Odyssey takes place about a hundred years after the Phyrexian invasion that ended in Apocalypse. Otaria came through the war less damaged than most of Dominaria, and its society has turned rough and mercenary. The **Cabal**, a criminal organization devoted to black magic, runs Cabal City and its famous pit fights, where wizards and warriors battle for money and glory. The **Order**, a militant white faction of aven bird-folk and humans, opposes the Cabal. Across the land live barbarian dwarves and humans in the Pardic Mountains, druid and centaur tribes in the Krosan Forest, nomads in the deserts, and the cephalids and merfolk of the Mer Empire in the seas.

The plot centers on the **Mirari**, a mysterious silver sphere that appears in the pit fights. It seems to grant its holder's wishes and magnify their power, but it also eats at their mind. The barbarian mage **Kamahl**, a pit fighter from the Pardic Mountains, is drawn to Cabal City and into the hunt for it. Nearly everyone else wants it: the Cabal; the Order's aven leader **Kirtar**, who briefly gets hold of it; and the cephalid ambassador **Laquatus**, who serves the Mer Empire's emperor **Aboshan**. A minor Cabal summoner named **Chainer** helps Kamahl find his way in Cabal City, and the orb changes hands again and again. By the end of the novel *Odyssey* by Vance Moore, Kirtar is dead and the Mirari has gone into the sea with the Mer Empire's agents.

That is only the opening. In Torment, Chainer rises within the Cabal and the orb's corruption spreads. In Judgment, Kamahl takes the Mirari back, its influence drives him toward madness, and his sister **Jeska** is badly wounded, which sets up her transformation into Phage in Onslaught and, later, her role in [Future Sight](/blog/future-sight-mtg-set-guide). For where the story goes next, see our [Onslaught set guide](/blog/onslaught-mtg-set-guide).

## Mechanics

### Flashback

A card with flashback can be cast a second time from your graveyard by paying its flashback cost, and then it's exiled. **Call of the Herd** makes a 3/3 Elephant token for `{2}{G}`, then makes another from the graveyard for `{3}{G}`, so one card gives you two creatures. **Firebolt** deals 2 damage for `{R}`, then does it again later for `{4}{R}`. Flashback turned every spell into potential card advantage and has returned in many sets since, including the Innistrad blocks.

### Threshold

Threshold abilities turn on when you have seven or more cards in your graveyard. **Werebear** is a mana creature that becomes a 4/4 at threshold, **Nimble Mongoose** is a 1/1 with shroud that becomes a 3/3, and **Krosan Beast** goes from a 1/1 to an 8/8. Threshold rewarded discarding and self-mill, and the set gave you plenty of ways to do it. Lead designer Mark Rosewater has since named threshold as one of the set's design mistakes.

### Filling the graveyard

Odyssey gave players many tools to make threshold happen: looting cards like **Careful Study** (draw two, discard two), discard outlets like **Wild Mongrel**, and lands with threshold abilities, such as **Cephalid Coliseum** and **Barbarian Ring**, that you sacrifice for an effect once your graveyard is full. Black had **Entomb**, which puts any card from your library straight into your graveyard, and **Buried Alive**, which puts up to three creature cards there. Both became reanimator staples.

Odyssey's color identities were clear:

- **Green** had the strongest threshold creatures and token makers, including Werebear, Nimble Mongoose, Call of the Herd, Wild Mongrel, Krosan Beast and Squirrel Nest.
- **Blue** focused on drawing and discarding, with Careful Study, Standstill, Upheaval, Time Stretch and the flashback bounce spell Dematerialize.
- **Black** had Entomb, Buried Alive, Zombify, Diabolic Tutor, Mortivore and Cabal Patriarch.
- **Red** offered Firebolt and pit fighters like Kamahl, Pit Fighter, a 6/1 haste creature that pings for 3.
- **White** had the Order's soldiers and birds, along with Kirtar's Wrath, a board wipe that also leaves you two 1/1 flying Spirits at threshold.
- **Gold** cards included Psychatog, Shadowmage Infiltrator and Mystic Enforcer.

## Limited

Odyssey was drafted in the modern booster-draft style, first as triple Odyssey and later with Torment and Judgment added. The graveyard was the main resource: drafters valued looting, cheap flashback spells and creatures that grew at threshold, and they had to decide whether a card in the graveyard was helping them reach threshold or simply gone.

Green was deep because of its threshold creatures and flashback tokens. Black had removal like **Ghastly Demise**, which scales with your graveyard, and **Innocent Blood**, which makes each player sacrifice a creature. Red had Firebolt, one of the best commons in the set because of its two uses. Blue's card filtering made threshold reliable, and white had fliers and solid soldiers like **Patrol Hound**. The format tended to reward patience over racing, since flashback and threshold cards got better as games went longer.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Standard (2001-2003).** Odyssey's biggest Standard card was Psychatog. Blue-black "Tog" decks used countermagic and card draw, often including Invasion block's Fact or Fiction, then won by discarding and exiling cards to pump Psychatog for a huge attack. Psychatog decks were everywhere at the 2002 World Championship, where six of the Top 8 players used them, including the winner, Carlos Romão. Later in the block, Odyssey cards fed two more archetypes: U/G Madness, an aggro deck built on Wild Mongrel with Torment's madness cards, and Mirari's Wake, which combined Mirari with Judgment's Mirari's Wake to copy expensive spells. None of Odyssey's cards were banned in Standard.

**Legacy.** Entomb was a core part of Reanimator for years: put Griselbrand or another fatty in the graveyard, then return it with a cheap spell. Wizards banned Entomb in Legacy on November 10, 2025, citing the long dominance of Reanimator decks. Nimble Mongoose was a key threat in green-blue threshold tempo decks, Werebear was played alongside it, Standstill appears in tempo and control, and Cephalid Coliseum is part of graveyard combos. For more on the format, see our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

**Vintage.** Odyssey cards see occasional play in graveyard decks, and none of them are restricted as of October 2026.

**Commander.** Buried Alive, Diabolic Tutor, Mirari, the filter lands and Squirrel Nest are the most common Commander picks. Upheaval is banned in Commander.

**Pauper.** Odyssey is a big supplier of Pauper commons: Firebolt, Careful Study, Werebear, Chatter of the Squirrel, Moment's Peace and Ghastly Demise have all been played, and Nimble Mongoose, an uncommon in Odyssey, is Pauper-legal through later common printings. For more, see [what is Pauper](/blog/what-is-pauper-mtg).

## Cards from Odyssey worth owning

- **Psychatog**: a piece of Standard history and a fun Legacy and casual card.
- **Buried Alive**: a Commander reanimator staple.
- **Nimble Mongoose** and **Werebear**: threshold creatures with Legacy and Pauper roles.
- **Call of the Herd**: one of the best flashback cards ever printed.
- **Firebolt** and **Chatter of the Squirrel**: Pauper commons.
- **The filter lands**: Mossfire Valley, Darkwater Catacombs, Shadowblood Ridge, Skycloud Expanse and Sungrass Prairie for Commander mana.
- **Entomb**: still legal in Vintage and Commander, though banned in Legacy.

## The takeaway

Odyssey taught Magic that the graveyard is a second hand. Flashback, threshold and its looting cards set the template for every graveyard set since, and Psychatog showed how strong that idea could be in Constructed. If you want to play its cards today, Legacy is the best home: a green-blue threshold tempo deck built around Nimble Mongoose is a classic.
