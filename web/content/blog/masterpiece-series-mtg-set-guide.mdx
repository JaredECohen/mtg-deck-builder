The Masterpiece Series was Magic: The Gathering's line of ultra-rare, premium foil reprints hidden inside regular booster packs, running from Zendikar Expeditions in October 2015 through Amonkhet Invocations in 2017. Each block's Masterpieces reprinted powerful older cards in a frame and art style that matched the plane, and opening one was the biggest pull a pack could offer. The idea outlived the name: Mythic Edition, box toppers, the Mystical Archive, Special Guests and other bonus sheets all carry it forward.

## What the set was for

Masterpieces did two jobs at once. For collectors, they were a chase card far rarer than a regular mythic, with unique art and frames. For the wider market, they were a way to put valuable reprints into the most-opened packs of the year, which drew more players to buy boosters and slightly increased supply of expensive cards.

The inspiration was the original Zendikar's "Priceless Treasures" promotion, when Wizards slipped older valuable cards into packs. Battle for Zendikar made that a permanent part of the set as Expeditions, and the idea was renamed the Masterpiece Series when it continued into Kaladesh and Amonkhet. Masterpieces were rarer than a regular mythic, though slightly more common than a foil mythic; for Kaladesh, roughly one in every 144 packs contained one.

There's no storyline in the Masterpiece cards themselves, but each sheet's look tells you about its plane: Zendikar's wild ruins, Kaladesh's filigree workshops, Amonkhet's carved hieroglyphs.

## Zendikar Expeditions (2015 to 2016)

The first Masterpiece sheet was all lands, appropriate for a plane obsessed with them. Battle for Zendikar carried 25 Expeditions: the ten shock lands (such as Steam Vents and Breeding Pool), the ten Onslaught and Zendikar fetch lands (such as Polluted Delta and Scalding Tarn) and the five Battle for Zendikar dual lands like Prairie Stream. Oath of the Gatewatch added 20 more: the Shadowmoor and Eventide filter lands like Cascade Bluffs, plus a run of famous utility lands including Ancient Tomb, Strip Mine, Wasteland, Mana Confluence, Eye of Ugin, Horizon Canopy and Forbidden Orchard.

The full-art landscapes made them some of the most beloved Masterpieces ever, and they remain a favorite way to upgrade a Commander mana base.

## Kaladesh Inventions (2016 to 2017)

Kaladesh is a plane of inventors, so its Masterpieces were all artifacts, shown in an ornate filigree frame. Kaladesh carried 30 and Aether Revolt 24. The list was stacked: Sol Ring, Mana Crypt, Mana Vault, Chrome Mox, Mox Opal, Lotus Petal, Aether Vial, Crucible of Worlds, Scroll Rack, Sword of Fire and Ice, Sword of Feast and Famine, Wurmcoil Engine, Chalice of the Void, Arcbound Ravager and Paradox Engine, alongside the five Kaladesh Gearhulks like Torrential Gearhulk. Inventions were widely considered the high point of the series, with a clean theme and cards nearly everyone wanted.

## Amonkhet Invocations (2017)

Amonkhet's Masterpieces were spells shown as carved, hieroglyph-framed works: 30 in Amonkhet and 24 in Hour of Devastation. They included Force of Will, Counterspell, Cryptic Command, Daze, Thoughtseize, Wrath of God, Damnation, Omniscience, Blood Moon and Through the Breach, plus the Amonkhet gods such as Oketra the True and The Scarab God.

Invocations were the least loved of the three. The theme was looser than "lands" or "artifacts", and the stylized frame and hieroglyph-like text made some cards hard to read. Head designer Mark Rosewater has since said reception dipped with Invocations, and the planned Ixalan Masterpieces were cancelled, ending the original series.

## After the Masterpieces: Mythic Edition and box toppers

Wizards next tried selling the premium experience directly. **Mythic Edition** (2018 to 2019) was a box of booster packs for Guilds of Ravnica, Ravnica Allegiance and War of the Spark, sold online through Hasbro, with eight packs per box each holding a special planeswalker in a unique frame. The 24 planeswalkers included Jace, the Mind Sculptor, Ugin, the Spirit Dragon, Karn, Scion of Urza, Teferi, Hero of Dominaria and Liliana, the Last Hope. The first sale went badly, with orders cancelled after it sold out within minutes, and Wizards later said the format wasn't quite right, which fed into the creation of Secret Lair.

**Ultimate Masters Box Toppers** (December 2018) put one of 40 borderless, full-art reprints on top of each Ultimate Masters booster box, including Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Karn Liberated, Tarmogoyf, Liliana of the Veil, Demonic Tutor, Snapcaster Mage and Through the Breach.

## The bonus-sheet era (2020 onward)

From 2020, Wizards made special reprints part of most premier sets, usually at a much higher frequency than the old Masterpieces:

- **Zendikar Rising Expeditions (2020):** 30 new Expeditions, including all ten fetch lands, the five Scars of Mirrodin fast lands, the battlebond lands like Luxury Suite, and Cavern of Souls, Prismatic Vista and Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle.
- **Strixhaven Mystical Archive (2021):** 63 iconic instants and sorceries, such as Demonic Tutor, Counterspell and Swords to Plowshares, in one slot of every Strixhaven booster, each with a Japanese alternate-art version too.
- **The Brothers' War Retro Artifacts (2022), Multiverse Legends (2023) and Wilds of Eldraine: Enchanting Tales (2023):** bonus sheets of artifacts in old-style frames, legendary creatures from across the Multiverse, and storybook enchantments like Rhystic Study, Smothering Tithe and Doubling Season.
- **Special Guests (from November 2023):** introduced in The Lost Caverns of Ixalan, these are high-value reprints with new borderless art matched to each set's world. They appear in a small share of packs, and Wizards has kept the line going in later sets, including more Secret Lair-style artwork since Aetherdrift.
- **Outlaws of Thunder Junction (2024):** two sheets at once. Breaking News reprinted 65 spells in a newspaper-style frame, and The Big Score added 30 new heist-themed cards. The Big Score was a true expansion, so its cards are legal in Standard alongside the main set as of October 2026.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Masterpieces never changed what decks were good, since they reprinted cards already in a format or followed the host set's normal legality. Their effect was mostly on supply and on collectors. Bonus sheets like the Mystical Archive and Enchanting Tales added real reprints at a much higher rate, which made cards like Demonic Tutor and Rhystic Study easier to find.

Several Masterpiece cards are notable for Commander rules as of October 2026. Mana Crypt was banned in Commander in September 2024, Paradox Engine is also banned there, and Force of Will, Ancient Tomb, Mana Vault, Chrome Mox, Demonic Tutor and Rhystic Study are on the Game Changers list used by the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained). Our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) covers how those lists are managed.

## Cards from the Masterpiece Series worth owning

- **Fetch lands (Expeditions):** useful in nearly every eternal deck and every multicolor Commander deck.
- **Sol Ring (Inventions):** the one artifact every Commander deck plays.
- **Sword of Fire and Ice (Inventions):** one of the strongest Equipment cards ever printed.
- **Wurmcoil Engine (Inventions):** a resilient colorless threat that fits any deck.
- **Force of Will (Invocations):** the defining free counterspell of Legacy.
- **The Scarab God (Invocations):** a strong blue-black commander and Constructed threat.
- **Doubling Season and Rhystic Study (Enchanting Tales):** two of Commander's most popular enchantments.

The Masterpiece Series is mostly a collector's story, but its cards are ordinary Magic cards in fancy clothes, so every one of them can go straight into a deck. The easiest place to use a handful is a Commander artifact deck.
