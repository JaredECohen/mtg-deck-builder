Legends is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1994, the game's third expansion after Arabian Nights and Antiquities. It introduced legendary creatures, legendary lands and multicolored "gold" cards, ideas that every Magic set since has built on, and it printed Mana Drain, Moat, The Abyss, Sylvan Library, Land Tax, Chain Lightning and the five original Elder Dragons, including Nicol Bolas.

## The setting and flavor

Legends has no single storyline. It was designed by Steve Conard and Robin Herbert as a collection of heroes, villains and places, a "time of legends" (the expansion symbol is the top of a classical column). Most of its 55 legendary creatures were individual characters with names and personalities, like **Tetsuo Umezawa**, **Hazezon Tamar**, **Johan**, **Rubinia Soulsinger** and **Sol'kanar the Swamp King**, rather than generic soldiers or beasts.

A few of them grew far bigger than the set. The five **Elder Dragons**, **Nicol Bolas**, **Chromium**, **Palladia-Mors**, **Vaevictis Asmadi** and **Arcades Sabboth**, each cost three colors and eight mana. Nicol Bolas went on to become the biggest villain in Magic's story, the scheming dragon planeswalker behind the War of the Spark. Tetsuo Umezawa, a descendant of the Umezawa family later seen on Kamigawa, became Bolas's rival in a full novel trilogy.

That trilogy, *Legends Cycle II* by Scott McGough (2002-2003), and an earlier one by Clayton Emery, *Legends Cycle I* (2001-2002), which follows Johan, Jedit Ojanen and Hazezon Tamar, were written years after the cards to give these characters stories. The set's legendary lands, such as **Karakas**, **Pendelhaven**, **Tolaria** and **Urborg**, also became recurring places in Dominaria's lore. For Dominaria in the modern era, see the [Dominaria set guide](/blog/dominaria-mtg-set-guide).

## Mechanics

### Legendary creatures and the legend rule

Legends introduced "Summon Legend," later rewritten as Legendary Creature. Under the original legend rule, if a legend was on the battlefield, no player could cast another copy. Today's rule is narrower: if you control two or more legendary permanents with the same name, you keep one and put the rest into the graveyard. Legendary creatures are also why Commander exists; see [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) for how the format picks its general.

### Multicolored cards

Legends was the first set with cards that need more than one color of mana. Many of its legends cost three colors, such as **Tetsuo Umezawa** (`{U}{B}{R}`), which destroys a tapped or blocking creature, or **Sol'kanar the Swamp King** (`{2}{U}{B}{R}`), a 5/5 swampwalker.

### Enchant World

An Enchant World (now called a World Enchantment) changes the game for everyone, and only one can exist at a time: a new one destroys the old. **The Abyss** destroys a nonartifact creature of each player's choice during that player's upkeep, and **Concordant Crossroads** gives all creatures haste.

### Bands with other, rampage and poison

Bands with other is a narrow variant of banding that lets creatures of a certain type attack together as a group; most of the set's lands that grant it, like **Seafarer's Quay**, are curiosities today. Rampage pumps a creature for each blocker beyond the first, as on **Chromium**. And **Pit Scorpion** gives players a poison counter when it deals damage, an early version of the idea that became infect and toxic.

## Limited

Booster drafting wasn't an organized format in 1994, and Legends wasn't designed with Limited in mind. Players mostly opened packs to build Constructed decks, and with no basic lands in the product, the set wasn't meant to be played on its own. There are no draft archetypes to describe, and any claim about "the best Legends commons in draft" would be guesswork.

Legends also had a famous distribution problem. Some booster boxes contained only one half of the uncommons and others the other half, so players opening the same set could see very different cards. Wizards responded with a Legends Exchange Program that let players trade cards from one group for the other.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Early tournament Magic in 1994 was the format later known as Type I, now Vintage, with a restricted list of single-copy cards. Legends cards shaped it right away. The MTG Wiki notes that Moat was the main creature defense of The Deck, one of the first recognized tournament decks, and Mana Drain became the defining counterspell of blue control. Recall was restricted in August 1994.

When Type II launched on January 10, 1995, it allowed Revised Edition and the latest expansions, The Dark and Fallen Empires, so Legends never entered the format as a set. Legends cards reached Type II only through Chronicles, the 1995 reprint set, which brought back cards such as the Elder Dragons and Recall.

As of October 2026:

- **Vintage**: Mana Drain, Moat, Chains of Mephistopheles and The Abyss are all legal, and none of the set's cards are restricted. Banned in Vintage are the ante cards Rebirth and Tempest Efreet, the dexterity card Falling Star, and four cards Wizards removed from all formats in 2020 for racist or offensive content: Invoke Prejudice, Cleanse, Imprison and Pradesh Gypsies.
- **Legacy**: Mana Drain is banned, along with the same ante, dexterity and removed cards. The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale is legal and is a signature card of Lands decks; Sylvan Library and Chain Lightning are regulars.
- **Commander**: Karakas is banned. The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale is on the official Game Changers list. Mana Drain, Sylvan Library, Land Tax, Moat and the Elder Dragons all see play.
- **Pauper**: Chain Lightning is a common and a staple of Pauper red decks.
- **Modern**: Legends predates Eighth Edition. Some cards, like Underworld Dreams and Nicol Bolas, are Modern-legal through later reprints.

Seventy-two Legends cards are on the Reserved List, including Moat, The Abyss, Chains of Mephistopheles, Mirror Universe, Eureka and The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale, which means Wizards has committed not to reprint them in tournament-legal form. For more on these formats, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained) and the [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide).

## Cards from Legends worth owning

- **Sylvan Library**: card selection for any green deck in Legacy or Commander.
- **Chain Lightning**: a burn staple in Legacy and Pauper.
- **Mana Drain**: the best counterspell in Vintage and Commander.
- **Land Tax**: a strong land engine in Commander.
- **The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale**: the centerpiece of Legacy Lands.
- **Moat**: a classic control lock against ground creatures.
- **Nicol Bolas**: the original version of Magic's most famous villain.

Legends invented the building blocks of modern Magic: legends, gold cards and named characters people care about. Its strongest cards still matter in the eternal formats, especially Legacy.
