Visions is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 1997, the second set of the Mirage block. It continues the war for the continent of Jamuraa on Dominaria, and it's best remembered for creatures that do something the moment they enter the battlefield, like Man-o'-War, Nekrataal and Uktabi Orangutan. It also printed Vampiric Tutor, Natural Order, Fireblast and Quirion Ranger, cards that still matter in Legacy, Vintage, Pauper and Commander.

## The story

The Mirage block's story was told mostly through backstory articles, flavor text and Duelist magazine features rather than a novel tied to the set. It's set in Jamuraa, a continent inspired by African cultures and mythology, and the conflict is a war for its kingdoms.

The villain is Kaervek, a necromancer from Urborg. He, the white mage Mangara and the nature mage Jolrael had all been drawn to Jamuraa by a burst of energy caused by the planeswalker Teferi, who had accidentally pulled his own island out of time. Kaervek allied with Jolrael, convinced her that Mangara wanted to rule the continent, and trapped Mangara in an Amber Prison. War followed across Zhalfir, Femeref and Suq'Ata, with Kaervek's forces including nightstalkers and his panther warrior lieutenant, Purraj.

By Visions, Femeref has fallen and the other nations are fracturing from within. Hope arrives when Jolrael, disillusioned and urged on by Teferi, turns on Kaervek and ambushes his fleet at sea. Teferi sinks the armada, the heroes Rashida Scalebane and Asmira free Mangara, and the Jamuraan leaders unite against Kaervek, who ends up sealed in an Amber Prison of his own. The set's name comes from the prophecies of Asmira that guided the alliance.

Visions also shows early glimpses of the Weatherlight Saga. Sisay, later captain of the Weatherlight, appears in the backstory, and the common artifact Sisay's Ring carries her name.

## Mechanics

Visions introduced no brand-new keywords. It reused Mirage's mechanics and leaned into one new design idea.

### Enters-the-battlefield creatures

Visions was the first set to make creatures with "when this enters" effects a real theme. At the time these were called "comes into play" or CIP effects. They let a creature do the job of a spell and leave a body behind:

- **Man-o'-War** returns a creature to its owner's hand.
- **Nekrataal** has first strike and destroys a nonartifact, nonblack creature on entry.
- **Uktabi Orangutan** destroys an artifact.
- **Shrieking Drake** is a one-mana flier that returns one of your own creatures, which combos with other entry effects.

These cards were efficient enough that they redefined what a good creature was, and they combined well with bounce effects to reuse the triggers.

### Flanking

Flanking is a combat keyword: whenever a creature without flanking blocks a flanking creature, the blocker gets -1/-1 until end of turn. Knight of Valor and Fallen Askari are examples. It made small attackers awkward to block.

### Phasing

Phasing permanents phase in or out before you untap each turn. While phased out, a permanent is treated as though it doesn't exist. Breezekeeper, a four-mana 4/4 flier, is only around every other turn, which is the cost of its stats.

### Cumulative upkeep

Cumulative upkeep makes you pay a growing cost each upkeep or sacrifice the permanent. Elephant Grass stops black creatures from attacking you and taxes everyone else, with a cost that keeps rising.

### Charms and karoo lands

Each color got a one-mana charm with three modes, such as Emerald Charm and Funeral Charm. The karoo lands, like Karoo and Coral Atoll, return an untapped basic land to your hand when they enter and then tap for two mana. ## Limited

Visions came out in an era when booster drafting existed but wasn't the main way most people played. Visions boosters were usually drafted or played as Sealed alongside Mirage, and you won't find modern-style archetype guides for it. Honestly, the best way to describe it is through the cards: Visions was the first set to put genuinely strong cards at common, like River Boa and Man-o'-War, which made its commons more important than in earlier sets.

Entry-effect creatures were the premium picks, since a Nekrataal or Man-o'-War was both removal and a body. Flanking knights in white and black rewarded attacking, and phasing creatures like Breezekeeper were big for their cost but unreliable. If you play a retro draft today, prioritize removal and two-for-ones the way you would in any format; our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains the reasoning.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Visions entered Type II in March 1997. Through 1997 and 1998 the format changed shape several times, and from July 1997 it used the new structure of the latest core set plus the two most recent blocks, which kept Mirage block legal until Urza's Saga arrived.

- **Pros-Bloom** was a combo deck that used Prosperity, Squandered Resources and Mirage's Cadaverous Bloom to draw and make huge amounts of mana before finishing with a big Drain Life. Squandered Resources was banned in Mirage Block Constructed in July 1997.
- **Red burn decks** got much faster with Fireblast. Being able to sacrifice two Mountains for 4 free damage gave red the reach to finish games, and it stayed a staple of red aggro.
- **Green Stompy** decks used efficient commons like River Boa.
- **Undiscovered Paradise** helped four- and five-color decks work in 1997 and 1998.
- **Chronatog** found a home in Stasis lock decks, since skipping your own turn meant never having to pay for Stasis.

Visions rotated out of Type II at the start of November 1998, shortly after Urza's Saga released.

Its long-term impact is in eternal formats. Vampiric Tutor is restricted in Vintage, banned in Legacy, and a staple of high-power Commander. Goblin Recruiter has been banned in Legacy since the format began in 2004. Natural Order powers Legacy decks that cheat out huge green creatures, and Necromancy is a Reanimator staple. Fireblast is a key card in Legacy and Pauper Burn, and Quirion Ranger is a mainstay of Pauper Stompy. Summer Bloom, also from Visions, is banned in Modern through its Ninth Edition reprint. See our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) for how these fit today's eternal decks.

### The Reserved List

Roughly 40 Visions cards are on the Reserved List, Wizards' policy not to reprint certain older cards. They include Undiscovered Paradise, Squandered Resources, Femeref Enchantress, City of Solitude and Tithe. Vampiric Tutor and Natural Order are not on it, which is why both have been reprinted.

## Cards from Visions worth owning

- **Vampiric Tutor**: an instant-speed tutor for any card in Vintage and Commander.
- **Natural Order**: the engine of green "cheat a big creature" decks.
- **Necromancy**: flexible reanimation for Legacy and Commander.
- **Fireblast**: free reach for Burn in Legacy and Pauper.
- **Quirion Ranger**: a cheap Pauper staple for Stompy and Elves.
- **Man-o'-War**: the template for every bounce creature since.

## The takeaway

Visions is a small set that changed how Magic thinks about creatures, proving that a cheap body with an entry effect could be as strong as a spell. Its best cards, led by Vampiric Tutor, Natural Order and Fireblast, still show up wherever older cards are legal. If you want to play them, Legacy is the best fit: a Burn deck with Fireblast, or a green deck built around Natural Order.
