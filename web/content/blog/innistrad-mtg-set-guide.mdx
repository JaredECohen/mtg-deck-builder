Innistrad is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in September 2011 that took the game to a gothic horror plane of vampires, werewolves, zombies, ghosts and frightened villagers. It introduced double-faced cards, brought back flashback and gave the world Snapcaster Mage, Liliana of the Veil and Delver of Secrets. Many players still rank it as the best set Magic has ever made.

## The story

Innistrad is a world where humanity is the prey. Vampire families rule the province of Stensia, werewolves stalk the forests of Kessig, mad scientists called stitchers sew corpses into new creatures in Nephalia, and restless spirits haunt the countryside. For a thousand years, the people survived because of the Church of Avacyn, named for an archangel who protected them with holy magic.

Avacyn was not a gift from the heavens. The ancient vampire planeswalker Sorin Markov created her, because he realized his own kind would starve if humans were wiped out. He made Avacyn as a guardian, and he made the Helvault, a huge silver monolith that could imprison demons.

When the set opens, Avacyn has vanished. Before the story began she fought the demon Griselbrand for days and drove him into the Helvault, but his spear struck her as the binding took hold, and she was trapped inside with him. Without her, the church's blessed protections are failing. The dead no longer rest quietly, the monsters are bolder, and a demon-worshipping cult called the Skirsdag grows in the shadows. The Lunarch Mikaeus and the cathedral city of Thraben are holding on, but barely.

Two planeswalkers arrive in the middle of this. Liliana Vess comes hunting Griselbrand. Years earlier she made a pact with four demons for youth and power, and she now wants to kill them to get her soul back. Garruk Wildspeaker comes hunting Liliana. She used a dark artifact called the Chain Veil to curse him, and the curse is slowly turning the great hunter into something more savage, which is why his card, Garruk Relentless, flips into the black-green Garruk, the Veil-Cursed. Meanwhile, the necromancer siblings Geralf and Gisa Cecani feud through their creations, and the vampire Olivia Voldaren schemes from her castle.

The first set ends with Innistrad still in the dark. Dark Ascension follows humanity's lowest point, and Avacyn Restored resolves the block: Liliana pressures the Helvault's guardian, Thalia, into breaking it open, which frees both Griselbrand and Avacyn. The archangel returns and drives the demons back.

## Mechanics

Innistrad was designed "top down", with horror tropes first and mechanics built to match them.

### Double-faced cards and transform

The headline innovation was a card with a face on each side and no Magic back. A double-faced card always enters the battlefield front face up, and its rules tell you when it transforms, which means turning it over. Auras, Equipment and counters stay on it when it flips. Werewolves were the main example: a card like Village Ironsmith is a Human with first strike, but if no spells were cast in the previous turn it becomes Ironfang, a bigger Werewolf. If a player casts two or more spells in one turn, the Werewolves flip back. Delver of Secrets was a different kind of double-faced card: on your upkeep you look at your top card, and if it's an instant or sorcery you can reveal it to turn your 1/1 into a 3/2 flier. Our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) covers the rules in detail.

Players without opaque sleeves could use checklist cards, which stood in for the double-faced card while it was hidden in a hand or library.

### Flashback

Flashback lets you cast a spell a second time from your graveyard by paying its flashback cost, after which it's exiled. It first appeared in Odyssey. In Innistrad, it carried the theme of things returning from the dead. Think Twice draws a card for `{1}{U}` and draws another later from the graveyard. Many common flashback spells had a flashback cost in a second color, like Forbidden Alchemy, which casts for blue and flashes back for black, so they quietly pointed drafters toward two-color pairs.

### Morbid

Morbid is an ability word: the card gets a bonus if a creature died this turn. Brimstone Volley deals 3 damage, or 5 with morbid. The mechanic made combat and removal set up your next play, so the order of your spells mattered.

### Fight

Innistrad introduced "fight" as a keyword action. When two creatures fight, each deals damage equal to its power to the other. Prey Upon, a one-mana green sorcery, made fight the standard way green kills creatures, and the word is now evergreen.

### Curses and tribes

Curses are Auras that enchant a player rather than a creature, like Curse of Death's Hold, which gives that player's creatures -1/-1. The set also split its five monster tribes across allied color pairs: Spirits in white-blue, Zombies in blue-black, Vampires in black-red, Werewolves in red-green and Humans in green-white. Cards like Champion of the Parish rewarded you for staying in one tribe, and cards like Slayer of the Wicked punished the monsters.

## Limited

Triple Innistrad draft is widely regarded as one of the best Limited formats ever, and it is still cited that way by long-time players and podcasters. The tribes gave each allied color pair a clear identity, while flashback, self-mill and morbid created enemy-color archetypes that rewarded experienced drafters.

- **Green-white Humans** attacked early, with Travel Preparations as the signature common. Casting it on two creatures and then flashing it back on turn four could end games quickly.
- **White-blue Spirits** played cheap fliers like Chapel Geist, Voiceless Spirit and Moon Heron and tapped blockers with Feeling of Dread.
- **Blue-black Zombies and self-mill** used Armored Skaab and Dream Twist to fill the graveyard for Stitched Drake and flashback spells.
- **Black-red Vampires** were aggressive, using Bloodcrazed Neonate, Vampire Interloper and Crossway Vampire.
- **Red-green Werewolves** punished slow opponents, flipping when a turn passed without spells.
- **Black-green Spider Spawning** was the famous fringe deck: mill yourself, then cast and flash back Spider Spawning for a wall of 1/2 reach Spiders.
- **Blue-red flashback** decks built around Burning Vengeance or simply drew cards and burned creatures.

Removal was plentiful at common, including Dead Weight, Victim of Night, Brimstone Volley, Claustrophobia and Prey Upon, so games were interactive. The format was fast but not reckless, and the two-color flashback cards made every pick a small puzzle. If you are new to drafting, our guide to [evaluating cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) covers the basics.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Innistrad joined a Standard format that still had the Scars of Mirrodin block and Magic 2012. In its first weeks, the graveyard control deck Solar Flare was the early favorite, using Forbidden Alchemy and Dissipate alongside Unburial Rites to bring back Sun Titan. Wolf Run Ramp, built on Primeval Titan and Kessig Wolf Run, soon pushed it aside. The 2011 World Championship that November was won by Guillaume Matignon with a blue-black control deck.

The deck that defined the set's two years, though, was Delver. Blue-white Delver decks used Delver of Secrets, Snapcaster Mage, Geist of Saint Traft and cheap cantrips to attack in the air while holding up counterspells, and it was the most successful archetype of 2012. Dark Ascension and Avacyn Restored then added Zombies, Spirits tokens and Humans decks. Intangible Virtue and Dark Ascension's Lingering Souls were banned in Innistrad Block Constructed, but no Innistrad card was banned in Standard. The set rotated out when Theros released in September 2013.

### Eternal formats

Innistrad is one of the most important sets for older formats, and as of October 2026 none of its cards is banned in Modern, Legacy or Vintage.

- **Snapcaster Mage** is a long-time staple of blue decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Liliana of the Veil** was a pillar of Modern Jund and black midrange decks for years.
- **Delver of Secrets** anchors tempo decks in Legacy and Pauper, and appears in Modern.
- **Past in Flames** has fueled storm combo decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Champion of the Parish** helped start the Humans archetype, and **Unburial Rites** is a common reanimation spell.
- **Stony Silence** is a well-known sideboard card against artifact decks.

In Commander, Blasphemous Act, Parallel Lives, Laboratory Maniac and Olivia Voldaren remain popular. ## Cards from Innistrad worth owning

- **Snapcaster Mage**: a flexible blue creature that turns every cheap spell into two.
- **Liliana of the Veil**: a three-mana planeswalker that still shapes how black midrange decks play.
- **Delver of Secrets**: a cheap common threat that is a staple in Pauper and Legacy.
- **The enemy check lands**: Sulfur Falls, Woodland Cemetery, Isolated Chapel, Clifftop Retreat and Hinterland Harbor are easy mana fixing for Commander.
- **Blasphemous Act**: one of the best board wipes for red Commander decks.
- **Parallel Lives**: a token doubler for go-wide Commander decks.
- **Past in Flames**: a key piece for storm players.

Innistrad combined a haunting world, new technology in double-faced cards and a deep draft format, and its best cards are still played everywhere outside Standard.
