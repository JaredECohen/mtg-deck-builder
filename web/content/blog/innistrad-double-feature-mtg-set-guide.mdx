Innistrad: Double Feature is a Magic: The Gathering supplemental draft set released in January 2022 that combines Innistrad: Midnight Hunt and Innistrad: Crimson Vow into one booster. Every card from the two sets came back in a black-and-white "silver screen" style, and each pack held cards from both sets, so drafters could build werewolf, vampire, zombie and spirit decks from the whole Innistrad year. It added no new cards; its appeal was the format and the look.

## The story

Double Feature retells the two halves of Innistrad's 2021 storyline, the way a cinema double bill shows two horror films back to back. The black-and-white frames are a nod to classic monster movies.

The first feature is Midnight Hunt. The days on Innistrad were getting shorter and the werewolves bolder, and without the archangel Avacyn the humans were losing ground. The Dawnhart Coven, led by the witch Katilda, revived the ancient Harvesttide festival so that a ritual at the Celestus, a huge astronomical device, could restore the balance between day and night. Planeswalkers Arlinn Kord, Kaya, Teferi and Chandra joined the cathar Adeline to defend the festival from the werewolf alpha Tovolar, and Arlinn finally beat Tovolar in a duel. Then the vampire Olivia Voldaren swooped in and stole the Moonsilver Key the ritual needed. For the full story, see our [Midnight Hunt guide](/blog/innistrad-midnight-hunt-mtg-set-guide).

The second feature is Crimson Vow. Olivia planned to use the Key and the captured archangel Sigarda to bend Innistrad's angels to her will, and she needed Edgar Markov, Sorin's ancient grandfather, to do it. She woke him from his coffin, charmed him and announced their wedding. The heroes crashed it: Sorin attacked at the altar, Katilda's spirit broke free of the Key and released Sigarda, and in the chaos Olivia lost the Key and fled with Edgar. The heroes returned the Key to the Celestus and the cycle of day and night was restored. Our [Crimson Vow guide](/blog/innistrad-crimson-vow-mtg-set-guide) covers the wedding in more detail.

## Mechanics

Because every card came from Midnight Hunt or Crimson Vow, Double Feature shared their mechanics, and they mixed well.

### Day/night and daybound

Daybound werewolves transform when it becomes night, which happens when a player casts no spells during their own turn, and turn back when a player casts two or more spells in a turn. Brutal Cathar exiles an opposing creature each time it enters or flips back to its human side. Both sets used this system, so Double Feature werewolf decks were deep. Our [double-faced cards guide](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) explains transforming cards.

### Disturb and flashback

Disturb lets you cast a creature from your graveyard transformed into its Spirit side, like Malevolent Hermit returning as Benevolent Geist. Flashback lets you cast an instant or sorcery a second time from your graveyard, as with Faithful Mending.

### Coven and decayed

Coven rewards controlling three or more creatures with different powers (Augur of Autumn lets you cast creatures off the top of your library once it's on). Decayed Zombie tokens can't block and are sacrificed after they attack; Jadar, Ghoulcaller of Nephalia makes one every turn.

### Training, cleave, Blood and exploit

From Crimson Vow: training creatures get a +1/+1 counter when they attack alongside a bigger creature (Hopeful Initiate). Cleave spells have a second, pricier cost that removes the bracketed words for a stronger effect (Dig Up, Fierce Retribution). Blood tokens let you pay one mana, discard and draw, and are made by Vampires like Bloodtithe Harvester. Exploit lets a creature sacrifice another creature as it enters for a bonus, like Rot-Tide Gargantua forcing each opponent to sacrifice.

## Limited

Double Feature drafted as a three-pack mix of both sets. Each pack held a rare from each parent set, so drafts had more bombs than either original format, and every pack was effectively a blend of two sets' archetypes.

The two sets lined up neatly in most color pairs, which is why the mix worked:

- **White-blue:** Spirits and fliers, using disturb from both sets plus Midnight Hunt flashback.
- **Blue-black:** Zombies, with Midnight Hunt's self-mill and decayed tokens alongside Crimson Vow's exploit.
- **Black-red:** Vampires, mixing Midnight Hunt aggression with Crimson Vow's Blood tokens.
- **Red-green:** Werewolves, with day/night supported by both sets.
- **Green-white:** Humans, combining coven from Midnight Hunt with training and counters from Crimson Vow.
- **White-black, blue-red, black-green, red-white and green-blue** each combined the two sets' themes (sacrifice and life gain, spells, graveyard value and toughness, aggro, self-mill) into flexible decks.

Removal was plentiful at common, with Bleed Dry, Flame-Blessed Bolt, Abrade and Fierce Retribution from Crimson Vow sitting next to Midnight Hunt's interaction. Because each pack carried two rares, the format was a little bomb-heavy, and getting to two colors with strong removal early was a sound plan. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Double Feature itself didn't change any format, since all its cards were already Standard-legal through Midnight Hunt and Crimson Vow when it released. What it did was put more copies of those cards into circulation, in an eye-catching style.

Those two sets' cards were heavily played:

- **Standard:** during their long stay in Standard (late 2021 to August 2024), Bloodtithe Harvester and Wedding Announcement were central to the Grixis and Esper Midrange decks that dominated the 2022 World Championship, which Nathan Steuer won with Grixis Midrange. Midnight Hunt's Consider and Play with Fire filled out Izzet decks, and Brutal Cathar and Adeline, Resplendent Cathar powered white aggro. **The Meathook Massacre** was banned in Standard on October 10, 2022, the only card from either set to be banned there.
- **Pioneer:** as of October 2026, Infernal Grasp, Fateful Absence, Bloodtithe Harvester, Graveyard Trespasser, Sorin the Mirthless and The Meathook Massacre are all Pioneer-legal and have long histories in its midrange decks.
- **Modern and Legacy:** Consider is the most-played card here, a cheap cantrip for blue tempo decks. Thalia, Guardian of Thraben is a Legacy regular.
- **Commander:** Toxrill, the Corrosive, Hullbreaker Horror, Lier, Disciple of the Drowned, Wrenn and Seven and Voldaren Estate are popular, and the silver screen foils are prized versions of each.

For a quick map of where Standard, Pioneer and Modern sit, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## Cards from Innistrad: Double Feature worth owning

- **Consider**: a Modern and Pioneer cantrip for any blue deck.
- **Infernal Grasp** and **Fateful Absence**: efficient removal in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Bloodtithe Harvester**: a strong two-drop for Rakdos decks.
- **The Meathook Massacre**: a sweeper and drain engine in Pioneer and Commander.
- **Toxrill, the Corrosive** and **Hullbreaker Horror**: Commander bombs.
- **Voldaren Estate**: a must for Vampire decks.
