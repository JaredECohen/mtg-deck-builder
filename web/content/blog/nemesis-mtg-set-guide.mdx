Nemesis is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2000, the second set of the Masques block. It leaves Mercadia behind and returns to Rath, the Phyrexian-built plane from the Tempest block, where the vampire Crovax is being shaped into the new Evincar as Phyrexia prepares to invade Dominaria. Its headline new mechanic is fading, and its best-known cards are Daze, Parallax Wave and Blastoderm.

## The story

Nemesis is part of the Weatherlight Saga, the long storyline that runs from Weatherlight to Apocalypse. The previous set, Mercadian Masques, followed the crew of the skyship Weatherlight on the plane of Mercadia. Nemesis cuts away from them to Rath, the artificial plane Phyrexia built as a staging ground for its invasion of Dominaria. The tie-in novel is Nemesis by Paul B. Thompson.

Rath's old ruler, the Evincar Volrath, has abandoned his post to chase his own schemes in Mercadia. Phyrexia needs a replacement, and it chooses **Crovax**, a former Weatherlight crew member whose vampiric curse has consumed him. Phyrexian vat priests transform him into something far more powerful, the card **Ascendant Evincar**. To judge whether he's fit to rule, Phyrexia sends an emissary named **Belbe**, a new kind of Phyrexian built from the body of Avila, the murdered daughter of the elf rebel leader Eladamri.

Belbe is the heart of the story. She watches Crovax's cruelty firsthand and begins to doubt him, while Rath's rebels, led by Eladamri and the Kor warrior **Lin Sivvi**, keep fighting the Phyrexian regime. The young mage Ertai, captured and corrupted by Phyrexian machinery, ends up at Crovax's side. When Volrath returns to reclaim his throne, Crovax defeats him, with Ertai's help, and the old Evincar is executed. Belbe has no real choice but to confirm Crovax as Evincar.

The ending is ominous. Rath's flowstone production restarts, and the plane is prepared for the overlay, the moment when Rath will be laid over Dominaria and its armies poured out. That invasion is the subject of the next block, starting with Invasion later in 2000. Nemesis is the set where the Saga's villains finish their preparations.

## Mechanics

### Fading

Fading is Nemesis' new keyword. A permanent with fading N enters with N fade counters. At the beginning of your upkeep you remove one, and when you can't, you sacrifice it. Many fading cards also let you spend fade counters for an effect, which shortens their lifespan. **Blastoderm** is a four-mana 5/5 with shroud and fading 3, so it attacks for three turns and then disappears. **Parallax Wave** has fading 5 and spends its counters to exile creatures, returning them when it leaves. **Tangle Wire** forces each player to tap down their permanents for several turns. Fading gives you a lot of power in exchange for a clock.

### Rebels and Mercenaries

From Mercadian Masques, Rebels are white creatures that tap and pay mana to search your library for another Rebel and put it onto the battlefield. **Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero** is the strongest of them, fetching any Rebel for X mana. Mercenaries are the black version, such as **Rathi Assassin**. A single searcher could turn into an army over a few turns.

### Spellshapers

Spellshapers are creatures that discard a card to produce a spell-like effect. **Avenger en-Dal** exiles an attacking creature, and **Stronghold Biologist** counters a creature spell. They turn excess cards into repeatable effects, at a cost to card advantage.

### Free spells

Nemesis has a group of spells you can cast without paying their mana cost if you meet a condition. **Daze** lets you return an Island to your hand instead of paying, and **Massacre** is free if an opponent controls a Plains and you control a Swamp. **Submerge** and **Mogg Salvage** work the same way against Forests and Islands. Free spells are powerful because they let you act while tapped out. For help reading alternative costs, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

### Seals

The five Seals are cheap enchantments you sacrifice for a one-time effect, letting you "hold up" a spell without leaving mana open. **Seal of Fire** deals 2 damage, **Seal of Cleansing** destroys an artifact or enchantment and **Seal of Doom** destroys a nonblack creature.

## Limited

Nemesis was drafted with Mercadian Masques, one Nemesis pack alongside two Masques packs. Drafting in 2000 was well established at the Pro level, but the archetype language of today's formats didn't exist yet, and detailed records of the format are thin, so take any "best deck" claims with caution.

What we can say from the cards: Rebels were the strongest common engine in the Masques block, and Nemesis added cheap Rebels such as **Defiant Falcon** that could fetch more of them, making white a popular color. Spellshapers gave every color repeatable effects, fading creatures like **Cloudskate** were efficient but temporary, and removal such as **Seal of Doom** and **Topple** was valued highly. Games tended to be slower than modern Limited, because Masques-era creatures were less efficient. For modern drafting principles, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Nemesis joined Sixth Edition, the Urza's block and Mercadian Masques in a Standard that's often described as a lower-power era. Nemesis fed two of the best-remembered decks of its Standard life.

**Rebels** used Mercadian Masques' Ramosian Sergeant and Nemesis' Lin Sivvi, Defiant Hero to chain creature searches into a board that controlled the game. Later, after Invasion arrived in fall 2000, green-red **Fires** decks used that set's Fires of Yavimaya to give creatures haste, with Nemesis' Blastoderm and Saproling Burst among their best threats. A hasty Blastoderm on turn three was a famous problem, and Fires was one of the decks to beat in 2001. Parallax Wave also saw play for its ability to clear blockers for several turns or reset your own creatures. Nemesis left Standard with the rest of the Masques block when Odyssey was released in fall 2001.

Its lasting impact:

- **Legacy**: Daze is one of the format's defining tempo cards. Legacy blue decks use it alongside Brainstorm and Force of Will to punish opponents who tap out early. Tangle Wire has been a prison piece in artifact decks, and Seal of Fire appeared in older Burn lists. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains the format.
- **Vintage**: Tangle Wire is a classic in Mishra's Workshop prison decks.
- **Pauper**: Daze is banned in Pauper. Accumulated Knowledge, Blastoderm, Skyshroud Claim and the Seals are Pauper-legal.
- **Commander**: Kor Haven, Seal of Cleansing, Skyshroud Claim, Ascendant Evincar, Moggcatcher and Saproling Burst all have niches.
- **Modern**: Nemesis itself isn't Modern-legal, but several of its cards, like Seal of Fire and Blinding Angel, were reprinted later and are legal there.

## Cards from Nemesis worth owning

- **Daze**: a Legacy staple for blue tempo decks.
- **Tangle Wire**: a Vintage and Legacy prison piece.
- **Parallax Wave**: a flexible enchantment for combo and creature control.
- **Kor Haven**: a useful utility land for Commander control decks.
- **Seal of Cleansing** and **Seal of Fire**: cheap, flexible removal that also fits Pauper.
- **Skyshroud Claim**: strong green ramp for Commander.
- **Blastoderm**: a fun Pauper threat.

## The takeaway

Nemesis is a lower-power set from a lower-power era, but Daze and Tangle Wire keep it relevant in eternal formats, and its story is the last stop before the Phyrexian Invasion. If you're building today, start with Daze in a Legacy blue tempo deck.
