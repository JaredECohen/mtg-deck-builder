## Short answer

A combo deck is worth building when you can name the cards that win the game and you would rather lose to disruption than win a combat race. In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), a combo is a small set of cards that ends the game, or comes close, when they resolve together. Everything else in the deck finds those cards or keeps the opponent from stopping them. If you cannot point at the pile, you do not have a combo deck. You have a pile of cards that are good in the abstract.

Lotus Field Combo, the 2021 Pioneer Challenger product, is a named combo deck with a public list. Its goldfish record is the teaching tool: zero wins in 120 games. The simulator never put Lotus Field, a payoff, and the mana together as a plan. It attacked with whatever creatures existed and then the game ended. That is the right failure. A combo deck that goldfishes like burn is usually just burn. Use the zero as a check that you understand the deck does not win by combat, then practice the actual line, which this simulator will not play for you.

The money tells you what the combo costs as cardboard. The product total is $55.97 on its printings. The top three nonbasic names are 46.8% of that, led by Lotus Field itself. You are buying the land the deck is named for, plus two expensive spells, and a pile of cheaper pieces around them. If Lotus Field is the card you cannot afford, you cannot play this list's mana. A cheaper same-type spell from another official product is not a replacement for the land. The [control decision](/blog/should-you-play-control) is the other non-race plan, and it fails the goldfish for a different reason: answers, not a combo.

### Lotus Field Combo

Lotus Field Combo is an official Pioneer Challenger Deck released 2021-10-15. The decklist and the Wizards source link come from MTGJSON file LotusFieldCombo_Q06. Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/news/pioneer-challenger-decks-2021-08-24.

Read this as a deck whose win is a specific pile of cards, which a combat goldfish will not find.

The nonland mana curve is 8 at mana value 1, 15 at mana value 2, 4 at mana value 3, 1 at mana value 4, 4 at mana value 5, 4 at mana value 7 or more. The list runs 24 lands in the library.

Colored sources, using Scryfall produced_mana and counting basic lands as the color on their type line, restricted to this deck's colors: 14 blue, 4 black, 16 green.

MTGJSON AllPricesToday (2026-11-01) prices the exact printing in the product at $55.97 for main deck, sideboard, and commander, using TCGplayer nonfoil retail when it exists. The three most expensive nonbasic names are 4 Lotus Field at $10.76 total, 1 Approach of the Second Sun at $8.39 total, 2 Peer into the Abyss at $7.02 total, which is 46.8% of the priced total. Basic lands in the product add $1.50 on their own, because the price is the printing Wizards put in the deck, not the cheapest Plains on the market.

The Deck Lab goldfish played 120 solitaire games (seed 1729, combo policy, opponent at 20 life, 12 turns) and never won one. The simulator casts spells and turns creatures sideways. It does not assemble a named combo, storm off, or mill an opponent out. A zero win rate means this list does not win a goldfish race. It does not mean the deck cannot win a real match.

Budget benchmarks, not upgrade advice: the cheapest same-type card within one mana value in the other official lists we priced, still legal in the format. Those stand-ins do not do the same job. The gap is how much you are paying for the specific card, not a sleeve-in recommendation.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a 0% goldfish win rate as a broken deck. The metric does not assemble Lotus Field. It was not built to.
- Replacing Lotus Field with a cheaper land because a benchmark said a land can be cheap. The deck is named after the land. The mana is the combo.
- Adding creatures so the goldfish has something to attack with. You have turned a combo deck into a bad midrange deck.
- Forgetting the deck is several colors. The source count is not a mono-color basic count. The expensive lands are doing a job.
- Registering the 2021 product at a tournament without checking bans. The bulk file's legality is dated 2026-11-01.

## FAQ

### What is the combo in the Lotus Field product?

The deck is built to make large amounts of mana with Lotus Field and then cast a payoff that ends the game, including Approach of the Second Sun in the published list. Read the list in the deck block rather than a paraphrase of the line.

### Why can't the goldfish win with it?

The goldfish casts spells and attacks. It does not sequence a mana engine into a two-card kill. 0 wins in 120 games, seed 1729, is that limitation measured on this exact list.

### Is combo the same as control?

No. Control's zero comes from answering threats and never attacking. Combo's zero comes from winning with a pile the attacker-simulator does not play. The decks feel different in a real game: one says no, the other tries to go off.

### How much of the price is Lotus Field?

It is the top line item on the product printing. Together with the next two nonbasic names it is 46.8% of the $55.97 total. The rest of the deck is the cheaper shell around that core.
