Zendikar is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2009 that introduced the plane of Zendikar, a wild "adventure world" of floating ruins, deadly terrain and treasure-hunting expeditions. Its headline mechanic, landfall, made playing a land each turn feel like casting a spell, and the set also brought back the enemy-colored fetch lands, which remain some of the most important lands in Magic.

## The story

Zendikar is a plane that fights back. Its land is alive with mana, and a phenomenon called the **Roil** constantly reshapes it: rocks float, rivers change course, and whole regions can shift overnight. Scattered across the world are **hedrons**, huge carved stones that hang in the air, and ancient ruins full of treasure. The kor are nomadic climbers, the merfolk keep the old lore, the elves are split into rival tribes, and the vampires of Malakir rule much of the plane's dark interior.

The hedrons are not decoration. Six thousand years earlier, three planeswalkers, the vampire Sorin Markov, the kor stoneforger Nahiri and the spirit dragon Ugin, trapped three colossal beings called the **Eldrazi** on Zendikar. The hedrons are the bars of that prison, and the Roil is partly the plane's reaction to what is imprisoned inside it. Most of Zendikar's people know nothing about this; they only know that the land is dangerous and that the ruins hold power.

The Zendikar set itself is mostly about exploration and setup. Its cards show expeditions, ruins, traps and quests rather than a single war. The plot comes together across the block. The novel *Zendikar: In the Teeth of Akoum* by Robert B. Wintermute follows the elf planeswalker **Nissa Revane**, who joins Sorin and the vampire scholar Anowon on a journey to the Eye of Ugin, the hidden chamber at the heart of the prison. In the block's web fiction, Chandra Nalaar, Jace Beleren and Sarkhan Vol also converge on the Eye. The result is the same in every telling: the seal breaks, and the Eldrazi titans Ulamog, Kozilek and Emrakul are released in the next large set, [Rise of the Eldrazi](/blog/rise-of-the-eldrazi-mtg-set-guide). Zendikar's story, and its later return in Battle for Zendikar, grows out of that moment.

## Mechanics

### Landfall

Landfall abilities trigger whenever a land enters the battlefield under your control. **Steppe Lynx** is a 0/1 for `{W}` that gets +2/+2 for each land you play that turn, and **Plated Geopede** is a first striker with the same bonus. Fetch lands count twice, once for the fetch and once for the land it finds, which made the set's own fetch lands perfect partners. **Lotus Cobra** turns each land drop into an extra mana of any color, and **Bloodghast** returns from the graveyard to the battlefield whenever you play a land.

### Quests

Quests are cheap enchantments that collect quest counters until they pay off. **Beastmaster Ascension** gets a counter whenever one of your creatures attacks, and with seven counters it gives your creatures +5/+5. **Quest for the Holy Relic** gains counters as you cast creature spells and can be cashed in to put an Equipment onto the battlefield attached to a creature.

### Traps

Traps are instants with an alternate cost of `{0}` (or less) when an opponent has done something specific. **Mindbreak Trap** becomes free if an opponent cast three or more spells in a turn and exiles any number of spells, and **Archive Trap** mills an opponent thirteen cards for free if they searched their library that turn. **Summoning Trap** is free if an opponent's spell or ability countered your creature spell.

### Allies

Allies are a creature type with abilities that trigger whenever another Ally enters under your control. **Kazandu Blademaster** grows with a +1/+1 counter each time another Ally arrives, and **Umara Raptor** does the same in blue. Allies were drafted across all five colors and rewarded committing to the type.

### Kicker

Kicker returned from the Invasion block: you can pay an extra cost when casting a spell for a bonus effect. **Goblin Bushwhacker** is a one-drop that, if kicked, gives all your creatures +1/+0 and haste for the turn. For a full rundown of keywords, see the [MTG keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary).

### Full-art basic lands

Zendikar printed full-art basic lands in regular packs, showing off the plane's scenery. They became so popular that full-art basics have returned many times since.

By color, the set's strengths break down like this:

- **White** had Day of Judgment, the Emeria Angel landfall flier, Felidar Sovereign (which wins the game at 40 life) and efficient commons like Kor Skyfisher and Journey to Nowhere.
- **Blue** had the traps, Sphinx of Jwar Isle, Spreading Seas, Hedron Crab and the tempo spell Into the Roil.
- **Black** was the vampire color, with Vampire Nighthawk, Malakir Bloodwitch, Bloodghast, Vampire Hexmage, Sorin Markov and Ob Nixilis, the Fallen, a landfall demon.
- **Red** had Goblin Guide, Goblin Bushwhacker, Plated Geopede, Burst Lightning, Hellkite Charger and the planeswalker Chandra Ablaze.
- **Green** had Lotus Cobra, Rampaging Baloths, Oracle of Mul Daya, Harrow, Khalni Heart Expedition, Beastmaster Ascension and Nissa Revane.

## Limited

Zendikar draft is remembered as one of the fastest Limited formats Magic has had. Landfall creatures like Steppe Lynx and Plated Geopede could deal a lot of damage in the first few turns, kicked Goblin Bushwhacker could end games out of nowhere, and many decks wanted to curve out and attack rather than block. Players had to value cheap removal and early defense highly, because a slow start was often fatal.

Cheap removal was the key resource. Journey to Nowhere, Burst Lightning and Disfigure were premium commons, and Kor Skyfisher was one of the best white commons. Aggressive decks often paired white or red with landfall creatures, black vampires attacked early with cards like Vampire Lacerator, and Allies rewarded drafters who collected them across colors. The format was mostly two-color, and there were no set gold signpost cards for each pair, so archetypes came from drafters' preferences rather than from the design.

Zendikar was drafted as triple Zendikar at first, then with Worldwake added in early 2010. If you're new to drafting, see [how to evaluate cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

**Standard (2009-2011).** Zendikar arrived in a Standard dominated by Jund, the black-red-green midrange deck from Shards of Alara block built around cards like Bloodbraid Elf. Zendikar's fetch lands, especially Verdant Catacombs, improved Jund's mana, and Jund stayed the deck to beat through much of its first year. The first real answer was a green-white-blue deck usually called Mythic, which used Lotus Cobra to ramp into powerful threats and later added Eldrazi Conscription from Rise of the Eldrazi. Landfall aggro decks built around Steppe Lynx and Plated Geopede and blue-white control with Day of Judgment also had their seasons. After Magic 2011 added Primeval Titan, Valakut Ramp, which used Zendikar's Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle and plenty of Mountains, became a major deck. No Zendikar cards were banned in Standard.

**Modern.** The five fetch lands are format pillars. Goblin Guide is a Burn staple. Valakut powers Scapeshift and Titan-based ramp decks. Hedron Crab sees play in mill, Spreading Seas in Merfolk, Expedition Map in land-based combo decks, and Bloodghast in graveyard decks. Punishing Fire is banned in Modern; its combination with Future Sight's Grove of the Burnwillows was a repeatable damage engine.

**Legacy and Vintage.** The fetch lands are everywhere. Goblin Guide shows up in Burn, Vampire Hexmage combos with Dark Depths, Iona, Shield of Emeria is a target for cheat decks, and Bloodghast feeds Dredge-style graveyard strategies.

**Commander.** Lotus Cobra, Oracle of Mul Daya, Rampaging Baloths, Eldrazi Monument and Beastmaster Ascension are popular, and landfall commanders keep Zendikar's cards relevant. Iona, Shield of Emeria is banned in Commander.

**Pauper.** Kor Skyfisher, Journey to Nowhere, Burst Lightning and Khalni Heart Expedition have all been played in Pauper.

## Cards from Zendikar worth owning

- **Arid Mesa, Marsh Flats, Misty Rainforest, Scalding Tarn, Verdant Catacombs**: the enemy fetch lands are core mana-base cards in Modern, Legacy and Commander.
- **Goblin Guide**: a long-running Burn staple.
- **Valakut, the Molten Pinnacle**: the engine behind Modern Scapeshift.
- **Bloodghast**: a recurring threat for graveyard decks.
- **Lotus Cobra** and **Oracle of Mul Daya**: Commander landfall staples.
- **Spreading Seas** and **Expedition Map**: cheap commons with lasting Modern roles.

## The takeaway

Zendikar is the set that made lands exciting. Landfall, the fetch lands and the Eldrazi prison all came from it, and its fetches alone keep it relevant in every nonrotating format. If you want to play its cards today, Modern is the place: a landfall or Burn shell built from Zendikar's best cards is easy to start.
