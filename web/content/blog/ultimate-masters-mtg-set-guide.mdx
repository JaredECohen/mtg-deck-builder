Ultimate Masters is the Magic: The Gathering reprint set released in December 2018, billed as the final, all-out celebration of Magic's first 25 years. Built around a graveyard theme, it packed Liliana of the Veil, Tarmogoyf, Snapcaster Mage, Karn Liberated, Cavern of Souls and the five allied manlands into one draftable set, and it is still remembered as one of the strongest Masters products ever printed.

## What the set was for

Masters sets exist to put expensive, hard-to-find older cards back into circulation while giving players a draft format with a high power level. Ultimate Masters was the last in a run that began with Modern Masters in 2013, and Wizards said at the time it would be the final Masters set "for the foreseeable future." That didn't hold: Double Masters arrived in 2020.

The design team, co-led by Adam Prosak and Jules Robins, started from a graveyard theme. Its working name was reportedly "Immortal Masters," and the skulls on the set symbol (two skulls forming an infinity sign) are a leftover of that idea. Every color has cards that use or fill the graveyard, so a typical draft deck looks back at its own discard pile for value.

The set was sold only through local game stores in booster boxes, with three-pack blisters at big retailers. Wizards said supplies would be limited.

### The Ultimate Box Toppers

Each booster box included one extra card on top: a foil "Ultimate Box Topper" with semi-borderless art that stretches across most of the frame. There were 40 of them, all versions of cards that also appear in the main set, from Liliana of the Veil and Karn Liberated to Cavern of Souls and the manlands. They arrived about a year before Collector Boosters, introduced with Throne of Eldraine in 2019, made special frames and alternate art a routine part of every release. On Magic Online the toppers came out of Treasure Chests instead.

Every pack also contained a foil of any rarity in place of a basic land, since the set had no basic lands at all. That made opening a pack feel closer to opening two.

## The reprints that mattered

Ultimate Masters had very few "filler" rares. Here are the reprints people opened packs for, grouped by where they mattered.

### Modern staples

- **Liliana of the Veil**: the three-mana planeswalker that makes each player discard, plus a removal ability. It was the backbone of Jund and other black midrange decks.
- **Tarmogoyf**: the two-mana green creature whose size counts card types in all graveyards.
- **Snapcaster Mage**: a flash 2/1 that gives an instant or sorcery in your graveyard flashback.
- **Karn Liberated**: the seven-mana planeswalker at the top of Tron decks.
- **Cavern of Souls**: the land that makes creature spells of a chosen type uncounterable, key for tribal decks like Humans.
- **Noble Hierarch**: the exalted one-mana mana creature.
- **The manlands**: Celestial Colonnade, Creeping Tar Pit, Lavaclaw Reaches, Raging Ravine and Stirring Wildwood, each a dual land that can turn into a creature.
- **Engineered Explosives, Fulminator Mage and Back to Basics** for sideboards. Our [sideboarding guide](/blog/sideboarding-101) covers how cards like these get used.

### Legacy and Vintage cards

- **Reanimate, Entomb and Through the Breach** for reanimator and combo decks.
- **Dark Depths and Thespian's Stage**, the two-land combo that makes a 20/20 Marit Lage.
- **Leovold, Emissary of Trest**, a Legacy card that stops opponents from drawing more than one card a turn.
- **Karakas**, the legendary land that bounces legendary creatures, legal in Legacy and Vintage.

### Commander favorites

- **Mana Vault, Ancient Tomb and Demonic Tutor**: fast mana and the best tutor ever printed.
- **Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth, Phyrexian Altar and Phyrexian Tower** for combo and sacrifice decks.
- **The Eldrazi titans**: Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Kozilek, Butcher of Truth and Ulamog, the Infinite Gyre.
- **Eternal Witness, Kodama's Reach and Rogue's Passage** at lower rarities.

Several cards changed rarity to suit draft. Dark Depths, Mana Vault and Temporal Manipulation moved up to mythic, while Demonic Tutor, Reanimate and Ancient Tomb went from uncommon to rare.

## Limited

Ultimate Masters was designed to be drafted, and it was one of the more complex Masters formats. Instead of ten tidy guild archetypes, it was built around themes that cross colors, so reading what's flowing matters a lot (see our [signals guide](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals)).

The main themes:

- **Self-mill and graveyard value (green, blue, black).** Satyr Wayfinder, Sultai Skullkeeper, Crow of Dark Tidings and Deranged Assistant fill the graveyard. Payoffs include Spider Spawning, Rise from the Tides, Boneyard Wurm, Gurmag Angler and Hooting Mandrills. Laboratory Maniac sat here as a joke-turned-real win condition.
- **Heroic and Auras (white, with green or red).** Hero of Iroas makes Auras cheaper, Phalanx Leader and Wingsteed Rider grow off targeting spells, and Umbras like Hyena Umbra and Mammoth Umbra protect your investment from removal.
- **Madness and discard (red and black).** Mad Prophet and Wild Mongrel let you discard on purpose, and cards like Fiery Temper, Reckless Wurm and Twins of Maurer Estate reward it with a cheaper cast.
- **Reanimation (black, white).** Resurrection, Unburial Rites and Miraculous Recovery bring back big creatures like Walker of the Grove or Penumbra Wurm.
- **Spells and flashback (blue, red).** Young Pyromancer, Thermo-Alchemist, Think Twice and Faithless Looting fuel a deck that counts instants and sorceries.

At common and uncommon, removal was unusually rich: Last Gasp, Chainer's Edict, Fire // Ice, Slum Reaper, Unholy Hunger, Faith's Fetters, Murderous Redcap and Shriekmaw all showed up regularly. Because so much of the set recurs, games tended to run long and the best decks had card advantage built in. It's a good format to practice drafting by theme rather than by color pair.

A few practical habits helped. Enablers were plentiful, but payoffs weren't, so a second Spider Spawning or Rise from the Tides was worth taking over a slightly better creature. Persist creatures like Kitchen Finks, Safehold Elite and Scuzzback Marauders were good in every graveyard deck because they come back once on their own. And the set's flashback spells meant a two-for-one was rarely really a two-for-one, so counting what's in each graveyard before combat paid off.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Ultimate Masters added no new cards, so its effect on Constructed was about supply. Putting Liliana, Tarmogoyf, Snapcaster and the manlands into packs made established Modern decks easier to build in early 2019.

A few of its cards sit on ban lists, and their stories happened around the set:

- **Bridge from Below** was banned in Modern on July 8, 2019, to slow the Hogaak graveyard deck. Hogaak itself and **Faithless Looting** followed on August 26, 2019. Faithless Looting was unbanned in Modern in the December 16, 2024 announcement.
- **Dig Through Time, Treasure Cruise, Golgari Grave-Troll and Dark Depths** are banned in Modern as of October 2026, all from earlier decisions.
- **Entomb** was banned in Legacy on November 10, 2025, joining Demonic Tutor, Mana Vault, Frantic Search, Dig Through Time and Treasure Cruise on the Legacy list.
- In Commander, **Emrakul, the Aeons Torn, Leovold, Emissary of Trest and Karakas** are banned, and **Demonic Tutor, Ancient Tomb, Gamble and Mana Vault** are on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026.

For context on how these decisions work, see our [ban list explainer](/blog/mtg-ban-list-explained) and the [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide).

## Cards from Ultimate Masters worth owning

- **Liliana of the Veil**: still a Modern and Legacy staple in black decks.
- **Snapcaster Mage**: fits any deck built on cheap instants and sorceries.
- **Cavern of Souls**: playable in nearly every tribal deck, in every format.
- **The five manlands**: dual lands that win games on their own.
- **Noble Hierarch**: the best green one-drop for aggressive Modern decks.
- **Demonic Tutor and Ancient Tomb**: core Commander pieces at higher brackets.
- **Eternal Witness and Kodama's Reach**: cheap, useful Commander cards from the lower slots.

## The takeaway

If you see Ultimate Masters cards for trade, you're mostly looking at format staples with an infinity-skull symbol: Liliana, Tarmogoyf, Snapcaster and the manlands are the first to grab.
