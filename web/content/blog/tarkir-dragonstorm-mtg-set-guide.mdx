Tarkir: Dragonstorm is the April 2025 expansion that returned Magic: The Gathering to Tarkir, a plane where five warrior clans now live alongside dragons born from violent storms. It's a three-color "wedge" set, with one mechanic for each clan, and it produced one of the most dominant Standard cards of the decade in Cori-Steel Cutter.

## The story

Tarkir has had a complicated history. In its first visit, the plane was ruled by five clans after the dragons died out. Then Sarkhan Vol traveled back in time, saved the dragons' progenitor, and created an alternate timeline where five Elder Dragons ruled the clans as Dragonlords. Tarkir: Dragonstorm picks up long after that.

The clans eventually rose up and overthrew the Dragonlords. To do it, they performed a great ritual, the Stormnexus, which gave birth to five Spirit Dragons, one bonded to each clan: Betor for the Abzan, Shiko for the Jeskai, Teval for the Sultai, Neriv for the Mardu, and Ureni for the Temur. The clans are restored to their old names and identities, and they live in an uneasy partnership with dragons rather than under them.

The ritual had a side effect. Tarkir now suffers regular dragonstorms that spawn wild dragons nobody controls. That became a multiversal problem once the Omenpaths, the planar portals that opened after the Phyrexian invasion, let those dragons fly out to other worlds. Storm-born dragons had already turned up on Bloomburrow and in Duskmourn, so heroes from across the multiverse came to Tarkir looking for the source.

Elspeth leads the investigation. Narset, now khan of the Jeskai, is reluctant to help at first, as is Felothar of the Abzan, but both eventually commit their clans, with Ajani rallying the Abzan. The real threat turns out to be Sarkhan himself. He has lost his planeswalker spark, longs to be a dragon again, and is manipulated (in part by the Jeskai monk Taigam) into a ritual that turns him into a colossal dragon bent on spreading dragons through the multiverse. The heroes fight him, and Ugin, the Spirit Dragon, returns to defeat Sarkhan with his ghostfire. Ugin explains that the storms grew wild partly because of his long absence, and he promises to bring them under control.

The ending isn't a clean win. In a parallel thread, Jace's attempt to reshape the multiverse ends in disaster, collapsing the Meditation Realm where Nicol Bolas had been imprisoned. Ugin reveals that Bolas is free, setting up the next stretch of Magic's story.

## Mechanics

### Endure (Abzan)

When a creature "endures N," you choose: put N +1/+1 counters on it, or create an N/N white Spirit token. Anafenza, Unyielding Lineage endures 2 whenever another nontoken creature you control dies. The flexibility is the point: pump a creature that's already safe, or make a separate body when you're worried about removal.

### Flurry (Jeskai)

Flurry triggers when you cast your second spell in a turn. Cori-Steel Cutter, the set's breakout card, makes a 1/1 Monk with prowess on each flurry trigger and can attach itself to the new token. Flurry rewards cheap spells and turns that chain two spells together.

### Renew (Sultai)

Renew is an ability you activate from your graveyard by exiling the card, usually to put counters on a creature. Qarsi Revenant is a flying, deathtouch, lifelink creature that, once it's in the graveyard, can be exiled to give another creature all three keywords as counters. Renew makes creatures valuable twice: on the battlefield and in the graveyard.

### Mobilize (Mardu)

Whenever a mobilize creature attacks, it creates that many tapped and attacking 1/1 red Warrior tokens, which are sacrificed at the end of the turn. Voice of Victory has mobilize 2 and stops opponents from casting spells during your turn. Mobilize adds pressure without overextending into sweepers.

### Harmonize (Temur)

Harmonize lets you cast a spell again from your graveyard for its harmonize cost, then exile it. While casting, you can tap one of your creatures to reduce the cost by that creature's power. Nature's Rhythm, which puts a creature from your library onto the battlefield, has harmonize, as does the card-draw spell Winternight Stories.

### Omens and behold

Omen cards are dragons with a second, cheaper spell attached, a bit like an Adventure. When you cast the Omen half, you shuffle the card back into your library rather than exiling it. Bloomvine Regent's Omen, Claim Territory, fetches two Forests early, and you might draw the dragon later. Behold appears on dragon-matters cards: to behold a Dragon, you either point to one you control or reveal one from your hand. Molten Exhale normally deals 4 damage at sorcery speed but gains flash if you behold a Dragon.

The set also brings back storm on Stormscale Scion and includes a cycle of Siege enchantments that make you choose between two clans as they enter.

## Limited

Tarkir: Dragonstorm was a wedge format. The five clans each had a three-color plan:

- **Abzan (white-black-green):** midrange creatures and +1/+1 counters with endure.
- **Jeskai (blue-red-white):** flurry tempo, from aggressive Monk decks to spell-heavy control.
- **Sultai (black-green-blue):** graveyard value and renew, usually from a green base.
- **Mardu (red-white-black):** mobilize aggro, often played as just red-white or black-white.
- **Temur (green-blue-red):** big creatures and harmonize.

Each two-color pair belongs to two clans, so the usual advice was to settle into two main colors and splash the third, taking dual lands and mana fixers higher than in a typical set. Aggressive two-color decks, especially red-white, punished opponents with greedy mana. Experienced drafters also favored a five-color "dragons and bombs" deck when the fixing flowed.

The format was slow overall. Removal was cheap and plentiful, and many creatures had high toughness, so games went long and card quality mattered. The Exhale removal spells, Snakeskin Veil, Ainok Wayfarer and the Omen dragons like Twinmaw Stormbrood were some of the commons and uncommons drafters valued most. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard

Tarkir: Dragonstorm's Standard story is mostly Cori-Steel Cutter. In the first weekend after release, Izzet Prowess built around the Cutter tied for the most successful deck in Wizards' metagame review. By May 2025, Izzet Prowess was more than 30% of the field at Regional Championships. The card was hard to answer: removing the Monk tokens didn't stop the Equipment from making more.

Wizards banned Cori-Steel Cutter in Standard on **June 30, 2025**, as part of a seven-card update that also removed Abuelo's Awakening, Monstrous Rage, Heartfire Hero, Up the Beanstalk, Hopeless Nightmare and This Town Ain't Big Enough. Wizards described the Cutter as slightly stronger than everything else in the format.

Other cards from the set have had longer, quieter careers. Elspeth, Storm Slayer anchors token decks. Voice of Victory has been a good fit for aggressive white decks that want to attack without worrying about instant-speed tricks. Nature's Rhythm gave its name to "Rhythm" creature decks that were still showing up in Standard events in 2026.

### Pioneer, Modern and Commander

Cori-Steel Cutter later dominated Izzet decks in Pioneer, and Wizards banned it there on **May 18, 2026**. It remains legal in Modern, where it was the most-played new card from the set and slotted into prowess decks. Ugin, Eye of the Storms found a Modern home in colorless ramp shells like Tron and Eldrazi Ramp, where its cast trigger and colorless-spell triggers clear colored threats. Voice of Victory and Clarion Conqueror saw Modern play as well.

In Commander, the set's three-color legends and Spirit Dragons all lead their clans' themes, Elspeth, Storm Slayer is a strong token doubler, and the Craterhoof Behemoth reprint made a classic finisher easier to find. If you want a refresher on the format, see [what Commander is](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

## Cards from Tarkir: Dragonstorm worth owning

- **Ugin, Eye of the Storms:** a Modern staple for colorless ramp and a strong Commander card.
- **Cori-Steel Cutter:** banned in Standard and Pioneer, but still a strong option in Modern prowess.
- **Elspeth, Storm Slayer:** a token doubler and threat in Standard and Commander.
- **Voice of Victory:** a two-drop that keeps opponents from interacting on your turn.
- **Craterhoof Behemoth:** one of Commander's most famous finishers.
- **Nature's Rhythm:** a reusable creature tutor with a Standard home.
- **Snakeskin Veil:** a cheap protection spell that's useful in every green deck.

Tarkir: Dragonstorm rewards players who like multicolor decks. In Standard, its best surviving cards are Elspeth, Storm Slayer, Voice of Victory and Nature's Rhythm. In Limited, prioritize removal and fixing, and pick your two main colors before your splash.
