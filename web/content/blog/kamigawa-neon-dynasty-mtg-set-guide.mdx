Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in February 2022 that brought players back to Kamigawa more than a thousand years after the original Kami War. The plane had become a neon-lit, cyberpunk version of itself, with mech suits, hacker ninjas and street gangs living alongside shrines and ancient spirits. The headline was that clash between tradition and modernity, plus a stack of cards that shaped Standard, Pioneer and Modern for years: The Wandering Emperor, Fable of the Mirror-Breaker and the five channel lands.

Neon Dynasty was one of the sets that got an extra year in Standard when Wizards moved to a three-year rotation, so it stayed through the 2023 rotation and left with Innistrad: Midnight Hunt, Crimson Vow and Streets of New Capenna when Bloomburrow arrived.

## The story

Twelve centuries after the war between the mortal and spirit worlds, Kamigawa is a plane of contrasts. The capital, Towashi, is a vertical city of holograms and hover-bikes run by the Futurists of the Saiba Moonfolk. Out in the countryside, the Jukai Forest and the Asari monks keep the old ways. In between sit the Imperials of Eiganjo, the samurai who serve a young Emperor, and the Uprisers, a rebel gang of akki and humans led by the outlaw Risona.

The problem at the center of the plot is that the Emperor has been missing for ten years. Kaito Shizuki, a ninja and planeswalker raised alongside the Emperor as her guardian, has spent that decade searching the Multiverse for her. She turns out to be the planeswalker players first met as the Wanderer in War of the Spark: her spark ignited years earlier, and she has been flung between planes ever since, unable to stay anywhere for long. Her bond with the great kami Kyodai is what ties her to home.

Kaito's search pulls in two other planeswalkers. Tamiyo, the Moonfolk storyteller, is on Kamigawa researching the strange disturbance in the planar fabric. Tezzeret, the artificer, has come to steal from Kamigawa's technology, and he has brought a passenger: Jin-Gitaxias, the Phyrexian praetor of progress, who wants to study planeswalker sparks. A device called the Reality Chip becomes the key to everything. Fused to the Emperor's hand, it lets her stay anchored on Kamigawa, and Tezzeret wants it for his own reasons.

The climax comes during a raid on Tezzeret's compound and a running fight across Eiganjo. Kaito and the Emperor beat back Risona's Uprisers, and Kaito wounds Tezzeret. But Tezzeret uses the Reality Chip to tear open a portal and escapes with Tamiyo, and Jin-Gitaxias's half-destroyed body vanishes with them. The Emperor, her spark failing without the chip, realizes she cannot stay. With Kyodai's blessing she names the fox Light-Paws regent, then planeswalks away. Kaito sets out to find both her and Tamiyo.

The epilogue is the hook for everything that followed. Tamiyo wakes in a Phyrexian laboratory, compleated by Jin-Gitaxias and loyal to Phyrexia. That is why the set has Tamiyo, Compleated Sage, the first compleated planeswalker card, and why the story runs straight into Dominaria United and Phyrexia: All Will Be One.

## Mechanics

### Ninjutsu

Ninjutsu returns from the original Kamigawa block. You pay the ninjutsu cost and return one of your unblocked attackers to your hand, and the Ninja enters the battlefield tapped and attacking. The trick works best with cheap evasive creatures and Ninjas that trigger on combat damage. Moon-Circuit Hacker, a two-mana common, can come in for just one blue mana and draws you a card when it connects.

### Channel

Channel cards let you discard them from your hand for a one-time effect. In Neon Dynasty, channel lives on lands, enchantments and artifacts instead of Spirits. The rare cycle of legendary lands is the best example: Boseiju, Who Endures is a Forest-like land that you can instead discard to destroy an opponent's artifact, enchantment or nonbasic land. Because the land counts as a land in your deck, channel effects are close to free.

### Modified

Modified is a shorthand term rather than a keyword: a creature is modified if it has a counter on it, is equipped, or is enchanted by an Aura you control. Lots of red, green and black cards ask about it. Kami's Flare deals 3 damage to a creature or planeswalker, plus 2 to its controller if you control a modified creature.

### Reconfigure

Reconfigure creatures are Equipment that can also act as creatures. You can pay the reconfigure cost to attach them to another creature, where they stop being creatures, or pay it again to unattach them. Lion Sash is a two-mana white example that eats cards from graveyards to grow, then passes its bonus to a bigger body.

### Saga creatures and Vehicles

Many double-faced cards in the set are Sagas on the front and enchantment creatures on the back. Kumano Faces Kakkazan pings each opponent, makes your next creature bigger, then flips into a 2/2 haste creature. Vehicles also return as the mechs of the modern side of the plane; Reckoner Bankbuster is the one players remember. If you are new to cards with two faces, our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) covers the rules.

## Limited

Neon Dynasty drafted around ten two-color archetypes, each with a gold signpost uncommon.

| Pair | Theme | Signpost uncommon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White-Blue | Vehicles | Prodigy's Prototype |
| Blue-Black | Ninjas and Rogues | Silver-Fur Master |
| Black-Red | Artifact sacrifice | Oni-Cult Anvil |
| Red-Green | Modified creatures | Invigorating Hot Spring |
| Green-White | Enchantments | Jukai Naturalist |
| White-Black | Artifacts and enchantments together | Naomi, Pillar of Order |
| Blue-Red | Artifacts | Enthusiastic Mechanaut |
| Black-Green | Graveyard recursion | Gloomshrieker |
| Red-White | Samurai and Warriors attacking alone | Asari Captain |
| Green-Blue | Channel ramp | Colossal Skyturtle |

The big structural idea was that the "modern" side of the set (blue, red and parts of black) leaned on artifacts, while the "traditional" side (white, green and parts of black) leaned on enchantments. Many cards counted one or the other, so reading which type your colors cared about mattered more than usual.

At common, the removal you wanted was Kami's Flare in red, Lethal Exploit and Debt to the Kami in black, and Mothrider Patrol as a cheap white tapper. Ninjutsu decks punished slow starts: an unblocked one-drop on turn two could turn into a Ninja that draws a card on turn three. The modified decks wanted cheap Equipment and Auras, so commons like Ninja's Kunai picked up value they would not have in other sets. If you are new to drafting, our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains how to rank removal, creatures and tricks.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

In its first months in Standard, The Wandering Emperor became a centerpiece of white control and midrange decks. Flash lets her ambush an attacker on the opponent's turn and then threaten a fresh Samurai every turn after, which made her very hard to play around. Kumano Faces Kakkazan slotted into red aggro, and Kaito Shizuki gave blue-black decks a sticky card-advantage engine.

The longer story is about three cards. Fable of the Mirror-Breaker, Reckoner Bankbuster and Invoke Despair were core pieces of the black-red and Grixis midrange decks that dominated Standard in late 2022 and early 2023. Bankbuster in particular showed up across almost every archetype because colorless card draw fits anywhere. Because Neon Dynasty had been given an extra year in Standard, Wizards banned all three in Standard on May 29, 2023, to keep the coming season from being more of the same. They remained legal everywhere else.

In Pioneer, the set created a new archetype. Greasefang, Okiba Boss returns a Vehicle from your graveyard every combat, so Abzan Greasefang decks fill the graveyard with self-mill creatures and reanimate a big Vehicle such as Parhelion II as early as turn three. Fable of the Mirror-Breaker became a pillar of Rakdos midrange there too, and The Wandering Emperor appears in Azorius and Orzhov lists.

In Modern and Legacy, the channel lands were the quiet winners. Boseiju, Who Endures and Otawara, Soaring City replace basic lands in many green and blue decks because they give you an extra spell for free, and Fable shows up in midrange lists in both formats. As of October 2026, none of these cards are banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy.

In Commander, Neon Dynasty supplied popular legends: Kodama of the West Tree for counters decks, Satoru Umezawa for Ninja decks that sneak big creatures into play, Jin-Gitaxias, Progress Tyrant for spellslingers, and Isshin, Two Heavens as One for attack-trigger decks. The set also came with two Commander precons.

## Cards from Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty worth owning

- **The Wandering Emperor**: a flexible planeswalker that still anchors white Pioneer decks.
- **Fable of the Mirror-Breaker**: one of the strongest three-mana cards of its era, legal in Pioneer, Modern and Legacy.
- **Boseiju, Who Endures**: a green land that doubles as removal in nearly every format.
- **Otawara, Soaring City**: the blue equivalent, popular in Legacy and Commander.
- **Greasefang, Okiba Boss**: the engine of a Pioneer combo deck.
- **Kodama of the West Tree** and **Satoru Umezawa**: two strong Commander leaders.

## The takeaway

Neon Dynasty is a set where the flashy mechs were fun, but the cards that lasted were flexible ones: a flash planeswalker, a value Saga and lands that turn into spells. If you're building a Pioneer deck with it, start by deciding whether you want the Fable midrange plan or the Greasefang graveyard plan, since they want very different supporting cards.
