Magic 2014, usually called M14, is the Magic: The Gathering core set released in July 2013, in the game's 20th anniversary year. It spotlighted Chandra Nalaar, brought back Slivers with a cleaner modern design, and arrived alongside a set of rules changes that still shape how the game works. It is also one of the oldest sets legal in Pioneer, and it supplied long-lived cards like Mutavault, Scavenging Ooze, Elvish Mystic and Young Pyromancer.

## What the set was for

Core sets of this era had two jobs. They were the first product a new player was likely to pick up, so they were full of clean, readable cards and classic reprints like Serra Angel and Sengir Vampire. They also had to feed Standard, which in 2013 meant sitting next to Return to Ravnica block and then Theros block. Magic 2014 did both, and it leaned on the digital game Duels of the Planeswalkers 2014 as a way to bring new players in.

There is no big plot in Magic 2014, but there is a face. Chandra Nalaar, the fire-wielding planeswalker, is the set's spotlight character. She got a new planeswalker card aimed at Constructed, Chandra, Pyromaster, and a handful of support cards like Chandra's Outrage and Chandra's Phoenix. The other planeswalkers were Ajani, Caller of the Pride, Jace, Memory Adept, Liliana of the Dark Realms and Garruk, Caller of Beasts, one for each color.

The set's logo was printed in the mythic orange-bronze color instead of the usual style, a small nod to the 20th anniversary.

### The rules changes

Magic 2014 launched with several rules updates, and two of them matter to every game played today.

- **The legend rule changed.** Before M14, if two players each controlled a legendary permanent with the same name, both went to the graveyard. After M14, the rule only applies to one player at a time: if you control two legendary permanents with the same name, you choose one to keep. Your opponent's copy no longer affects yours. The same change applied to planeswalkers of the same type.
- **Sideboards became flexible.** Before, a Constructed sideboard had to be exactly 15 cards or none at all, and swaps had to be one for one. After M14, a sideboard could be up to 15 cards, and you no longer had to swap exactly one card for one card as long as your deck still met the minimum size.

Indestructible also became an official keyword, and "unblockable" was reworded to "can't be blocked." Our [keywords glossary](/blog/mtg-keywords-glossary) covers indestructible and the other evergreen keywords.

## Mechanics

Magic 2014 didn't introduce new keywords. Its signature mechanic was a returning creature type, and the rest of the set used familiar keywords like flying, lifelink, deathtouch and first strike.

### Slivers

Slivers are creatures that share their abilities with each other. In the original versions, a Sliver's ability applied to all Slivers on the battlefield, including your opponent's. Magic 2014 redesigned them so each Sliver only grants its ability to "Sliver creatures you control." That one change made Slivers much safer to build around.

The effect stacks: Predatory Sliver gives your Slivers +1/+1, Galerider Sliver gives them flying, Syphon Sliver gives them lifelink and Bonescythe Sliver gives them double strike. Put a few on the battlefield and every one of them becomes a much bigger threat. Megantic Sliver, which gives +3/+3, is the set's top-end payoff, and Manaweft Sliver turns every Sliver into a mana source of any color.

### Lifegain and counters

Several of the set's rares rewarded gaining life. Archangel of Thune puts a +1/+1 counter on each of your creatures whenever you gain life, and since it has lifelink, it triggers itself when it deals damage. Angelic Accord makes a 4/4 Angel at each end step in which you gained 4 or more life. Sanguine Bond turns lifegain into damage to an opponent.

## Limited

Magic 2014's draft format was a classic core set: straightforward creatures, efficient removal and a few bombs. The Slivers gave it a unique twist, because they appear in all five colors at common and uncommon, and a deck with several of them can snowball quickly.

Common removal was solid. White had Pacifism and Celestial Flare, blue had Claustrophobia, black had Quag Sickness and Wring Flesh, and red had Shock and Chandra's Outrage. Doom Blade at uncommon was a top pick. Green leaned on big bodies and Deadly Recluse, a 1/2 with reach and deathtouch that held off attackers all game. Auras like Mark of the Vampire could take over a game, because lifelink on a big body is hard to race.

The best decks usually paired two colors with clear roles: one color for removal and one for evasive or efficient creatures. Sliver decks could work, but they needed enough Slivers to make their shared abilities matter, and a pile of 1/1s with no payoff is easy to beat. Because the cards are so readable, M14 is a good format for practicing Limited fundamentals; our guide to [evaluating cards for Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited) explains how to rank removal, creatures and tricks.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Magic 2014 spent its Standard season alongside Return to Ravnica block and, from September 2013, Theros block. Theros brought devotion, and several of the best decks of the season were mono-colored devotion decks.

- **Mono-Blue Devotion** used Tidebinder Mage as a cheap creature that added devotion and locked down a red or green blocker, along with Mutavault.
- **Mono-Black Devotion** also played Mutavault, which gave the deck an extra threat that dodged sorcery-speed removal.
- Green decks used Elvish Mystic, Scavenging Ooze and Kalonian Hydra, and Garruk, Caller of Beasts gave ramp decks a card-advantage engine.
- **Burning Earth** and **Ratchet Bomb** were common sideboard cards, punishing three-color decks built on nonbasic lands and token or low-cost strategies.

No Magic 2014 card was banned in Standard, and the set left with Return to Ravnica block when Khans of Tarkir released in September 2014.

The set's longest legacy comes from a handful of cards. Mutavault became a staple of mono-colored aggro decks in Modern and Pioneer, since a land that attacks is free value in a deck that doesn't need colored mana from it. Scavenging Ooze was a long-time graveyard answer in Modern and Legacy. Young Pyromancer gave blue-red tempo decks in Modern and Legacy a threat that fills the board with tokens. Elvish Mystic sees play in Pioneer green decks as a second copy of Llanowar Elves, and Tidebinder Mage became a fixture of Modern Merfolk.

In Commander, Archangel of Thune is a favorite in lifegain decks, Strionic Resonator copies powerful triggers, and the M14 Slivers are part of most Sliver decks. As of October 2026, no Magic 2014 card is banned in Pioneer, Modern, Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Magic 2014 worth owning

- **Mutavault**: a creature land that fits almost any mono-colored Pioneer or Modern deck.
- **Scavenging Ooze**: a flexible creature that doubles as graveyard hate.
- **Elvish Mystic**: one-mana ramp for green Pioneer decks.
- **Young Pyromancer**: a token engine for spell-heavy decks in Modern and Legacy.
- **Archangel of Thune**: a lifegain payoff that's a Commander favorite.
- **Strionic Resonator**: a cheap trigger-copying artifact for Commander.

## The takeaway

Magic 2014 is a core set that did its teaching job and then quietly left behind some of the most-played cards in Pioneer and Modern. It also gave us the modern legend rule, which you use every time you play a legendary permanent. If you're building a Pioneer deck with it, Mutavault and Elvish Mystic are the natural starting points, depending on whether you want a mono-colored aggro deck or a green ramp deck.
