Apocalypse is the third and final set of the Invasion block in Magic: The Gathering, released in June 2001. It's the climax of the Weatherlight Saga, the story Wizards had been building since 1997, ending with the defeat of Yawgmoth and the Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria. Mechanically it flipped the block's script: where Invasion and Planeshift celebrated allied color pairs, Apocalypse was all about enemy colors, which gave us Vindicate, Pernicious Deed, Fire // Ice and the enemy painlands.

## The story

The Weatherlight Saga follows Gerrard Capashen and the crew of the skyship Weatherlight, built to be part of the Legacy, a collection of artifacts the planeswalker Urza created over thousands of years to defeat Phyrexia and its god-like ruler Yawgmoth. Invasion (2000) began the long-feared Phyrexian assault on Dominaria, and Planeshift saw the war go badly for the defenders. Apocalypse, told in the novel of the same name by J. Robert King, is the end of it all.

The story moves on several fronts at once:

- **The Coalition.** Dominaria's surviving armies, an alliance of elves, Metathran soldiers, minotaurs and more, storm the Stronghold, the Phyrexian fortress of Rath that has been overlaid onto the swamps of Urborg. There, a crazed Crovax, now the Evincar, has taken control.
- **Phyrexia.** Urza leads the Nine Titans, a group of powerful planeswalkers, to strike Phyrexia directly. It goes badly. Gerrard and Urza are forced to fight each other in an arena, with Yawgmoth reviving whoever loses so they can fight again. Eventually Urza is beheaded, though a planeswalker isn't killed that easily, and Gerrard escapes carrying his head.
- **The Weatherlight crew.** Sisay, Karn, Tahngarth and the others pore over the Thran Tome, trying to work out how the Legacy and the ship are supposed to save the world.

When Yawgmoth himself comes to Dominaria, the final battle begins. Gerrard and his allies first try to channel power into a beam to destroy him and fail. Then Urza, still alive in his severed head, tells Gerrard to remove the powerstones that serve as his eyes and set them into Karn's chest. That completes the Legacy and creates the Legacy Weapon. Its light spreads across Dominaria and wipes out Yawgmoth's essence. Gerrard and Urza don't survive, the Weatherlight crashes into the sea with its powers burned out, and Karn emerges as a planeswalker in his own right. It's one of the biggest endings in Magic's story, and the reason Karn shows up again and again in later sets.

The card Legacy Weapon, a seven-mana artifact that exiles any permanent for `{W}{U}{B}{R}{G}`, is the set's nod to that climax, and Gerrard Capashen got a legendary creature card of his own.

## Mechanics

### Enemy-color gold cards

Apocalypse is built around the five enemy pairs: white-black, blue-red, black-green, red-white and green-blue. That theme shows up everywhere: two-color gold cards like Vindicate and Pernicious Deed, three-color "wedge" rares like Lightning Angel, kicker costs in enemy colors, and enchantments (the Sanctuaries) that reward you for controlling permanents of enemy colors.

### Kicker

Kicker is an optional extra cost you can pay when you cast a spell for a bigger effect. In Apocalypse, the kicker cost is usually in an enemy color. Orim's Thunder destroys an artifact or enchantment, and if you kick it with red it also deals damage equal to that permanent's mana value to a creature. The Volvers, a cycle of rare creatures like Degavolver, have two separate kicker costs in the two enemy colors, and each one makes the creature bigger and adds an ability.

### Split cards

Split cards are two spells on one card; you cast one half or the other. Apocalypse's five split cards each pair enemy colors: Fire // Ice (red and blue), Life // Death, Night // Day, Order // Chaos and Illusion // Reality. Fire // Ice is the standout: two damage split between up to two targets, or tap a permanent and draw a card.

### Smaller themes

Each color got a mini-mechanic. Green had **Penumbra** creatures that leave a black token of the same size when they die (Penumbra Wurm leaves a 6/6). Black had Phyrexian creatures that trade life for cards, like Phyrexian Rager, and the enchantment Phyrexian Arena. White had **Flagbearers**, which force opponents to target them. Blue had **Whirlpool** creatures that shuffle your hand away and redraw, and red had **Bloodfire** creatures that sacrifice themselves to deal damage.

### Enemy painlands

Apocalypse completed the painland cycle with the enemy-colored versions: Caves of Koilos, Llanowar Wastes, Battlefield Forge, Shivan Reef and Yavimaya Coast. Each taps for colorless for free or for one of two colors at the cost of 1 damage.

## Limited

Invasion block draft is often remembered as one of the great Limited formats of its era. Drafts of the time opened packs in release order, so once Apocalypse was out the format was Invasion, then Planeshift, then Apocalypse. Invasion rewarded allied two- and three-color decks with plenty of fixing; Apocalypse, in the last pack, pulled drafters toward enemy colors and splashes, which made reading what your neighbors were passing especially important. Our guide to [reading draft signals](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals) covers that skill.

At common and uncommon, cards that fit multiple decks were most valuable. Phyrexian Rager was card advantage on a body, Orim's Thunder was flexible removal, Fire // Ice and Night // Day gave you options, and Penumbra creatures like Penumbra Bobcat were hard to trade with profitably. The Volvers, though rare, were strong because a kicked one is usually much bigger than its mana cost suggests. Domain and multicolor cards from Invasion still mattered, so a Limited deck in this format was often three colors with a light splash, supported by Invasion's tap-lands and Apocalypse's fixers. Games tended to be midrange affairs where card advantage and evasive threats decided things.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Apocalypse was Standard-legal from July 2001 until Onslaught rotated Invasion block out in October 2002.

- **Black aggro-control.** Tom van de Logt won the 2001 World Championship with a black-red deck called Machine Head, and he ran Phyrexian Arena in his sideboard for slower matchups.
- **Black-green decks.** Spiritmonger and Pernicious Deed were natural partners in black-green midrange, and Deed gave those decks a one-card answer to an opponent's whole board.
- **Blue-red and green-blue decks.** Prophetic Bolt and Fire // Ice suited blue-red control, and Mystic Snake was a strong card in green-blue.
- **White-black** used Vindicate and Gerrard's Verdict as efficient disruption.

No Apocalypse card was banned in Standard. In Extended, Pernicious Deed became the signature card of black-green Rock decks.

The set's longest-lasting impact is in eternal formats and Commander. As of October 2026:

- **Legacy and Vintage.** Every Apocalypse card is legal. Vindicate is a flexible answer for white-black decks, Pernicious Deed has seen play in black-green midrange decks, and Fire // Ice fits blue-red tempo. Our [Legacy format guide](/blog/mtg-legacy-format-guide) explains how these decks work.
- **Modern.** The set predates Eighth Edition, so only cards reprinted later are Modern-legal. Vindicate, Fire // Ice, Mystic Snake, Lightning Angel, Phyrexian Arena and the enemy painlands all qualify through reprints; Pernicious Deed, Spiritmonger and Prophetic Bolt don't.
- **Pauper.** Commons such as Fire // Ice (through a later common printing), Phyrexian Rager, Orim's Thunder and Goblin Legionnaire are legal.
- **Commander.** Vindicate, Pernicious Deed, Phyrexian Arena, Mystic Snake and the painlands are all familiar sights. Our [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers the format.

None of Apocalypse's cards are on the Reserved List, so its staples have been reprinted many times. No Apocalypse card is banned in Legacy, Vintage or Commander.

## Cards from Apocalypse worth owning

- **Vindicate**: three mana to destroy any permanent, for any white-black deck.
- **Pernicious Deed**: a flexible board wipe for black-green Legacy and Commander decks.
- **Fire // Ice**: a two-for-one spell that's always useful.
- **Phyrexian Arena**: steady card advantage for black Commander decks.
- **Mystic Snake**: a counterspell on a body for green-blue.
- **The enemy painlands**: untapped two-color mana for any format.

## The takeaway

Apocalypse ended Magic's first great saga with a real finale and a set full of cards that still matter. If you want to build with it, a black-green Legacy midrange deck with Pernicious Deed, or a white-black deck built around Vindicate, captures what the set does best.
