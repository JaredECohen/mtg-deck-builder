The Hobbit is a Magic: The Gathering Universes Beyond expansion released in August 2026 that retells J.R.R. Tolkien's 1937 novel, from Bilbo Baggins's front door at Bag End to the Battle of Five Armies. It's a smaller set than a usual premier release, built around Thorin's company of Dwarves, a Smaug who hoards Treasure, and two new mechanics: storied and recruit.

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Set code | HOB |
| Release date | August 14, 2026 (prerelease August 7) |
| Universe | Middle-earth, adapting Tolkien's *The Hobbit* |
| Main set size | About 190 cards, a small set |
| Headline mechanics | Storied, recruit, amass Goblins, Adventures, hone counters |
| Standard status | Legal in Standard as of October 2026 |

Because it's a 2026 set, The Hobbit is not part of the large rotation Wizards has scheduled for early 2027, and it's expected to stay in Standard until a later rotation Wizards announces.

## The story

The set follows the book closely, and most of its legendary cards are a scene you can point to. It opens in the Shire, where Gandalf arrives with thirteen Dwarves and sets up the "unexpected party" that pulls a comfortable Halfling out of his hole. The Dwarves are led by Thorin Oakenshield, heir to the kingdom under the Lonely Mountain, which the dragon Smaug seized long ago along with its treasure. Bilbo is hired as their burglar.

The road east is a string of famous set pieces, and the card list tracks them. Three trolls nearly cook the company (Tom, Bert, and William appear as one legendary Troll card). Elrond reads the hidden moon-letters on Thrór's map in Rivendell. In the Misty Mountains, the party is captured in Goblin-town under the Great Goblin, and Bilbo, separated in the dark, trades riddles with Gollum and pockets a magic ring. The Wargs and Goblins trap the company in the trees until the Eagles carry them off, and the shape-shifter Beorn shelters them before Mirkwood.

Mirkwood is where Bilbo grows into the hero. He frees the Dwarves from the giant spiders with his sword Sting, then breaks them out of the Elvenking Thranduil's halls by floating them downriver in barrels. They reach Lake-town, ruled by its greedy Master, then the Mountain itself, where Bilbo talks with Smaug and steals a cup. The furious dragon descends on Lake-town, and Bard the Bowman brings him down with the Black Arrow.

The ending is about greed. Thorin, gripped by the hoard and desperate for the Arkenstone, refuses to share the treasure with the Men and Elves who have claims on it. Bilbo hands the Arkenstone to Bard to force a deal, and is cast out for it. Then the Goblins and Wargs, led by Bolg, attack, and Men, Elves and Dwarves (with Dáin Ironfoot's army from the Iron Hills) unite in the Battle of Five Armies. The Eagles and Beorn turn the battle, but Thorin is mortally wounded and makes peace with Bilbo before he dies. Bilbo goes home with a modest share of gold to find his relatives, the Sackville-Bagginses, auctioning off his belongings. Cards like Thorin's Last Stand, The Black Arrow and The Arkenstone each capture one of those beats.

## Mechanics

### Storied

Storied appears on a group of legendary Dwarves from Thorin's company. Once you control a storied permanent along with three or more artifacts, legendary permanents and/or Sagas, you gain an "enduring story" for the rest of the game. The key word is *enduring*: if those permanents later die, you keep it. Each storied card then checks whether you have an enduring story to switch on a bonus.

Thorin Oakenshield is the flagship. For `{R}{W}` it's a 3/2 trampler, and once you have an enduring story, your artifacts and creatures all gain ward 1. Fíli the Pathfinder gives your creatures +1/+1 under the same condition. In play, storied rewards a deck that leans on Equipment, legendary creatures and Sagas, which the set prints in quantity. The condition is easy to meet in a dedicated Dwarves deck and nearly impossible by accident.

### Recruit

Recruit is a keyword action: draw a card, then discard a card, and if you discarded a nonland card, create a 1/1 white Human Soldier token. It's a loot that pays you a body for pitching spells. Esgaroth Garrison and Patient Instructor recruit when they enter, while The Queen of Dale recruits whenever an opponent casts their first noncreature spell each turn. Recruit is mainly a white mechanic and powers the white-blue Humans deck in Limited, where the draw-and-discard also feeds "second card drawn each turn" payoffs.

### Amass Goblins

Amass returns from The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth, now building Goblin Armies. Amass Goblins N means putting N +1/+1 counters on your Army, creating a 0/0 Goblin Army token first if you don't have one. Tidings of War and Rage into the Valley are typical, and The Great Goblin pays off counters landing on Goblins and Armies. The Army is one creature that keeps growing, which makes it a strong attacker but a single target for removal.

### Adventures and hone counters

Adventures return: a card like Bilbo Baggins, Burglar lets you cast its cheap sorcery half first (Take a Glance, scry 2), then cast the creature later from exile. Several big legends in the set work this way, including Smaug, the Great Calamity, whose Adventure Spew Flame is a five-damage removal spell.

Hone counters are new and simple. Each hone counter on an Equipment gives the equipped creature +1/+0. Sting, Bilbo's Sword enters with a hone counter for each creature an opponent controls, and Dwalin, Weaponmaster puts one on each of your Equipment whenever he enters or attacks.

## Limited

The Hobbit was built for MTG Arena's fast Pick-Two Draft as well as traditional draft. Because it's small, it has five two-color archetypes instead of the usual ten, each tied to a group from the book:

- **White-blue Humans (Lake-town):** recruit, tokens and "second card each turn" payoffs like Bard the Bowman and Lakeshore Apothecary.
- **Black-red Goblins:** amass, Goblin Armies and sacrifice, with Bolg of the North and The Great Goblin as payoffs.
- **Black-green Wolves:** ferocious (bonuses while you control a creature with power 4 or greater) with The Chief Warg and Ravening Warg.
- **Green-blue Elves (Mirkwood):** landfall and Elf synergies, led by Thranduil, Sindarin Liege and Thranduil's Company.
- **Red-white Dwarves:** storied, Equipment and Dwarf tokens, with Thorin Oakenshield and Dáin's Company.

Each pair has a common dual land that also works as a late-game mana sink: Iron Hills, Goblin-town, Lake-town, Mirkwood and Elvenking's Halls enter tapped, tap for two colors, and can be sacrificed to put two +1/+1 counters on a creature of the matching tribe.

Early data from 17lands and the reviews that followed it showed black-red as the most drafted pair and one of the most successful, with white-blue climbing as players learned recruit. Black was the deepest color at common: Crude Bent Blade, Ravening Warg and Gollum, Silent Slinker all performed well. White's commons were thin, so white drafters leaned on uncommons like Stone by Sunlight and Celebrate the Mountain-king. For general Limited fundamentals, see [how to evaluate cards in Limited](/blog/how-to-evaluate-cards-limited).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

The Hobbit arrived during a busy summer for Standard. On August 10, 2026, just days before release, Wizards banned Badgermole Cub, Gran-Gran and Stormchaser's Talent, so the set entered a format already being reshuffled.

As of late October 2026, about ten weeks after release, the format is still settling, but three Hobbit-fueled decks have shown up in tournament results tracked by MTGGoldfish:

- **Boros Dragons** plays four Smaug the Magnificent as its headline threat. A four-mana flying, haste Dragon that makes a Treasure every turn and turns those Treasures into damage gives red decks a threat that grows if it goes unanswered.
- **Boros Dwarves** is the set's own archetype translated to Constructed: Dwarven Mauler, Dwalin, Weaponmaster and Thorin, Mountain-king, with The Lonely Mountain as a land that turns spare mana into Dwarf tokens. It's a smaller share of the field, but it's a genuine new deck rather than a few cards slotted into an old one.
- **Jund Sacrifice** runs four The Sackville-Bagginses, which turns a fodder creature into a card, a Treasure and a stream of life loss.

Beyond those, Settle the Wreckage returned as a sideboard answer to wide aggressive decks, and Warg Tactics and Elven Passage have appeared in landfall lists. No Hobbit card has been banned in any format as of October 2026.

In Commander, the set's highest-ranked cards on EDHREC so far are the reprinted Wood Elves and Settle the Wreckage, plus new cards like Gleaming Splendor, The Queen of Dale, Wizard's Staff and Smaug the Magnificent. Commander players have also embraced the Dwarf legends, who give the creature type real depth for the first time. If Commander is your main format, our [Commander overview](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) explains how legends become commanders.

## Cards from The Hobbit worth owning

- **Smaug the Magnificent**: a Constructed-quality Dragon that also makes Treasure for big Commander turns.
- **The Sackville-Bagginses**: a two-mana value engine for any sacrifice deck.
- **Gleaming Splendor**: a Treasure engine that punishes opponents who draw extra cards, which happens constantly in Commander.
- **The Queen of Dale**: cheap, steady card filtering and tokens in any white deck.
- **Settle the Wreckage**: a proven answer to big attacks, now back in Standard.
- **Wizard's Staff**: doubles the triggers of the creature it equips, which Commander players love.
- **Orcrist, Goblin-cleaver**: a strong Equipment that pairs with any tribal deck.

The Hobbit is a compact set with a clear identity: Dwarves with Equipment, Goblins with Armies, and a dragon on a pile of Treasure. For Standard, start with Smaug the Magnificent or The Sackville-Bagginses, since both slot into existing strategies. If you want something new, Boros Dwarves is the set's own deck and the most fun way to use storied.
