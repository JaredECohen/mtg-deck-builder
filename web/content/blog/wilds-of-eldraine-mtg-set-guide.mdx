Wilds of Eldraine is the fairy-tale premier set that returned Magic: The Gathering to the plane of Eldraine in September 2023, in the aftermath of the Phyrexian invasion. A sleeping curse is spreading across the realm, a half-fae farm boy sets out to break it, and the set's Adventures, Roles and Food tokens turn storybook ideas into game mechanics. It's now the oldest set in Standard, and it produced some of the format's most-played and most-banned cards.

## The story

The Phyrexian invasion hit Eldraine hard. High King Kenrith and Queen Linden died defending it, and the five courts were shattered. To stop the invaders, a spell put them to sleep, but it didn't stop there. The **Wicked Slumber** began putting Eldraine's own people to sleep too, and more of the realm drifts off every day.

Two storylines run side by side. In the first, the royal twins argue over what comes next. Will Kenrith has named himself High King and wants to reunite the realms through diplomacy, while many knights mock him as the "Boy King." His sister Rowan, grieving and newly cut off from planeswalking, sees diplomacy as weakness. When her uncontrolled lightning wrecks Will's attempt to bring the rebel knight Imodane to heel, the siblings split. Rowan sets out to fix things by force.

In the second, Kellan, a half-fae farm boy who's bullied in his village, is summoned by Talion, the Lord of the Fae. Talion sends him to stop a trio of witch sisters, and in return promises the truth about Kellan's father. Kellan teams up with Ruby, a girl searching for her lost brother Peter. They take down Agatha by pushing her into her own cauldron, which frees Peter and Imodane. Then they climb a beanstalk to a giants' castle and reach the icy fortress of the second sister, Hylda. There they learn the curse began as a deal between Talion and the witches to stop the Phyrexians, and that the third sister, Eriette, let it spread beyond control.

The threads meet at Castle Ardenvale. Eriette, backed by the planeswalker Ashiok, has drawn Rowan into her circle and revealed that they're related. Rowan turns on her brother. Will's knights, Imodane, Kellan and Ruby storm the throne room, and in the chaos Ashiok is wounded and flees, Ruby holds a blade to Eriette's throat, and Rowan blasts her way out and goes into hiding. Kellan goes home, and his mother finally tells him his father is Oko, the fae trickster planeswalker. The story ends with Kellan stepping through a portal to find him, the thread that later picks up in Outlaws of Thunder Junction.

## Mechanics

### Bargain

Bargain is an optional extra cost: as you cast the spell, you may sacrifice an artifact, enchantment or token to get a stronger effect. **Torch the Tower** (common, `{R}`) deals 2 damage to a creature or planeswalker, or 3 plus a scry if bargained, and exiles whatever it kills. Food, Treasure and Role tokens give you plenty of fodder.

### Celebration

Celebration checks whether two or more nonland permanents entered the battlefield under your control this turn. Tokens count, so a turn that makes a creature and a Food is often enough. **Goddric, Cloaked Reveler** becomes a 4/4 flying Dragon when you celebrate.

### Role tokens

Roles are predefined Aura tokens that attach to a creature. There are seven, from the helpful Monster Role (+1/+1 and trample) and Young Hero Role to the Cursed Role, which makes the creature a 1/1 and can be stuck on an opponent's threat. A creature can only have one Role from each player, and a new Role from you replaces your old one. **Monstrous Rage** gives +2/+0 and a Monster Role for one mana.

### Adventure and Food

Adventures return from the first Eldraine set. These cards have a smaller spell attached. You cast the Adventure first, the card goes to exile, and later you can cast the creature or permanent from exile. The rare cycle of **Virtues** puts the Adventure on an enchantment, so the cheap half is a useful spell and the expensive half is a game-ending effect. Food tokens are back too: artifacts you can sacrifice for 3 life, which also feed bargain and other synergies. For a refresher on how these frames read, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## Limited

Each two-color pair has a gold signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue (Sharae of Numbing Depths): tap and stun.** A tempo deck that taps blockers and attacks in the air.
- **Blue-black (Obyra, Dreaming Duelist): Faeries.** Flash and flying Faeries that drain on entry.
- **Black-red (Totentanz, Swarm Piper): Rats.** Can't-block Rat tokens and sacrifice-flavored aggression.
- **Red-green (Ruby, Daring Tracker): big creatures.** Ruby rewards you for having a creature with power 4 or greater.
- **Green-white (Syr Armont, the Redeemer): Roles.** Stack Roles on your creatures and push through damage.
- **White-black (Neva, Stalked by Nightmares): enchantments.** Value from enchantments entering and leaving.
- **Blue-red (Johann, Apprentice Sorcerer): spells and Adventures.** Instants, sorceries and Adventure halves.
- **Black-green (Greta, Sweettooth Scourge): Food.** Make Food, then sacrifice it for counters and cards.
- **Red-white (Ash, Party Crasher): celebration.** Go wide fast so celebration turns on every turn.
- **Green-blue (Troyan, Gutsy Explorer): big spells.** Ramp into mana value 5+ and X spells.

Strong commons include removal like Candy Grapple, Torch the Tower and **Feed the Cauldron**, along with value creatures such as **Sweettooth Witch** and the reach-and-ramp **Beanstalk Wurm**. Bargain gives every color a reason to hold onto tokens, so Food and Roles matter even outside their main archetypes. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Wilds of Eldraine was the first set of Standard's three-year rotation window, and it stayed relevant for a long time. At the 2023 World Championship, a few weeks after release, **Virtue of Persistence** was the set's most-played card, showing up in Golgari Midrange, ramp, reanimator and five-color decks. **Virtue of Loyalty** was close behind in Esper Midrange. Monstrous Rage gave mono-red aggro a new one-mana trick right away, and Goddric, Cloaked Reveler added to it.

The bans tell the rest of the story:

- **Up the Beanstalk** was banned in Modern in December 2023. Wizards judged it too efficient a card-draw engine that was hard to interact with.
- **Monstrous Rage**, **Up the Beanstalk** and **Hopeless Nightmare** were all banned in Standard on June 30, 2025, in a seven-card shake-up that also hit Heartfire Hero and Cori-Steel Cutter. For Up the Beanstalk, Wizards pointed to the risk of cost-reduction cards in upcoming sets.

As of October 2026, the set is still part of Standard. Cards like Virtue of Persistence, Werefox Bodyguard and the Restless lands keep turning up in midrange decks, but much of the format's speed now comes from newer sets. Wizards' rotation schedule has Wilds of Eldraine leaving Standard at the first rotation of 2027, which arrives with that year's first premier set.

Outside Standard, Agatha's Soul Cauldron has a home in Modern creature combo decks, including Selesnya life-gain builds with Walking Ballista. Commander is where the set has had its widest reach. Beseech the Mirror, Agatha's Soul Cauldron, Virtue of Persistence, Moonshaker Cavalry and Talion, the Kindly Lord are among its most popular cards, and tutors like Beseech the Mirror are worth weighing against the [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained) your table uses.

## Cards from Wilds of Eldraine worth owning

- **Virtue of Persistence**: removal early, a reanimation engine late, and strong in Commander.
- **Agatha's Soul Cauldron**: a combo piece in Modern and a staple in creature-ability Commander decks.
- **Beseech the Mirror**: one of the strongest tutors printed in recent years, for Commander.
- **Moonshaker Cavalry**: a classic finisher for go-wide Commander decks.
- **Virtue of Loyalty**: a flexible white card that wins board stalls.
- **The Restless land cycle**: manlands that improve Pioneer and Commander mana bases.
- **Up the Beanstalk**: banned in Standard and Modern but still legal in Pioneer and Commander, where it's a strong ramp engine.

Wilds of Eldraine is easiest to understand through its tokens. Food, Treasure and Roles fuel bargain and celebration, so count your token makers when you build.
