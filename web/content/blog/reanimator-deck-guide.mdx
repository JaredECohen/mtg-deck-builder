## Short answer

Reanimator is faster than hard-casting only on the turns you have both a creature in the graveyard and a reanimation spell in hand. In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), the rest of the time you are a deck with expensive creatures and a hand full of setup. The comparison is not reanimate versus a six-drop on turn six. It is reanimate on turn three or four versus doing nothing until turn six, and only in the games where the graveyard actually has the creature.

Two official Commander decks show different eras of the same idea. Revenant Recon, Mirko, is a $136.88 product whose top nonbasics are Necromancy at $17.89, Reanimate at $9.71, and Animate Dead at $7.82. That is the reanimation suite as the price, 25.9% of the product in three enchantments and one sorcery's company. The curve has one-drops. Those are the enablers that fill the graveyard, not the monsters. Plunder the Graves, the original Meren precon, is $97.26 and its top costs are a big creature, an anthem, and Skullclamp, not the reanimation spells. Meren reanimates by being on the battlefield and collecting experience. Mirko's product reanimates by casting the famous spells. They are not interchangeable just because both touch the graveyard.

The goldfish clocks, turn 11.50 and turn 11.34, are combat again. The simulator will cast a creature if it can pay for it. It will not Entomb a creature and Reanimate it as a plan. A turn-11 combat win does not prove reanimator is slow, and it does not prove it is fast. It proves the creature plan, played fairly, takes that long. The speed you built the deck for is the unfair line, which you have to count by hand: how many cards put a creature in the yard, how many bring one back, and how many of those you need in the first four turns. The [combo page](/blog/mtg-combo-decks-explained) is the other place a goldfish zero or a goldfish combat clock hides the real line.

## When to choose this

| Situation | Lean in | Walk away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The reanimation spells are the expensive cards | Mirko's top three are Necromancy, Reanimate, and Animate Dead | You are buying the unfair line |
| The commander is the reanimation | Meren, and the receipt is creatures and Skullclamp instead | A different failure mode: the commander dies and stays dead |
| You want a measured unfair clock | The goldfish will not give it to you | Count enablers and reanimation spells yourself |
| You have the big creatures and not the enablers | You are hard-casting | Add the one-drops before you add another seven-drop |

### Revenant Recon

Revenant Recon, commanded by Mirko, Obsessive Theorist is an official Commander Deck released 2024-02-09. The decklist and the Wizards source link come from MTGJSON file RevenantRecon_MKC. Source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/murders-at-karlov-manor-commander-decklists.

Read this as a deck that cheats on mana by putting something into the graveyard and returning it. The one-drops are the enablers, not the threats.

The nonland mana curve is 1 at mana value 0, 7 at mana value 1, 18 at mana value 2, 10 at mana value 3, 8 at mana value 4, 7 at mana value 5, 5 at mana value 6, 6 at mana value 7 or more. The list runs 37 lands in the library (the commander is not one of them).

Colored sources, using Scryfall produced_mana and counting basic lands as the color on their type line, restricted to this deck's colors: 22 blue, 21 black.

MTGJSON AllPricesToday (2026-11-01) prices the exact printing in the product at $136.88 for main deck, sideboard, and commander, using TCGplayer nonfoil retail when it exists. The three most expensive nonbasic names are 1 Necromancy at $17.89 total, 1 Reanimate at $9.71 total, 1 Animate Dead at $7.82 total, which is 25.9% of the priced total. Basic lands in the product add $4.23 on their own, because the price is the printing Wizards put in the deck, not the cheapest Plains on the market. These cards had no TCGplayer nonfoil price on that printing, so the total undercounts: Marvo, Deep Operative, Mirko, Obsessive Theorist.

Across 120 goldfish games (seed 1729, midrange policy, opponent at 40 life) it won 78.3% and the average winning game ended on turn 11.5 (median 11.0). There is no opponent interaction in that number. A goldfish clock is a ceiling on speed, not a match win rate.

## Common mistakes

- Adding a reanimation spell without a way to fill the graveyard. Reanimate on an empty yard is a blank card.
- Judging the deck by the turn-11 combat goldfish. That number is the fair plan. The deck's reason to exist is the unfair one.
- Treating Meren and Mirko as the same build. One cares about the commander staying alive. The other cares about resolving a one- or two-mana spell.
- Paying for Necromancy, Reanimate, and Animate Dead and then cutting the one-drops that make them castable on time. The curve on Mirko's list has those one-drops for a reason.
- Ignoring graveyard hate because the goldfish never brought any. A real table will have it. The backup plan is the combat clock you just measured, and it is turn 11.

## FAQ

### Is reanimator a combo?

Sometimes. A two-card pile that wins on the spot is a combo. A deck that reanimates a good creature and then plays a normal game is a cheat-on-mana deck. Mirko's product includes the famous spells. It does not, by itself, tell you the creature ends the game alone.

### Why are the reanimation spells so expensive on Mirko's list?

Necromancy $17.89, Reanimate $9.71, Animate Dead $7.82, together 25.9% of the $136.88 product-printing total. Those are old, heavily played cards printed into a precon.

### Does Meren need those same spells?

The 2015 product's top costs are Pathbreaker Ibex, Eldrazi Monument, and Skullclamp. The commander is the engine. Buying Reanimate for Meren is a different deck from the one Wizards printed.

### How many creatures do I need in the yard?

At least one you are happy to bring back, on the turn you have the spell. If your list has two reanimation spells and no discard or mill, you will not have that creature.
