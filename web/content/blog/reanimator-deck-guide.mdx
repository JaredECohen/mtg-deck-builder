In Magic: The Gathering (MTG), a Reanimator deck puts a huge creature into its own graveyard early, then uses a cheap spell to return it to the battlefield, skipping the creature's real mana cost. Instead of casting an eight-mana monster on turn eight, you discard it on turn one and bring it back on turn two. The plan is fast and powerful, but it depends entirely on the graveyard, which makes it vulnerable to cards that exile graveyards.

Reanimator is a cousin of combo: it needs two specific pieces, an enabler and a reanimation spell, and wins quickly when it assembles them. The [combo decks guide](/blog/mtg-combo-decks-explained) explains how that kind of deck thinks. This guide covers the pieces of a Reanimator deck, how to sequence them and how the strategy falls apart.

## How the plan works

Every Reanimator deck has three parts.

1. **Enablers** get a big creature card from your hand or library into your graveyard.
2. **Reanimation spells** return that creature card from the graveyard to the battlefield.
3. **Targets** are the creatures worth returning, usually ones with a huge mana cost and an effect that ends the game quickly.

The key idea is that most reanimation spells don't care about a creature's mana cost. Reanimate costs a single black mana whether it returns a 2/2 or a 7/7 flier. That gap between what you pay and what you get is the whole engine.

### A sample sequence

On turn one, you play a land and cast Entomb, putting Griselbrand from your library into your graveyard. On turn two, you cast Animate Dead targeting Griselbrand. You now have a 7/7 flier with lifelink on turn two, and you can pay life to draw seven cards. Most opponents can't come back from that.

That's the dream. Real games often take a turn or two longer, because you need the right combination of cards.

## Enablers: getting creatures into the graveyard

The enabler package decides how consistent the deck is. There are three main kinds.

### Tutors to the graveyard

These cards put a creature directly from your library into your graveyard.

- **Entomb** is a one-mana instant that searches your library for any card and puts it into your graveyard. Being an instant matters: you can cast it at the end of your opponent's turn, then reanimate on your turn.
- **Buried Alive** costs three mana and puts up to three creature cards from your library into your graveyard. It's slower, but it sets up several targets at once.

### Discard outlets

These let you discard a creature from your hand.

- **Faithless Looting** draws two cards, then discards two, for one red mana, and has flashback.
- **Careful Study** is the blue version without flashback: draw two, then discard two.
- Creatures and spells with discard costs work too. Any time you can discard a card as a cost, you can discard your big creature.

Discard enablers need the target to already be in your hand, which is why Reanimator decks often play more targets than you'd expect.

### Self-mill

Self-mill puts cards from the top of your library into your graveyard. Stitcher's Supplier is a one-mana 1/1 that mills three cards when it enters and again when it dies. Self-mill is less reliable than a tutor, since you might miss, but it also fills the graveyard for other cards. The [mill deck guide](/blog/mill-deck-guide) covers self-mill as its own plan.

## Reanimation spells

Once a creature is in the graveyard, you need a way to return it. These are the long-standing staples.

| Card | Cost | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reanimate | One black | Return a creature card from any graveyard; you lose life equal to its mana value |
| Animate Dead | Two mana, one black | An Aura that returns a creature card from any graveyard; the creature gets -1/-0 |
| Exhume | Two mana, one black | Each player returns a creature card from their own graveyard |
| Unburial Rites | Five mana | Return a creature card from your graveyard; has flashback |
| Living Death | Five mana | Every player swaps the creatures on the battlefield for the creatures in their graveyard |

Each one has a trade-off:

- **Reanimate** is the cheapest, but the life loss is real. Returning an eight-drop costs you 8 life. It can also target a creature in an opponent's graveyard.
- **Animate Dead** is an Aura, so if the Aura is destroyed, the creature is sacrificed. It's a weak point opponents can exploit with enchantment removal.
- **Exhume** gives your opponent a creature too. That's fine when their graveyard is empty and bad when they've discarded something big of their own.
- **Unburial Rites** is slow but gives you two attempts thanks to flashback, which also makes it good to discard.
- **Living Death** works as a combo and a board wipe at once, best when your graveyard is full and theirs is empty.

## Choosing targets

A good target either wins the game in a few turns or makes the game unwinnable for the opponent. Classic picks include:

- **Griselbrand:** a 7/7 with flying and lifelink that lets you pay 7 life to draw seven cards. It refills your hand and gains the life back.
- **Archon of Cruelty:** a flier that makes the opponent sacrifice a creature or planeswalker, discard a card and lose 3 life, while you draw and gain 3 life. It does this when it enters and every time it attacks.
- **Atraxa, Grand Unifier:** a seven-mana flier that digs through your library for a handful of cards of different types when it enters.

Avoid creatures that punish being in the graveyard. Emrakul, the Aeons Torn shuffles its owner's graveyard into their library when it's put into a graveyard from anywhere, so discarding it to Reanimate it doesn't work. The same is true of some other Eldrazi titans.

Also check your reanimation spell against your targets. Reanimate's life loss makes a ten-mana creature a dangerous choice when your life is already low.

## Building the deck

A Reanimator list balances the three parts so that you draw enablers and reanimation spells together often.

- **Redundancy beats raw power.** Play several enablers and several reanimation spells, because you need one of each to function.
- **Pick a small number of targets.** Too many targets clog your hand when you don't draw an enabler. Too few means a discard enabler has nothing to discard. A handful is typical.
- **Run a backup plan.** Some lists play cards that let them hard-cast a medium creature or win another way when the graveyard plan is shut off.
- **Protect the combo.** Discard like Thoughtseize clears counterspells from the opponent's hand, and counterspells of your own can protect the key turn.

Reanimator works in any format where the core cards are legal, and it's a natural fit for Commander, where graveyards are full and big creatures are everywhere. In Commander, be aware that reanimating opponents' creatures with Reanimate or Animate Dead is often just as good as reanimating your own.

## Weaknesses: graveyard hate and more

Reanimator's biggest strength, using the graveyard as a second hand, is also its biggest weakness. Opponents can play cards that make the graveyard useless.

### Graveyard hate

- **Rest in Peace** exiles all graveyards when it enters, and as long as it's on the battlefield, cards that would go to a graveyard are exiled instead. It shuts the plan off entirely.
- **Leyline of the Void** exiles any card that would go to an opponent's graveyard, and it can start the game on the battlefield if it's in the opening hand.
- **Relic of Progenitus** and **Tormod's Crypt** are cheap artifacts that exile graveyards at instant speed.
- **Bojuka Bog** is a land that exiles a player's graveyard when it enters.

Instant-speed graveyard hate is the most dangerous kind, because an opponent can respond to your reanimation spell by exiling the target. When a spell's only target is gone, the spell doesn't resolve. The [stack guide](/blog/how-the-stack-works-mtg) explains why.

### Other answers

- **Exile removal** like Swords to Plowshares deals with the reanimated creature permanently.
- **Counterspells** stop the reanimation spell itself.
- **Discard** takes the reanimation spell before you can cast it.
- **Enchantment removal** kills a creature returned with Animate Dead by destroying the Aura.

### Playing through hate

Expect graveyard hate after the first game of a match, since opponents bring it in from their sideboards. You can answer it with enchantment and artifact removal, hold enablers until you can reanimate the same turn, or bring in cards that win without the graveyard. [Sideboarding 101](/blog/sideboarding-101) covers how to plan those swaps.

Sequencing also helps. Discarding a creature at the end of the opponent's turn and reanimating it on your turn gives them fewer windows than discarding it a full turn early.

## The takeaway

Reanimator cheats a giant creature onto the battlefield by putting it in the graveyard and bringing it back for a fraction of its cost. Build it with enough enablers and reanimation spells that you draw both together, choose a few targets that end the game quickly, and avoid creatures that shuffle away from the graveyard. Expect graveyard hate, plan for it, and you'll win plenty of games before the opponent has finished setting up.
