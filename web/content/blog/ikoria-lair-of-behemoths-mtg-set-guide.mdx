Ikoria: Lair of Behemoths is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in spring 2020, set on a plane where giant monsters rule the wilds and humans hide behind fortress walls. It introduced mutate, which lets you stack creatures into custom monsters, and companions, a mechanic so strong it forced Wizards to rewrite its rules weeks after release. It also printed the triome lands and a string of cards that ended up banned across several formats.

## The story

Ikoria is a world where the monsters are winning. Huge, intelligent beasts roam the land, and they keep evolving into stranger and deadlier forms. Humanity survives in a handful of fortified places, the most important being the walled city of Drannith. Its elite soldiers, the Coppercoats, hold a simple belief: the only good monster is a dead one. A small number of people, called bonders, have started forming mental links with monsters. In Drannith that's a crime punishable by death.

The story, told in Django Wexler's novella *Sundered Bond*, follows Lukka, a proud Coppercoat captain. On a mission to kill a winged tiger, he suddenly and unwillingly bonds with it. He also senses that something is pushing the monsters to attack Drannith, and catches a glimpse of orange crystals. General Kudro, the city's hard-line commander and the father of Lukka's fiancée Jirina, orders him imprisoned. Jirina helps him escape, and the planeswalker Vivien Reid, who has come to Ikoria to study its unusual magic, finds him in the wilds.

Lukka and Vivien trace the strange influence to the Ozolith, a giant crystal formation that some unseen voice is using to drive the monsters toward the city. Hunters, including Jirina, pursue them, and the confrontation at the Ozolith turns into a three-way fight. Lukka connects his mind to the crystal, and the presence behind it, which hints that it comes from another plane, lets him use its power. He seizes control of the monsters to protect Drannith on his own terms, and then tries to take over the bonders' companions too. Vivien, who sees monsters as partners rather than tools, turns against him.

Things fall apart quickly. The winged tiger, acting on its own, saves Jirina, but Drannith captures and executes it. Lukka marches on the city with a monster army, still convinced he can win his old life back. At a parley, General Kudro tries to have Jirina assassinate him. She refuses, Lukka kills Kudro, and Jirina escapes. She then takes command of Drannith's defenses, with Vivien and many bonders fighting beside her. When the battle turns, Lukka tries to seize every bonded monster at once, overloads the Ozolith and destroys it. The blast ignites his planeswalker spark, and he vanishes from Ikoria. Drannith rebuilds with more tolerance for bonders, and Jirina becomes a colonel. Lukka wakes on another world, still able to command creatures and vowing to return.

Narset is the set's third featured planeswalker, appearing as Narset of the Ancient Way, although the novella only mentions her in passing.

## Mechanics

### Mutate

Mutate lets you cast a creature for its mutate cost and put it on top of or underneath a non-Human creature you own. The two become one creature: it has the characteristics of the card on top plus the abilities of everything underneath. Many mutate creatures have an ability that triggers whenever the creature mutates, so building a big stack piles up triggers. **Gemrazer** (rare) is a 4/4 reach trampler that destroys an artifact or enchantment each time it mutates. The risk is obvious: one removal spell takes out the whole stack. That's why the set's mutate creatures read best as "a spell plus a body," not as permanent investments.

### Companion

Each of the set's ten companions has a deckbuilding restriction. If your whole starting deck meets it, you can keep the companion outside the game and bring it into play later. **Lurrus of the Dream-Den** (rare) requires every permanent card in your deck to have mana value 2 or less, and in return lets you recast a cheap permanent from your graveyard each turn.

When the set launched, you could cast your companion directly from outside the game, which gave every companion deck a free eighth card in hand. Companions took over Constructed almost immediately. On June 1, 2020, Wizards changed the rule: now you pay `{3}` as a sorcery to put your companion into your hand, and then you cast it normally. That fix brought most companions back to earth, but several were banned anyway.

### Keyword counters

Ikoria introduced counters that grant keywords, such as flying, lifelink, menace, trample and deathtouch counters. A creature with a flying counter has flying for as long as the counter stays. **Luminous Broodmoth** (mythic) returns your dying non-fliers to the battlefield with a flying counter. Removing counters became a removal mode too: **Heartless Act** (uncommon) destroys a creature with no counters or takes up to three counters off one.

### Cycling and Humans

Cycling returns: pay the cost and discard the card to draw a new one. It's on many commons, on the triomes, and on payoff cards like **Zenith Flare**, which deals damage equal to the number of cycling cards in your graveyard. Humans, meanwhile, are the monster hunters. Mutate can't target them, and white-black is built around them. The set is also mostly about enemy colors and three-color wedges, with each wedge getting an "Apex" mutate legend and a triome.

## Limited

Ikoria's draft environment leans on enemy colors. Each enemy pair has two gold signposts (a creature and a spell), while the allied pairs got hybrid uncommons built around a single keyword:

- **White-black (General's Enforcer, Dire Tactics): Humans.** Monster hunters that punish mutate decks.
- **Blue-red (Sprite Dragon, Channeled Force): noncreature spells.** Cheap spells and a growing flier.
- **Black-green (Skull Prophet, Back for More): graveyard.** Mill yourself and bring creatures back.
- **Red-white (Savai Thundermane, Zenith Flare): cycling.** Cycle cards for damage and life.
- **Green-blue (Parcelbeast, Primal Empathy): mutate.** Build the biggest monster on the table.
- **White-blue (Jubilant Skybonder): flying.**
- **Blue-black (Cunning Nightbonder): flash.**
- **Black-red (Sonorous Howlbonder): menace.**
- **Red-green (Proud Wildbonder): trample.**
- **Green-white (Alert Heedbonder): vigilance.**

The best commons were removal and efficient creatures that also played well with mutate. **Ram Through** was a standout green removal spell, and **Fire Prophecy**, **Blitz Leech** and **Mutual Destruction** filled out the removal suite in other colors. **Mysterious Egg** and **Glimmerbell** were cheap bodies that mutate decks loved to build on. Cycling smoothed draws in nearly every deck, and the triomes, which show up in the land slot, made splashes easy. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard in 2020 and 2021

Companions defined Ikoria's first weeks. Before the rule change, top Standard decks often ran a companion as a free extra card, with Lurrus, Yorion, Obosh, Jegantha, Keruga and Umori all seeing play. After the June 1, 2020 change, most dropped out, but **Yorion, Sky Nomad** got stronger relative to the rest. "Yorion piles," 80-card midrange and control decks full of enter-the-battlefield enchantments from Theros Beyond Death, became one of the defining strategies of the format.

Outside the companions, the triomes made three- and four-color decks easy to build, Shark Typhoon gave control decks a flexible finisher, and Sprite Dragon and Fire Prophecy fueled Izzet and red decks. Winota, Joiner of Forces paired with Agent of Treachery in non-Human aggro shells, until Agent of Treachery was banned in Standard in August 2020. No Ikoria card itself was banned in Standard. The set rotated out in September 2021 with Innistrad: Midnight Hunt's release.

### The ban list

Ikoria has one of the longest ban records of any modern-era set:

- **Lurrus of the Dream-Den** and **Zirda, the Dawnwaker** were banned in Legacy within weeks of release, and Lurrus was banned in Vintage too. Lurrus later returned to Vintage once the companion change had settled in.
- **Lutri, the Spellchaser** was banned in Commander the day it was previewed, because every singleton deck already meets its restriction.
- **Winota, Joiner of Forces** was banned in Pioneer in June 2022.
- **Lurrus of the Dream-Den** was banned in Modern and Pioneer on March 7, 2022, after Wizards found it in a large share of winning online decks.
- **Yorion, Sky Nomad** was banned in Modern on October 10, 2022.
- **Jegantha, the Wellspring** was banned in Pioneer and Modern in December 2024, as Wizards judged it was discouraging cards with repeated mana symbols.

### Pioneer, Modern, Legacy and Commander today

As of October 2026, Ikoria's biggest legacy is its lands. The triomes are fixtures in multicolor decks in Pioneer and Modern, where their basic land types also let fetchlands find them in Modern and Legacy. Fire Prophecy, Heartless Act and Drannith Magistrate keep showing up in Pioneer decks or sideboards. Companions as a group have faded from competitive play since the rule change and the bans, with the remaining ones filling small niches at most.

In Commander, Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy is a well-known competitive commander, Luminous Broodmoth and Shark Typhoon are popular, and the triomes are common upgrades. The Apex legends, like Brokkos, Apex of Forever, give mutate fans a commander to build around. Our [Commander primer](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers how those decks are built.

## Cards from Ikoria worth owning

- **The triomes**: three-color lands with cycling, useful in every non-rotating format.
- **Shark Typhoon**: a flexible instant-speed threat for control and Commander.
- **Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy**: a strong competitive Commander leader.
- **Luminous Broodmoth**: recursion that powers sacrifice decks and combos.
- **Heartless Act**: efficient black removal that's still legal everywhere.
- **Fire Prophecy**: red removal that also fixes your hand.
- **Drannith Magistrate**: a hate piece against graveyard and flashback strategies.
- **Fiend Artisan**: a growing threat that also tutors creatures.

Ikoria rewards two habits: count your counters (keyword counters and mutate stacks are both easy targets) and value flexible cards, since cycling and triomes kept every deck running smoothly. For Constructed today, start with the triomes and the cheap removal.
