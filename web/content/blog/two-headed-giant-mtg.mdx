Two-Headed Giant (often shortened to 2HG) is a team format for Magic: The Gathering where two teams of two players face off. Each team shares a single life total that starts at 30, and both players on a team take their turn at the same time. You still have your own deck, hand and graveyard, but you win and lose together.

It's one of the most fun ways to play Magic with a friend, and it shows up at prerelease events and kitchen tables alike. This guide covers how the format works, what changes from a normal game and how to build and play decks for it. For how 2HG fits among the other ways to play, see the [MTG formats overview](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

## How Two-Headed Giant works

The core rules are simple. Here's what makes 2HG different from a regular two-player game.

### Teams and shared life

- **Two players per team.** Teammates usually sit next to each other, facing the opposing team.
- **Shared life total of 30.** Damage and life loss to either player come out of the team's total. Life gain for either player adds to it.
- **A team loses together.** When the team's life total reaches 0, both players lose.
- **Shared poison.** Poison counters are also shared, and a team loses at 15 poison counters instead of 10.

### Each player keeps their own cards

You have your own library, hand, graveyard and permanents. You draw your own cards, play your own lands and cast your own spells. Your teammate's cards stay theirs, though you can talk about them freely.

### Shared turns

Instead of each player taking a turn, each **team** takes a turn. Both players on the team untap, draw and play their turn together. They share the same phases and steps, so both teammates are in their main phase at the same time and both can attack in the same combat.

The team that goes first skips the draw step on its first turn, just as the starting player does in a normal game. Each player on that team skips their draw.

### Talking to your teammate

Teammates can show each other their hands and discuss plays openly. Planning together is a core part of the format. Just remember that your opponents can usually hear you.

## What changes in practice

Shared life and shared turns have knock-on effects that change how many cards play.

### "Each opponent" effects get stronger

A card that says "each opponent loses 2 life" affects both players on the other team. Since they share a life total, the team loses 4 life. Cards that hit each opponent are effectively doubled in 2HG.

### Your effects can help your teammate

You can cast spells that target your teammate's creatures. A pump spell or protection effect on your partner's attacker works the same as on your own. Effects that say "you" or "creatures you control" still only affect you, so read carefully.

### Board wipes hit your partner too

A Wrath of God destroys every creature on the battlefield, including your teammate's. Coordinate before casting anything that affects both sides of the table, ideally when your team has fewer creatures at risk than theirs.

### Combat is bigger

Both opponents can attack in the same combat, so you face two players' creatures at once. That means more attackers at the same time than you'd usually see, and more blockers on defense. The defending team plans its blocks together.

For the edge cases of 2HG combat and targeting, the Two-Headed Giant section of the Comprehensive Rules is the official source.

## Two-Headed Giant strategy

### 30 life isn't as much as it sounds

Thirty life sounds like more than 20, but you're facing two opponents' worth of attackers and burn. Per player, a shared 30 is less than each player would have in a normal game. Aggressive teams can close games quickly, so don't assume you have time.

### Build complementary roles

The best teams don't just play two good decks. They play decks that cover each other's weaknesses.

- **Aggro and control.** One player applies pressure while the other answers threats and protects the team.
- **Board and support.** One player plays creatures, the other plays pump, removal and card draw that helps both.
- **Shared plan.** Both players pursue the same goal, such as going wide with tokens, so their cards boost each other.

### Coordinate your turn

Because you take your turn together, you can sequence plays across both players. One teammate might cast removal to clear a blocker before the other attacks. One might hold up a counterspell or trick while the other taps out. Talk through your turn before acting.

### Focus your removal

Since both opponents share one life total, whichever opponent is more dangerous is the one to slow down. Identify the threats that matter most to your team, and spend removal there rather than spreading it evenly. The thinking is similar to threat assessment in Commander, with only two opponents to track.

### Evasion is premium

With two opponents' worth of potential blockers, ground creatures often get stalled. Flying, menace and other evasion become more valuable than in a normal game. On defense, the [blocking strategy guide](/blog/blocking-strategy-mtg) applies, but remember that losing a blocker now leaves your teammate exposed too.

## Limited Two-Headed Giant

Limited 2HG, usually Sealed, is the version you're most likely to see at a store event. Each team opens a pool of booster packs together, then splits the cards between two decks. The [draft vs. sealed guide](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide) covers Sealed in general; here's what's different with a partner.

### Splitting the pool

- **Divide by color first.** A common approach is for each player to take two colors, so neither deck fights over the same cards.
- **Assign the bombs.** The most powerful cards should go to whichever deck can use them best, not split evenly for fairness.
- **Share removal sensibly.** Removal is precious. Make sure both decks have answers rather than piling them into one.
- **Each deck needs at least 40 cards,** as in any Limited game, built only from the team's pool plus basic lands.

### Limited tips

- **Play to the board.** Limited 2HG games are often decided by who controls combat. Creatures with evasion and good combat stats matter more than slow card advantage.
- **Combat tricks gain value.** With more creatures in each combat, a well-timed trick can swing a whole team attack.
- **Watch the pace.** Both teammates have to decide their plays each turn, so agree on a rhythm and keep games moving.

Event organizers set the exact product counts and structure, so check the event's details for how many packs each team gets.

## Constructed Two-Headed Giant

Constructed 2HG uses each player's own Constructed deck, usually 60 cards minimum, built for a format like Standard or Pioneer. The format is popular at kitchen tables, where friends can pair any two decks they like.

Things to consider:

- **Pair decks with synergy.** Two decks that make the same kind of resource, or one that protects the other's threats, beat two unrelated decks.
- **Avoid friendly fire.** A deck full of symmetrical effects, like board wipes or "each player" discard, can hurt your partner as much as the opponents.
- **Look at "each opponent" cards.** Cards that already hit every opponent gain value, since they hit both.
- **Check the deck rules for organized events.** Some events apply deck-building limits to the team as a whole, such as how many copies of a card both players can run together. Read the event rules before you build.

## Two-Headed Giant compared to Commander

Commander is the more common multiplayer format, and it's worth knowing how they differ. In Commander, everyone plays for themselves with their own life total, and alliances shift. In 2HG, the teams are fixed, life is shared and turns are taken together, so games are faster and more cooperative. The [Commander guide](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers that format if you want a free-for-all instead.

## The takeaway

Two-Headed Giant is two teams of two, sharing a 30-point life total and taking turns together. Each player still has their own deck and hand, but you plan and win as a team. Build decks with complementary roles, coordinate your turns, focus removal on the bigger threat, and remember that 30 life against two opponents goes quickly. Grab a partner and give it a try.
