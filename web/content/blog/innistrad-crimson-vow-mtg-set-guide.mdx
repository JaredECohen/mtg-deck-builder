Innistrad: Crimson Vow is the gothic-horror Magic: The Gathering expansion released in November 2021, the second half of a two-set return to the plane of Innistrad that began with Midnight Hunt. Olivia Voldaren throws the vampire wedding of the century, the Gatewatch crashes it, and the set's Blood tokens, cleave spells and training creatures turn a ballroom brawl into game mechanics. It also printed Wedding Announcement and Bloodtithe Harvester, two of the defining cards of their Standard era.

## The story

Crimson Vow picks up straight after Midnight Hunt. In that set, the humans of Innistrad tried to end a long Eternal Night with a ritual at the Harvesttide festival, using an artifact called the Moonsilver Key. The ritual was nearly finished when Olivia Voldaren, the ancient matriarch of the Voldaren vampire bloodline, swooped in and stole the Key, which by then held the soul of the witch Katilda.

Olivia had bigger plans than spoiling a festival. She had already captured the archangel Sigarda, and she meant to combine Sigarda's blood with the Key's power to bend Innistrad's angels to her will. To pull it off she needed the blood magic of Edgar Markov, the eldest vampire on the plane and grandfather of the planeswalker Sorin. Edgar had been sleeping in his coffin beneath Markov Manor. Olivia stole the coffin, woke him with her own blood, and charmed him while he was still confused. He agreed to marry her, and invitations went out to every vampire family on Innistrad.

The planeswalkers who'd come to help with the night problem, Arlinn Kord, Chandra Nalaar, Kaya and Teferi, joined the human cathar Adeline in a plan to get the Key back. Sorin, furious that Olivia had stolen his grandfather, wanted the wedding stopped too.

The ceremony fell apart at the vows. Sorin attacked Olivia at the altar, and in the chaos Katilda's spirit burst out of the Key and freed Sigarda. The angel's rage shattered the stained glass of the hall, killing or wounding many guests, and it also broke the wards around Voldaren Manor. The Gatewatch poured in. Kaya broke Olivia's hold over the train of ghosts that formed her veil, and Arlinn chased the bride through the manor. Edgar, meanwhile, fled to the manor's blood vault, where Sorin caught up with him. Their fight was as much an argument as a duel: Edgar sneered at his grandson's sentimentality, and Sorin was left beaten and humiliated.

In the end, Arlinn and her werewolf pack cornered Olivia and Edgar. Edgar was ready to give up the scheme, Olivia dropped the Key, and the newlyweds escaped through a broken window. The heroes carried the Key back to the Celestus, the great astronomical device at the heart of the ritual, and joined it to its matching lock. A beam of light ran from the Celestus to the moon, and the balance between day and night was restored. Katilda later lent the Key to Teferi for his time-travel work, which pays off in The Brothers' War. Olivia, still fuming, eventually turns up on Thunder Junction.

## Mechanics

### Blood tokens

Blood is Crimson Vow's vampire resource. A Blood token is a colorless artifact with "`{1}`, `{T}`, Discard a card, Sacrifice this artifact: Draw a card." It works like a delayed rummage, letting you turn an extra land or a dead card into a fresh one. Many vampires make Blood, and some count it. **Bloodtithe Harvester** (uncommon, `{B}{R}`) makes a Blood token when it enters and can sacrifice itself to give a creature −X/−X, where X is twice the number of Blood tokens you control.

### Cleave

Cleave is the set's new mechanic, found on instants and sorceries. A cleave spell has some words in square brackets. Cast it normally and the bracketed text applies. Pay the more expensive cleave cost and you remove those words, which usually makes the spell broader. **Fierce Retribution** (common, `{1}{W}`) destroys an attacking creature, or any creature at all if you pay its `{5}{W}` cleave cost. **Path of Peril** (rare) wipes creatures with mana value 2 or less, or every creature when cleaved.

### Training

Training is the human mechanic. Whenever a creature with training attacks alongside a creature with greater power, it gets a +1/+1 counter. It rewards attacking with a mix of small and big creatures. **Hopeful Initiate** (rare, `{W}`) grows through training and can cash in two +1/+1 counters to destroy an artifact or enchantment.

### Disturb

Disturb returns from Midnight Hunt. You can cast a disturb card from your graveyard for its disturb cost, and it comes back transformed into its back face. In Crimson Vow the front faces are Spirit creatures and the backs are Auras, so a dead ghost becomes a buff for another creature. **Kindly Ancestor** (common) is a 2/3 lifelinker that later returns as an Aura that grants lifelink.

### Daybound and nightbound

The werewolf mechanic also returns. Cards with daybound transform to their nightbound side when it becomes night, which happens when a player casts no spells during their own turn. It becomes day again when a player casts two or more spells in a turn. **Child of the Pack**, the red-green signpost uncommon, is a 2/5 that can make Wolf tokens by day and pumps your team as a trampling Werewolf by night. Double-faced cards like these are covered in our guide to [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

### Exploit

Exploit comes back from Dragons of Tarkir on the set's zombies. When a creature with exploit enters, you may sacrifice a creature, and if you do, you get a bonus. **Overcharged Amalgam** (rare) is a flash 3/3 flier that counters a spell or ability when it exploits.

## Limited

Each two-color pair has a signpost uncommon:

- **White-blue (Brine Comber): Spirits and Auras.** Fliers, disturb and Auras that make extra Spirits.
- **Blue-black (Skull Skaab): Zombies and exploit.** Sacrifice creatures for value and make Zombie tokens.
- **Black-red (Bloodtithe Harvester): Vampires and Blood.** Make Blood, use it, and turn it into removal.
- **Red-green (Child of the Pack): Werewolves.** Flip day and night to your advantage.
- **Green-white (Sigardian Paladin): Humans and counters.** Training and +1/+1 counters.
- **White-black (Markov Purifier): life gain.** Gain life in small chunks and draw off it.
- **Blue-red (Wandering Mind): spells.** Instants, sorceries and noncreature payoffs.
- **Black-green (Ancient Lumberknot): toughness matters.** Big-butted creatures that deal damage equal to their toughness.
- **Red-white (Markov Waltzer): aggro.** Hasty fliers and team pumps.
- **Green-blue (Vilespawn Spider): self-mill.** Fill the graveyard and cash it in.

Removal was the backbone of most decks. Bleed Dry, Flame-Blessed Bolt, Abrade and Fierce Retribution were the commons people fought over, alongside two-for-one creatures like **Rot-Tide Gargantua**, which forces each opponent to sacrifice a creature when it exploits. Blood tokens quietly improved every deck that made them, since smoothing out draws matters a lot over a long Limited game. The set also drafted alongside Midnight Hunt in Innistrad: Double Feature, which reprinted both sets together in early 2022. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Crimson Vow's Standard life was unusually long. Wizards' move to a three-year Standard window meant there was no rotation in 2023, so the set stayed legal from November 2021 until summer 2024, when the arrival of Bloomburrow removed it along with Midnight Hunt, Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty and Streets of New Capenna.

In its first months, Standard was dominated by cards from other sets, and Wizards' January 2022 bans hit Alrund's Epiphany, Divide by Zero and Faceless Haven rather than anything from Crimson Vow. Its cards became central after Dominaria United arrived that fall. At the World Championship in October 2022, Esper Midrange made up most of the Standard field, and **Wedding Announcement** was the single most-played card in the event, a three-mana enchantment that made bodies or drew cards and then turned into an anthem. **Bloodtithe Harvester** was the most-played creature, a key part of Rakdos and Grixis Midrange lists. Nathan Steuer won the event with Grixis Midrange. No Crimson Vow card was ever banned in Standard.

As of October 2026, Crimson Vow's biggest Constructed footprint is in Pioneer. Bloodtithe Harvester has been a regular in Rakdos Midrange, Sorin the Mirthless has shown up in black and Rakdos Vampires builds, and the reprint of Thalia, Guardian of Thraben gave white aggro decks access to a classic tax effect. No Crimson Vow card is banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy. The set has little presence in Modern and Legacy, where faster cards dominate.

Commander is where the set gets the most play today. Toxrill, the Corrosive, Hullbreaker Horror, Cultivator Colossus and Necroduality are popular in their own colors, Voldaren Estate shows up in nearly every Vampire deck, and Olivia, Crimson Bride and Edgar, Charmed Groom both lead Vampire builds. ## Cards from Crimson Vow worth owning

- **Bloodtithe Harvester**: a Pioneer staple that's both a threat and removal at two mana.
- **Wedding Announcement**: a top Standard card of its era and still strong in white token decks.
- **Sorin the Mirthless**: card advantage and fliers in black midrange and Vampire decks.
- **Hullbreaker Horror**: a Commander and control finisher that's very hard to answer.
- **Toxrill, the Corrosive**: a one-sided board wipe on a body, and a Commander favorite.
- **Cultivator Colossus**: a big payoff for land-heavy Commander decks.
- **Voldaren Estate**: a utility land that every Vampire deck wants.
- **Thalia, Guardian of Thraben**: a Pioneer-legal printing of a long-running staple.

Crimson Vow is easiest to understand through Blood: count your token makers and your discard outlets, and the set's best cards fall into place.
