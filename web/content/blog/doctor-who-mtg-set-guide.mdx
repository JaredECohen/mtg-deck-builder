Doctor Who is a Magic: The Gathering Universes Beyond release from October 2023 that brought the long-running BBC science-fiction series to the game as four preconstructed Commander decks. It landed in the show's 60th anniversary year, and each deck covers a different stretch of the Doctor's history, from the black-and-white era to the most recent Doctors, plus a deck of villains. The headline mechanics were time travel, paradox, villainous choice and Doctor's companion, a new way to pair two commanders.

## The story

Doctor Who follows the Doctor, a Time Lord from the planet Gallifrey who travels through time and space in the TARDIS, a ship disguised as a blue police box that is famously bigger on the inside. When a Doctor is close to death, they regenerate into a new body and personality, which is how the role has passed between many actors since 1963. The Doctor almost never travels alone: companions, usually humans from Earth, join for a stretch of adventures and keep the alien hero grounded.

Rather than adapting one story, the Magic release samples the whole show. Each deck is built around an era. **Blast from the Past** covers the classic Doctors, with the Fourth Doctor in front and companions like Sarah Jane Smith, Jo Grant, Romana II, Ace and the robot dog K-9, Mark I. **Timey-Wimey** covers the Ninth, Tenth and Eleventh Doctors of the 2005 revival, with Rose Tyler, Donna Noble, Martha Jones, Amy Pond and Rory Williams, and also includes the War Doctor from the 50th anniversary special. **Paradox Power** covers the Twelfth and Thirteenth Doctors and their friends, including Yasmin Khan, Clara Oswald, Bill Potts, Graham O'Brien and Ryan Sinclair, along with River Song and the Paternoster Gang (Madame Vastra, Jenny Flint and Strax).

**Masters of Evil** turns the camera around. Its face commander is Davros, Dalek Creator, the scientist who built the Daleks, and its supporting cast includes several incarnations of the Master and Missy, the Cyber-Controller, the Dalek Emperor, Rassilon, the Weeping Angels and the Vashta Nerada. The card names and flavor text lean on well-known episodes and catchphrases ("Allons-y!", "Everybody Lives!"), and many cards reference specific stories like the Pyramid of Mars or the City of Death.

There is no single plot to resolve. The set's "story" is the show's structure: a Doctor, a TARDIS, a revolving door of companions and a gallery of returning villains, all of it translated into card abilities.

### Mechanics in the decks

- **Doctor's companion:** a companion with this ability can be your second commander if the other commander is a Doctor. Sarah Jane Smith, Rose Tyler, Yasmin Khan, Donna Noble, Clara Oswald and K-9, Mark I all have it. It works like Partner, but only one side of the pair has to be a Time Lord Doctor.
- **Time travel:** for each suspended card you own and each permanent you control with a time counter, you may add or remove a time counter. In practice it speeds up suspend cards and extends or shortens anything that counts time counters. The Tenth Doctor can time travel three times in one activation.
- **Paradox:** an ability word for effects that trigger when you cast a spell from anywhere other than your hand, such as from exile, the graveyard or the top of your library. The Thirteenth Doctor and Iraxxa, Empress of Mars both use it.
- **Villainous choice:** an opponent chooses between two bad outcomes. Davros, Dalek Creator forces each opponent who lost 3 or more life in a turn to either let you draw or discard a card themselves. It feels cruel by design: whichever option they pick, you benefit.
- **Returning mechanics:** suspend, rebound, cascade, myriad and investigate show up throughout the decks.

## How it plays

These decks were built to be played against each other straight out of the box, and they do that reasonably well. All four are three-color decks with a strong commander and a clear plan, which makes them good teaching tools for a first Commander game.

**Blast from the Past** is a value deck. The Fourth Doctor lets you treat the top of your library as an extra card in hand for historic spells, and Sarah Jane Smith turns those spells into Clues. It rarely wins fast, but it rarely runs out of things to do.

**Timey-Wimey** is the most unusual deck. The Tenth Doctor turns each attack into a delayed free spell, and time travel effects let you speed those spells up. It asks you to count time counters carefully, and it rewards planning a turn or two ahead.

**Paradox Power** wants spells cast from exile, the graveyard or the top of the library. The Thirteenth Doctor grows a creature each time and untaps everything with counters, which helps you attack and still hold back blockers. Cascade and foretell-style cards keep the paradox triggers flowing.

**Masters of Evil** is the most aggressive and the best at punishing a full table. Davros, the Daleks and the villainous choice cards make opponents lose life or give you resources, and its Dalek tokens have menace. It's also the deck most likely to make enemies at the table, which is part of its charm.

As preconstructed decks, they sit in the casual middle of the power range. Under the official [Commander brackets](/blog/commander-brackets-explained), an unmodified deck is a reasonable fit for the core bracket, though adding Game Changers like Farewell or stronger tutors will push it up.

### Upgrading the decks

The cleanest upgrades lean into each commander's specific trigger. Timey-Wimey wants more cards with suspend and more cheap ways to add or remove time counters. Paradox Power gets better with every cascade or "cast from exile" card you add. Blast from the Past wants more cheap legendary permanents and artifacts. Masters of Evil benefits from more ways to make each opponent lose a little life every turn, so Davros triggers reliably. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Doctor Who is a Commander product, so it never entered Standard, Pioneer or Modern. Its new cards are legal in Commander, Legacy and Vintage, but none became a fixture in competitive Legacy or Vintage decks.

In Commander, its impact has been steady rather than explosive. Quantum Misalignment, Sonic Screwdriver and Everybody Lives! spread well beyond the Doctor Who decks, and the clone and myriad cards (Auton Soldier, The Master, Multiplied) found homes in token and copy decks. Clara Oswald became a favorite partner because she doubles a Doctor's triggers and can be any color. The reprinted Farewell is on the official Game Changers list as of October 2026, which matters if your playgroup uses the brackets. None of the set's new cards are banned in Commander.

## Cards from Doctor Who worth owning

- **Quantum Misalignment:** two copies of your best creature for one card, in any blue deck.
- **Sonic Screwdriver:** a mana rock that keeps being useful later in the game.
- **Everybody Lives!:** a two-mana answer to a lethal attack or a combo turn.
- **Clara Oswald:** the best companion for any Doctor commander.
- **The Master, Multiplied:** a strong engine for token-copy decks.
- **Auton Soldier:** a flexible clone that also attacks multiple opponents with myriad.
- **Farewell:** a modular board wipe you can aim at whatever your opponents rely on most.

The Doctor Who decks are some of the most flavorful Commander products Wizards has made, and they play well against each other as a four-player set. If you're picking just one, Timey-Wimey is the most distinctive and Masters of Evil is the most aggressive. New to the format?
