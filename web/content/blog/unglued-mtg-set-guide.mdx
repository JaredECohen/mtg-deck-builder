Unglued is the first Magic: The Gathering "Un-set," a deliberately silly expansion released in August 1998 that broke the game's rules for laughs. It introduced the silver border that marks cards as not tournament-legal, and it filled its pages with chickens, dice, coin flips and cards that ask you to stand up, cluck or wear denim.

## What the set was for

By 1998, Magic had a few years of tournament play behind it and a culture full of in-jokes. Mark Rosewater, fresh off leading design on Tempest, pitched a set that would poke fun at all of it, and Unglued was the result. It was Magic's first supplemental set released outside the normal run of expansions, and it set the template for every Un-set that followed: cards that can't work in a tournament, paired with a silver border so nobody mistakes them for legal ones.

The humor runs in a few directions. Some cards parody famous Magic cards: Blacker Lotus is a riff on Black Lotus that you tear into pieces when you use it, and Jack-in-the-Mox rolls a die to decide which color of mana it makes. Some mock the culture around the game: Look at Me, I'm the DCI lets you ban a card for the rest of the match, and Timmy, Power Gamer is named for the player profile who loves big creatures. Others just ask you to do something ridiculous at the table, like Mesa Chicken, which gains flying only if you stand up, flap your arms and cluck.

Unglued also printed the first of a long Un-set tradition: oversized, impossible creatures. B.F.M. (Big Furry Monster) is a single 99/99 split across two cards, and you need both in hand to cast it for fifteen black mana.

## Mechanics

Unglued didn't have keyword mechanics the way a normal set does. Instead, it had recurring themes.

### Dice and coin flips

Many cards roll a six-sided die. Chicken Egg rolls each upkeep and hatches into a 4/4 Giant Bird on a 6. Growth Spurt pumps a creature by the result of a roll, and Strategy, Schmategy rolls to pick among wildly different effects, from nothing at all to destroying every land. Flock of Rabid Sheep flips coins to make Sheep tokens, and Goblin Bookie lets you reroll a die or reflip a coin.

### Physical actions

Plenty of Unglued cards care about what you do in the room. Knight of the Hokey Pokey prevents damage if you do the Hokey Pokey, Hurloon Wrangler has "denimwalk," and Censorship damages any player who says a chosen word. Charm School asks you to balance it on your head, and Chaos Confetti is torn up and thrown onto the table to destroy whatever the pieces touch.

### Cards that cross games

A cycle of "Double" cards carries an effect into your next game against the same player. Double Deal deals 3 damage now and 3 more at the start of your next game together, and Double Take, Double Dip, Double Play and Double Cross follow the same pattern for cards, life, lands and discard.

### Chickens, birds and silly creature types

Chickens and birds are the set's mascot animals. Chicken à la King pumps every Bird whenever someone rolls a 6, Poultrygeist grows when creatures die, and Fowl Play turns a creature into a vanilla 1/1 Bird. Creature types are part of the joke too, from Clamfolk to a creature type of "Gus."

## Limited

Unglued was sold in booster boxes built for drafting, and with 10-card packs and a small card pool, it's a quick, chaotic draft. Strategy matters less than in a normal set: dice decide a lot, cards like Krazy Kow can blow up the board on a bad roll, and some of the best plays are getting your opponent to say a censored word. The colors still have identities. White has small fliers and protection, blue has tricks and odd counterspells like Denied!, black has recursive creatures like Deadhead, red has random burn and Goblins, and green has big creatures and dice-driven pump.

If you draft it today, it works best as a party format with friends rather than a competitive event. For a closer look at how drafting works in general, see [draft versus sealed](/blog/draft-vs-sealed-limited-guide).

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Unglued had no Constructed meta, and that was the point. Silver-bordered cards have never been legal in Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper or Commander, and that remains true as of October 2026. The only cards from the set that are legal anywhere are the five black-bordered full-art basic lands, which work in every format and are still prized by players who like distinctive basics.

Its influence shows up in later sets instead. Unglued established the Un-set line, which continued with Unhinged, Unstable and Unfinity. Unfinity in 2022 finally printed black-bordered Un-cards that are tournament-legal and used an acorn symbol for the casual-only ones; our [Unfinity set guide](/blog/unfinity-mtg-set-guide) covers that change. In Commander, Unglued cards are rule-zero cards: fine if your playgroup agrees, not legal by default. Our guide to [Commander rule zero](/blog/commander-rule-zero-guide) explains how to bring them up, and [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh) covers the format itself.

## Cards from Unglued worth owning

- **The full-art basic lands:** legal in every format and an early example of full-art basics.
- **Blacker Lotus:** the most iconic Un-card parody, and a conversation piece in any collection.
- **B.F.M. (Big Furry Monster):** a two-card creature that's a fixture of casual "Un" cubes.
- **Chicken à la King and Chicken Egg:** the mascots of the set and of Un-set humor in general.
- **Timmy, Power Gamer:** a fun casual-cube card with a famous name.

Unglued is a time capsule of 1990s Magic humor. Collect the basics, draft it for laughs, and save the silver-bordered cards for game nights where everyone's in on the joke.
