To read a Magic: The Gathering card, go from top to bottom: the name and mana cost tell you what it is and what it costs, the type line tells you what kind of card it is and when you can play it, the text box tells you what it does, and the numbers in the bottom corner tell you how big it is in a fight. Everything else on the card (the set symbol, the italic text, the small print at the bottom) is useful context but rarely changes how the card plays.

## The Name

The card's name sits in the top-left corner. It is how you refer to the card in conversation and deckbuilding, and it matters for rules too: constructed formats let you play at most four copies of any card with the same name (basic lands excepted), and Commander allows only one.

Two cards with the same name are the same card, even with different sets or artwork.

## The Mana Cost

The symbols in the top-right corner are the card's **mana cost**: what you must pay to cast it. There are two main kinds of symbol:

- **Colored mana symbols.** A white sun, a blue water drop, a black skull, a red fireball, or a green tree. Each one must be paid with one mana of that color.
- **Generic mana.** A number inside a gray circle. It can be paid with mana of any color, or colorless mana.

Take Serra Angel, which costs a gray 3 followed by two white symbols. You pay it with two white mana plus any three other mana, for five mana in total.

### X in a mana cost

Some cards have an X in their cost. You choose the value of X when you cast the spell and pay that much extra generic mana. A spell that costs X plus one red, cast with X equal to 4, costs five mana total, and the card's text uses that 4 wherever it says X.

### Other symbols you may see

A circle with a diamond shape inside stands for colorless mana specifically, which must be paid with colorless mana such as that from a Wastes land, not with colored mana. Half-and-half circles are **hybrid** symbols, which can be paid with either of the two colors shown.

## Mana Value

**Mana value** is the total amount of mana in a card's cost, ignoring color. Serra Angel's mana value is 5. Lightning Bolt's is 1. Lands have a mana value of 0. (Older articles and cards call this "converted mana cost.")

Many cards refer to mana value, and deckbuilders use it to plan when they can cast their spells, which is your [mana curve](/blog/mana-curve-explained).

For a card with X in its cost, X counts as 0 everywhere except on the stack. So a card costing X plus one red has a mana value of 1 in your hand or graveyard.

## The Type Line

The line across the middle of the card, just below the art, is the **type line**. It has up to three parts.

### Supertypes

Some cards have a supertype before their card type. The two you will meet most:

- **Basic.** Basic lands (Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest, and Wastes) are "Basic Land." You can put as many copies of a basic land in your deck as you like.
- **Legendary.** A legendary permanent represents a unique character or object. If you control two legendary permanents with the same name, you choose one to keep and put the other into your graveyard. In Commander, your commander is usually a legendary creature.

### Card types

Next comes the card type itself: Land, Creature, Instant, Sorcery, Artifact, Enchantment, Planeswalker, or Battle. A card can have more than one, such as "Artifact Creature." The card type tells you whether the card stays on the battlefield (a permanent) and when you can cast it. Instants can be cast at almost any time; most other spells only during your own main phase. Our [how to play Magic guide](/blog/how-to-play-magic-the-gathering) covers what each type does in play.

### Subtypes

After a long dash come the subtypes. For creatures these are creature types: Llanowar Elves is a "Creature — Elf Druid," meaning it is both an Elf and a Druid. Serra Angel is a "Creature — Angel." Subtypes matter when other cards care about them, like a card that boosts every Elf you control.

Other types have their own subtypes. Basic lands have land types (a Forest is a "Basic Land — Forest"), enchantments can be Auras, and artifacts can be Equipment.

## The Set Symbol and Rarity

On the right side of the type line is the **set symbol**, a small icon that shows which set the card was printed in. Its color shows the card's **rarity**:

| Symbol color | Rarity |
|---|---|
| Black | Common |
| Silver | Uncommon |
| Gold | Rare |
| Orange-red | Mythic rare |

Rarity reflects how often a card appears in booster packs, not how strong it is. Lightning Bolt has been printed at common.

## The Text Box

The large box below the type line holds three different kinds of text, and telling them apart is a big part of learning how to read a Magic card.

### Rules text

Plain, upright text is **rules text**: what the card actually does. Lightning Bolt's rules text says it deals 3 damage to any target. Llanowar Elves has a single ability: tap it to add one green mana. Each paragraph is usually a separate ability.

Abilities written as "cost: effect," with a colon, are **activated abilities**: you pay the part before the colon to get the part after. Abilities starting with "when," "whenever," or "at" are **triggered abilities** that happen automatically when their condition is met. Anything else is usually a **static ability**, which is simply always true while the card is on the battlefield.

### Keywords and reminder text

Many abilities are shortened to a single **keyword**. Serra Angel's text box says just "Flying, vigilance." Flying means it can only be blocked by creatures with flying or reach, and vigilance means attacking doesn't cause it to tap.

Cards meant for newer players often follow a keyword with **reminder text**: a short explanation in italics, inside parentheses. Reminder text has no rules effect of its own. It is just a reminder, and the card works the same with or without it.

### Flavor text

Italic text without parentheses, usually at the bottom of the text box and often separated by a thin line, is **flavor text**. It is a quote or story snippet that adds to the world of the game. It never affects how the card plays, so you can skip it during a game.

## Power and Toughness

Creatures have two numbers in a box in the bottom-right corner, written like 4/4. The first is **power**, the damage the creature deals in combat. The second is **toughness**, how much damage it can take in a single turn before it is destroyed.

- Llanowar Elves is a 1/1.
- Serra Angel is a 4/4.

Damage wears off at the end of each turn, and effects like "+2/+2" add to both numbers.

## Loyalty on Planeswalkers

Planeswalkers have a loyalty number in their bottom-right corner instead of power and toughness. That is how many loyalty counters the planeswalker enters the battlefield with.

The text box lists loyalty abilities, each with a cost such as +1, −2, or 0. Once per turn, during your main phase, you can activate one of them, adding or removing that many loyalty counters. Damage dealt to a planeswalker removes loyalty counters too, and a planeswalker with no loyalty is put into its owner's graveyard. Battles work similarly but show defense instead of loyalty.

## The Collector Information

The small print along the bottom of the card is the **collector information**. Depending on the card's age it can include the collector number (its position in the set), a letter for its rarity, the set code, the language, and the artist's name. None of it matters during play; it helps with collecting and trading.

## Worked Examples

Putting it all together with three classic cards:

- **Llanowar Elves.** One green mana, mana value 1. Creature — Elf Druid. One activated ability that taps it for one green mana. A 1/1. It is a creature that helps you cast your other spells sooner, but summoning sickness means you can't tap it for mana the turn it arrives.
- **Lightning Bolt.** One red mana, mana value 1. Instant, so you can cast it during either player's turn. It deals 3 damage to any target: a creature, a player, a planeswalker, or a battle. No power or toughness, because it isn't a permanent.
- **Serra Angel.** Three generic plus two white, mana value 5. Creature — Angel. Flying and vigilance. A 4/4 that can attack in the air and still stay back to block.

## Double-Faced and Adventure Cards

A few special card layouts are worth recognizing.

**Double-faced cards** have a front face and a back face, and no normal card back. Delver of Secrets, for example, starts as a small 1/1 creature and can transform into Insectile Aberration, a 3/2 flier, when its condition is met. You cast it using its front face. A transformed permanent's mana value is the mana value of its front face.

**Adventure cards** have a second, smaller spell printed inside the text box. Bonecrusher Giant is a creature with an instant adventure called Stomp. You can cast either half. If you cast the adventure, the card goes into exile instead of the graveyard after it resolves, and later you can cast the creature from exile. You get two spells from one card.

## Where to Look Up Keywords and Rulings

If you meet a keyword you don't recognize, there are two reliable places to check:

- **The Comprehensive Rules.** Wizards of the Coast publishes the full rules of Magic for free. Its glossary at the end defines every game term, and the section on keyword abilities explains each keyword in detail.
- **Official card rulings.** Card databases such as Gatherer list the current official rules text (called the Oracle text) for every card, along with rulings for tricky interactions. If an old printing reads differently from a newer one, the Oracle text is what counts.

## Takeaway

Read every card in the same order: name, cost, type line, rules text, then power and toughness or loyalty. Skip flavor text during play, treat reminder text as a hint, and look up unfamiliar keywords in the Comprehensive Rules glossary. After a few games, reading a card takes a second.
