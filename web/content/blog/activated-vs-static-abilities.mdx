Magic: The Gathering has three main kinds of abilities. An **activated ability** is written as "cost: effect" and works only when you choose to pay the cost. A **triggered ability** starts with "when," "whenever" or "at" and happens automatically when its event occurs. A **static ability** is simply true while the card is on the battlefield, with no cost and no trigger. Once you can tell these three apart at a glance, most rules text becomes easy to read.

This guide shows how to recognize each type, what the tap symbol in a cost means, why mana abilities are special, and the mistakes new players make with each one. If you're still learning where rules text sits on a card, start with [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

## What counts as an ability

An ability is a piece of rules text on a card that does something, whether that's letting you pay for an effect, reacting to an event, or changing the rules while the card is around. Keywords like flying and trample are abilities too. They're shorthand for longer rules, and each keyword belongs to one of the three types.

Not everything on a card is an ability, though. The text of an instant or sorcery is the spell's effect: it happens when the spell resolves and then the card goes to the graveyard. Reminder text, the italic text in parentheses, isn't an ability either. It just explains a keyword. And flavor text, the italic quote at the bottom, has no rules meaning at all.

The quick way to sort rules text:

| If the text looks like... | It's a... | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A cost, then a colon, then an effect | Activated ability | "`{T}`: Add `{G}`." |
| Starts with "When," "Whenever" or "At" | Triggered ability | "When this creature enters, draw a card." |
| A plain statement with no colon and no trigger word | Static ability | "Creatures you control get +1/+1." |

## Activated abilities: "cost: effect"

An activated ability always has a colon. Everything before the colon is the cost, and everything after it is the effect. You decide when to use it, and you can use it as many times as you can pay the cost, unless the card says otherwise.

Some classic examples:

- **Prodigal Sorcerer:** "`{T}`: Prodigal Sorcerer deals 1 damage to any target." The cost is tapping the creature.
- **Shivan Dragon:** "`{R}`: Shivan Dragon gets +1/+0 until end of turn." The cost is one red mana, and you can pay it again and again to pump the Dragon as high as your mana allows.
- **Mother of Runes:** "`{T}`: Target creature you control gains protection from the color of your choice until end of turn."

Costs aren't limited to mana. A cost can include tapping the card, paying life, sacrificing a permanent, discarding a card, or removing counters. You have to pay the whole cost to activate the ability, and you can't activate it if you can't pay.

### When you can activate

By default, you can activate an ability any time you have priority, which is the same timing as casting an instant. That means you can use most activated abilities on your opponent's turn, in response to a spell, or in the middle of combat.

Some abilities limit this. "Activate only as a sorcery" means you can use it only during your own main phase, when the stack is empty, the same as casting a sorcery. Equip is the best-known example: you can move Equipment between your creatures only at sorcery speed. Planeswalker loyalty abilities are also activated abilities with sorcery timing, and you can activate only one loyalty ability per planeswalker each turn.

### Activated abilities use the stack

When you activate an ability, it goes on the stack just like a spell, and both players get a chance to respond before it resolves. The stack is how Magic decides the order things happen in, and the [guide to the stack](/blog/how-the-stack-works-mtg) explains it step by step.

Two consequences surprise new players:

- **An ability isn't a spell.** Counterspell says "Counter target spell," so it can't stop an activated ability. You need a card that specifically counters abilities, like Stifle.
- **The ability doesn't depend on its source.** Once an ability is on the stack, it exists on its own. If your opponent activates Prodigal Sorcerer and you destroy the Sorcerer in response, the damage still happens. The cost was already paid when the ability was activated.

### Abilities that work from other zones

Most activated abilities work only while the card is on the battlefield. A few say or imply they work elsewhere. Cycling is an activated ability that you use from your hand: you pay the cycling cost, discard the card, and draw a new one. If an ability works outside the battlefield, the card will make that clear.

## The tap symbol in costs

The tap symbol, `{T}`, in a cost means "turn this permanent sideways." A tapped permanent can't pay a `{T}` cost again until it untaps, which normally happens during your untap step. So a `{T}` ability can usually be used once per turn cycle.

There's also an untap symbol, `{Q}`, which means the permanent must be tapped and you untap it as part of the cost. It's rare, but you'll see it on a handful of older cards.

### Tapping and summoning sickness

A creature can't pay a `{T}` or `{Q}` cost unless you've controlled it continuously since the start of your most recent turn. This is the same rule that stops a freshly cast creature from attacking, and it's why you can't cast Llanowar Elves and tap it for mana on the same turn. Haste removes the restriction.

The rule applies only to creatures. A noncreature artifact like Sol Ring can tap for mana the turn it comes down. Abilities without the tap symbol in their cost aren't affected either: you can pump a Shivan Dragon on the turn you cast it. The [summoning sickness guide](/blog/summoning-sickness-explained) goes through the edge cases.

## Mana abilities don't use the stack

A mana ability is an activated ability that could add mana, doesn't target, and isn't a loyalty ability. "`{T}`: Add `{G}`" on Llanowar Elves is a mana ability. So is the ability every basic land has: a Forest is treated as having "`{T}`: Add `{G}`" printed on it.

Mana abilities follow a special rule: **they don't go on the stack.** They resolve immediately when you activate them, and nobody can respond. That's what makes paying for spells smooth. If tapping a land for mana used the stack, your opponent could respond to every single land you tapped.

What this means in practice:

- **You can't respond to a mana ability.** If your opponent taps Birds of Paradise for mana, you can't kill the Birds in response to stop the mana. You'd have to remove it before they activate it.
- **You can activate mana abilities while casting a spell.** The rules let you activate mana abilities as you pay for a spell, which is how you normally tap lands to cast things.
- **Mana spells are different.** Dark Ritual adds mana, but it's an instant, not an ability. It goes on the stack like any spell and can be countered.

Birds of Paradise is a nice example of how abilities stack up on one card. It has flying, which is a static ability, and "`{T}`: Add one mana of any color," which is an activated mana ability.

## Static abilities: always on

A static ability has no cost and no trigger. It just describes something that's true while the card is in the relevant zone, which for permanents is usually the battlefield. You don't activate it, and it doesn't use the stack.

Examples:

- **Glorious Anthem:** "Creatures you control get +1/+1." While Glorious Anthem is on the battlefield, your creatures are bigger. The moment it leaves, they shrink back.
- **Keywords like flying, vigilance, deathtouch, trample and first strike** are static abilities. A creature with flying simply has it; nothing needs to happen for it to work.
- **Rules changes and restrictions.** A card that says spells cost more to cast, or that a creature can't block, is using a static ability.

Because static abilities don't use the stack, there's no window to respond to them. If your opponent controls Glorious Anthem, the only way to stop its effect is to remove the Anthem itself. And as soon as the card is gone, the effect stops immediately, with no delay.

### Static abilities that work outside the battlefield

Some static abilities work from your hand, graveyard or the stack. A card that says "This spell costs `{1}` less to cast for each..." is a static ability that works while you're casting it. Flashback is another: it's a static ability that lets you cast an instant or sorcery from your graveyard by paying its flashback cost.

## Triggered abilities: "when," "whenever" and "at"

A triggered ability waits for an event and then goes on the stack automatically. The trigger word tells you what kind of event:

- **"When"** usually marks a one-time event, like a creature entering or dying.
- **"Whenever"** marks something that can happen repeatedly, like casting a spell or attacking.
- **"At"** marks a specific point in the turn, like "at the beginning of your upkeep."

You don't pay anything or choose to use a triggered ability. If the event happens, the ability triggers, even if you'd rather it didn't, unless the text says "you may." Triggered abilities use the stack, so players can respond to them.

Triggered abilities have enough wrinkles of their own, like how several triggers are ordered and what happens if the source leaves the battlefield, that they get their own deep dive in [triggered abilities explained](/blog/mtg-triggered-abilities-explained). For this guide, the important part is spotting them: if the text begins with one of those three trigger words, it's a triggered ability, not an activated or static one.

Keywords can be triggered abilities too. Prowess, for example, is a triggered ability: whenever you cast a noncreature spell, the creature gets +1/+1 until end of turn.

## Common mistakes with ability types

- **Trying to counter an ability with Counterspell.** Counterspells that say "target spell" only hit spells.
- **Killing a creature to stop its ability after it's activated.** Too late: the ability already exists on the stack. Remove the creature before its controller can use it.
- **Tapping a new creature for its `{T}` ability.** Summoning sickness applies to tap costs, not just attacking.
- **Expecting a window to respond to mana.** Mana abilities resolve instantly.
- **Forgetting that static effects end immediately.** When an anthem leaves, a creature that was surviving only because of the bonus toughness can die right away.

## The takeaway

Look for the shape of the text. A colon means an activated ability you choose to use, and it goes on the stack unless it makes mana. A trigger word means an ability that happens on its own when its event occurs. Everything else is a static ability that's simply on. Learn to read those three shapes and you'll know exactly when each card on the table can affect the game.
