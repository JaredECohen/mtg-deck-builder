Guilds of Ravnica is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in October 2018 that returned to the city-plane of Ravnica for the third time. It is the first of three sets that build to War of the Spark, with Nicol Bolas closing in on the city while the guilds argue among themselves. The set features five of Ravnica's ten guilds (Boros, Dimir, Golgari, Izzet and Selesnya), each with its own mechanic, and it delivered cards that still matter years later: Arclight Phoenix, Assassin's Trophy and a full cycle of shock lands.

## The story

It is autumn on Ravnica, and the mood is noir: fog, shadows and a city that senses something coming. Nicol Bolas, the elder dragon planeswalker, has spent years laying plans to take the plane, and his agents have worked their way into the guilds. The question running through the whole block is which guild leaders will resist him and which have already been bought.

The main plot follows Ral Zarek, the Izzet storm mage and planeswalker. Niv-Mizzet, the dragon who leads the Izzet League, reveals that he has known about planeswalkers all along and that Bolas is planning an invasion. He gives Ral a near-impossible job: convince all ten guilds to rewrite the Guildpact, the magical treaty that binds them, so that Niv-Mizzet can hold its power and stand against Bolas. As a backup, Ral is to turn an old Izzet project into a beacon that can call other planeswalkers to Ravnica for help.

Ral's job is complicated by Vraska. The gorgon planeswalker now rules the Golgari Swarm, and in secret she is still working for Bolas. When the guild leaders finally gather for a summit, Isperia, the sphinx who leads the Azorius Senate, argues for cooperation, and the meeting breaks for the night. Vraska assassinates Isperia before it can resume, and the summit collapses. The Assassin's Trophy card shows that moment. The Story Spotlight cards Thousand-Year Storm, Guild Summit, Assassin's Trophy and Firemind's Research all mark points in this arc.

Much of this plot was told in Django Wexler's novella The Gathering Storm, which covers both Guilds of Ravnica and Ravnica Allegiance. The official web fiction during the set's release was a series of shorter stories about ordinary Ravnicans in each of the five guilds. The arc continues in Ravnica Allegiance, where the other five guilds take the spotlight, and pays off in War of the Spark, when Ral's beacon turns out to be exactly what Bolas needed.

## Mechanics

Each guild gets one mechanic. Every one of them is simple on its own, which made Guilds of Ravnica a popular set to teach with.

### Mentor (Boros)

When a creature with mentor attacks, it puts a +1/+1 counter on another attacking creature with lower power. Boros Challenger is a two-mana 2/3 that grows an attacking 1/1 into a 2/2 every turn. Mentor rewards going wide with small creatures and attacking every turn.

### Surveil (Dimir)

Surveil N means look at the top N cards of your library and put any of them into your graveyard, the rest back on top in any order. It's a better version of scry when your deck uses the graveyard. Thought Erasure is a two-mana discard spell with surveil 1 attached, and Doom Whisperer can pay 2 life at a time to surveil 2 as often as you like.

### Undergrowth (Golgari)

Undergrowth is an ability word: the card gets better based on the number of creature cards in your graveyard. Kraul Harpooner is a two-mana 3/2 with reach that can fight an opposing flier on entry, growing for every creature card in your graveyard. Undergrowth pairs naturally with surveil-style self-mill and with trading creatures in combat.

### Jump-start (Izzet)

Jump-start lets you cast an instant or sorcery a second time from your graveyard by discarding a card in addition to its normal cost, then exile it. Chemister's Insight draws two cards, then draws two more later from the graveyard. Jump-start turns spells into two-for-ones and fills the graveyard for cards that count instants and sorceries.

### Convoke (Selesnya)

Convoke lets your creatures help pay for a spell: each creature you tap pays for one generic mana or one mana of its color. Conclave Tribunal is a four-mana removal spell that often costs one or two mana if you have a board. Venerated Loxodon adds a twist by putting a +1/+1 counter on each creature that helped cast it.

### Split cards and Guildgates

Every guild got a split card, with a cheap hybrid half and a bigger gold half, such as Find // Finality for Golgari. Each booster also replaced the basic land slot with a Guildgate, which made two-color draft decks easier to build.

## Limited

Guilds of Ravnica was drafted as a five-guild format rather than the usual ten color pairs, so each guild had more cards supporting it and it was easier to stay on plan. Most drafters committed to one guild and leaned on the Guildgates and Lockets to splash.

| Guild | Colors | Plan | Gold uncommon example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boros | Red-White | Fast mentor aggro | Boros Challenger |
| Dimir | Blue-Black | Surveil and card advantage | Dimir Spybug |
| Golgari | Black-Green | Graveyard value with undergrowth | Golgari Findbroker |
| Izzet | Blue-Red | Instants, sorceries and jump-start | Crackling Drake |
| Selesnya | Green-White | Go wide and convoke | Conclave Guildmage |

Removal at common was relatively plentiful: Luminous Bonds and Capture Sphere were reliable Pacifism-style answers, while Direct Current (with jump-start), Deadly Visit and Artful Takedown gave red, black and Dimir players ways to kill things outright. Lava Coil at uncommon was a premium pick. The format rewarded aggressive Boros and Selesnya decks for punishing slow starts, while Golgari and Dimir decks won longer games. The guild structure also made it a good format for learning to read signals; our guide to [drafting signals](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals) explains how.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Guilds of Ravnica arrived alongside Dominaria, Ixalan, Rivals of Ixalan and Core Set 2019, and it immediately shaped Standard. In its first months, the most-played decks drew heavily from the set:

- **Golgari Midrange** combined Assassin's Trophy, Find // Finality, Midnight Reaper and Doom Whisperer with Jadelight Ranger from Rivals of Ixalan. It grinded out games with card advantage and flexible removal.
- **Boros Aggro** used Legion Warboss and mentor creatures to swarm the board.
- **Izzet Drakes** paired Crackling Drake, Arclight Phoenix, Chemister's Insight and cheap cantrips to cast many spells per turn.
- **Jeskai Control** used Deafening Clarion as a sweeper alongside Teferi, Hero of Dominaria, with Niv-Mizzet, Parun showing up in some Izzet and Jeskai spell decks.
- **Mono-red** decks picked up Runaway Steam-Kin and Experimental Frenzy.

No Guilds of Ravnica card was banned in Standard during its two years in the format, though the set was in Standard during several bans of other sets' cards.

The set's most famous card outside Standard is Arclight Phoenix. In Modern, Izzet Phoenix rose quickly as a top deck by looting Phoenixes into the graveyard and bringing them back with cheap spells. Faithless Looting, one of the deck's key enablers, was banned in Modern in August 2019, but Phoenix decks adapted. In Pioneer, Izzet Phoenix has been one of the format's longest-lasting archetypes, using cheap blue and red spells to trigger the Phoenix. As of October 2026, Arclight Phoenix is legal in every format it's eligible for.

Assassin's Trophy saw play in Modern and Legacy Golgari decks as a catch-all answer, and Knight of Autumn became a common sideboard card in Pioneer and Modern. The shock lands are permanent fixtures of Pioneer and Modern mana bases. In Commander, Niv-Mizzet, Parun is a popular spellslinger commander, and Divine Visitation, Thousand-Year Storm and Chromatic Lantern are widely played.

## Cards from Guilds of Ravnica worth owning

- **Arclight Phoenix**: the engine of Izzet Phoenix in Pioneer and Modern.
- **Shock lands**: Sacred Foundry, Watery Grave, Overgrown Tomb, Steam Vents and Temple Garden belong in many Pioneer, Modern and Commander mana bases.
- **Assassin's Trophy**: efficient, flexible removal for Golgari decks in many formats.
- **Knight of Autumn**: a flexible sideboard creature for green-white decks.
- **Niv-Mizzet, Parun**: a classic Izzet Commander leader.
- **Divine Visitation** and **Thousand-Year Storm**: Commander favorites for token and spell decks.

## The takeaway

Guilds of Ravnica is a set where each guild had a clear identity, and that clarity is why it drafted well and why its Constructed cards found homes so quickly. If you're building a Pioneer deck from it today, start with Arclight Phoenix and the shock lands; the Izzet Phoenix plan has been a reliable Pioneer archetype for years.
