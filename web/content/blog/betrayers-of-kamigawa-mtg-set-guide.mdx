Betrayers of Kamigawa is the second set of Magic: The Gathering's original Kamigawa block, released in February 2005. It continued the story of a war between the mortal world and its spirits on a plane inspired by feudal Japan, and it introduced ninjas to the game with the ninjutsu mechanic. It also printed Umezawa's Jitte, one of the most dominant Equipment cards ever made, along with Goryo's Vengeance, Ninja of the Deep Hours and the five Shoals.

## The story

Kamigawa is a plane where every object, place and idea has a spirit, called a kami, living in a spirit world beside the material one. Mortals and kami had lived side by side for ages, until the kami suddenly turned on the mortal world. That war, the Kami War, had been raging for about twenty years when the block begins.

The cause was a crime. Takeshi Konda, the daimyo who ruled from the fortress of Eiganjo, used a forbidden ritual to steal something from the spirit world: part of the offspring of O-Kagachi, the greatest of the kami. Konda wanted immortality and unrivaled power. The kami, without knowing exactly what had been taken, attacked everything.

Betrayers of Kamigawa covers the middle of the story, told in Scott McGough's novel *Heretic*. Konda's daughter, Princess Michiko, is effectively under house arrest in Eiganjo, trying to get word out to the one person who might help her uncover what her father did. That person is Toshiro Umezawa, a thief and outlaw samurai with a talent for dark magic, who has bargained with a powerful kami of the night. Toshi pursues his own goals as much as Michiko's, and his old dealings with the ogre shaman Hidetsugu come back to haunt him; the set's Heartless Hidetsugu shows what the ogre became. Meanwhile, the nezumi (rat-folk) and the ninja clans move through the shadows, led by figures like Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni.

The "betrayers" of the title are everywhere: Konda betrayed the kami and his own people, Toshi betrays nearly everyone he works with, and several clans switch sides. The story ends in Saviors of Kamigawa, the block's third set, where Michiko and Toshi free the stolen spirit and end the war.

## Mechanics

### Ninjutsu

Ninjutsu is Betrayers' signature mechanic. If you attack and a creature isn't blocked, you can pay the ninjutsu cost and return that unblocked attacker to your hand, then put the Ninja from your hand onto the battlefield tapped and attacking. The Ninja then deals combat damage and triggers its ability.

Ninja of the Deep Hours is the classic example: for two mana you swap in a 2/2 that draws a card when it hits. Mistblade Shinobi bounces a creature, Okiba-Gang Shinobi makes the player discard two cards, and Throat Slitter destroys a nonblack creature. The trick is that you can return a creature with an enters-the-battlefield ability and cast it again, and that the Ninja appears after blockers have been declared, so it can't be blocked that turn.

### Offering

Offering appears on the five legendary Patrons. You can cast a Patron at instant speed by sacrificing a creature of the matching type, like a Fox for Patron of the Kitsune or a Moonfolk for Patron of the Moon, and paying only the difference in mana costs. It is a way to cheat a big creature out early in a tribal deck.

### Shoals

The five Shoals are rare Arcane instants with an alternative cost: instead of paying mana, you can exile a card of the same color from your hand, and that card's mana value becomes X. Blazing Shoal pumps a creature's power, Disrupting Shoal counters a spell with matching mana value, Sickening Shoal gives a creature -X/-X, Shining Shoal redirects damage, and Nourishing Shoal gains life. They are "pitch" spells in the tradition of Force of Will, and they let you cast a spell while tapped out.

### Returning mechanics

- **Bushido**: a creature gets +N/+N when it blocks or becomes blocked. Samurai like Isao, Enlightened Bushi and Indebted Samurai have it.
- **Splice onto Arcane**: when you cast an Arcane spell, you can reveal a card with splice and pay its splice cost to add its effect to the spell. Goryo's Vengeance, Horobi's Whisper and Torrent of Stone all splice.
- **Soulshift**: when a Spirit with soulshift dies, you can return a smaller Spirit from your graveyard to your hand.
- **Flip cards**: creatures like Callow Jushi and Budoka Pupil collect ki counters from Spirit and Arcane spells, then flip into a legendary Spirit.
- **Genju**: a cycle of Auras that enchant a basic land and turn it into a creature for two mana, returning to your hand if the land dies. Genju of the Spires makes a 6/1 out of a Mountain.

## Limited

Betrayers was drafted with two packs of Champions of Kamigawa followed by one pack of Betrayers: until Mirrodin Besieged in 2011, block drafts opened the oldest set first. Champions-block Limited was slow and grindy compared with many formats, with lots of Spirits, soulshift recursion and defensive samurai. Betrayers sped it up a little with ninjutsu, which rewarded cheap evasive creatures that could connect and then swap into a Ninja.

What mattered:

- **Blue-black Ninjas** were the set's flagship archetype. Cheap fliers and unblockable creatures carried Ninjas like Ninja of the Deep Hours, Mistblade Shinobi and Throat Slitter into combat.
- **Spirit and Arcane decks** used flip cards, Waxmane Baku and splice spells for value over a long game.
- **Removal**: Horobi's Whisper and Torrent of Stone at common were premium, and splice let you copy their effects onto later Arcane spells.
- **Samurai** in white and green used bushido to win combat on both offense and defense.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard, 2005-2007

Betrayers joined a Standard dominated by Mirrodin block artifacts. In March 2005 Wizards banned a group of Affinity cards in Standard, and in the format that followed, Umezawa's Jitte became the card every creature deck wanted. Each time the equipped creature deals combat damage, Jitte gets two counters, and each counter can kill an x/1, pump the creature or gain life. It won almost every creature combat it was involved in. In Kamigawa Block Constructed, the format was often described as Jitte decks against Gifts Ungiven control.

The rest of the set's Standard impact was smaller than Jitte's; most of Betrayers' other famous cards earned their reputations later, in Eternal formats and Commander. Betrayers left Standard with the rest of Kamigawa block when Lorwyn released in October 2007.

### Modern

Every Kamigawa card is legal in Modern except those on its banned list, and Betrayers has had a busy Modern life:

- **Goryo's Vengeance** is the core of a long-running Modern combo deck that discards a big legendary creature and returns it with haste on turn two or three.
- **Blazing Shoal** was banned in Modern in September 2011, after Infect decks used it to pump an Inkmoth Nexus or Glistener Elf for a sudden, often turn-two or turn-three, poison kill. It is still banned as of October 2026.
- **Umezawa's Jitte** was on Modern's original 2011 banned list and stayed there for nearly fifteen years, until Wizards unbanned it in the May 18, 2026 Banned and Restricted update. In the months since, players have been trying it in Boros, Izzet and Dimir creature decks. As of October 2026 it is too early to say how big a role it will settle into.

### Legacy, Pauper and Commander

In Legacy, Jitte has been an option for creature decks for years, and Disrupting Shoal gives blue decks a free, if narrow, counterspell. In Pauper, Ninja of the Deep Hours is a long-time blue staple. Commander players love Ink-Eyes, Toshiro Umezawa, Kira and the Ninjas, and Yuriko-style Ninja decks lean on Betrayers' ninjutsu cards. As of October 2026, nothing from Betrayers is banned in Legacy or Commander.

## Cards from Betrayers of Kamigawa worth owning

- **Umezawa's Jitte**: legal again in Modern and still one of the strongest Equipment cards in Legacy.
- **Goryo's Vengeance**: a Modern and Legacy combo staple.
- **Ninja of the Deep Hours**: the most-played ninjutsu card, from Pauper to Commander.
- **Kira, Great Glass-Spinner**: protection for your creatures in Commander.
- **Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni** and **Toshiro Umezawa**: favorites for black Commander decks.
- **Final Judgment**: an exile-based board wipe that gets around indestructible.

## The takeaway

Betrayers of Kamigawa is a small, flavorful set that keeps coming back. The ninjutsu mechanic still shows up in new sets, Goryo's Vengeance has been a Modern combo deck for over a decade, and Jitte's 2026 return to Modern gave the set a new chapter. If you want to try it, an aggressive creature deck carrying Jitte is the obvious Modern starting point.
