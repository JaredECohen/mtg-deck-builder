Commander 2011 is the first official product Wizards of the Coast made for the Commander format of Magic: The Gathering, a set of five preconstructed 100-card decks released in June 2011. Each deck was built around a new three-color legend, and the 51 new cards included future staples like Command Tower, Chaos Warp, Scavenging Ooze and Flusterstorm.

## The decks

Commander began as a fan-made format, often called EDH (Elder Dragon Highlander), and by 2011 it had grown big enough that Wizards made a dedicated product for it. Each deck covered one of the five "wedge" color combinations (an ally pair plus the color both oppose), and each included three new legends: a face commander, a second three-color legend and a two-color legend that could lead a smaller version of the deck.

### Heavenly Inferno (white-black-red)

Led by **Kaalia of the Vast**, a flier that puts an Angel, Demon or Dragon from your hand onto the battlefield attacking whenever she attacks an opponent. The deck is packed with big fliers to cheat out, plus creature removal. Its other legends are Tariel, Reckoner of Souls and Basandra, Battle Seraph.

### Mirror Mastery (blue-red-green)

Led by **Riku of Two Reflections**, who lets you pay extra to copy your instants and sorceries and the other nontoken creatures you play. The deck ramps hard into big spells and creatures worth copying. Its other legends are Animar, Soul of Elements and Edric, Spymaster of Trest.

### Counterpunch (black-green-white)

Led by **Ghave, Guru of Spores**, a Fungus that trades +1/+1 counters for Saprolings and Saprolings back for counters. The deck is a tokens and counters engine. Its other legends are Karador, Ghost Chieftain and Vish Kal, Blood Arbiter.

### Political Puppets (red-white-blue)

Led by **Zedruu the Greathearted**, who draws you cards and gains you life for each permanent you've given away, and can donate permanents to opponents. It's a politics deck that wins by shaping who attacks whom. Its other legends are Ruhan of the Fomori and Nin, the Pain Artist.

### Devour for Power (black-green-blue)

Led by **The Mimeoplasm**, an Ooze that exiles two creature cards from graveyards as it enters, becoming a copy of one and getting counters equal to the other's power. The deck fills graveyards to power that up. Its other legends are Damia, Sage of Stone and Skullbriar, the Walking Grave.

## Mechanics and themes

### Join forces

Join forces is a group mechanic: starting with you, each player may pay any amount of mana, and everyone gets the effect based on the total. Minds Aglow lets each player draw X cards, Collective Voyage gives each player X basic lands, and Alliance of Arms makes Soldiers for everyone. These cards create table talk, since everyone benefits but you choose the timing.

### The Vows

The Vow cycle (Vow of Duty, Vow of Flight, Vow of Lightning, Vow of Malice and Vow of Wildness) are Auras that boost a creature, but that creature can't attack you or your planeswalkers. You can put one on your own creature, or put one on an opponent's creature to protect yourself while pointing it at someone else.

### Multiplayer politics

Many cards care about multiple opponents. Hydra Omnivore copies combat damage onto each other opponent, Syphon Flesh makes every other player sacrifice a creature, and Martyr's Bond punishes everyone when your permanents die. Ruhan of the Fomori attacks a random opponent each turn, and Scythe Specter makes each opponent discard.

## How it plays

The 2011 decks feel different from modern precons. They were designed to play against each other at a casual table, with a mostly basic mana base and lots of slower, swingy cards. That makes them a good fit for low-power groups, and each one still has a clear plan.

Each deck also gives you three commanders to choose from. Kaalia is the most explosive, since a single attack can put a seven-mana Angel or Dragon onto the battlefield for free, but she draws attention fast. Riku and Animar both lead strong Temur decks, Ghave and Karador lead long-running Abzan builds, and The Mimeoplasm and Damia are graveyard and card-draw commanders with plenty of room to upgrade.

The easy upgrades are:

- **Mana:** two- and three-color lands and better mana rocks. The original decks run many taplands and basics.
- **Interaction:** cheaper removal and counterspells to replace six- and seven-mana answers.
- **Focus:** cut cards that don't serve the commander, and add more payoffs, like more Angels, Demons and Dragons for Kaalia or more sacrifice outlets for Ghave.

For the full process, read [how to upgrade a Commander precon](/blog/how-to-upgrade-a-commander-precon), and if you're new to the format, start with [what is Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh).

## The meta: how it played in Commander

Commander 2011 shaped the format in a few lasting ways. Command Tower debuted here and became one of the most-played lands in Commander. Chaos Warp gave red one of its few answers to any permanent and still shows up in red decks today. The product also proved there was demand for Commander precons, and new Commander decks became a yearly release starting with Commander 2013.

Several of its legends became long-term favorites. Kaalia of the Vast became a famously aggressive commander that many tables treat as a priority threat, and Edric, Spymaster of Trest found a home in competitive Commander as a tempo commander that draws heaps of cards. Animar, Ghave, Karador and Riku all remained popular for years.

Outside Commander, two cards made the jump. Flusterstorm became a Legacy and Vintage staple for counter wars, and a later reprint in Modern Horizons made it legal in Modern. Scavenging Ooze was reprinted in Magic 2014, which put it into Standard and Modern, where it was a graveyard-hate staple for years.

As of October 2026, none of the 51 new Commander 2011 cards are banned in Commander. For how power level works today, see [Commander brackets explained](/blog/commander-brackets-explained).

## Cards from Commander 2011 worth owning

- **Command Tower:** the default land for any multicolor Commander deck.
- **Chaos Warp:** red's flexible answer to anything.
- **Scavenging Ooze:** graveyard hate on a body for green decks.
- **Flusterstorm:** a cheap counterspell for Legacy, Vintage and cEDH stack fights.
- **Hornet Queen:** a strong top-end blocker and finisher.
- **Homeward Path:** cheap insurance against control-stealing effects.
- **Kaalia of the Vast and Edric, Spymaster of Trest:** commanders that still lead strong decks.
