The Brothers' War is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in November 2022 that finally put the game's oldest origin story on cards: the artifact war between Urza and Mishra on ancient Dominaria. It is an artifact set through and through, with a new mechanic called prototype, the return of unearth and meld, and a pair of brothers you can literally fuse into a planeswalker and a giant Phyrexian war machine.

| --- | --- |
| Set code | BRO |
| Release date | November 18, 2022 |
| Plane | Dominaria, during the Brothers' War (with a present-day framing story) |

Packs also carried a bonus sheet of Retro Artifacts (old-frame reprints such as Mishra's Bauble, under their own set code) and, in Set and Collector Boosters, a handful of Transformers cards from Universes Beyond. Neither bonus sheet was Standard-legal.

## The story

The Brothers' War tells two stories at once. The main one is history: the conflict that, in Magic's timeline, happened thousands of years before any of the modern planeswalkers were born. The second is a present-day frame that picks up where Dominaria United left off, with the Phyrexian invasion closing in.

### The war itself

Urza and Mishra are brothers who grow up as apprentices to the archaeologist Tocasia, digging through the ruins of the ancient Thran civilization. In the Caves of Koilos they find a powerstone that splits in two when they both touch it. Urza ends up with the Mightstone and Mishra with the Weakstone, and the stones seem to amplify everything that's already wrong between them. A fight between the brothers leaves Tocasia dead, and Mishra flees into the desert.

The two rise along separate paths. Mishra is taken in by the Fallaji, tames an ancient dragon engine with his Weakstone and becomes one of their leaders. Urza becomes a master artificer in the city of Kroog and marries Kayla bin-Kroog, its princess. A peace meeting between them collapses, Mishra's dragon engines destroy Kroog, and the rivalry turns into a decades-long war fought with ever-larger machines.

The real villain hides in Mishra's camp. The Brotherhood of Gix, servants of Phyrexia, slowly corrupt Mishra until he is more machine than man. Characters on both sides change allegiances along the way: Ashnod, Mishra's ruthless apprentice, turns on him, and Urza's apprentice Tawnos survives her cruelty to help end the war. The final battle comes on the forested island of Argoth, home to the nature spirit Titania, which both brothers have been stripping for resources. When Urza sees that his brother has become a Phyrexian, he activates the Golgothian Sylex. The blast wipes out Argoth and Mishra, plunges Dominaria into an ice age, and ignites Urza's planeswalker spark, with the two halves of the powerstone fused into his eyes.

### The present-day frame

In the present, the heroes need to understand the Sylex to use it against Phyrexia. With the help of Saheeli's inventions and Kaya's spirit magic, Teferi reaches back through time to witness how Urza triggered it the first time. He learns that the Sylex is fueled by a person pouring their whole self into it, and the set ends with Teferi lost somewhere in time while the other planeswalkers gather for an assault on New Phyrexia. That thread continues in Phyrexia: All Will Be One and March of the Machine.

## Mechanics

### Prototype

Prototype is the set's new mechanic. A prototype card is a large colorless artifact creature with a smaller, colored, cheaper version printed in a box inside its text box. You choose which version to cast. Phyrexian Fleshgorger is a 7/5 for seven generic mana, or a 3/3 for `{1}{B}{B}`, and either way it has menace, lifelink and a ward cost of paying life equal to its power. Prototype gives every one of these cards two jobs: an early play when you need board presence and a top-end threat when the game goes long.

### Unearth

Unearth returns from Shards of Alara. You pay the unearth cost to bring a creature back from your graveyard with haste, but it's exiled at the end of the turn (or if it would leave the battlefield). Scrapwork Cohort, a four-mana 3/1 that also makes a 1/1 Soldier, can unearth for `{2}{W}` and make another token. Unearth turns your graveyard into a second hand for aggressive decks and a supply of sacrifice fodder for black-red.

### Meld

Meld lets two specific cards combine into one oversized card. The Brothers' War has three pairs: Urza, Lord Protector with The Mightstone and Weakstone (making Urza, Planeswalker), Mishra, Claimed by Gix with Phyrexian Dragon Engine (making Mishra, Lost to Phyrexia), and Titania, Voice of Gaea with the land Argoth, Sanctum of Nature (making Titania, Gaea Incarnate). Each half is playable alone, so meld is a bonus, not a requirement.

### Powerstones

Powerstone tokens tap for one colorless mana that can't be spent on nonartifact spells. Plenty of commons make them as a side effect: Excavation Explosion deals 3 damage to any target and leaves a tapped Powerstone behind. In a set full of expensive artifacts and prototype creatures, that restricted mana is still real ramp, and it also powers activated abilities.

The Brothers' War played as a mid-speed artifact format. Prototype creatures meant almost every deck had sensible early plays and a late game, and Powerstones let the ramp decks reach seven- and eight-mana artifacts surprisingly often. The common removal was solid (Excavation Explosion, Overwhelming Remorse, Prison Sentence and Shoot Down), and the five self-mill commons, such as Fallaji Archaeologist, were useful glue for graveyard and unearth decks. The colorless prototype creatures were flexible picks early in a draft because they fit any deck at their full cost. ## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### In Standard (November 2022 to August 2025)

The Brothers' War arrived in a Standard dominated by black midrange decks, and it fed them. Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor slotted into black aggro and Rakdos Midrange lists, sometimes alongside Gix's Command, and remained part of Rakdos lists into 2024. Brotherhood's End became one of the most widely played sweepers of the period, because it wipes out boards of small creatures (and, in its other mode, cheap artifacts) for three mana. Mishra's Foundry gave red and black decks a land that could attack.

The set's Soldiers package was the backbone of Azorius Soldiers in 2023, with Myrel, Shield of Argive and Siege Veteran rewarding a board full of Soldiers while Myrel shut off the opponent's instant-speed answers during your turn. No Brothers' War cards were banned in Standard during its run. The set rotated out on August 1, 2025, along with Dominaria United, Phyrexia: All Will Be One, March of the Machine and its Aftermath set, when Edge of Eternities released. For how rotation works in general, see our [rotation explainer](/blog/mtg-rotation-explained).

### In Pioneer, Modern and Legacy

Brotherhood's End crossed into every major format as a sweeper or anti-artifact sideboard card. Cityscape Leveler found a home in Pioneer as a target for Karn, the Great Creator in Mono-Green Devotion sideboards before Karn was banned in Pioneer in December 2023. Third Path Iconoclast showed up in Izzet spell decks, and Haywire Mite became a one-mana toolbox answer for green decks, especially those that search for artifacts. As of October 2026, no card from the main Brothers' War set is banned in Pioneer, Modern or Legacy.

### In Commander

The Brothers' War is a deep Commander set. Urza, Lord Protector, Mishra, Claimed by Gix and Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor are all popular commanders, Portal to Phyrexia is a dramatic top end for big-mana decks, and Titania, Voice of Gaea leads land-recursion strategies. Two Commander precons released alongside the set, and the Retro Artifacts sheet added nostalgic old-frame versions of classic artifacts.

## Cards from The Brothers' War worth owning

- **Brotherhood's End**: a flexible three-mana sweeper that answers small-creature and cheap-artifact strategies in every format.
- **Mishra's Foundry**: a colorless manland that fits any artifact or creature-light deck.
- **Gix, Yawgmoth Praetor**: card draw stapled to a three-mana body, good in aggressive black decks and Commander.
- **Haywire Mite**: a cheap, colorless-cost answer to artifacts and enchantments for green decks.
- **Portal to Phyrexia**: a game-ending Commander bomb for any deck that ramps or cheats out big artifacts.
- **The Mightstone and Weakstone**: ramp, card draw or removal in one artifact, and a meld half for Urza.

## The takeaway

The Brothers' War gave Magic a satisfying version of its oldest story and a set of artifacts that still earn deck slots years later. If you're building in Pioneer, start with the cards that proved themselves in Standard (Brotherhood's End, Gix and Mishra's Foundry) and build a black-red shell around them.
