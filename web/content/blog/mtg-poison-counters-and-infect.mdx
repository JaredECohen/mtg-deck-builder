Poison counters are a second way to lose in Magic: The Gathering (MTG): a player with ten or more poison counters loses the game, no matter how much life they have. Infect is the keyword that delivers them. A creature with infect deals damage to players in the form of poison counters instead of life loss, and deals damage to creatures in the form of -1/-1 counters instead of regular damage.

That means a poison deck ignores your life total entirely. It only needs to connect for ten total, which is far less than the 20 life you start with in most formats. This guide explains how poison counters work, how infect changes damage, how toxic and proliferate fit in, and how to play against a poison deck.

## What poison counters are

A poison counter is a counter given to a player, not to a card. Players can have counters just like permanents can, and poison is the most common kind. You track them with a die or a note next to your life total.

The rule is simple:

- **Ten or more poison counters and you lose.** This is a state-based action, which means the game checks it constantly. The moment you reach ten, you lose; there's no window to respond.
- **Life total doesn't matter.** A player at 40 life with ten poison counters loses just as surely as a player at 1 life.
- **Poison counters usually stay.** Very few cards remove them, so once you have some, plan as if they're permanent.

In Two-Headed Giant, poison is shared by the team, and a team loses at fifteen poison counters instead of ten. The [Two-Headed Giant guide](/blog/two-headed-giant-mtg) covers the rest of that format's shared rules.

If you're new to counters in general, including +1/+1 counters, loyalty counters and how counters on players differ from counters on permanents, see [MTG counters explained](/blog/mtg-counters-explained).

## How infect works

Infect is a keyword ability on creatures and sometimes other permanents. It changes what damage from that source does.

| Damage dealt to | Normal damage | Damage from a source with infect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A player | That player loses that much life | That player gets that many poison counters |
| A creature | Marked on the creature until end of turn | That many -1/-1 counters on the creature |

A few consequences are worth spelling out:

- **Infect damage to a player causes no life loss.** A 2/2 with infect that connects gives two poison counters and the player's life total doesn't change. Gaining life doesn't help against it.
- **Infect damage to a creature is permanent.** Normal damage wears off at the end of the turn. -1/-1 counters stay. A 4/4 blocked by a 1/1 infect creature survives as a 3/3, and stays a 3/3.
- **It's still damage.** Anything that prevents damage, like a fog effect, also prevents infect damage. Anything that redirects or doubles damage affects it too.
- **Infect works with any damage, not just combat.** An infect creature that deals damage through an ability also gives poison counters or -1/-1 counters.

### -1/-1 counters and +1/+1 counters

If a creature has both +1/+1 counters and -1/-1 counters, they cancel out in pairs as a state-based action. A creature with three +1/+1 counters that gets blocked by a 2/2 infect creature ends up with one +1/+1 counter. That's one reason +1/+1 counter decks hold up a little better against infect than other creature decks.

### Wither

Wither is a related keyword. A source with wither deals damage to creatures as -1/-1 counters, just like infect, but its damage to players is normal life loss. Infect is wither for creatures plus poison for players.

## Classic infect cards

Infect has been printed on many cards over the years. A few long-standing examples show how the mechanic plays:

- **Glistener Elf:** a one-mana green 1/1 with infect. Small, but a single pump spell turns it into a real threat.
- **Blighted Agent:** a two-mana blue 1/1 with infect that can't be blocked.
- **Inkmoth Nexus:** a land that can pay to become a 1/1 flying artifact creature with infect until end of turn. Because it's a land the rest of the time, sorcery-speed removal can't easily touch it.

These creatures are small on purpose. An infect deck doesn't need big bodies, because it doesn't need to deal 20. It needs to deal ten, usually all at once, by attacking with one infect creature and pumping it.

### Why pump spells matter so much

Pump spells are the engine of most infect decks. Giant Growth gives a creature +3/+3 until end of turn, which on an infect creature means three extra poison counters. Mutagenic Growth gives +2/+2 and costs a green Phyrexian mana, which can be paid with 2 life instead of mana, so it can be cast even with no lands untapped.

A typical infect turn looks like this: attack with an unblockable or evasive infect creature, wait for blocks, then cast two or three pump spells. A 1/1 that gets +3/+3, +2/+2 and +4/+4 deals ten poison counters in one hit. The [combat tricks guide](/blog/combat-tricks-guide) explains the timing that makes this work.

## Toxic

Toxic is a newer keyword that gives poison counters without replacing normal damage. A creature with toxic followed by a number gives that many poison counters to any player it deals combat damage to, in addition to the damage itself.

The differences from infect matter:

- **Toxic only works on combat damage to players.** Noncombat damage and damage to creatures work normally.
- **The damage still causes life loss.** A 2/2 with toxic 1 that connects makes the player lose 2 life and get one poison counter.
- **The poison amount is fixed.** Pumping a creature with toxic increases its damage, but not the number of poison counters. Toxic 1 gives one poison counter whether the creature is a 1/1 or a 6/6.

Toxic is slower than infect, but it threatens two ways to win at once. Some cards also care about whether an opponent is "corrupted," which means that opponent has three or more poison counters, and get stronger once that happens.

## Proliferate

Proliferate is a keyword action that makes counters grow. When you proliferate, you choose any number of permanents and players that already have counters, and each one gets one more counter of each kind it already has.

For poison decks, proliferate is a finisher. Every opponent with at least one poison counter can be given another, and you can skip any player or permanent you don't want to add counters to, like your own creature with -1/-1 counters on it. Repeated proliferate can push a player from a few poison counters to ten without another attack.

Atraxa, Praetors' Voice is the best-known example. It's a four-color legendary creature that proliferates at the beginning of your end step, which is why it's a popular Commander for decks built around counters of every kind.

## Poison in Commander

Poison is especially strong in Commander, because the format starts players at 40 life but keeps the losing threshold at ten poison counters. An infect creature effectively deals four times as much "damage" relative to an opponent's life total as a normal creature would.

The trade-off is that the rest of the table often treats a poison player as the biggest threat, since lifegain and big life totals do nothing to slow it down. Poison decks in Commander usually pair evasive infect or toxic creatures with proliferate so they can spread poison across several opponents.

## How to play against poison

Playing against an infect deck feels different from playing against normal aggro. The key shift is that your life total no longer measures how safe you are; your poison count does.

- **Kill infect creatures early.** They're usually small, so cheap removal handles them. Don't wait for a better target.
- **Hold removal for the pump.** The most effective moment to kill an infect creature is in response to the first pump spell, which wastes that spell too.
- **Watch for manlands.** Inkmoth Nexus and similar lands only become creatures when they're about to attack or block. Instant-speed removal is the way to answer them.
- **Count to ten, not twenty.** At six or seven poison counters, a single unblocked infect creature with one pump spell is lethal. Block or chump accordingly.
- **Don't count on lifegain.** Gaining life does nothing against infect. It does help against toxic, since toxic damage still causes life loss.
- **Be careful with blocks.** Blocking an infect creature with a big creature still leaves -1/-1 counters on your blocker. Sometimes that's fine; sometimes it shrinks your best attacker for the rest of the game.
- **Use fog effects and damage prevention.** Because infect is damage, preventing it stops the poison too.

Many of the general principles for [playing against aggro](/blog/how-to-play-against-aggro), like valuing early blockers and cheap removal, apply here as well.

## The takeaway

Poison counters give you a second win condition: ten and the opponent loses, regardless of life. Infect turns damage into poison counters for players and permanent -1/-1 counters for creatures, toxic adds a fixed number of poison counters on top of normal combat damage, and proliferate grows whatever counters are already there. Against poison, count to ten, kill infect creatures before the pump spells come, and stop trusting your life total.
