Urza's Destiny is the Magic: The Gathering expansion released in June 1999 as the third and final set of the Urza's block. Its story follows Urza's Bloodline Project, a centuries-long attempt to breed the perfect heroes to stop a Phyrexian invasion of Dominaria. It was the first expert-level set printed under the Sixth Edition rules, and it contains some of the most powerful cards of the era: Masticore, Yawgmoth's Bargain, Metalworker, Academy Rector and the Donate that powered Trix.

## The story

Urza's Destiny closes the Urza's block, which tells the history leading up to the Weatherlight Saga. By this point, Urza has spent thousands of years preparing for Phyrexia's return. He has built the *Weatherlight*, the flying ship at the center of the later story, and he has an arsenal of artifacts called the Legacy. What he lacks are the people who can use them. The set's story, told in Loren Coleman's novel *Bloodlines*, the last book of the Artifacts Cycle, is about how he makes them.

From his academy on the island of Tolaria, Urza launches the **Bloodline Project**. Researchers led by the wizard Barrin and the chancellor Rayne quietly arrange marriages among Dominaria's strongest families for generation after generation, aiming to produce champions and an heir worthy of the Legacy. Urza takes a special interest in the warrior lines of Benalia and Jamuraa, including the Capashen family. When he decides one hero won't be enough, the project expands to create the **Metathran**, an artificial race of soldiers grown in vats and hidden in stasis for the coming war. The ethics of all this split the academy, and Barrin becomes one of Urza's sharpest critics.

One researcher, **Gatha**, goes further than anyone approved. Expelled for experimenting with Phyrexian material, he takes his methods to the Keldon warriors, creating unstable, battle-crazed hybrids under the warlord Kreig. Meanwhile, Phyrexia learns what Urza is doing. The Phyrexians strike the project's sites, killing thousands, including much of the Capashen line. The infant **Gerrard Capashen** survives only because the silver golem **Karn** carries him to safety in Jamuraa. The Phyrexians also build Rath, the artificial plane they'll use as a staging ground for the invasion.

Urza's Destiny ends with the pieces in place: a ship, an heir and an army waiting in stasis. The story moves on to Gerrard and the *Weatherlight* crew, and Phyrexia finally invades in the Invasion block. ## Mechanics

### Sixth Edition rules

Sixth Edition's rules overhaul took effect in mid-1999. It introduced the stack, turned interrupts into instants and made mana abilities uncounterable. Urza's Destiny was the first expert set printed for those rules, and the most visible change is the type line: creatures say "Creature" instead of the old "Summon." For a refresher on reading modern cards, see [how to read a Magic card](/blog/how-to-read-a-magic-card).

### Graveyard triggers

Many cards in the set do something when they go to the graveyard. **Academy Rector** (rare, `{3}{W}`) is a 1/2 that, when it dies, lets you exile it to put any enchantment from your library onto the battlefield. **Gamekeeper** (uncommon) can be exiled when it dies to dig up the next creature in your library. **Aura Thief** steals every enchantment on the battlefield when it dies.

### Cycling from play and echo

Cycling (discard a card to draw a new one) continued from Urza's Saga, and Destiny added creatures that cycle from the battlefield: "`{2}`, sacrifice this creature: draw a card." **Yavimaya Elder** (common) does that and also fetches two basic lands to your hand when it dies, which made it a top value card. Echo returns too: **Keldon Champion** (uncommon) is a 3/2 haste creature that deals 3 to a player on entry but must be paid for again on your next upkeep.

### Seers and Scents

These cards scale with how many cards of a color you reveal from your hand. **Scent of Cinder** (common) deals damage equal to the number of red cards you reveal. **Cinder Seer** (uncommon) is the repeatable creature version. They rewarded mono-colored decks.

## Limited

Booster draft was growing in popularity in 1999, but Urza block Limited isn't remembered the way modern formats are, and records of how Urza's Destiny drafted are thin. What's clear from the card file is that the set was built around graveyard value. Creatures like **Kingfisher** (a flier that draws a card when it dies), **Plague Dogs** and **Disease Carriers** traded well, and **Yavimaya Elder** smoothed mana. Removal included **Scent of Nightshade** and **Chime of Night**, and echo creatures like Keldon Champion hit hard early. Rather than invent archetypes for a format with little surviving coverage, the honest summary is that Destiny packs rewarded mono- or near-mono-colored decks that could use the reveal cards and that valued creatures with death triggers.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

### Standard (1999-2000)

Urza's Destiny joined a Standard (still often called Type II) that had just been through "Combo Winter," when Wizards banned a string of Urza's Saga and Urza's Legacy cards. Destiny's power showed up quickly. **Yawgmoth's Bargain** fueled black combo and control decks known as "Zvi Bargain" and later "Sabre Bargain." **Masticore** became one of the most feared threats of its time, played across many artifact and control decks. **Replenish** decks returned piles of enchantments at once, often with Opalescence to turn them into creatures. **Covetous Dragon** and **Opposition** lists also appear among the period's notable archetypes. The whole Urza's block left Standard when Invasion arrived in November 2000.

### Extended: Trix

In the Extended format, **Donate** made history. Paired with Ice Age's Illusions of Grandeur, it let a player gain 20 life, then hand the enchantment to the opponent. Once its growing cumulative upkeep went unpaid and it left the battlefield, its controller lost 20 life. Necropotence drew the cards to set it up, and the deck, known as **Trix**, became one of the most famous combo decks ever.

### Legacy and Vintage

Urza's Destiny predates Eighth Edition, so it isn't Modern-legal, but it's important in eternal formats. **Yawgmoth's Bargain** has been banned in Legacy for the format's entire life and was unrestricted in Vintage in 2018. **Metalworker** was banned in Legacy from the format's start until 2009 and now appears in artifact decks. **Academy Rector** still powers enchantment combos, especially with Omniscience. **Replenish** was banned in Legacy at the start and unbanned in 2007. For format differences, see [MTG formats explained](/blog/mtg-formats-explained).

### Commander

In [Commander](/blog/what-is-commander-edh), **Thran Dynamo** is a near-automatic include in many artifact and colorless decks. **Rofellos, Llanowar Emissary** is banned. **Yawgmoth's Bargain** is banned too. **Academy Rector**, **Pattern of Rebirth**, **Opalescence**, **Replenish**, **Treachery**, **Elvish Piper**, **Gamekeeper** and **Urza's Incubator** (a cost reducer for one creature type) are all popular.

## Cards from Urza's Destiny worth owning

- **Thran Dynamo**: a Commander staple ramp artifact.
- **Academy Rector**: a key enchantment combo piece in Legacy and Commander.
- **Yawgmoth's Bargain**: a Vintage card-draw engine.
- **Treachery**: a free creature steal in blue decks.
- **Masticore**: one of the most iconic creatures of the late 1990s.
- **Urza's Incubator**: a strong cost reducer for tribal Commander decks.
- **Opalescence** and **Replenish**: the core of an enchantment combo shell.
