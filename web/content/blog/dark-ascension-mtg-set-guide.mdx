Dark Ascension is the second set of the Innistrad block in Magic: The Gathering, released in February 2012. It takes the gothic horror plane of Innistrad at its lowest point, with the monsters winning and humanity's guardian angel still missing, and it added two new mechanics, undying and fateful hour. For a 158-card small set, it left a remarkable trail of staples: Thalia, Guardian of Thraben, Lingering Souls, Huntmaster of the Fells, Faithless Looting and Grafdigger's Cage among them.

## The story

Innistrad is a world of vampires, werewolves, ghosts, zombies and the frightened humans who live among them. For centuries the humans survived because of Avacyn, an archangel created by the vampire planeswalker Sorin Markov to keep the balance between predators and prey. Her church, the Church of Avacyn, protected villages with blessed silver and holy wards.

By the start of the block, Avacyn has vanished. The wards are failing, and Dark Ascension is the point where that loss really bites. Its expansion symbol, the Innistrad symbol turned inward, was meant to show humanity huddling together, and the set's tagline was "Command the Night." The monster tribes have the upper hand, villages are being overrun, and the humans are on the edge of extinction.

Three threads carry the plot.

- **Sorin comes home.** Sorin returns to Innistrad to find out why the angel he made has gone silent. He learns that Avacyn is sealed inside the Helvault, the great silver monolith in the city of Thraben that was built to imprison demons. She had trapped the demon lord Griselbrand inside it, and the prison took her too. Sorin, Lord of Innistrad is his planeswalker card from this set.
- **Mikaeus falls.** Mikaeus, the Lunarch, the head of the church, is murdered by the necromancer Geralf Cecani and raised as an undead servant. His card, Mikaeus, the Unhallowed, is a six-mana Zombie that hates Humans and grants undying to the rest of your monsters. It's the set's starkest image of how far the church has fallen.
- **The humans hold the line.** Thalia, a knight-cathar of Thraben, leads the defense of the city while the church's leadership is gone. Her card, Thalia, Guardian of Thraben, is one of the most-played white creatures ever printed.

Meanwhile, the planeswalker Liliana Vess is hunting Griselbrand, one of the demons she made her pact with, which pulls her toward the Helvault as well. Dark Ascension ends with the plane still in darkness. The resolution comes in the next set, Avacyn Restored, when the Helvault is broken open, Avacyn returns with the demons she had imprisoned, and the tide turns back toward humanity.

## Mechanics

Dark Ascension kept almost everything from Innistrad and added two new pieces. If you're new to two-sided cards, our guide to [double-faced cards](/blog/double-faced-cards-explained) explains how they work.

### Undying

When a creature with undying dies, if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, it comes back to the battlefield with a +1/+1 counter. So it effectively has two lives, and the second one is bigger. Undying was designed as a mirror of persist from Shadowmoor, which returns creatures with a -1/-1 counter instead.

Young Wolf is the purest example: a one-mana 1/1 that becomes a 2/2 after it dies. Strangleroot Geist is a two-mana 2/1 with haste that comes back as a 3/2, and Geralf's Messenger drains your opponent for 2 every time it enters. Undying creatures are miserable to trade with, and they pair naturally with sacrifice effects, because sacrificing one gets you a free return trip.

### Fateful hour

Fateful hour is an ability word for effects that turn on when you're at 5 life or less. Gather the Townsfolk makes two 1/1 Humans normally and five of them under fateful hour. Thraben Doomsayer makes a Human each turn and, at 5 life or less, gives your other creatures +2/+2. Clinging Mists is a Fog that also locks down the attackers if you're low. Fateful hour was a flavor win (humans fighting hardest at the edge of the abyss) but rarely mattered in competitive play, since being at 5 life is usually the problem rather than the plan.

### Returning: double-faced cards, flashback and morbid

- **Transform.** Werewolves are double-faced cards that flip to their monstrous side if no spells were cast in the previous turn, and flip back if a player casts two or more. Huntmaster of the Fells is the best of them.
- **Flashback** lets you cast a spell a second time from your graveyard for its flashback cost. Dark Ascension ran three flashback cycles: the "Increasing" rares, which double their effect when flashed back (Increasing Ambition, Increasing Devotion and friends), and two cycles whose flashback costs use a second color, like Lingering Souls and Fires of Undeath.
- **Morbid** checks whether a creature died this turn. Tragic Slip is the headline: a one-mana instant that's -1/-1 normally and -13/-13 with morbid.

The set also leaned harder into the monster tribes, with an allied-color lord for each: Drogskol Captain for Spirits, Diregraf Captain for Zombies, Stromkirk Captain for Vampires and Immerwolf for Werewolves.

## Limited

Dark Ascension was drafted with one pack of Dark Ascension first, followed by two packs of Innistrad. That meant your first pick came from the small set, and the rest of the draft leaned on Innistrad's commons, so Dark Ascension mostly added a layer on top of a format players already knew.

The format kept Innistrad's tribal pairs:

- **White-blue Spirits** played fliers and tempo, and Drogskol Captain made a pile of 1/1 fliers much harder to deal with.
- **Blue-black Zombies and self-mill** used cards like Screeching Skaab and Thought Scour to power flashback and graveyard value. Diregraf Captain rewarded the tribal version.
- **Black-red Vampires** were the aggressive removal deck, with Stromkirk Captain as the payoff.
- **Red-green Werewolves** tried to flip their creatures by making the opponent pass turns without casting spells. Immerwolf kept them flipped.
- **White-green Humans** went wide and pumped, though Dark Ascension gave this pair the least direct support, since the monster tribes got the new lords.

Removal at common and uncommon was what you prioritized. Fires of Undeath, Death's Caress, Tragic Slip, Griptide, Burning Oil and Farbog Boneflinger all answered creatures, and flashback removal gave you two uses from one card. Lingering Souls was close to a bomb in Limited, since four flying bodies for one card can win the air on their own. Undying creatures like Young Wolf and Strangleroot Geist were good at blunting aggressive starts.

The format played at a moderate speed: fast enough that werewolves and Vampires could punish slow keeps, but with enough flashback and two-for-one cards that long games were common. Because the first pack was the small set, the cards you took early were a strong signal for which tribe you were in. Our guide to [reading draft signals](/blog/how-to-draft-mtg-signals) covers that skill.

## The meta: how it played in Constructed

Dark Ascension entered a Standard dominated by Innistrad and Scars of Mirrodin cards, and it fed several of the top decks right away.

- **Wolf Run Ramp.** Brian Kibler won Pro Tour Dark Ascension in Honolulu in February 2012 with a green-red ramp deck built around Primeval Titan, Kessig Wolf Run and four copies of Huntmaster of the Fells, beating Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa in the final. Huntmaster's two bodies, lifegain and flip damage made it a staple of green-red decks for the rest of its season.
- **Zombies.** Gravecrawler and Geralf's Messenger gave black aggro decks a core of resilient attackers, and Diregraf Ghoul from Innistrad rounded out the curve.
- **Spirits and tokens.** Lingering Souls and Drogskol Captain powered white-blue and white-black token decks, and Sorin, Lord of Innistrad was a natural fit next to them.
- **Aristocrats.** After Return to Ravnica arrived in October 2012, Falkenrath Aristocrat became a finisher in black-red-white sacrifice decks, alongside cards like Blood Artist from Avacyn Restored.

No Dark Ascension card was banned in Standard. The set rotated out with the rest of Innistrad block when Theros released in September 2013.

The long tail is where Dark Ascension really shines. In Modern, Lingering Souls was a pillar of token and midrange decks for years, and Thalia became a cornerstone of Death and Taxes in both Modern and Legacy, where her tax on noncreature spells punishes the cheap cantrips those formats depend on. Faithless Looting fueled so many graveyard decks that Wizards banned it in Modern in August 2019, alongside Hogaak, Arisen Necropolis; it was unbanned in December 2024, and as of October 2026 it is legal in Modern, Legacy, Vintage and Pauper. Grafdigger's Cage became one of the standard sideboard answers to graveyard and Collected Company style decks. Young Wolf and Strangleroot Geist found new homes in Modern combo decks built around Yawgmoth, Thran Physician, and Young Wolf is a fixture of Pauper aggro. Our [Modern format guide](/blog/mtg-modern-format-guide) explains how these decks fit together.

In Commander, Mikaeus, the Unhallowed is famous for an infinite combo with Triskelion: Triskelion removes its own counters to deal damage, dies, and comes back with a new counter from undying, over and over. As of October 2026, no Dark Ascension card is banned in Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper or Commander.

## Cards from Dark Ascension worth owning

- **Thalia, Guardian of Thraben**: a two-drop that taxes your opponent's spells in Legacy and Modern Death and Taxes.
- **Lingering Souls**: four flying bodies from one card, a long-time Modern and casual favorite.
- **Huntmaster of the Fells**: two bodies, life and removal in one midrange card.
- **Grafdigger's Cage**: a cheap, colorless graveyard answer for any sideboard.
- **Faithless Looting**: card filtering that powers graveyard decks in Modern and Pauper.
- **Mikaeus, the Unhallowed**: a Commander combo piece and Zombie lord.
- **Vault of the Archangel**: a land that turns any board into a wall of deathtouch and lifelink.

## The takeaway

Dark Ascension is a small set that punched far above its size. Its story is the bleakest chapter of the original Innistrad trilogy, and its cards, especially Thalia and Lingering Souls, defined decks in Modern and Legacy for a decade. If you want to build with it today, white-black tokens in Modern is the natural place to start, using Lingering Souls as the engine.
